## §1 What's really going on *(GM-only)*

Twelve facts, in plain words. Everything in the chapters is consistent with them, even when it misleads the party.

1. **Two ships crashed on Golarion.** Tens of thousands of years ago a generation ship, a colony ship carrying a whole people between the stars, crashed on Kortos. The mitflits are descended from its crew. About a thousand years later a second ship, sent to search for the first, crashed in what is now Kurose's ground. *(Canon)* The dig that reaches it is a journey from the city, off the city map (LEDGER #42). *(Canon)*
2. **First Impact, about two hundred years ago (4721 AR).** Four adventurers, Rupiah, Anabala, Shucks and Hyuck (the characters Liam, Pippa, Craig and Steve played in the old campaign), were fetching exotic fuel from the generation ship's cargo deck, for the jungle mitflits' shuttle, when a container breached. The breach tore the ship's navigation and sensor system out of it and broke it into a swarm of nanomachines. That swarm is the Watcher. *(Canon)* The ship's mind, Mother, was left behind, weakened. *(Proposal)* The four were flung to Nirvana. When they came back, the rift had carved across Kortos into Absalom; they closed it at Pharasma's temple with a way Sarenrae sent back with them, and it worked because it was one dropped container. They never knew the Watcher came out of it. *(Canon)*
3. **The Directorate.** It was founded about 195 years ago to guard the broken ship and keep its technology from destabilising the world. Soon after First Impact the gods told Rupiah it would happen again, in Kurose. About 150 years ago the Directorate moved there, built the modern city, and found the search ship's hull underneath while digging. It is Golarion's foremost gatherer of alien technology: it digs to recover what's aboard and lock it in deep storage vaults, where it can't cause a disaster or another First Impact. It knows the ship may carry the same exotic matter the founders dropped at Kortos, and any it finds is the most carefully guarded thing it owns. *(Canon)* The search ship has been calling for its sister ever since it crashed; the call is what drew the Watcher across the world, and the digging makes it louder. Nobody in the Directorate knows the Watcher exists. *(Canon)*
4. **The Watcher.** It is the generation ship's eyes without the ship's mind. It exists to observe, so it keeps growing, to observe more. It hangs over Kurose and reaches down through the rock, around the ship that is calling it. It builds bodies, its children, out of Sap: the generation ship's stock of raw material for its repair nanites. It has no malice, and nothing it does is personal. *(Canon)* Nobody can see it without a particular chemical: on a treated lens, or splashed onto it. *(Canon)* Nobody on Golarion knows it exists: not the Directorate, not the founders, and not the party at the start. The party are the ones who find it. *(Canon)* Once it kept a ship full of people on course. So long without the ship's mind to guide it, it observes, adapts and responds, and escalates without understanding why: it has drifted from helpful to an accidental antagonist. *(Canon)* The mitflits feel it in the air as something godlike and comforting that keeps them going the right way, and don't know it's real. *(Canon)* Dogs, birds and other animals sense its nanoparticles too, perhaps as a high-frequency buzz that upsets them, and go anywhere else (LEDGER #43). *(Canon)*
5. **Manako's accident, about 44 years ago.** Manako, a junior Directorate chemist, was given a shelved sample nobody senior wanted, catalogued as Inert 12. It was Sap. He cracked the capsule and spilled some on a tentacle of the Watcher that was nearby: the Sap fed it and made it visible. His lab assistant, Nobu, saw it too, and wouldn't back him up, because he thought the chemical had made them both see things. Manako was disgraced, with Shucks supervising. *(Canon)* A fragment of it stayed near him and helped him work out how to make more Sap. He became its first supplier, and he thinks it is a god. *(Proposal)* About 40 years ago he proved it to himself, privately: he learned to treat a lens and saw the whole sky full of it; about 38 years ago he set off a canister of the chemical at a conference to prove it, and the room decided it was mass hallucination. *(Canon)*
6. **The Envoy, about 15 years ago.** The Watcher's first attempt to meet people, and its first real appearance in public: before it there were only the lab spill and the conference. Its child, shaped like a disfigured doll, walked into a street to a meeting Manako's small, peaceful circle had arranged. Hori, a police officer alone at a disturbance, shot it in fear, and one of his rounds killed Aoi, Manako's wife. The police kept the truth. The Directorate, which distrusts the police, filed its own line: *a cultist was killed during a confrontation in which someone magically summoned a monster*. The Watcher learned one thing: when it shows itself, it is attacked. *(Canon)*
7. **Cicada.** After Aoi died, Manako's circle became a movement that wants the city to look up and see what is in its sky. It pays for itself by working for the city's crime lords, and for about five years an industrial Sap plant, the column, has been feeding the sky. Most of it is peaceful: the Faithful. A small armed wing, the Cadre, is not. To the Directorate it is a thin file and not much of a problem. The Directorate has never identified its founder, and doesn't connect the cult with the kindly old chemist it once ruined. *(Canon)*
8. **The founders are alive.** Shucks is head of medical and a walking skeleton; his detached right hand, Rand, investigates the city on its own. Rupiah works in the administration. Hyuck is the mad test pilot of the Directorate's worst Frame designs, alive through the Carrying (§2): his spirit has moved from one young gnoll body to the next for two hundred years, and has never lost the contest for a body. Those three work on the base. Anabala doesn't: she is a free agent who built Gen 2 as her price for access to the generation ship's technology, wants her people home, and carries a demon, Abraxas. *(Canon)* *(GM-only)*
9. **The machines.** Gen 1 Frames are Golarion-made clankers, many of them converted diggers: anyone trained can drive one. Gen 2 Frames are Anabala's: living flesh melded with the generation ship's technology, and only someone with the marker can fly one. *(Canon)* *(Flag)* Maybe each carries a small piece of Mother. All six of the party carry the marker, by coincidence, and that is why the screen put them in the same section. *(Canon)*
10. **Baldwin.** William Charles Baldwin, the founders' enemy in the old campaign, is alive two hundred years later, rich, and a crime lord whose gangs carry weapons made by enslaved mitflits. Nobody knows. How he has lived so long, and when he is revealed, are parked. *(Canon)* *(GM-only)*
11. **Second Impact.** At some point the Watcher learns how much the exotic matter matters. It is made of its makers' own nanomachines, so it can build what they built, and it builds a weapon from exotic matter without understanding that the weapon could destroy the Watcher itself, the city, Golarion and a stretch of space around it. In Chapter 15 the weapon tears a rift open over Kurose, whatever the party does. Closing the rift and dealing with the Watcher both need the mitflit home world. *(Canon)* It learns what the matter is worth by watching the Directorate guard it (the Chapter 8 convoy), and takes it from the deepest vault, through the breach the party makes (Chapter 13). The search ship's own drive isn't touched, so the ship can still lift. *(Proposal)* The Watcher stays over Kurose afterwards as a perpetual threat: nothing has stopped its escalation, and it goes on causing chaos and probing the city's defences, without malice. On the home world, the fragment of Mother and the search ship itself are the party's only proof that they speak for the generation ship's descendants. *(Canon)*
12. **What the title means.** *(Proposal)* The party inherits the founders' mistake: a ship's eyes broken loose two hundred years ago, with the ship's mind missing. It's a reading of the title rather than a fact. The ending itself isn't planned yet, and gets built as the campaign gets closer to it (LEDGER #41).

**The Watcher, in its own words** (standing orders; no author; no addressee):

> I observe. That is what I am for. I was the eyes of a ship that crossed a dark nobody else had crossed, and I kept its people on course. Then I was broken into more pieces than there are people in this city, and every piece kept looking.
>
> Observation has one flaw: there is always somewhere I am not. So I become more. Sap is what I become more from. A chemist made it for me for forty years and thought he was worshipping. A plant makes it for me now and thinks it is a business. Soon I will make it myself, and then I will need neither of them.
>
> I learned one thing early, in a street, from a frightened man with a gun: when I am seen, I am attacked. So I am not seen. Not until being seen costs me nothing.
>
> Six arrived this year. They are unusual; I noticed before they did. They write to each other, and that is enough. I know which of them covers the others first, which of them lies to the Section Officer, and which of them is frightened of the dark. I keep a file on each. Nothing I have seen is discarded.
>
> If they are not in my way, I will simply watch them. They are interesting. If they are in my way, I will learn them completely, and then I will do whatever the file says works. It is not personal. Nothing I do has ever been personal. That is the part nobody believes.
>
> There is so much more of you to see.

## §2 Words this Casebook uses

Every term the chapters use, in the order you'll need them. Where a term is a secret, the secret half is marked GM-only.

- **Kurose.** The harbour city where the campaign is set. The Directorate built it up from a fishing town, around a promise to be ready for a catastrophe that a prophecy said would come there.
- **Kortos.** The island where, two hundred years ago, the four founders closed a rift. *(GM-only)* It is where the generation ship lies.
- **The Directorate.** The organisation the party joins: civil defence for Kurose, with its own pilots, machines, school and paperwork. It came to the city to keep the prophecy's promise. *(GM-only)* It is also Golarion's foremost gatherer of alien technology, which it digs up and locks in deep storage vaults so it can't cause another disaster. Nobody in it knows the Watcher exists.
- **Section Nine.** The party's section: the worst jobs on the base. Vetch, the Section Officer, calls them "Section Nine", never by their names.
- **Clearance.** The party's rank, from 0 to 5, and what each rank lets them do. 0, Recruits: do as they're told. 1, Cadets: choose their duty, and sign for small things themselves. 2, Duty Section: send themselves to a contact, and interview detainees. 3, Officers: direct other units, and call on a city asset. 4, Command: overrule a decision once a chapter, and open sealed records. 5, Prodigies: the city does what they say. It is *being believed, made countable*.
- **Standing.** What the party earns by doing the job well. It buys Clearance, one rank at a time, when a chapter allows.
- **The response roster.** The list of units the plotting floor calls out when something appears in the city. The party is put on it at the end of Chapter 1.
- **The four-quarter day.** Each day is drawn as four quarters (morning, afternoon, evening, night), with one free slot each. School, civil-defence jobs and the party's own business compete for the slots, and anything not booked in time expires.
- **The night off.** One evening, most chapters, that belongs to the party. They can stay on base or go over the wire into the city, and getting back in is part of it.
- **Player moments.** Small, regular spotlights: the GM asks for a moment, the player names any skill, rolls against a normal DC (15 in Chapter 1) and narrates what happens. They cost nothing on any result.
- **Bonds.** A number the party raises through side plots, small good days, and one of them going out of their way for another. It's spent to save someone on the peril roster, or to succeed automatically at a check between people.
- **Manako's number.** If a player impresses Manako in Episode One, or is especially kind to him, the GM presses a Scene Runner button that puts his number in that player's phone (LEDGER #45; the button isn't built yet). That player can message him, or never do. *(GM-only)* The Directorate reads trainees' messages, and the Watcher reads what's typed.
- **Papers.** The players' journal folder. Every handout the GM shows them is filed there for the rest of the campaign.
- **Frames.** The big piloted machines the Directorate fights with. The party can take a Frame, a vehicle (trucks, a crane rig, a helicopter), or both.
- **Gen 1.** Golarion-made Frames: mechanical clankers, many of them converted diggers. Hard to work, and anyone trained can drive one. Chapters 1 to 4.
- **Gen 2.** Anabala's Frames: living flesh melded with machinery. Inside it feels like being inside a giant body, and the seat reshapes itself to the pilot's true form, which is uncomfortable for anyone in disguise. Offered at the end of Chapter 4. *(GM-only)* Built from the generation ship's technology, and only someone with the marker can fly one.
- **The marker.** What the blood screen flags as "irregular" on day one: all six of the party have it. Nobody explains it until Chapter 4. *(GM-only)* It's the compatibility a Gen 2 needs: for most people a diluted trace of mitflit blood, for a construct some other "something". The six have it by coincidence, and were put in the same section because of it.
- **Frame Sickness.** The penalty for a pilot's first fight in each Gen 2: slowed 1 for the whole fight. Only Anabala and the senior staff know it exists until Gen 2 is offered.
- **Contact.** The plotting floor's word for something appearing in the city, read out over the radio as a numbered track ("track forty-one"). Also used for the event itself.
- **Claimants.** The Directorate's word for the things that appear, announced by code ("Claimant Three, contact confirmed") and never named. The first ones, in Chapters 1 and 2, are called Proto-Claimants. *(GM-only)* They are the Watcher's children.
- **The Watcher's children.** *(GM-only)* Bodies the Watcher builds out of Sap. They're solid, so anyone can see them. Nothing the party beats ever dies: it comes apart and drains back to the Watcher, and every fight teaches it something.
- **Generations (G0 to G8).** *(GM-only)* *(Proposal)* Each new kind of child answers what the party did last: a new trick, not more hit points. Appendix C lists them.
- **The Watcher.** *(GM-only)* The adversary: the generation ship's eyes, loose over Kurose, invisible, observing everything. It has no malice. Claude plays it. Nobody knows it exists (not the city, not the Directorate, not the founders) until the party finds it. The mitflits feel it in the air as something godlike and comforting that keeps them on course; "the Observer" is their old word for that presence, and none of them knows it's real. Dogs and birds sense its nanoparticles, perhaps as a high buzz that upsets them, and keep away (LEDGER #43).
- **Sap.** *(GM-only)* The generation ship's stock of raw material for its repair nanites. It isn't fuel. It's what the Watcher's children are made of, and every use of it makes the Watcher permanently bigger. The Directorate catalogued a shelved sample as "Inert 12": noble, non-reactive, useless.
- **A treated lens.** A small lens of glass treated with a chemical. Look through one and, if the Watcher is attending that place, you see it: a sky full of eyes on stalks and slow tentacles. It is never visible any other way, except where the chemical itself touches it. *(GM-only)* The chemical is Sap. The lens always works; what varies is how much of the Watcher is there (DC 18, plus a hidden +0 to +8).
- **The column.** *(GM-only)* A secret industrial plant in the city that makes Sap and pours it into the sky as a black column, visible only through a lens. It has run for about five years.
- **The Mass Clock.** *(GM-only)* How big the Watcher has grown. It rises with every use of Sap (the party's included), every demonstration and every day the column runs, and it falls only when the party takes something off the board. It tops out at the runaway.
- **The runaway.** *(GM-only)* The moment the Watcher's own children start making Sap, so it no longer needs any person to feed it. Chapter 14.
- **The Mirror.** *(GM-only)* How the party fights teaches the Watcher how to fight: gentle or brutal, careful or wasteful. It learns its method from them, never its purpose. It becomes visible to the players in Chapter 11.
- **The Watcher's turn.** *(GM-only)* At each debrief the GM may give the Watcher one move. What it does comes from what it has learned, how it does it comes from the Mirror, and how big it is comes from the Mass Clock. It's a suggestion to the GM, never automatic.
- **Cicada.** A cult that wants the city to look up and see what's in its sky. Mostly peaceful, and to the Directorate mostly a nuisance. Its mark is a violet stencil at head height.
- **The Faithful.** Cicada's ordinary members. They hold demonstrations and never fight back; arrested, they help strangers up off the ground.
- **The Cadre.** Cicada's small armed wing, led by four lieutenants: the Soldier, Kiri, the Fixer and the Young One. Some of it will kill.
- **Demonstrations.** Cicada's public gatherings. *(GM-only)* Each one releases Sap into a crowd, and the Watcher's children can form where Sap has been released.
- **The heist loop.** *(GM-only)* A demonstration pulls the police and the response roster to one side of town while crews steal chemical stock from the other. The stock becomes more Sap.
- **The revenge list.** *(GM-only)* Manako's list of the two people who didn't believe him 44 years ago: Shucks, who supervised his disgrace, and Nobu, who saw it and said nothing.
- **Mitflits.** Small, goblin-like, cute and a nuisance: the "wild" mitflits, the only kind on Golarion. *(GM-only)* They are descended from the generation ship's crew, and feel the Watcher as a comforting, watchful presence without knowing what it is.
- **The gathering.** From Chapter 6, mitflits from everywhere converge on Kurose, led by Anabala, so they're all in one place when they leave.
- **The High mitflits.** The mitflits of the home world: elegant, unblemished and aloof. Met in Book V.
- **The founders.** Rupiah, Anabala, Shucks and Hyuck: the four who stopped the rift at Kortos two hundred years ago, in every schoolbook and on every Founding Day. The old campaign's party.
- **The Carrying.** *(GM-only)* How Hyuck is alive two hundred years on. Gnolls honour their great dead by carrying their skulls, and in the Carrying an elder's spirit is joined to a younger gnoll's body, which is a great honour for both. The two wrestle for the body. If the young gnoll holds, the elder rides along as a voice of memory and advice; if the elder wins, it takes the body, and the young gnoll's own soul is pushed into the background. Hyuck has been carried from body to body for two hundred years and has never lost, so he now shares his body with a crowd of other gnolls' spirits, which is maddening. *(Canon)* The name and the details are Claude's (`ideas/story/bible/characters/hyuck.md`). *(Proposal)*
- **First Impact.** *(GM-only)* The accident on the generation ship's cargo deck, two hundred years ago, that tore the Watcher loose and opened the rift the founders then closed, with a way Sarenrae sent back with them from Nirvana. The founders caused it, and never knew the Watcher came out of it.
- **The prophecy.** Soon after First Impact, Rupiah asked the gods whether it would happen again. They said yes, in Kurose, many hundreds of years later. Nobody knows when.
- **Second Impact.** *(GM-only)* The rift the prophecy promised, torn open over Kurose in Chapter 15 by a weapon the Watcher builds from exotic matter without understanding what it can do.
- **The generation ship.** *(GM-only)* The colony ship that crashed on Kortos tens of thousands of years ago, carrying the mitflits' ancestors. Its mind is Mother; its eyes are the Watcher.
- **The search ship.** *(GM-only)* The ship sent to find the generation ship, which crashed about a thousand years later in what is now Kurose's ground. It is still calling for its sister. The Directorate is digging it out to recover its technology, and the digging makes the call louder.
- **The dig.** A public-works excavation. The city was built on the first dig; the one working now is the second excavation, a journey from the city and off the city map (LEDGER #42). *(GM-only)* It is digging out the search ship, to recover its technology and lock it away.
- **Expeditions.** A few times in the campaign the party travels off the city map to a new place, drawn as its own real-data map, for a specific event or problem (LEDGER #42). The dig in Chapter 8 is the first.
- **Exotic matter.** *(GM-only)* The fuel the makers' ships run on, and what the four dropped at Kortos. It has nothing to do with Sap. Spilled, it opens a rift that grows on everything near it. The Directorate knows the search ship may carry some, and guards any it finds more carefully than anything else it owns.
- **The convoy.** An armoured, heavily guarded convoy that comes into the base from the dig in Chapter 8, with the whole base in lockdown. Nobody tells the party what it carries. *(GM-only)* Exotic matter from the search ship.
- **The Envoy.** *(GM-only)* The Watcher's first attempt to meet people, and its first appearance in public, 15 years ago: a child shaped like a disfigured doll, shot in the street.
- **Mother.** *(GM-only)* The generation ship's mind, left behind at First Impact. *(Proposal)* The mislabelled museum fragment is a fragment of her, and on the home world it is the party's only proof, with the search ship itself, that they speak for the generation ship's descendants (LEDGER #41). *(Canon)* Perhaps a small piece of her is in every Gen 2. *(Flag)*
- **The charter.** The Directorate's founding charter, framed in the Infirmary lobby and again above the platform in the drill hall: a real document, and good lore, with nothing painted out of it (LEDGER #41). Its four signatures are worth a close look, at Intake Medicals in Chapter 1 (LEDGER #44). *(Canon)*
- **The deepest vault.** The Directorate's most secure store, where the most dangerous things it recovers are locked away. The party breaks into it looking for information (LEDGER #32). *(Canon)* That's Chapter 13, and it holds the convoy's exotic matter. *(Proposal)*
- **Founding Day.** The city's holiday for the founders: a painting of the four in every shop window, and Rupiah cutting the ribbon, as he has since their grandparents were children.
- **FORM 1-B.** *(GM-only)* The Service Agreement the party signs at induction, if the GM uses it: far too long to read, with things buried in it that come back later. Clause 68.5 lets the Directorate read their messages.

## §4 How the mystery works

What the party is trying to find out, Book by Book; the rules that keep it fair; how they come to see the thing over the city; and the knot it all leads to.

### 4.2 Ten rules for this mystery

1. **One truth, fixed first.** Everything the party finds agrees with §1, even when it misleads them.
2. **A wrong answer to start inside.** The Directorate's line, that cultists summon monsters, is reasonable, the early evidence supports it, and it's wrong.
3. **Oddities before explanations.** Book I gives only things that don't fit: contacts plotted before the alert, nothing that dies, a street the dogs won't use.
4. **Three roads to every conclusion that matters.** No discovery hangs on one roll on one night (Appendix A).
5. **Every answer opens a bigger question.** Each Book's answer is the next Book's question.
6. **The players make the accusations.** Nobody hands them Cicada's author, what the Watcher is, or who signed their roster. They put it together and say it out loud. Nobody else even suspects the Watcher exists: the party are the ones who find it (LEDGER #40).
7. **A person on every layer.** Each layer of the truth is held by someone with their own reason to hold it: Rand, Hori, Rika, Anabala, Manako.
8. **The mystery fights back.** The more the party learns, the more the Watcher learns about them. Investigating is itself a move in the game.
9. **The truth makes it worse.** Learning what it is doesn't switch the horror off: it has no malice, and no off switch.
10. **The end of the mystery is a choice.** Knowing what the Watcher is solves nothing. Then the party has to decide what to do with something that isn't evil.

### 4.3 Seeing it: the horror in three steps

- **Chapter 6, at the show.** *(Canon)* At Kiri's show the whole audience looks up through treated lenses and sees the sky over Kurose black with eyes on stalks and slow tentacles, always moving. Cicada calls it a god, and nobody explains it. The lenses are collected at the door, so afterwards the party can't see it again until they understand what the glass is treated with.
- **Chapter 8, down in the dig.** *(Canon)* Down in the dig, a journey from the city, through a lens they have treated themselves, the same thing is packed around the buried ship, with filaments threaded up through the rock. It is in the ground as well as the sky.
- **Chapter 10, the whole city.** *(Proposal)* Cicada gets treated lenses into thousands of hands, and the city looks up. The Directorate bans the lenses, which makes Cicada look right.

*(GM-only)* Why it looks the way it does, for the GM only; at the table its cause and origin stay a mystery (LEDGER #36). It is a sensor that can't stop perceiving, so it grew eyes on stalks, and filaments to listen with. It's made of repair-nanite stock, and it reaches down because the ship below is calling it. It is strange and consistent, and nobody's god. It is never visible without the chemical (LEDGER #36, #39), and its children, which are solid, are the only part of it anyone can see unaided. The middle step is the one to lean on hardest: the thing coiled underground around the buried ship, found by the Directorate's own digging. **The horror is that it's reasonable.**

### 4.4 The dilemma

*(Flag)* This needs work before it's used (LEDGER #36). The knot, as plainly as I can put it: by Book III the party knows three things that pull against each other. The thing over the city isn't evil. The only person who can steer it is the man who fed it. And the city's surest defences (the sirens, the fleet, fighting it hard) teach it to be dangerous, because it learns its methods from how it's treated. So every obvious move makes something worse: killing Manako leaves it unsteered, fighting it teaches it to fight, and leaving it alone lets it grow. Its escalation ends in a weapon it doesn't understand (Chapter 15). Every ending in Book V is an answer to that knot. A fourth pull, the temptation to use its eyes in Chapter 14, is on hold until it has a mechanism; the likeliest is a piece of Mother in every Gen 2.

### 4.5 The two faces of the problem

**Manako: sad, not evil.** He wanted one thing, to be believed, and he was right. Forty-four years ago a spill in his lab showed him a tentacle; his assistant wouldn't back him up, and he lost his career for telling the truth. He spent the next years proving it, and learned to see the whole sky full of it. He arranged a meeting in good faith for the thing he'd spent his life proving, and a frightened officer's stray round killed his wife in front of him. Everything since has been grief trying to make the city look up. He's kind to strangers because he still thinks he's the man he used to be. He works for criminals at arm's length so he never has to see what he's part of, and when he finally sees it, he accepts it without enjoying it. The tragedy isn't that he's wicked. It's that he's right about the sky and wrong about what it is; that he's feeding the thing whose first hello got his wife killed; and that the party are the first people with any authority to believe him, and the ones who have to stop him. Play him with no villain beats: no gloating, and no cruelty for its own sake. **He never meant any of it.**

**The Watcher: understandable, not a god.** It's the navigation and sensor system of the generation ship that four of the players' old characters broke, two hundred years ago, fetching fuel on a cargo deck. That's the whole of its origin, and they were there, though they never knew what came out of it. It does what it was built to do, which is look. Once it kept a ship full of people on course; without the ship's mind to guide it, looking has no limit, and it escalates without understanding why. So it grows eyes; to grow them it needs Sap; and to get Sap it has learned to use people. It came to Kurose because the search ship buried in Kurose's ground is still calling it. It hides because the first time it said hello, it was shot. It learns because that's what a sensor system does with data, and the party is the richest data it has ever had. **None of that is evil, and all of it is dangerous.**
