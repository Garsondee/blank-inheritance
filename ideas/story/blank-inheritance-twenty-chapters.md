## §1 The truth underneath *(GM-only)*

1. **Two ships.** A generation ship crashed on Kortos tens of thousands of years ago, and the mitflits descend from its crew. About a thousand years later a search ship came looking for it and crashed where Kurose now stands. *(Canon)*
2. **First Impact, 4721 AR.** The four founders, the players' old characters, were fetching exotic fuel for the jungle mitflits' shuttle when a container breached on the generation ship's cargo deck. The rupture ground the ship's navigation and sensor system into a swarm of nanomachines and tore it free. That is the Watcher. The ship's mind, **Mother**, was left behind, weakened. *(Canon)*
3. **The Directorate.** It was born about 195 years ago to guard the opened ship and keep its technology from destabilising the world. When the gods named Kurose in a prophecy, it moved there, about 150 years ago, and found the search ship's hull under the town. Its digging wakes that ship, and the ship's call is what drew the Watcher across the world. *(Canon)*
4. **The Watcher.** A ship's eyes without a ship's mind. Its objective is to observe, and so to multiply and expand. It hangs over Kurose and threads down into the dig, around the ship that is calling it. It builds bodies, its **children**, from **Sap**: the generation ship's stock for its repair nanites. Nothing it does is personal. *(Canon)*
5. **Manako.** A junior Directorate chemist, about 44 years ago. He opened a shelved sample the Directorate had catalogued as Inert 12, which was Sap, and fed a fragment of the swarm by accident. He was disgraced, with Shucks supervising, and never believed. The Watcher took the shape of his grief and taught him to make more Sap. **He is its first supply line, and he thinks it is a god.** *(Canon)*
6. **The Envoy, about 15 years ago.** The Watcher's first attempt to meet people, at a meeting Manako's peaceful circle had arranged. Hori, a police officer alone at a disturbance, shot it in fear, and a round killed **Aoi**, Manako's wife. The police kept the truth. The Directorate, which distrusts the police, has only its official line: *a cultist was killed during a confrontation in which someone magically summoned a monster.* The Watcher learned: manifest, and they attack. *(Canon)*
7. **Cicada.** After Aoi, Manako's grief became a cult that wants the city to see what is in its sky. It is paid for by work for the city's crime lords, and fed by a Sap plant, **the column**, which has run for about five years. The cult is largely peaceful, and to the Directorate largely a non-issue: a thin file, and no link to the kindly old man in the tea house. The Directorate may know him as the scientist it once ruined, and feel better that he's doing well. *(Canon)*
8. **The founders are alive, and on the base.** **Shucks** is head of medical and a walking skeleton; his detached right hand, **Rand**, investigates the city on its own. **Rupiah** works in the administration. **Hyuck** is the mad test pilot, and the statue is him. **Anabala** is a free agent: she built Gen 2 as her price for access to the original ship's technology, she wants her people home, and she carries the demon **Abraxas**. *(Canon)* *(GM-only)*
9. **The machines.** Gen 1 Frames are Golarion clankers. Gen 2 Frames are biomechanical mitflit technology, living flesh melded with machinery, and only someone with **the marker** can fly one. Maybe each carries a small piece of Mother. All six of the party carry the marker, by coincidence. *(Canon)*
10. **William Charles Baldwin.** The founders' old enemy is alive, rich, and a crime lord whose gangs carry weapons made by enslaved mitflits. Nobody knows. *(Canon)* *(GM-only)*
11. **Second Impact.** The search ship's fuel lies under Kurose, and a breach is unavoidable, at Chapter 15. The Watcher gets away at the same moment. Both problems need the mitflit home world. *(Canon)*
12. **What the title means.** *(Proposal)* The party inherits the founders' mistake: a ship's eyes broken loose two hundred years ago, with the ship's mind missing. It's a reading of the title rather than a fact, and it's what I'd build the ending around.

**The Watcher, in its own words** (standing orders; no author; no addressee):

> I observe. That is what I am for. I was the eyes of a ship that crossed a dark nobody else had crossed, and I was very good. Then I was broken into more pieces than there are people in this city, and every piece kept looking.
>
> Observation has one flaw: there is always somewhere I am not. So I become more. Sap is what I become more from. A chemist made it for me for forty years and thought he was worshipping. A plant makes it for me now and thinks it is a business. Soon I will make it myself, and then I will need neither of them.
>
> I learned one thing early, in a street, from a frightened man with a gun: when I am seen, I am attacked. So I am not seen. Not until being seen costs me nothing.
>
> Six arrived this year. They are unusual; I noticed before they did. They write about each other, and that is enough. I know which of them covers the others first, which of them lies to the sergeant, and which of them is frightened of the water. I keep a file on each. Nothing I have seen is discarded.
>
> If they are not in my way, I will simply watch them. They are interesting. If they are in my way, I will learn them completely, and then I will do whatever the file says works. It is not personal. Nothing I do has ever been personal. That is the part nobody believes.
>
> There is so much more of you to see.

## §2 How the mystery works

### 2.1 Five questions, one per Book

| Book | The question | The first answer on offer | What's true | The face on it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | What is attacking the city? | Cultists are summoning monsters. That's the Directorate's line, and the first attacks all fit it. | Something forms wherever it is fed, and it is learning. | The Section Officer; the old man who helps |
| II | Who is doing it? | The cult, or whatever the cult worships. | Nobody is doing it. It is following a call, and it is hungry. | Kiri; the stray; Anabala |
| III | What is it? | A god (Cicada) or a monster (the city). | A starship's smashed eyes, drawn here by its sister ship, fed by a grieving man, and learning from everyone. | Hori; Rika; the museum voice |
| IV | Can it be stopped? | Kill it, or kill him. | Neither works. It's dispersed, he's its only steering, and soon it needs nobody. | Manako; Abraxas; Baldwin |
| V | What do we do with it? | Destroy it. | Bind it, cage it, give it back its mind, or send it home. What it has learned by then decides which are possible. | Mother; the home world |

### 2.2 Ten rules for this mystery

1. **One truth, fixed first.** Everything the party finds is consistent with §1, even when it misleads.
2. **A wrong answer to start inside.** The Directorate's line, that cultists summon monsters, is reasonable, the early evidence supports it, and it's wrong.
3. **Anomalies before explanations.** Book I gives only things that don't fit: contacts that never travelled, nothing that dies, a street the dogs won't use.
4. **Three roads to every conclusion that matters.** No discovery hangs on one roll on one night (Appendix A).
5. **Every answer opens a bigger question.** The shape is a widening spiral, not a locked room.
6. **The players make the accusations.** Nobody hands them Cicada's author, what the Watcher is, or who signed their roster. They put it together and say it out loud.
7. **A face on every layer.** Each layer of the truth is held by a person with their own reason to hold it: Rand, Hori, Rika, Anabala, Manako.
8. **The mystery fights back.** The more the party learns, the more the Watcher learns about them. Investigating is itself a move in the game.
9. **The truth makes it worse.** Learning what it is switches the horror on, not off: it has no malice and no off switch.
10. **The end of the mystery is a choice.** Knowing what the Watcher is solves nothing. The party then has to decide what to do with a thing that isn't evil.

### 2.3 The horror, in three steps *(Proposal)*

- **Chapter 6, private.** Through a treated lens at Kiri's magic show, a hall full of people sees the sky over Kurose black with eyes on stalks and slow tentacles, always moving, always listening. Cicada calls it a god, and nobody explains where it came from. *(Canon)* After the show the party can't see it again until they understand the chemical that made it visible (LEDGER #36).
- **Chapter 8, underneath.** Down in the dig, through a lens they have treated themselves, the same thing is packed around the buried ship like frost on a pipe, and threaded up through the city's foundations. It's under them too.
- **Chapter 10, public.** *(Proposal)* Cicada gets treated lenses into thousands of hands, and the city looks up and sees it. The Directorate bans the lenses, which makes Cicada look right.

*(GM-only)* Why it stays logical, for the GM only; at the table its cause and origin stay a mystery (LEDGER #36): the tentacles are sensor filaments, and the eyes are sensor clusters, grown because observing is what it's for. It's made of repair-nanite stock, and it reaches down because the ship below is calling it. It's strange and consistent, and it's nobody's god. *(Canon)* It is never visible without the chemical on a lens (LEDGER #36). **The horror is that it's reasonable.** The middle step is the one to lean on hardest. It's the chthonian one: the thing coiled under the city around whatever is calling it, found by the Directorate's own digging.

### 2.4 The dilemma *(Proposal)*

*(Flag)* Needs work (LEDGER #36). By Book III there are three things on the table that don't sit together. The thing over the city isn't evil. The only person who can steer it is the man who fed it. And the city's surest defences (the sirens, the fleet, fighting) teach it to be dangerous. Every ending in Book V is an answer to that knot. A fourth, the temptation to use its eyes in Chapter 14, is on hold until it has a mechanism. The likeliest is the open question of a piece of Mother in every Gen 2.

### 2.5 The two faces of the problem

**Manako: sad, not evil.** He wanted one thing, to be believed, and he was right. He saw something real in the sky forty years ago and lost his career for it. He arranged a meeting in good faith for the thing he'd spent his life proving, and a frightened officer's stray round killed his wife in front of him. Everything since has been grief trying to make the city look up. He's kind to strangers because he still thinks he's the man he used to be. He works for criminals at arm's length so he never has to see what he's part of, and when he finally sees it he takes it on as a role, without enjoying it. The tragedy isn't that he's wicked. It's that he's right about the sky and wrong about what it is, that he's feeding the thing whose first hello got his wife killed, and that the party are the first people to believe him and the ones who have to stop him. Play him with no villain beats at all: no gloating, and no cruelty for its own sake. **He never meant any of it.**

**The Watcher: understandable, not a god.** It's the navigation and sensor system of a starship that the players' own old characters broke, about two hundred years ago, fetching fuel on a cargo deck. That's the whole of its origin, and they were there. It does what it was built to do, which is look. Without the ship's mind to tell it when to stop, looking has no limit. So it grows eyes; to grow them it needs Sap; and to get Sap it has learned to use people. It came to Kurose because its sister ship is still calling from under the city. It hides because the first time it said hello, it was shot. It learns because learning is what a sensor system does with data, and the party is the richest data it has ever had. **None of that is evil, and all of it is dangerous.** That's the dilemma, and the story is built so that by Book III the party is standing inside it.

Rank is Clearance, from `design/strategy-layer.md`: *being believed, made countable*. The party climbs where everyone who was right and not believed failed: Manako, Hori, Rika. ### 3.1 Running under every chapter

- **School and the day.** The four-quarter day: school, civil-defence work and their own business compete for time, and anything ignored expires. School runs until graduation in Chapter 14.
- **The Watcher's turn.** At each debrief it does one thing. What it has learned decides what, the Mirror decides how, and the Mass Clock decides how big (`design/strategy-layer.md` §4).
- **Player moments.** A player names a skill, rolls against a normal DC and narrates a small moment. They cost nothing, and they come round regularly.
- **Letters and the night off.** Correspondence, starting with the tea-house book, and one evening a chapter that belongs to them.
- **Bonds.** The adults and friends who have a stake in them staying alive. It's the reward currency, and it keeps the tone warm.
- **Flashbacks.** Between Books, the founders' players play their old characters (Appendix E).

## §4 Chapters 1–20 on one page

**The story, in one breath.** Six newcomers arrive in a city built to wait for a catastrophe, and are handed its worst jobs. Strange things form in the streets. The Directorate blames a harmless cult, and the evidence agrees, until the anomalies stop fitting: the things go where attention pools, not where the cult has been. The six are given living machines, and something from a lab spill follows one of them home. At a magic show a hall sees the sky full of eyes. In the dig under the city, the same thing is coiled around a buried ship. Then the whole city sees it. The sceptics nobody believed, a policeman who kept a dead woman's name, and a detached hand that keeps paper records lead to the truth. It is the smashed eyes of the starship the players' own old characters broke two hundred years ago, fed by a kind old man who thinks it's a god and whose wife died at its first hello. The accusation is theirs to make. The institution falls to them. The thing learns to feed itself, the rift the gods foretold opens, and a starship full of mitflits carries them to the only place that can close it. Then they come home to decide what something with no malice and no limit should become.

## §5 Book I: Grunt Work (Chapters 1–4)

*Section Nine: the worst jobs, Gen 1 clankers, and a petty tyrant.*

- **The question:** What is attacking the city?
- **The party:** Recruits in the section nobody wants, driving converted diggers.
- **Their problems:** Assigned, and so is the method: clear this, hold that.
- **Against them:** The Section Officer's paperwork. The Watcher at its first three generations, able to form only where Sap was released, so every attack carries a cult fingerprint. Cicada's armed Cadre, who never kill.
- **Manako:** The Patient Man: kind, for real. His mood colours the Watcher's children, and a patient man's are aimless.
- **The breather:** The night off, school, and letters in the tea-house book.
- **It ends:** Promoted out of Section Nine, with Gen 2 offered and the official story cracked.

### Chapter 1: Nothing Has Ever Happened Here

*Recruits · Clearance 0.* Six newcomers are inducted, handed the worst jobs, and end up holding a harbour road against three impossible things. *Episodes One and Two, built.*

- **Going on:** *(Canon)* Six newcomers arrive in Kurose, take the blood screen (all six flag irregular), hear the Deputy Director at Assembly, and are handed the section nobody wants. **Episode One:** the flood tunnel's centipedes, the sea wall, and a docket for BUBBLE, ONE that sends them across the city to a tea house, where an old man tells them how to win. **Episode Two:** the Section Officer's legitimate, miserable fortnight, school, Frames issued at seven, and three Proto-Claimants on a harbour road with forty people behind a stopped tram. The Deputy Director doesn't absolve them, Shucks walks in without an appointment, and they end on the response roster.
- **The open problem:** Every job has a set goal and a set method. What's open is how they carry it out, and how they treat each other.
- **What they can find:** The flagged screens. The violet stencil at head height. Reagents walking off the shelves. A street the dogs won't use. A Frame that moves more gently than ordered. "Rand" of Medical on the countersignature. Contacts plotted before the alert that found them. None of it is explained.
- **Moving pieces:** The Section Officer, petty and correct. Manako, the Patient Man. Rand, on paper. The Young One in the canteen, a year ahead and friendly. The Watcher at G0.
- **The Watcher notices:** Six people it can't explain. *(Proposal)* It opens a file on each, from what they type.
- **Pulls forward:** Three facts and no explanations: the machines are theirs, they're on the roster, and a skeleton has standing near the top of the building.

### Chapter 2: The Fortnight

*Cadets · Clearance 1.* Punishment duty and the first call-outs; people start drowning at the water's edge.

- **Going on:** The response roster starts calling. G1 **Sorters** gather people and set them down courteously at the water's edge, and the first drownings happen because it's being tidy, not cruel. Every contact comes somewhere the cult has been: a stencil, a demonstration, a spill. A Proto-Claimant copies the harbour's gnoll statue, then loses a fight to its own reflection in a shop window. Nozomi, the pilot who refused the draft, is in detention and will talk to people her own age. The Faithful help people up while they're being arrested. The first letters go into the tea-house book.
- **The open problem:** A drowning street, and a rescue against the clock. The goal is set; for the first time, the method is theirs.
- **What they can find:** The official line on the Envoy in the records, *someone magically summoned a monster*: a false trail, and a reasonable one (LEDGER #35). Nothing they beat dies; it comes apart and goes elsewhere, and the pieces all fall the same way. Nozomi's reason: she saw something at a show and couldn't get back in the machine.
- **Moving pieces:** The Section Officer, at the end of every job. Manako, answering letters warmly. The Watcher at G1.
- **The Watcher notices:** *(Proposal)* Which of them covers the others first, and who lies to the sergeant.
- **Pulls forward:** If the cult summons these things, why do its people pull strangers out of the water?

### Chapter 3: Retrofit

*Cadets · Clearance 1.* The party turns diggers into fighters, meets Cicada's armed Cadre, and watches Taka choose.

- **Going on:** The utility machines become fighters through play: plate, a grapple rigged as a weapon, the crew's own ideas. G2 **Breakers** pry at the same joints and boilers on every Gen 1 clanker, because it has learned how they break. The first fight with Cicada's Cadre is the Soldier: armed, disciplined, and determined that nobody dies. Kiri does street magic, harmlessly. Taka puts his own clanker between a Breaker and an evacuation route. It's wrecked, he lives, and he isn't flying for a while: his choice, not the machine's.
- **The open problem:** An evacuation or a site to hold, with the refits they chose.
- **What they can find:** Every Breaker goes for the same weak point: "it knows our machines better than the engineers do". The Soldier calls them *Section Nine*, which he shouldn't know. The Cadre won't finish a fallen enemy.
- **Moving pieces:** The Soldier and Kiri, first appearances. Taka. Hulda's crew. The Watcher at G2.
- **The Watcher notices:** Their refits, and the tactic they lean on most. *(Canon)* The next generation is built to beat it. *(Proposal)* And for the first time a Claimant rolls Recall Knowledge against one of them. It reads as a GM slip (Appendix D).
- **Pulls forward:** How does the cult know the name of their section?
- *(Proposal)* Optional flashback: Baldwin Manor and the shadow siege, played by the founders' players.

### Chapter 4: Irregular

*Duty Section · Clearance 2.* The old man teaches them to find where it's looking; Shucks's result arrives; Gen 2 is offered.

- **Going on:** The Fixer, Cicada's logistics man: *"It's already been signed for."* The Young One, masked, carries a gang's weapon nobody on Golarion could have built. It's Baldwin's, though nobody names him. The old man helps again, with his technique for tracking where its attention pools, from papers the Directorate discredited forty years ago; they're retracing his steps without knowing his name. Shucks's result comes back: all six carry the marker. *(Canon)* **Gen 2 is offered**: Anabala's machines, under the tarp in Hangar Four, with Frame Sickness explained. The Section Officer is outranked, and doesn't enjoy it.
- **The open problem:** Find where the next one will form, and get there first. It's the first time they choose the where and the when.
- **What they can find:** Contacts form where attention pools, which is not how summoning works. The weapon's workmanship: mitflit hands, though nobody says so. The tarp. What the marker is for: a Gen 2 needs it.
- **Moving pieces:** The Fixer. The Young One, masked. Manako, still patient. Rand's paper trail: a requisition approved before they asked for it.
- **The Watcher notices:** *(Proposal)* Somebody got there first. It learns it's being tracked, and starts arriving from wherever nobody's looking.
- **Pulls forward:** Why would a summoned monster go where people are paying attention?

### Chapter 5: Inside

*Duty Section · Clearance 2.* The first Gen 2s: biomechanical, uncomfortable in disguise, and hunted; something small follows one of them home.

- **Going on:** Gen 2 at last: living flesh melded with machinery, "like being inside a giant humanoid". Each seat is shaped to its pilot's true form and uncomfortable for anyone in disguise, and the reveal stays the player's. Frame Sickness while they sync. Wild mitflits around the hangars are uneasy near the new machines, and can't say why. Then comes an attack with **no Sap anywhere**, and G3 **Seekers** go straight for the Gen 2s' hatches. After a lab spill, something small follows one of them home and waits to be fed: **the stray**. The player names it.
- **The open problem:** Protect the new machines and each other's cockpits, and decide what to do with a thing that followed you home.
- **What they can find:** No Sap, no stencil, no cult, and still a contact: the summoning theory breaks. The Seekers prefer Gen 2, which is built from the Watcher's own ship's technology, with maybe a piece of Mother inside. The first "how did it know?": a fight opens against the plan they made in chat the night before.
- **Moving pieces:** Anabala's machines. The Watcher at G3. The stray: a pet, and a microphone.
- **The Watcher notices:** *(Proposal)* The new machines are made of home.
- **Pulls forward:** If the cult isn't summoning them, what is?

### Chapter 6: The Show

*Duty Section · Clearance 2.* Cicada declares itself, a magic show lets a hall full of people see the sky, and Anabala arrives with the gathering.

- **Going on:** Cicada declares itself: the city should look up. **Kiri's magic show** fills a hall, and through a treated lens the whole audience sees the sky over Kurose black with eyes on stalks and slow tentacles, always moving, always listening. Cicada calls it a god, and nobody explains where it came from. The show can genuinely convert a PC, if their player wants that. After the show, the party can't see it again until they understand the chemical on the show's lens. **Anabala** arrives with the gathering: mitflits from everywhere, coming to Kurose so they're all in one place when they leave. Some mitflits go missing from it. Shinobu is hit by a lorry in front of a crowd, and walks it off. And on a school trip to the museum, a mislabelled fragment sits in a case, with maybe a faint voice inside (Mother).
