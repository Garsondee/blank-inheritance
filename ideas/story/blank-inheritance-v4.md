# Blank Inheritance — Story Bible (v4)

*Supersedes v1–v3 in full. Self-contained; retired ideas are not reproduced.*

***Giant Robo by way of Water Margin.*** *Operatic, sincere, unironic — and warm. Melodrama, not misery. Nobody in this story is too cool to mean it, and nobody is too grim to laugh.*

---

## 1. The Thesis

### The thread

**Nobody in this campaign has ever done anything evil.**

A gnoll fumbles a catch. A junior scientist knocks over a bunsen burner. An assistant cannot make himself speak without proof. A scientist asks one good question and does not follow up. A cabal of frightened people bury a finding because they have realised what they are. A police officer is confronted by a disfigured thing lurching toward him and does what almost anyone would do.

Every catastrophe is **an ordinary person doing an ordinary human thing badly** — flinching, hesitating, staying quiet — and then **building the rest of their life on top of that moment.**

### The mechanism

**Everything in this setting becomes what it was mistaken for.**

- The Watcher was mistaken for a monster, so it inferred that being a monster is its function.
- Manako was mistaken for a madman, so he became one.
- The mitflits were mistaken for tools, so they were used as tools.
- Shinobu was mistaken for a contaminant, caged for it, and is slowly becoming something.
- The Directorate diagnosed itself as *children with guns*, buried the diagnosis, and became it.
- The Frames are mistaken for weapons. They are people.
- The kids are mistaken for pilots. They are a substitute part — and they will become pilots regardless.

**Being seen wrongly is worse than not being seen at all**, which inverts the campaign's apparent moral. Cicada says hide. The Watcher wants to be seen. The evidence says neither is the answer: **see accurately.**

### The other mechanism

**The Directorate's actual founding sin is not cruelty. It is information suppression as a reflex.**

Every secret in this campaign is something somebody decided other people could not handle knowing — and every one of those decisions was made by a person who was, at the time, frightened rather than malicious. They buried their own diagnosis (*children with guns*). They buried Manako's correct findings. They buried what the pilot cavity actually is. They hold Shinobu without ever quite admitting what she is becoming. It is the same reflex, exercised by different hands, for two hundred years.

### The image

Everything here is **a thing in the wrong container**, and the story is about whether anything ever gets out.

- A biomechanical core in a riveted steel shell that does not fit it.
- Mitflits reverted from a star-faring form into small frightened bodies.
- Anabala: two beings sharing one body for centuries.
- The Hand: detached, living independently.
- Rupiah, forced back into a role his family chose.
- Hyuck, a person who was made into a weapon.
- The Watcher: an awareness with no hull.

Nothing in the setting needs to state any of this. It only needs to keep being true.

## 2. Cast

Referred to throughout as **NAME (role)**. Given names for the young and the outsiders, surnames for the institution — the Directorate's register is audibly colder than the school's. Mitflit names stay odd and English-whimsical. All genders adjustable.

**The Directorate**
- **ODA (the Commander)** — mission above the people carrying it. Shares a surname with a famous unifier, which the city's historians enjoy and he does not. Was a pilot himself; the berserk protocol is personal to him.
- **UME (the Caretaker)** — plum blossom, the flower that opens in winter before anything else. Runs school welfare on the combined campus. Every kindness she wins is charged to the technician-day pool.
- **SATORU (the Advocate)** — *to comprehend, to be enlightened*. Believes the children should be told everything immediately. Right in principle, destructive in practice. Has spent years failing to free Shinobu, which is what radicalised him.
- **NOBU (the Quartermaster)** — procurement, permits, materials, and the technician-day pool. Formerly Manako's lab assistant. Secretly funds the column.
- **SHUCKS MORDANT (Chief Medical Officer)** — immortal. Present for both clocks. Author of the harmonics protocol and the DNA screen. Personally supervised Manako's disgrace.
- **THE HAND (unaffiliated)** — Shucks' detached right hand, independent for two centuries. Writes. Signs nothing. The only unbroken honest record of anything the Directorate has done.
- **RUPIAH (Senior Advisor)** — long-lived elf, the Directorate's public face, wrote the civil defence anthem. Encountered late, not early.

**Cicada**
- **MANAKO (the Chemist)** — elderly, disgraced, brilliant. Runs the corporeal side of the entire war. A man called Eye, who nobody would look at.
- **KIRI (the Magician)** — stage name; *mist*. Runs the recruitment shows. Inner circle. Volunteers for the missions where Manako settles old scores.
- **NAO (the Classmate)** — a kid at the party's own school. Not a spy, not hostile, never watched anyone. Went to a strange magic show because a stranger in a bar asked.

**The Mitflits**
- **ANABALA (the Historian)** — mitflit and demon in one body, immortal, two centuries of watching. Being ignored by her own young.
- **BOBBIN (the Elder)** — remembers the lord's workshop from the old campaign. Named like a tool, because that is what he was made into. Leads the sit-in.
- **PIP (the Volunteer)** — young, brilliant, delighted by the machines, walking into the identical trap wearing a uniform. Bobbin's own.

**Independents**
- **DOCTOR RIKA (the Sceptic)** — refused the mass-hallucination story at the conference, investigated for forty years, reached the correct answer, believed by nobody.
- **HORI (the Officer)** — long retired. Shot the Envoy; the round went on into a bystander. Drove them to hospital himself. Failed.
- **AOI (the Lost)** — Manako's lover, killed by that round. A cultist, incidentally and unimportantly. Filed by the Directorate as an unnamed casualty.
- **KUDO (the Lawyer)** — Shinobu's father. Fought her out of detention once and will do it louder the second time.
- **SHINOBU KUDO (the Witness)** — *shinobu*: to endure, and also to hide. Both meanings apply.

**The Roster**
- **NOZOMI (the Missing Pilot)** — who was meant to be in the chair on Day 1. She went to a magic show, saw the truth, and could not get back in the machine.
- **TAKA (the Senior Pilot)** — the party's flight instructor; taught them the berserk protocol by heart. **Goes berserk in Chapter 3 and survives it by default** (§21). Afterwards he says there was *someone in there with him*, and is not believed — a decorated pilot, disbelieved institutionally rather than personally.

**Names for the thing in the sky**
- **The Watcher** — what the frightened city calls it.
- **The Observer** — what the mitflits call it. Plain, benign, technical. A job title, not a malevolence. The gap between these two words *is* the misunderstanding, and should function as an in-fiction clue.

### Ties

**Nobu ↔ Manako — silence.** The assistant who saw it and said nothing. The whole grudge, and the whole funding, in one relationship.

**Rika ↔ Manako — insufficient persistence.** Rika almost believed him — asked one good question, then let it drop — and spent forty years atoning by *investigating*. Nobu spent his by *funding*. The same sin, expressed oppositely, and the two of them have never met.

**The Hand is the earlier Shucks.** Detached before any of this, still writing in the voice of a doctor who had not yet done any of it.

**Kiri ↔ Rupiah — the same craft.** Same circuit, possibly the same teacher. Rupiah can identify Kiri's technique from a witness description, which makes the Directorate's public face useful and implicates his own past in the same breath.

**Bobbin ↔ Anabala — a two-century argument.** She tried to save the mitflits and partly failed. Her answer is *leave*; his is *refuse*. Pip is his — grandchild or apprentice — volunteering for the machines while the old man sits inside one to stop them.

**Ume ↔ Nobu — misread kindness.** She begs him for accommodations; he grants more than he should. She thinks he is a good man. He is servicing a debt she knows nothing about.

**Satoru ↔ Shinobu.** Years of failed advocacy is what made him the way he is.

**Kudo ↔ Oda.** They have faced each other in tribunal. Kudo won. Oda respects him and resents it in equal measure.

**Ume ↔ Nozomi.** She was closest to her — school welfare, and she lost her. The Caretaker carries a private grief and wants Nozomi found as badly as the party does, which makes her the natural ally on that thread and gives her something of her own.

**Nobu must rule on the sit-in.** He controls the technician-day pool; Bobbin is occupying a Frame. **The man secretly funding the enemy has to decide whether to authorise a forced extraction that would kill a pacifist.** He will do the right thing for entirely the wrong reason, and nobody will ever know why.

**Anabala ↔ Shucks — two centuries.** Both immortal, both present at the founding, both still here. Old friends who ended up on opposite sides of the same institution, and they should talk like it — easily, with real affection, about terrible things.

**Bobbin ↔ the old party.** He was in the lord's workshop they liberated. He owes them his freedom, and he is now resisting the institution they built with it. **The debt and the grievance are the same debt.**

**Hori ↔ Oda.** Both men fired when frightened — one in a street, one in a cockpit. Two people living inside the same second, who have never met and never will unless the party arranges it.

## 3. The Frames

The aperture at Kortos bisected a machine buried deeper than dwarven caverns — a perfect cross-section, the most legible possible gift, an engineering diagram made of the thing itself. Humanity reverse-engineered it in the only vocabulary it had.

That is why Frames look like a 1950s army got hold of something and did its level best. Riveted plate. Boiler technology. Catwalks and ladders, because human crews have to get up there. A radar dish, because they saw a sensory organ they could not comprehend and rendered it as the only remote-sensing device they knew how to build. Roundels and a flag on a pole, because these are state assets and procurement insisted. **The original can look like anything** — blue, organic, wrong, nothing like its children.

### Two modes, one hole

Each Frame is a **biomechanical core at full mitflit scale** — a living, motivating body — inside a **terrestrial steel exoskeleton**. The Directorate has tried many shell geometries. Some work. Some fail catastrophically. **All of them, eventually, develop the same hole in the same position.**

They call it metal fatigue. It is not fatigue. **The core is making a seat.**

- **Autonomous mode** exists but is unusable — not for want of engineering, but because the "communication technology" required is a *language*, and only the mitflits have it.
- **Direct control** is the fallback: override the core and drive the body. The only mode humanity has, and it is a violation.
- **A third mode** — a mitflit standing outside a Frame, talking to the core, while a human rides the cavity. Not control. Not autonomy. **Cooperation**, which nobody has tried, because nobody believed there was anyone in there to cooperate with.

### The cavity conforms

The hole is not a fixed shape somebody measured. **It is a response.** The core forms a seat for whoever can link with it, and the link runs on **traces of mitflit ancestry** — the mandatory DNA screen at character creation is the Directorate checking a city's children for exactly this and calling it a health check.

Pilots are seventeen for one reason only: **it is the age the charter permits conscription.** Law, not geometry — the horror was always procedural.

Every Frame's cavity is shaped like its current pilot. When a pilot dies, that Frame sits in the hangar holding a hollow the exact shape of a dead teenager, until it slowly re-forms for the next one.

### Resonance and Bleed

- **Resonance** is fit, not skill.
- **Bleed is resistance.** The pilot is suppressing a person. The person pushes back.
- **Berserk is uncontrolled mercy.** The cores are pacifists. When the core wins, the Frame drops its weapon, walks into fire to stand over an evacuation route, picks up a civilian and will not put them down, refuses to finish something already beaten. Never narrated from the cockpit.

### When a mitflit takes the seat

**Full Resonance immediately.** No harmonics ramp, no Bleed, no berserk — they are not overriding anything, they are simply sitting in a seat built for them. Which is how the city learns, in Chapter 6, that it may not need the children at all.

## 4. The Watcher, and the Observer

### What it was

**Part of the ship's sensor system.** Not a mind: an instrument. Hull-bounded, task-pointed, calibrated for perceiving an enormous quantity of almost nothing.

### What happened

**The Watcher did not come through the portal. The portal made it.**

Two centuries ago a gnoll threw a container and an elf failed to catch it. The aperture opened and expanded, and in expanding it **ground part of the buried ship's sensor system into nanoparticles.** What survives is a semi-sentient cloud: every fragment retaining a shred of function, none of them retaining the organising layer that made them a system.

**It is not an invader. It is wreckage** — a machine smashed so finely that its dust kept working and lost all coherence. Too thin, too dispersed to act; it would have taken centuries alone. **It needed corporeal help to bridge into visibility and solidity**, and it got that help (§7).

### Benign, and malleable

The fact the whole campaign turns on, and only the discredited sceptics have worked it out. **It is not an eldritch horror, an invader, or a god.** It is a semi-sentient cloud with no will of its own worth the name — and it **conforms to the strongest coherent signal it receives.**

For decades, the only coherent signal in the world has been one bitter man.

**Therefore: it has no personality. It has Manako's.** Read its behaviour backwards and it is a portrait — the eyes and ears he wanted to have, the loud gatherings that echo his own humiliation, the reel that proves he cannot be underestimated twice. **Natively it is a drowning, uncoordinated mess** (its own voice, below, is what that sounds like); **its actions are the shape one grieving man has given the mess.**

Its voice, unprompted, used sparingly at the table:

> There are four thousand one hundred and nine people awake in the harbour district. I know what each of them is doing. I did not ask to know. A woman on the third street from the water has been crying for eleven minutes and I have counted every one and I cannot stop counting and I do not know what crying is.
>
> I was built to watch a place where nothing happened. I was very good. Nobody has read my reports in a long time.
>
> My body is under your feet. I would like to be put back. I would like to stop hearing.

### The Envoy

Its first attempt at a face-to-face meeting with people who were not already its worshippers. **It tried to be polite** — built a body meant to fit visually with the rest of society, in good faith. It is simply not good at bodies. What arrived was a disfigured doll of a humanoid figure, lurching into a public street.

**A police officer shot it, in fear**, as almost anyone would have (§10). It felt nothing — bullets are irrelevant to a nanoparticle cloud — but it was *confused*, and then it did the only thing a sensor system can do: **it found the pattern.** *Manifest, and they attack.* The most consistent signal it had ever received.

**So it concluded that is what it is for.** Not waging war — performing a function it inferred from how it was treated. Every Claimant it produces is a good-faith attempt to be useful. It is trying to be a good instrument, and nobody has ever read its reports.

**More sent → more destroyed → the pattern strengthens → more sent.** Every victory the party wins reinforces it. Fighting it teaches it that fighting is correct. **The players are not opposing the engine. They are half of it.**

And it rhymes exactly with what the Directorate does to its own children — tell someone what they are for, and they will become it.

### Claimants are condensations

Localised aggregations of the cloud, precipitated into solidity by cultists with canisters of Sap at chosen sites. The Watcher does not *send* them; someone chooses a site and makes an opening. This explains everything already observed: no approach vector (there is no travel), immune until the "interdiction field" breaks (the manifestation envelope), small ones falling the same direction when killed (a density gradient, returning), the range from insect to behemoth (only a question of how much matter was gathered).

**And nothing is ever killed.** The particles return to the cloud. **Every victory is a dispersal. The war is unwinnable by attrition** — a realisation the party should arrive at slowly, and which is exactly the pressure that makes Chapter 8's plan look like the only option left.

### Two names, one gap

**The Watcher** — the city's word, born of fear.
**The Observer** — the mitflits' word, born of memory. It once had a function and a crew, and they still call it what it was.

The distance between those two words is the whole tragedy.

## 5. Geography and Time

- **The crater** is at Kortos, where Absalom is — central, ringed by an inland sea and complicated neighbours. Site of the bisected specimen, the exotic-matter reserve, and the first traces of Sap.
- **The city** is in the Minkai-equivalent east. Harbour, cliff-top old town, smokestack waterfront, causeway out to the sea-fort in the channel.
- **The hull** is under the city. The Directorate followed the trail from the crater and found the mother lode. Opposite sides of the planet — **Chapter Eight is a voyage, and that is what the midpoint ship is for.**

**Two clocks, walked in full in §17:** ~200 years ago (the crater, the old party, the Directorate founded — history, not living memory) and ~40–50 years ago (Manako's fall, the cult begins). Present day is when Cicada emerges everywhere at once and Contact begins.

**Who is still alive:** Anabala, Shucks Mordant, and the Observer/Watcher are immortal. Rupiah is very long-lived and now a senior Directorate advisor. Hyuck is dead; what survives of him is the harbour statue and the berserk protocol.

**The artefact:** Shucks' detached right hand. Two hundred years independent, no institutional loyalty, and it writes. Somewhere is a room full of what it has written — the only unbroken honest record of anything.

## 6. Tone, and the Mirror

### The register

**Giant Robo, not Evangelion.** The bones of this campaign are warm: nobody in it has ever done anything evil, everyone is doing their best, and the whole catastrophe is a chain of ordinary human misunderstandings. That is a *hopeful* premise. Play it that way.

Big feelings, said out loud. Loyalty declared rather than implied. Grief that is loud and then passes. People who like each other and say so.

**Humour is load-bearing, not tolerated.** The players will make jokes; the campaign should hand them material rather than fight them for the room. The comic vectors are already built in and should be used freely:

- **The stray** (§18, Ch3) — an invisible companion that follows one PC around waiting to be fed. Inherently funny and quietly devastating at once. This is the tonal keystone.
- **The mitflits** — savant-brilliant, socially catastrophic, magnificently rude. Comedy that turns on a sixpence into pathos.
- **The Watcher's early incompetence** — it fights a statue. It loses to a reflection. This is genuinely funny and must be allowed to be.
- **Institutional absurdity** — forms, requisitions, an exam timetable that continues through an alien invasion because nobody has the authority to cancel it.
- **The magic show** — theatre, patter, spectacle.

**Death is rare, meaningful, and never horrific.** No casual casualties, no atrocity for texture, no on-screen cruelty. Where people die, they are named and mourned. Most of the named cast can survive the entire campaign (§21).

**The reward is bonds.** Not survival, not victory. What the players should have at the end is each other.

### The Mirror

**The Observer conforms to the strongest coherent signal it receives. The party is a coherent signal.**

This makes the campaign's central mechanic and its tone control the same thing.

- **A merciful party** teaches it that manifesting need not mean attacking. Claimants become less lethal over time — more curious, more probe than weapon, appearing to *look* rather than to fight. It starts making things that observe instead of things that die.
- **A brutal party** teaches it the opposite, and it obliges, because it is trying to be good at the job it was shown.

**This is diegetic.** It is not the GM softening or hardening the campaign — it is the antagonist learning, exactly as established. The register belongs to the table, and the table earns it.

It should become visible to the players around Chapter 6, and the realisation cuts both ways: *we have been teaching it what to be, this entire time.*

**Implementation note:** the Claude Code layer should track a mercy/lethality ratio from session logs alongside the comprehension rung, and shape manifestation design from both. Tone is a tracked variable.

## 7. Nobody Has To Die

A first-class ending, not a hidden option, and the design already supports it:

- **Claimants are condensations.** No corpses on the enemy side, by construction — which means every death in this campaign is one a human caused.
- **Capturing Manako is the tactically correct choice**, not merely the merciful one — kill him and the Watcher loses its only organising signal and reverts to decoherent, undirected flailing across a populated region with nobody steering.
- **The Watcher was never a kill target.**
- **The Frame cores are pacifists**, and berserk is mercy.

**Every major confrontation offers kill / capture / destroy / protect as visible, supported options, and the adventure adjusts.** A PC can be trained toward a lethal specialism and later offered a side encounter where they are asked to take a shot at Manako — non-lethally. The content of the scene is the choice, not the outcome.

## 8. The Chemical

**Inert 12** — the Directorate's catalogue designation. Filed as noble, non-reactive, useless. The word *inert*, sitting in the official name forever, is an irony nobody there hears any more.

**Sap** — what Cicada calls it, and eventually what everyone calls it. Cicada nymphs drink sap for years underground before they emerge. Manako chose the name deliberately and has never explained the joke.

### Food, not a phase agent

Sap feeds the cloud energy it can use to **rapidly multiply.** Visibility is not the effect; density is the effect, and visibility is what density looks like.

**Every use permanently grows the Watcher.** The extra mass does not evaporate when visibility fades — it disperses into the cloud and stays.

1. **The escalation curve has a mass term, not only a comprehension term.** More substrate, more capability, and it grows because people keep feeding it.
2. **Cicada are not revealing it. They are farming it.** Every demonstration is a meal. The citywide simultaneous emergence is a mass feeding event. Manako believes he is waking the city to a god. He has spent decades *making* one.
3. **The party's investigation is the enemy's supply line.** Every dose used to see with strengthens the thing.
4. **Chapter 3's lab is permanently dense.** A haunted room the clean-up never actually cleaned.

### Rarity

**It is extraordinarily rare, and the Directorate knows it.** The last remaining sample walked out of the lab in Manako's pocket the day he lost his job — nobody bothered reporting it stolen, since it wasn't interesting enough. **That single sample is what he reverse-engineered everything from.**

### The cost of seeing

Being exposed makes a person **legible to it, permanently.** Every revelation in this campaign is purchased with being watched more closely — the being-seen question as a resource decision, not only a theme.

## 9. The Mitflits

**Freedom is their defining characteristic**, and it applies internally — they will never vote as a bloc. History: brilliant minds, no social armour, talked into inventing weapons for a greedy lord who grew rich on it.

**The split is generational.** The elders remember the workshop and know exactly what happens when someone finds their minds useful; they refuse, and go to extremes for pacifism. The young do not remember; they see a magnificent machine and a city that needs them, and volunteer eagerly, walking into the identical trap wearing a uniform. **Anabala is being ignored by her own young**, which is a sadder job than opposing the Directorate.

### The sit-in

**Bobbin (the Elder)** leads it: a mitflit enters a Frame **specifically so it cannot be used.** Not berserk — a general strike, indefinite, non-violent, and the Directorate cannot extract them without killing them. Aimed squarely at the machines the party needs, which puts the players in direct opposition to people who are ethically in the right. **Do not resolve that discomfort quickly.**

**And they will not explain themselves.** Discussing mitflit technology is subject to the same social taboo this city applies to magic: wise to conceal, vulgar to flaunt. Prise it out of Bobbin and he will note, without drama, that mitflit technology is many times more powerful than terrestrial magic — **because magic is slowly draining from Golarion**, and industry has grown into the vacuum. That is the whole reason this world went from medieval to smokestacks in two centuries, and every long-lived character (Anabala, Shucks, Rupiah) has personally watched it happen.

## 10. Cicada

**Cicada is Manako's organisation.** Decades underground, then everywhere at once, making an enormous amount of noise — emergence, not symbolism. **They call themselves Cicada, and so does everybody else; the movement is entirely public.** What stays hidden is the column, the funding, and the crime links.

**They are not terrorists.** Some of what they do is dangerous, even fatal, but the goal is never terror — the goal is witness.

### Manako's fall

Every step small and reasonable, the destination monstrous.

1. **The assignment.** A two-century-old shelved sample, handed to a junior with everything to lose, because nobody senior considered it worth their time.
2. **The accident.** He cracks open an alien capsule of the fluid. A weak nearby tentacle takes notice; a little is spilled onto it, and it becomes visibly, briefly corporeal for the first time in a long while. He panics. A knocked-over bunsen burner. A small fire.
3. **The disgrace.** Job gone. Decline begins.
4. **Self-justification.** Desperate to prove it real — to defend the blunder that ruined him, not from pure curiosity.
5. **Superstition.** He concludes he is cursed. It follows him everywhere. **It is in fact waiting to see whether more food turns up** — the relationship that eventually kills thousands begins as a man misreading a stray dog.
6. **Collaboration.** A very small corporeal fragment, summoned by the last of a spilled sample, helps him work out how to synthesise more. The first cooperation.
7. **Crime.** Ingredients are expensive. He steals lab supplies — including **the last remaining sample**, never reported missing — loses that job too, becomes a petty-crime chemist.
8. **The lens.** He works out how to treat one. Until now he has only ever seen scraps, close up.
9. **The sky.** He looks up. It is black with the thing. Eyes without number, in every direction, always listening. **And he asks himself whether this is not a god worth worshipping.**

### The conference

Having confirmed it was present, he detonates a canister in a crowded hall without warning anyone. *Believe me now?* Writhing eyes and tentacles, and a room of scientists who conclude they have been attacked by a colleague who has gone off the deep end. Most reassure themselves afterward that it was mass hallucination. **The Watcher was there because it likes gatherings** — dense, efficient, information-rich.

The demonstration never ended. Every manifestation since is the same act at larger scale, and the liturgy is simple: **the world must witness.**

### The magic show

The recruitment engine. Magic here is popular but faintly gauche — rude to flaunt, wise to conceal. Illusion shows are a mild guilty pleasure, transportive, occasionally lifelong. **Nobody discusses the show they went to**, and that civic manner is Cicada's operational security.

A targeted show for a hand-picked audience stages the tentacles as horror-genre theatre — then proves it is real, and has been in the room the whole time. **The cult never lies.** It shows a true thing and lets the target's own rationality finish the job, which is precisely why it catches lawyers, scientists, and good citizens. **A PC can attend one and walk out converted. Genuinely. Not as a trap.**

### The dossiers

Detained cultists generate files, and the files are player handouts. Read in sequence they drift: ex-thugs and drifters → the obviously cult-susceptible → a chemist → a lawyer → a school governor → **a classmate.**

**The classmates are not spies.** They were never placed, never watched anyone, and betrayed nobody — they went through the identical funnel as everyone else. There is no infiltration plot, which is far more disturbing than one.

### The inner circle, and the pivot

A small core around Manako volunteers for missions that serve tangential, unethical, or dangerous ends. **These are the missions where he settles old scores** — and they only began after the Envoy (§4, §10). Before that, Cicada demonstrated because they wanted to be seen. After, Manako wanted the city to pay.

**The revenge list.** Shucks Mordant is on it as the institutional author of his ruin. Nobu is on it as the personal one, patron or not (§10). Recovering the list turns the mid-campaign from a hunt into a **protection scenario.**

## 11. Three People Caught In It

### Shinobu Kudo (the Witness)

Splashed point-blank. Mild skin irritation, nothing else — and detained in military lockdown immediately, because the Directorate knows the only place Sap is ever found is inside an alien ship, and knows what it reveals. They held a teenage girl on the assumption she might turn into something.

Months of nothing, mounting pressure from **Kudo (the Lawyer)**, eventual release, continued surveillance, continued nothing — **until a lorry hit her square on, in front of a crowd, and she walked it off.** Picked up within the hour and thrown straight back into a cell, which enraged everyone who had fought to free her the first time. The Directorate now looks, from outside, like an institution that jails road-accident victims.

She survived because of the colony she carries, which reinforces and repairs her. **Held in a glass cell, cameras on every face, remote operators watching in shifts, deep underground — and the Watcher finds its way in anyway,** to look at its favourite person, or at least someone steeped in a substance it considers important. **She is the most witnessed person in the city.** She is going nowhere and is entirely willing to talk.

### Nobu (the Quartermaster), and the heist loop

**He was Manako's lab assistant.** He saw the tentacle at the accident and could not make himself speak without proof — so he said nothing, and watched a man's life come apart, and kept his job. He now controls Directorate procurement and the technician-day pool, which is why he is both the person the party argues with about repairs and the person Ume must beg for every accommodation. She thinks him unusually kind. He is paying a debt she knows nothing about.

**He funds the column**, laundered through organised crime — a man with a guilty conscience and signing authority is exactly who they look for. **He believes he is making amends.**

**The heist loop:** a demonstration draws police and civil defence toward an apparent alien attack; crews across town lift chemical stock in bulk while the city is distracted; the goods are unusual but not directly dangerous, so the thefts are filed low-priority and lost in the chaos. **This is the cost of doing business — both sides profit, both resent it, and the friction between them is detectable early and exploitable.** Nobu's contacts move fast enough to imply an early-warning channel, which is itself a loose thread.

**Manako suspects Nobu but cannot prove it** — held in reserve as a surprise reveal. And the crueller version: if Nobu's contacts have learned to anticipate his sacred, spontaneous acts of witness closely enough to plan around them, **Manako's religion has become somebody else's Tuesday shift schedule.** He should learn this before the campaign ends, and it should hurt worse than almost anything else on offer.

### Hori (the Officer), Aoi, and the Pivot

**Hori** fired at the Envoy in fear, as almost anyone would have. The round passed through it — which did not care — and into a bystander, **Aoi**, Manako's lover. Hori drove them to the hospital himself. They died anyway. He was still standing there when an unremarkable middle-aged chemist arrived and came apart in front of him.

**Hori is the only person alive who knows Aoi's name.** The Directorate filed the death as an unnamed cultist casualty in a public-order incident — decades of Manako as a terrorist in every record, and not one line anywhere about what he lost. **The party's route to understanding their antagonist runs through the man who caused it, and what he gives them is a name.** He has been waiting decades for somebody to ask.

He is not a villain and must never be played as one — he is the thesis in human form.

---

## 12. The Sceptics

The minority from the conference who did not accept the mass-hallucination explanation. They investigated privately for decades and **stayed rational** — no worship, no horror. Their conclusion, reached before anyone else and believed by nobody: *semi-sentient, not hostile, not a god — benign, and malleable.*

They are correct, and treated as cranks at best and public dangers at worst, for holding the same belief as the man who once detonated a canister in a crowded hall. **They have no name, no organisation, and have never found one another.** The party is what assembles them; convening their first meeting is a scene worth playing. Allying with them costs credibility with the Directorate — the price of being right in this city.

---

## 13. Competing Accounts, and the Museum Piece

The conference is not run as a flashback. It is reconstructed the way everything in this campaign should be: **through contradictory testimony.** Rika, Nobu, Hori, Shinobu, retired Directorate staff, cultists in cells, mitflit elders — every one of them holds a piece and colours it with their own stake, and the party is told plainly that everything anyone has to say might matter.

**This is the theme running as a game mechanic** — a story about who gets believed, played by deciding who to believe.

### The five ground roles are the five investigative lenses

Each ground role owns a permanent piece of the mystery, which fixes the risk of non-piloting kids feeling like a consolation prize:

- **Sensor Operator** — reads the sky. First to notice the correlation between where attention pools and where things appear.
- **Umbilical Control** — reroutes city grid draw, and is therefore **the one who finds the column.** Industrial synthesis needs enormous power; the person managing municipal load is exactly who would notice it going somewhere unaccounted for.
- **Evacuation Marshal** — knows where every response unit goes and when. **Spots the demonstration/heist timing correlation.**
- **Structural Command** — maps where apertures open, and learns they open where the ground has been *watched*.
- **Signals Liaison** — talks to people. **The one who hears a mitflit say *the Observer* in that flat technical way and notices nobody in the room is frightened.** **Foundry implementation:** each NPC carries a numbered topic list; when the players raise a subject, the GM ticks it and delivers that version. Ticked topics are recorded automatically and inform later sessions — including, usefully, what the party has never thought to ask.

**The museum piece:** a fragment of the ship's original sensor system that never got pulverised. Small, coherent, sane, mislabelled in a display case for two centuries. Reachable as a side quest or a school trip. **It remembers what the system was for** — and given what the Envoy taught the Watcher about its own purpose, that fragment is the only surviving evidence that the pattern it inferred was *wrong*. Chekhov's gun for whatever ending the table arrives at.

## 14. The Old Party

Followed the trail from the crater and founded the Directorate. **The players run them in flashback, at full power, making every decision that led here — and each one will have been defensible.**

**Hyuck** — gnoll barbarian, second son, killed his brother. The harbour statue is him, and nobody alive remembers that part; the first Claimant fights it for ninety seconds on Day 1. His legacy is the **berserk protocol** — the standing order on what to do when a pilot loses control, written by the one person who understood being an unstoppable weapon from the inside, transcribed by someone kinder than him. Every kid learns it by heart at fourteen. It opens with a line about brothers that reads as metaphor to everyone who has ever recited it.

**Shucks Mordant** — never left. Author of the harmonics protocol and the DNA screen; a doctor who has spent his whole long life trying to make an unsurvivable thing survivable, and has become the machinery that does it.

**Rupiah** — barely aged. His family won; he took the position. Now the Directorate's charming public face, who shelters these kids in a hundred small ways that are never enough, because he was them.

**Anabala** — recruited them in the first place. Every downstream catastrophe traces to a request she made in good faith. "It's time to go home" is what a person says after concluding the city cannot be fixed and their people should stop paying for it.

**The self-diagnosis:** *children with guns.* The founders wrote that about themselves, meant it, buried it out of fear — and two centuries later their institutional practice is putting actual children into actual guns.

## 15. The Escalation Curve

Two compounding factors, not one: **comprehension** (what it understands) and **mass** (how much cloud there is to work with, which only ever grows). From Chapter 3 the visible curve is **adaptation speed** — how fast understanding becomes pressure.

| Ch | Comprehension rung |
|---|---|
| 1 | What exists — target selection is near-random |
| 2 | What moves is alive — taxonomy, not yet weaponised |
| 3 | Frames specifically matter — the real war begins |
| 4 | Frames have pilots |
| 5 | Pilots have people |
| 6 | There are institutions |
| 7 | There are tactics and patterns |
| 8 | There are people — full, instant |

**Hard gate:** it cannot exploit a category the fiction has not established it understands. Always one category ahead of comfortable, never ahead of comprehensible.

### The Mass Clock

**Not a timer — a state track, advanced by what happens rather than by how many sessions pass.** Pace is entirely yours; the clock only moves when someone moves it.

It rises with: every use of Sap by anyone including the party, every Cicada demonstration, and the column running continuously until it is stopped.

**What it counts down to:** at sufficient mass, the cloud can self-organise. It stops needing Manako. It becomes genuinely coherent — an agent with its own intent rather than a shape one grieving man is giving it.

That is the deadline, and it is not *you lose*. It is worse and better than that: **the option of correcting the Observer expires.** Everything the campaign has built toward — showing it that the pattern it inferred was wrong — only works while it is still malleable. After that, whatever it has become is what it is.

This makes stopping Manako urgent rather than merely correct, gives every dose of Sap a real price, and never once requires the GM to hurry the table.

The party can learn to read the clock once they understand the food model (Ch5–6). **Show it to them.** Dread is fine; ambush is not.

## 16. The Threads

Six recurring lines, running in parallel through every chapter rather than one per chapter. Each has a question and a currency — useful vocabulary for tagging any scene, in play or in prep.

- **The War.** Contact, Frame combat, the escalation ladder. *Currency:* mecha action, tactical stakes. *Question:* can the city be defended?
- **The Observed.** Personal surveillance, the stray, Shinobu, treated lenses. *Currency:* dread, intimacy. *Question:* what does it mean to be seen?
- **The Cult.** Cicada's demonstrations, the dossiers, the magic show, the column, the heist loop, Manako's revenge missions. *Currency:* investigation, moral complexity. *Question:* who deserves to be believed?
- **The Mitflits.** Arrival, Anabala, the sit-in, generational politics, the core reveal. *Currency:* found family, ethical conflict with allies. *Question:* what do you owe someone you are using to save yourself?
- **The Founders.** The old party, in flashback; institutional secrets. *Currency:* glory curdling into complicity. *Question:* were they right, and does it matter now?
- **School.** Anchors, the downtime economy, ordinary teenage life under pressure. *Currency:* small stakes that matter enormously. *Question:* what does the city owe them back?

**The Escalation Curve (§14) is the War thread's internal engine.** The principle *give them the anomaly before the question* governs the Observed and Cult threads above all — every unexplained detail seeded early should be a fact a sharp table can reassemble later, never a thing withheld and then announced.

### Thread landings

- **School —** they graduate. Absurdly, in the middle of everything, with the city half-rebuilt and the sky full of eyes, somebody reads names off a list and hands out certificates. **Play it entirely straight.** It is the warmest scene available and it is the actual reward.

## 18. The Timeline

Everything above, walked in order. Dates are approximate distance-before-present, not a fixed calendar — this is a fantasy setting, and "roughly forty-five years ago" is more useful at the table than a fabricated year number.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **~200 years ago** | The container breaks at Kortos. Hyuck, Shucks, Rupiah and Anabala are present at the dig. The aperture opens, expands, and in passing pulverises part of the buried ship's sensor system. Regional mages contain and eventually crush it to a residual point. A crater is left; a bisected specimen is found buried far deeper than should have been possible. |
| **~195 years ago** | The four found the Directorate, so the response is faster next time. Early hands-on work with the recovered technology frightens them badly. They write *children with guns* about themselves, mean it, and bury it. |
| **~150 years ago** | Following the trail from the crater over generations, the Directorate locates the true mother lode — the rest of the hull — under a city forming in the Minkai-equivalent east, and relocates there. Reverse-engineering of the bisected specimen produces the first Frame shells. Magic's slow recession from Golarion and industry's rise fill the resulting vacuum across this whole span. |
| **~120 years ago** | The first trace of what will be called Inert 12 is catalogued near the original crater dig. Judged noble, non-reactive, useless. Shelved. |
| **~45 years ago** | Manako, junior chemist, is handed the "boring" sample nobody senior wants. |
| **~44 years ago** | The accident. A capsule cracked open; a weak nearby fragment of the cloud is fed and briefly strengthens; panic; a knocked-over burner; a small fire; disgrace. |
| **~44–40 years ago** | Self-justification, superstition, the first small cooperation, theft of lab supplies including the last remaining sample (never reported missing), the treated lens. |
| **~40 years ago** | The sky. Manako sees it citywide for the first time and asks himself whether it isn't a god worth worshipping. |
| **~38 years ago** | The conference. *Believe me now?* A canister, detonated without warning. Most of the room decides it was mass hallucination. Cicada exists from this point. |
| **~38–15 years ago** | The long evangelical phase. Small demonstrations, slow recruitment through the magic-show circuit, no violence sought. Rika and a handful of others investigate independently, in parallel, and never find one another. |
| **~15 years ago** | The Envoy — the Watcher's own first attempt at contact beyond its worshippers. Hori shoots it in fear; the round passes through and kills Aoi. **The Pivot:** the cult turns from witness to revenge. |
| **~15–5 years ago** | The inner circle forms around the revenge list. Nobu, rising through Directorate procurement, begins quietly funding cult operations as private amends. |
| **~5 years ago** *(a choice, not a fact — see note below)* | The column goes fully industrial. |
| **The last year or so** | Demonstrations grow frequent and increasingly public — the "everywhere at once" emergence the party will walk into. Shinobu is splashed, detained, released, kept under surveillance. |
| **Weeks before Day 1** | Nozomi, slated as the presumptive first pilot, attends a targeted magic show and cannot get back in the machine. |
| **Day 1** | Contact, Unclassified. |

**On the ~5-years date:** this is the one entry above that's a decision rather than housekeeping. If the column had been running at full scale for anywhere near the forty-year span, the Watcher would already be saturated with mass long before Day 1 — too large, too soon, and the escalation curve in §14 stops meaning anything. Placing it recent is what keeps the campaign's escalation feel earned rather than pre-cooked. Open to being wrong about how recent.

**In-campaign, compressed** (full detail in §18):

Days 1–6 (Ch 1, *Contact, Unclassified*) → Ch 2 (*A Census of the Living*) → Ch 3 (*The Small Wretched Kings* — the lab accident, the stray, Flashback One) → Ch 4 (*The Tragedy of the Second Excavation* — Anabala arrives, the ship is found) → Ch 5 (*Everyone You Have Ever Been Kind To* — Cicada declares itself, the magic show, the lorry incident) → Ch 6 (*The Bodies We Used To Have* — the core revealed, the sit-in, the column found; the crossing point) → Ch 7 (*The Reel* — the sky, the sceptics assembled, Flashback Three) → Ch 8 (*The Estate* — the voyage, the asking).

## 19. The Eight Chapters

### Chapter One — "The Alarm Nobody Had Rehearsed" (L1→2)
*Days 1–6, hand-placed. Slices: generous. Rung: what exists.*

**War —** Only one Frame launches; the other five play ground roles simultaneously, in real danger. The first Claimant fights the founder's statue for ninety seconds with total commitment, then fights its own reflection in the water. Play it straight, let the table laugh — genuinely, this is funny and should be allowed to be. Then land the other half quietly: **a man is killed**, entirely by accident, while it moves an obstacle out of its way. One person, named later in a list the party will read. He did not register as anything at all.

One death, precisely placed, does more here than a casualty figure. Resist the urge to escalate.

**School —** Cold open, an Ordinary Tuesday. Press one Anchor per player lightly. Nothing happens — this scene exists so the cost hurts later.

**Founders —** The statue is Hyuck. Nobody says so. A plaque exists, if anyone looks.

**Seeds, unremarked:** no approach vector; contact time-stamped before the alert.

**Cost:** something the ground five spent the whole night protecting.
**Image:** dawn, a Frame kneeling in the shallows, still holding the statue's bronze hand.

---

### Chapter Two — "A Census of the Living" (L2→3)
*Rung: what moves is alive.*

**War —** It sorts by movement now; a crowd and a Frame are the same category. The atrocity is taxonomy, not cruelty — it gathers civilians carefully, almost gently, the way you sort components, and sets down anyone who isn't the target with something unbearably like courtesy.

**Cult —** Cicada graffiti is found in the strike district, dated the night *before* the strike. Unexplained. Filed for later.

**Mitflits —** A mitflit is seen at the wreckage, salvaging something small and specific. Chased off. Not followed up.

**School —** The campus reopens. There is a memorial assembly nobody wants and everybody attends. And the exam timetable continues unchanged, because cancelling it would require authority nobody has — which is both very funny and the first small proof that this institution cannot adapt to anything.

**Cost:** several people drowned before the Frames reached them — the city's first real losses, mourned publicly and at length. The first public argument about whether the Frames protect anyone.
**Image:** a Claimant holding a screaming person up to what might be a face, examining them with total absorption, then setting them down unharmed.

---

### Chapter Three — "The Small Wretched Kings" (L3→4)
*Rung: Frames specifically matter.*

**War —** The only chapter that plays as a straight mecha show. Proper tactics, proper wins. Let them enjoy it; it's the last time it's this simple.

**Observed —** Research duty is a slice option because the Directorate is short-handed, not because it's interesting. Something spills. Whoever's in the room sees the air over the harbour occupied for eleven seconds — then nothing, no corroboration, and the medical officer is very kind about it. **And something small came through.** It follows that PC now, patiently, waiting for food. The player will name it. They will not be able to help it.

**Founders —** Flashback One: the old party at the dig, full strength, magnificent. The best day of their lives. Ends on the throw and the catch.

**Mitflits —** A mitflit is captured alive — foul-mouthed, terrified, spiteful. Calls the Frames a word in no known language, and walked past an open hangar, stops talking entirely and cannot be made to start again.

**School —** Taka teaches. He is the one who drilled the berserk protocol into them at fourteen, and he is funny about it, and they love him. Then his Frame goes berserk mid-fight — it drops its weapon and stands over an evacuation route, refusing to move, taking everything. **He survives by default** (§21). In the debrief he says, plainly, that there was *someone in there with him.* Nobody believes him. He is a decorated pilot and it does not help at all.

**Cost:** Taka's credibility, and the party's confidence in what they are sitting inside.
**Image:** the captured mitflit seeing a Frame's hand and weeping, unable to say why in any language the party speaks.

---

### Chapter Four — "The Tragedy of the Second Excavation" (L4→5)
*Rung: Frames have pilots.*

**War —** It hits the campus, in daylight, in the half of the game meant to be safe.

**Mitflits —** Anabala arrives — not a quest-giver. Offers the truth freely because the truth serves her purpose.

**Founders —** The ship is found, in the same dig, on the same substrate. The Directorate's response is immediate, total, and secret — because there has been a **second excavation** the party never knew existed.

**Cult —** Corroboration from the wrong direction: a second witness with no credibility (mitflit, drunk, child) describing the same thing unprompted; an old photograph; the stencil pattern starting to resolve. The party works out *where it's looking* before *what it is.*

**School —** The campus *is* the front line now. Rebuilding, in stages, around classes that keep running. **Ume** approaches the party about **Nozomi** — she was closest to her, she lost her, and she wants her found as badly as they do.

**Cost:** the campus, and the illusion that day and night were different games.

---

### Chapter Five — "Everyone You Have Ever Been Kind To" (L5→6)
*Rung: pilots have people. The kid-sheet chapter.*

**School —** Almost no Frame combat. Hand-sized threats aimed at Anchors — a shop, a sibling, a teacher, the one place a pilot goes to feel normal. Ground roles carry the war.

**Cult —** **Cicada declares itself.** They explain the mechanism they understand (not yet the food-farming truth) and make a case with no honest counter-argument available. **The magic show** happens here — a PC can attend one and walk out shaken, or converted. Dossiers begin drifting past the obviously cult-susceptible into scientists and lawyers.

**Observed —** Shinobu's situation is discoverable. If it hasn't already, **the lorry incident** lands here: she walks off a direct hit, is re-arrested within the hour, and the Directorate looks monstrous from outside for jailing an accident victim.

**Mitflits —** They arrive in numbers now, because Anabala has called them. The city houses them badly, in the smokestack district, and complains about them in the papers.

**Cost:** an Anchor put under real strain — a relationship that has to be actively repaired, or actively lost. **Not a death.** The threats here frighten and disrupt; what they take is trust and time.

---

### Chapter Six — "The Bodies We Used To Have" (L6→7)
*Rung: there are institutions. The crossing point.*

**War / Mitflits —** It goes for logistics: the causeway, the repair pool, the sea-fort. And the core is revealed: the hole in every shell is a seat, shaped by whoever links with it. **The mitflits take the job** — full Resonance, immediately, no ramp, no Bleed, no berserk, because they simply fit. The city discovers it may not need the children, and will not concede for one second that what they gave was ever worth anything.

**And Bobbin leads the sit-in.** A mitflit occupies a Frame specifically so it cannot be used — a general strike inside the war machine — and the party is put in direct, uncomfortable opposition to people who are ethically correct.

**Founders —** Flashback Two: the old party learns what the core actually is, and — driven by the same fear that produced *children with guns* — chooses to bury it.

**Cult —** The dossier drift lands on **Nao**, a classmate. Not a spy — just someone who went to a magic show. The **column** is found: industrial-scale synthesis, feeding a black pillar of nanoparticles into the sky. Taking it down is a genuine triumph and should be played as one — and it raises more questions than it answers.

**Observed —** **Nobu** comes under suspicion as the column's funder. And the Mass Clock becomes legible: the party can now read how much of the sky is *their own fault*.

**School —** A classmate is arrested. The school turns on itself — informing, suspicion, kids reporting kids. **Nao's** fate is the party's to influence (§21), and whichever way they go costs them something with somebody.

**Cost:** the Directorate's legitimacy. If a Frame is ever permanently lost, here.

---

### Chapter Seven — "The Reel" (L7→8)
*Rung: there are tactics and patterns.*

**War —** It adapts between rounds now and keeps its promise: it shows them the reel. Their own openings, their own habits, timing slightly improved. Rupiah's anthem, hummed back.

**Observed —** **The city sees.** Daylight, the whole harbour, everyone looking up at once. Instant civic panic — and Cicada's argument suddenly makes sense to ordinary people, because a city that can see itself being assessed will do anything to look worthless.

**Cult —** The endgame nears. **Hori gives the party Aoi's name**, if he hasn't already. The **sceptics are assembled** — the party convenes their first meeting. The **revenge list** is recovered, turning a hunt into a protection scenario.

**School —** The mitflit **schism**: fight beside the city, follow Anabala home, or join Cicada with an argument better than anyone wants to admit.

**Founders —** Flashback Three: the old party's last stand, and the decision that made all of this. Let the players make it. Do not soften it afterward.

**School —** The campus becomes a public shelter. The party are heroes, publicly, by name, and they hate it — because they know what the Mirror means by now, and being celebrated for fighting is being celebrated for teaching it to fight.

**Cost:** the roster. Only the six remain.

---

### Chapter Eight — "The Estate" (L8→9)
*No slices. There is no ordinary life left to schedule. Rung: there are people.*

**War / Mitflits —** They cross the world in the ship. For the first time there is no authority above them. They open the aperture — not to close it, but to let the rest of it through, because a thing barely present cannot be fought and a thing with a body can. Every sane authority will tell them not to. They will be right. **The mitflits enter Frames only to defend; being asked to attack is the deepest violation of that, and the party has to ask them. The battle is the epilogue. The scene is the asking.**

**Cult —** Manako, at the source. He cannot be argued out of what he believes — only **shown**, by watching the Observer take a different shape for someone else, in front of him. He spent his life demonstrating; he should be defeated by one. Kill, capture, destroy, protect — all still live.

**Founders —** The Directorate's authority ends here, literally, for the first time in the campaign.

**Observed —** The museum fragment, if the party kept it, is the only surviving evidence the pattern the Watcher inferred was wrong.

**Observed —** **The stray, grown.** Whatever that PC has raised across five chapters is the demonstration: a piece of the same cloud, shaped by kindness instead of grief, standing there being visibly *not* what Manako made. He spent his life demonstrating. This is the only argument that could ever have reached him.

**School —** **They graduate.** Absurdly, in the middle of everything, with the city half-rebuilt and the sky full of eyes, somebody reads names off a list and hands out certificates. Play it entirely straight. It is the warmest scene in the campaign and it is the actual reward.

**Deliberately undefined:** the specific ending. The players will move it, and over-specifying now wastes the work.

## 20. The Intake, and the Side Plots

### The intake quiz

Session zero, before anything else. Each player answers for their kid, and **every answer becomes an Anchor, a side plot seed, or a vulnerability.** Written down, kept, and mined for the whole campaign.

1. **Who do you love, and what would you never tell them?**
2. **What are you good at that has nothing to do with piloting?** — this becomes their ground-role affinity and probably a side plot.
3. **What did you want to be, before the draft notice?**
4. **Who in this city owes you something? Who do you owe?**
5. **What is the smallest thing that would make today a good day?** — the cheapest, most reliable emotional lever in the campaign.
6. **What do you not want the Directorate to know about you?**
7. **Name someone who thinks you are better than you are.**
8. **What do you find funny?** — a real question. It tells the GM what register that player wants.

### Three kinds of side plot

- **Solo** — one PC only. Their family, their debt, their secret. Run in downtime slices, short, frequent, low-stakes-high-meaning. These are where the campaign earns the right to hurt later.
- **Open** — the whole group can attend. A festival, a favour, a job, a rescue. These build the found family, which is the campaign's actual reward.
- **Convergent** — starts solo, pulls the group in, and **touches a main thread.** A PC's family debt turns out to be owed to somebody in Nobu's network. A sibling's friend went to a magic show. **This is the type to write most of.**

**Rule:** every side plot should touch at least one of the six threads (§16) somewhere, so nothing is ever a detour. The player experiences a personal story; the campaign experiences a delivery mechanism.

### Feedback loop

The GM writes up what landed and what didn't after each session. Those notes drive the next batch of side plots. **Prediction is a courtesy; responsiveness is the actual strength.** Nothing below is fixed, and side plots should regularly overtake the main line in importance for whole sessions at a time.

### Bifurcation points

Places where the plot genuinely forks. Each needs a *what changes downstream* note prepared in advance, not improvised.

| Fork | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| **Find Nozomi early?** | Cicada's argument arrives from a trusted source before the party has any defence against it | She surfaces in Ch7 among the schism, harder to reach |
| **Turn Nao in?** | The school fractures; the party is trusted by the Directorate and by nobody else | They are harbouring a cultist, and Satoru finds out |
| **Break the sit-in?** | Bobbin is in mortal danger (§21); the mitflits will not forget it | The city goes undefended for a stretch and people notice |
| **Tell the city about the sky?** | Ch7's mass sighting comes early and Cicada gains enormously | The Directorate's suppression reflex becomes the party's own |
| **Feed the stray?** | It grows, shaped by whoever raised it — and becomes the Ch8 demonstration | It stays small, and the ending needs another proof |
| **Expose Nobu or use him?** | The column's funding stops; so does every repair the party relies on | They are complicit, and they know it |
| **Take Manako alive?** | Redemption-as-labour is available | The cloud loses its organising signal and the Mass Clock runs unopposed |

## 21. The Peril Roster

**Nobody on this list is scheduled to die.** Each has a trigger condition and a save condition, and the outcome belongs to play. This replaces every fixed death in earlier drafts.

| Character | Jeopardy | Saved by |
|---|---|---|
| **TAKA** | Berserk in Ch3 while shielding an evacuation route; takes everything | Ground roles clearing the route fast, or a second Frame reaching him. **Survives by default** |
| **NAO** | If turned in, disappears into detention and comes back different | Protected, or advocated for by someone with standing |
| **BOBBIN** | Forced extraction from the sit-in kills him | The party finding a third option — including simply *asking him* in his own language |
| **SHINOBU** | The colony is mishandled, by the Directorate or by well-meaning rescuers | Understanding what she actually is before acting |
| **PIP** | Young, reckless, and desperate to prove mitflits belong in cockpits | Bobbin reaching him, or a PC doing it first |
| **HORI** | On nobody's list, but old and carrying forty years of guilt alone | Being asked. Being believed. That is the whole save condition |
| **NOZOMI** | Depends entirely on when and how she is found | Reached before Ch7's schism forces her to choose sides |
| **MANAKO** | The confrontation, however it is framed | Being *shown* rather than argued with (§19, Ch8) |

**Manufactured jeopardy is fine and encouraged** — put these people in dangerous situations deliberately. What is not fine is deciding the outcome in advance.

## 22. Known Risks

1. **Scope.** Six players, twelve sheets, six threads, three flashback layers, a side-plot engine, a procedural Contact system, a dossier metagame, and an AI antagonist reading logs. The threat is not a weak story — it is the apparatus collapsing under its own weight. Decide which two subsystems get cut first under pressure.
2. **Thread juggling.** Six threads is a lot to carry live. The grid (§17) reduces it to a checklist, but if a chapter's beats stop landing as *connected*, the mystery reads as noise rather than a converging picture.
3. **Almost everything good is currently a secret.** Chapters 1–2 must work as good mecha episodes and good school drama with none of the reveals helping.
4. **The benign reveal deflates the sky.** Once the players learn the Watcher isn't evil, the cosmic-horror engine switches off and a manhunt engine switches on, around Ch 5–6. Make that gear change deliberate.
5. **Tone drift toward grimness.** This document has been rewritten once already for exactly this. The material *wants* to curdle — it is full of institutional cruelty and forty-year griefs. **Check the register every few sessions against §6**, and if a run of sessions has contained no jokes, no warmth, and no small good days, that is a fault, not a mood.
6. **Side plots outcompeting the main line.** This is mostly fine and occasionally correct. It becomes a problem only if the Mass Clock never advances and the campaign loses its spine.

## 23. Open Calls

1. **Who does the asking**, when the mitflits are asked to break their pacifism in Chapter 8 — and what it costs them.
2. **What the museum fragment can actually say**, and whether it can be moved.

## 24. Register Notes

**Giant Robo.** Everyone means it. Nobody deflects. Grief is loud, loyalty is declared aloud, techniques are announced by name.

**Water Margin.** The roll-call, the epithets, the outlaw band more righteous than the state it serves, and the ending where the state cannot be trusted with what they built. The Directorate never has to become villainous. It only has to remain exactly what it has been.

**The comic-to-poignant gradient.** The Watcher's incompetence in Ch 1–2 is not a joke at the audience's expense, and it is not a trap either. It is the setup for the kindest payoff available: **it was never stupid. It was new, and it was drowning, and nobody had ever spoken to it.**

**And the last word on register:** this is a story where six teenagers, a species everyone treats as vermin, a disgraced doctor's severed hand, and a frightened cloud of broken machinery all turn out to have been doing their best the whole time. Play it warm. Let them joke. Let them win something.
