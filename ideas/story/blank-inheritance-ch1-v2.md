## 1. The Encounter Budget

**A level is 1,000 XP.** Budgets assume four PCs and scale up for larger parties, but *awards* stay at the four-player value — so six players level at the same rate as four. Build bigger, award the same.

**Six-player budgets:**

| Threat | 4 PCs | 6 PCs (build to) | Award |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trivial | 40 | 60 | 40 |
| Low | 60 | 100 | 60 |
| Moderate | 80 | 120 | 80 |
| Severe | 120 | 180 | 120 |
| Extreme | 160 | 240 | 160 |

**Creature cost by relative level:** −4 = 10 · −3 = 15 · −2 = 20 · −1 = 30 · equal = 40 · +1 = 60 · +2 = 80 · +3 = 120 · +4 = 160.

**Accomplishment XP:** minor 10 · moderate 30 · major 80.

## 2. What This Chapter Is For

1. **Make the players like each other's characters** before anything is at stake.
2. **Plant mysteries**, none explained here.
3. **Teach the sheets through play**, one system at a time, only once that system is worth teaching.
4. **Show the history, the society, and the problem** without exposition.
5. **Deliver one impossible experience** — then have the city officially inform them it didn't happen.
6. **End on the first real Contact**, which nobody at any level has ever seen.

**It must not explain** the Frames, the Watcher, Cicada, or the mitflits. The players leave Chapter One holding anomalies and no framework, arguing between sessions.

*(Items 5 and 6 are chapter-level intent, not a schedule. Which episode they land in is now open.)*

## 3. The Premise

**The Directorate has never defended anything.** A century and a half of Frames, a charter, a chain of command, an anthem, a maintenance budget — and no war. It is a rite of passage, mildly boring, faintly prestigious, and entirely untested.

*(Full name: 特別対策本部, Tokubetsu Taisaku Honbu — "Special Countermeasures Headquarters." "The Directorate" is the standing shorthand used throughout.)*

The institution is **calcified rather than sinister**. The adults are not competent and don't know it. The tone can be warm and funny for a long stretch without strain. And when the alarm is finally real, nobody knowing what to do is *earned*.

## 7. Running Notes

**An episode is a unit of content, not a unit of time.** Standing order, 2026-09-13, from before the 2026-09-20 terminology fix, so "session" in it means what is now called an episode: *"Sessions aren't meant to be played in one go, a single combat can take a whole session, we play until we run out of content. The idea behind sessions is to organise mini arcs or organise content together into complimentary encounters."* Judge an episode by whether its encounters complement each other and form a coherent mini-arc. Never cut or compress content to fit a clock, and never split an episode for length — split only when two mini-arcs are genuinely better than one.

**Split the party when six players share one conversation** — two groups of three doing different duties is faster and gives everyone airtime.

**Write down after each session:** which plants they noticed, which NPC each player gravitated toward, and the theory they left holding. The theory feeds the Watcher. *(Since LEDGER #32: the Watcher itself may only act on what it has observed inside the fiction, recorded per PC in its GM-only file, `data/schema/watcher-file.schema.json`. A theory reaches the Watcher only if the characters said or wrote it where it could see or read it; otherwise it is the GM's note, not the Watcher's knowledge.)*
