## 1. The Encounter Budget

**A level is 1,000 XP.** Budgets assume four PCs and scale up for larger parties, but *awards* stay at the four-player value — so six players level at the same rate as four. Build bigger, award the same.

**Six-player budgets:**

| Threat | 4 PCs | 6 PCs (build to) | Award |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trivial | 40 | 60 | 40 |
| Low | 60 | 100 | 60 |
| Moderate | 80 | 120 | 80 |
| Severe | 120 | 180 | 120 |
| Extreme | 160 | 240 | 160 |

**Creature cost by relative level:** −4 = 10 · −3 = 15 · −2 = 20 · −1 = 30 · equal = 40 · +1 = 60 · +2 = 80 · +3 = 120 · +4 = 160.

**Accomplishment XP:** minor 10 · moderate 30 · major 80.

## 2. What This Chapter Is For

1. **Make the players like each other's characters** before anything is at stake.
2. **Plant mysteries**, none explained here.
3. **Teach the sheets through play**, one system at a time, only once that system is worth teaching.
4. **Show the history, the society, and the problem** without exposition.
5. **Deliver one impossible experience** — then have the city officially inform them it didn't happen.
6. **End on the first real Contact**, which nobody at any level has ever seen.

**It must not explain** the Frames, the Watcher, Cicada, the mitflits, or the Gap. The players leave Chapter One holding anomalies and no framework, arguing between sessions.

*(Items 5 and 6 are chapter-level intent, not a schedule. Which session they land in is now open.)*

## 3. The Premise

**The Directorate has never defended anything.** A century and a half of Frames, a charter, a chain of command, an anthem, a maintenance budget, a conscription statute — and no war. Civil defence here is national service. Everyone's parents did it. It is a rite of passage, mildly boring, faintly prestigious, and entirely untested.

The institution is **calcified rather than sinister**. The adults are not competent and don't know it. The tone can be warm and funny for five sessions without strain. And when the alarm is finally real, nobody knowing what to do is *earned*.

## 4. Show, Never Tell

**History —** A weathered gnoll statue in the harbour square, plaque worn past reading, pigeons, kids climbing it *(Hyuck)*. The berserk protocol recited sing-song at assembly, opening with a line about brothers everyone takes as metaphor *(it isn't)*. An anthem with no composer credited *(Rupiah)*. The founding charter framed in the lobby with **one paragraph blacked out** since before anyone alive was born *(children with guns)*. A skeletal physician who has done ten thousand intake medicals and is very good at it *(a founder, two centuries old)*.

**Society —** Someone mends a window with a cantrip and apologises for it. A shopkeeper blames a caster because a preservation charm doesn't hold like it used to, and is wrong. Sail-rigged traders tied up alongside coal cranes. Cliff-top red tile against the smokestack tenement line. A hand-lettered notice asking anyone who spots a mitflit to report it to the Harbour Office, not handle it themselves. Draft paperwork with a queue for every form.

**The problem —** A street dogs won't walk down. A good rooftop with no birds. Cicada stencils, always head height, always where crowds stand. Bulk theft of dull reagents. A girl held without charge and a father on the radio. A tarp in Hangar Four with a blank maintenance schedule.

## 6. Session One — "Mother's Maiden Name (Again)"

**Fully authored and intact.** `data/episodes/episode-c1s1-mothers-maiden-name.json`, five scene briefs, five challenges, one encounter, one optional subsystem.

**Morning** — Intake medicals; Shucks Mordant, forms that ask twice, and a flagged screen each of them is told privately not to worry about. *(`episode-c1s1--intake-medicals`)*
**Noon** — Kit issue; Nobu signs the slips personally and is oddly generous with the allowance. Personnel equipment only — vehicles and Frames are Session Two's business. *(`episode-c1s1--kit-issue`)*
**Afternoon** — **The Errand.** A collection run into the city, and the session's only exterior. The Directorate's docket lists the item under a catalogue name nobody has used in a century, so the task is unfinishable until a retired chemist in a tea house reads it in nine seconds. He is kind, he is useful, he says one true and unsettling thing about their employer, and he leaves them a way to contact him. **He is the campaign's primary antagonist and nothing in the scene suggests it.** *(`episode-c1s1--the-errand`, `c1s1-the-errand` — added 2026-09-13; see `ideas/story/dossier-cicada.md` §13)*
**Evening** — Canteen. Taka arrives uninvited and has renamed all six of them before the plates are cleared. This is also where Section Nine gets its assignment — no choice offered, read last and worst over a tannoy. *(`episode-c1s1--canteen`)*
**Night** — **ENCOUNTER 1: Flood tunnel clearance.** *(`episode-c1s1--flood-tunnel`, `c1s1-flood-tunnel`, optional `c1s1-compactor-cycle`)*


**The fight.** New recruits get the worst job. The storm drains under the old town have a centipede infestation and the tide is due. Standard vermin clearance — the exact job a level 1 party is given in every adventure ever written.

*Build (Moderate, 120):* six giant centipedes at party level −2 (20 each). Difficult terrain, poor light, and an optional compactor hazard that closes in from the centre outward.

**Why this fight and not another:** new recruits prove themselves through the worst job available, not the most impressive one, and nobody will ever tell them that's what just happened. They come out of the tunnels soaked and stinking, and that — not a speech, not a ceremony — is the institution's actual first lesson about what it values.

**What matters:** whether they kill them, drive them off, or find another way. **Record it.** Colour for this specific party's own established character. *(Centipedes are non-sentient animals with no connection to the Observer's Mirror mechanic — `dossier-observer.md` §13. Don't conflate the two.)*

*Ends on:* six exhausted, soaked teenagers climbing out of the drain, already knowing they were all flagged — that discovery happened this morning at Intake Medicals, not here.

**This session is personnel scale throughout.** No Frame launches, no vehicle, no ground roles in operation. That is deliberate and stays.

## 7. Running Notes

**A session is a unit of content, not a unit of time.** Standing order, 2026-09-13: *"Sessions aren't meant to be played in one go, a single combat can take a whole session, we play until we run out of content. The idea behind sessions is to organise mini arcs or organise content together into complimentary encounters."* Judge a session by whether its encounters complement each other and form a coherent mini-arc. Never cut or compress content to fit a clock, and never split a session for length — split only when two mini-arcs are genuinely better than one.

**Split the party when six players share one conversation** — two groups of three doing different duties is faster and gives everyone airtime.

**Write down after each session:** which plants they noticed, which NPC each player gravitated toward, and the theory they left holding. The theory feeds the Watcher.

## 8. Before anything else is written

Three concepts are currently load-bearing in the design and **not fit to build encounters on**. Resolve them before scheduling another session. Each is tracked in `data/campaign/open-questions.json`.

1. **Ground roles** (`data/campaign/ground-roles.json`) — under review, possibly for removal. Two of the five (Umbilical Control, Signals Liaison) only function when a Frame is on the field, and Umbilical Control in particular describes a player converting effort into someone else's resource, which is not agency. **The standing requirement: every player must have real agency over their own role and actions in an SF2E fight.**
2. **Resonance** — needs substantial work before it is playable or interesting. `design/resonance-and-bleed.md` already measured a 5.7× band spread nobody had run the arithmetic on.
3. **Bleed** — same. Four inflows, one undefined outflow, one threshold across ten points.

**Session Two's direction is agreed and unwritten:** a training exercise at Frame and vehicle scale that is interrupted by a real call-out — the team is part-way through a safe, scheduled drill when they are sent to something live, before they are ready. That is the shape. Nothing else about it is decided, and it should not be written until at least item 1 above is settled, because a Frame-scale encounter is exactly where ground roles either earn their place or get cut.

**Above all:** this is a heavily modified SF2E campaign. Whatever is built next has to make a good SF2E table experience first, and serve the genre fantasy second.
