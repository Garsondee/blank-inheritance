# Blank Inheritance — Chapter One (v2)
## "Nothing Has Ever Happened Here"

*Levels 1→2. Eight sessions, 19:30–22:00, six players. Rebuilt against published PF2E/SF2E encounter pacing. Supersedes the previous Chapter One document.*

## 1. The Encounter Budget

**A level is 1,000 XP.** Budgets assume four PCs and scale up for larger parties, but *awards* stay at the four-player value — so six players level at the same rate as four. Build bigger, award the same.

**Six-player budgets:**

| Threat | 4 PCs | 6 PCs (build to) | Award |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trivial | 40 | 60 | 40 |
| Low | 60 | 100 | 60 |
| Moderate | 80 | 120 | 80 |
| Severe | 120 | 180 | 120 |
| Extreme | 160 | 240 | 160 |

**Creature cost by relative level:** −4 = 10 · −3 = 15 · −2 = 20 · −1 = 30 · equal = 40 · +1 = 60 · +2 = 80 · +3 = 120 · +4 = 160.

**Accomplishment XP:** minor 10 · moderate 30 · major 80.

## 2. What This Chapter Is For

1. **Make the players like each other's characters** before anything is at stake.
2. **Plant twelve mysteries**, none explained here.
3. **Teach both sheets, the ground roles, and the four-slice day** through play.
4. **Show the history, the society, and the problem** without exposition.
5. **Deliver one impossible experience** — then have the city officially inform them it didn't happen.
6. **End on the first real Contact**, which nobody at any level has ever seen.

**It must not explain** the Frames, the Watcher, Cicada, the mitflits, or the Gap. The players leave Chapter One holding anomalies and no framework, arguing between sessions.

---

## 3. The Premise

**The Directorate has never defended anything.** A century and a half of Frames, a charter, a chain of command, an anthem, a maintenance budget, a conscription statute — and no war. Civil defence here is national service. Everyone's parents did it. It is a rite of passage, mildly boring, faintly prestigious, and entirely untested.

The institution is **calcified rather than sinister**. The adults are not competent and don't know it. The tone can be warm and funny for five sessions without strain. And when the alarm is finally real, nobody knowing what to do is *earned*.

---

## 4. Show, Never Tell

**History —** A weathered gnoll statue in the harbour square, plaque worn past reading, pigeons, kids climbing it *(Hyuck)*. The berserk protocol recited sing-song at assembly, opening with a line about brothers everyone takes as metaphor *(it isn't)*. An anthem with no composer credited *(Rupiah)*. The founding charter framed in the lobby with **one paragraph blacked out** since before anyone alive was born *(children with guns)*. A skeletal physician who has done ten thousand intake medicals and is very good at it *(a founder, two centuries old)*.

**Society —** Someone mends a window with a cantrip and apologises for it. A shopkeeper blames a caster because a preservation charm doesn't hold like it used to, and is wrong. Sail-rigged traders tied up alongside coal cranes. Cliff-top red tile against the smokestack tenement line. Mitflit traps on the harbour wall with a few coins' bounty, same as rat traps. Draft paperwork with a queue for every form.

**The problem —** A street dogs won't walk down. A good rooftop with no birds. Cicada stencils, always head height, always where crowds stand. Bulk theft of dull reagents. A girl held without charge and a father on the radio. A tarp in Hangar Four with a blank maintenance schedule.

## 6. The Eight Sessions

### Session One — "Mother's Maiden Name (Again)"

**Morning** — Intake medicals; Shucks Mordant, forms that ask twice, and a flagged screen each of them is told privately not to worry about.
**Noon** — Kit issue and ground role selection; Nobu signs the slips personally and is oddly generous with the allowance.
**Evening** — Canteen. Taka arrives uninvited and has renamed all six of them before the plates are cleared. *(Built out 2026-09-09 — see `data/scene-briefs/episode-c1s1--canteen.json`: this is also where Section Nine gets its assignment, no choice offered, read last and worst over a tannoy after several other sections' more interesting-sounding ones. The party can learn it's not just drains through hard Recall Knowledge or by asking Taka directly, but nothing changes the assignment itself.)*
**Night** — **ENCOUNTER 1: Flood tunnel clearance.**

**The fight.** New recruits get the worst job. The storm drains under the old town have a centipede infestation and the tide is due. Standard vermin clearance — the exact job a level 1 party is given in every adventure ever written.

*Build (Moderate, 120):* six giant centipedes at party level −2 (20 each). Difficult terrain, rising water on a timer, poor light.

**Why this fight and not another:** new recruits prove themselves through the worst job available, not the most impressive one, and nobody will ever tell them that's what just happened. They come out of the tunnels soaked and stinking, and that — not a speech, not a ceremony — is the institution's actual first lesson about what it values.

*(Changed 2026-09-09 from an earlier draft where the infestation was a mitflit nest — see `ideas/story/bible/species/mitflits.md`. Mitflits are established as rare at this point in the campaign and never treated as vermin; a level-1 party's throwaway first fight is the wrong place for either to be true of them. The tide/difficult-terrain/poor-light shape of the encounter is unchanged.)*

**What matters:** whether they kill them, drive them off, or find another way. **Record it.** This is the first entry in the tone ledger, and the campaign is watching.

*Ends on:* six teenagers comparing notes over the water and working out they were all flagged.

---

### Session Two — "The Timetable Stands"

**Morning** — Assembly: the anthem nobody can name a composer for, the protocol recited by heart, and an exam timetable standing firm against the apocalypse.
**Noon** — Ground role basics, taught practically; the Sensor instructor mentions the scopes sometimes register density where there's nothing.
**Evening** — **ENCOUNTER 2: The Sizing Board.**
**Night** — Free. Somebody notices a cicada stencil by the tram stop.

**The social encounter, run in initiative.** Their boots don't fit and the requisition needs a countersignature. What starts as a joke becomes a genuine encounter: a three-round appeal before a supply board chaired by **Nobu**, with real stakes — equipment quality for the whole chapter, and a first read on a man who is being strangely accommodating.

*Structure:* Society or a Charisma skill for initiative. Three rounds. Diplomacy, Deception, Intimidation, Lore all viable. Success improves their kit; failure means Chapter One is run in bad boots, which is funny and slightly costly.

**Establish stakes out loud before rolling.** This teaches the table that social scenes in this campaign can be encounters, which matters enormously later.

*Ends on:* the empty seat and the uncrossed name. **Nozomi.**

---

### Session Three — "Do Not Crush The Civilian"

**Morning** — Transport to the quarry; Taka's briefing, mostly stories of previous years going wrong.
**Noon** — **ENCOUNTER 3: the drill.**
**Evening** — Somebody suggests a race. It happens. **A Frame moves more gently than its pilot ordered.**
**Night** — Return; whoever asks Taka gets a beat of silence, something about hydraulic compensation, and a change of subject.

**Frame-scale AND Vehicle-scale hazard course, not real combat.** Changed 2026-09-09 from a single hazard to a multi-station drill on a real city-scale map (10x grid, per `scale-and-chassis.md` §14f) — this is also the first time vehicles are shown to the party and assigned, not just Frames. Training turrets and target arrays test accuracy and fire discipline; the original mock tenement — a weighted mannequin on the third floor, **DO NOT CRUSH THE CIVILIAN** painted on its chest, and a scoring sheet — stays as one station among several, since it was too good a beat to cut. Nothing on this course can seriously hurt a Frame or a vehicle; it's calibrated to be passable, even a little silly, on purpose.

*Build (Moderate, 120):* a complex hazard across several stations — Athletics/Crafting for the tenement, Piloting and attack rolls against static training turrets for the range stations, cascading failure states, no live enemy anywhere on the course.

**This is the session that teaches the two-sheet structure**, and it is a comedy. Six teenagers discover they command six-storey machines (and now vehicles) that can shrug through a wall, put a round through a target a city block away, and still cannot reliably lift a mannequin.

**Why it's deliberately imperfect, by design:** the Directorate hasn't faced a real Frame-scale threat yet, so this drill is allowed to teach the wrong lessons — confidence, comedy, "we're basically fine at this." *(The correction comes later: Session Eight's actual first Claimant fight is deliberately hard, even brutal, and should read as proof they weren't ready. Badly damaging or destroying a Frame or a vehicle there is expected and generates real repair backlog — see `data/campaign/config.json`'s `frameEconomy` — never a PC death.)*

**Make the table love Taka tonight.** Everything he is for later depends on it.

### Session Four — "Vermin"

**Morning** — Harbour patrol; flood barriers, tide marks, and a mitflit caught in a wall trap, furious and refusing help it obviously needs.
**Noon** — Lab inventory. Bulk quantities of unremarkable reagents unaccounted for across three months.
**Evening** — Free. Anchor scenes.
**Night** — **ENCOUNTER 4: The lab stakeout.**

**The fight.** The party works out the theft pattern and waits. Four thieves come through a window at two in the morning — professional, unbothered, there for the boring shelf.

*Build (Moderate, 120):* one level 2 leader (60) + three level −1 thugs (30 each). They fight to flee, not to the death. **At least one can be taken alive if the party tries.**

**What they find:** the crate manifest is dull chemistry in quantity. And **a lens** — ground glass, oddly coated, in a leather case. Someone looks through it.

**It does nothing.**

*(It works perfectly. The Observer simply isn't in that room. This is precisely what happened to Manako at his first failed demonstration, for precisely the same reason, and the party will not understand that for four chapters.)*

**If they take a prisoner:** the first arrest file in the campaign is one they generated. She is calm, polite, not remotely ideological, and answers everything except *why*. Pressed, she asks whether any of them has ever properly looked at the sky.

---

### Session Five — "Head Height"

**Morning** — Stencil sweep. Tedious, endless, and — if anyone maps it — sited with obvious deliberation.
**Noon** — Escort duty; Satoru takes a hostile question about the detained girl and answers it straight, to visible institutional discomfort.
**Evening** — **ENCOUNTER 5: The tunnel run.**
**Night** — A dog that will not walk down one particular street, and an owner exasperated enough to complain at length.

**The fight.** Following the stencils or the thieves' route takes them into the storm drains again — and this time somebody has been using them. Not cultists: a courier team moving crates, who would very much rather not be seen.

*Build (Low, 100):* two level 1 couriers (40 each) + a hazard — a sluice gate on a municipal timer, which Structural Command can read and nobody else can.

**Half this encounter is the water.** It teaches the party that the ground roles are not decoration, one session before they need that lesson badly.

*Ends on:* the stencil map, if they made one, and the fact that it is a map of *crowds*.

---

### Session Six — "Ninety Seconds"

**Morning** — Deployment briefing; the ceremony is expected to be tedious and everyone says so.
**Noon** — Founding Day. Band, speeches, Rupiah, several hundred people. Four canisters. **Ninety seconds of sky.**
**Evening** — **ENCOUNTER 6: The crush.**
**Night** — Triage on the quayside. Nobody sleeps.

**The set piece.** Two centuries of an institution that has never been needed, celebrated in the harbour square. The party is on crowd management — their first real deployment, standing about in uniform feeling slightly important.

Then a fine mist, and **the sky fills**: stalked, eyed, listening, past the horizon in every direction. Not descending. Not attacking. Simply there, and clearly always having been there.

*Build (Severe, 180):* a complex hazard across several rounds — a crowd of hundreds through gaps built for dozens. Routes, a collapsing stall, a child separated, a woman against railings, the platform officials making it worse, the square's lighting. **Every ground role has a job.** Real damage is on the table. **Nobody has to die.**

**No combat, deliberately.** The cultists do not run and do not fight. Several stand still, pointing upward. One stays with an elderly man until he is safely up and is arrested for it without resisting.

**The party will be ordered to detain people who will not resist.** That is the scene, and it is better than a fight.

---

### Session Seven — "A Documented Phenomenon"

**Morning** — Individual statements, taken kindly by people writing down something other than what was said.
**Noon** — Oda's address: an aerosolised hallucinogenic agent, a documented phenomenon, no cause for concern. **He is not lying.** He has been told the same thing.
**Evening** — The party's own argument. Let them have it at length, without a ruling.
**Night** — **ENCOUNTER 7: The reagent trail.**

**The fight.** For the first time, the party does something nobody asked them to do — they follow the crates. Which takes them out of cult territory entirely and into a bonded warehouse in the smokestack district, where the people handling the goods are **not believers.** They are professionals. They have no opinions about the sky. They are extremely interested in the fact that six teenagers in civil defence uniforms are standing in their loading bay at midnight.

*Build (Moderate, 120):* one level 2 foreman (60) + two level 1 hands (40 each), on their own ground.

**The tonal contrast is the point.** After a chapter of the numinous, the party meets organised crime, and it is banal, competent, and much more immediately dangerous than anything they've seen.

**And the paperwork is countersigned.** They won't recognise the signature. *(Nobu.)*

*Ends on:* six teenagers deciding, without permission, to find out for themselves.

---

### Session Eight — "The Wrong Alarm"

**Morning** — School. Ordinary to the point of tedium. Someone sits a test.
**Noon** — The wrong alarm, and an institution discovering in real time that it has never rehearsed this.
**Evening** — **ENCOUNTER 8: First Contact.**
**Night** — The casualty list. One name on it.

**The Frame combat.** A siren pattern they have heard exactly once, in a classroom, on a list of alarms never used. Conflicting instructions. A duty officer reading from a binder. **Oda** taking a decision nobody has standing to overrule because nobody has ever had to.

**One Frame launches. Five ground roles, live, for real.**

*(This is the encounter Session Three's drill was deliberately not — the drill taught confidence and comedy on purpose; this should read as proof they weren't ready. Frame and vehicle damage, even destruction, is expected and on the table here, generating real repair backlog. Never a PC death.)*

*Build (Severe at Frame scale, 180):* one Claimant built to the Frame's tier. **Do not overcomplicate the statblock** — this is one player's first Frame combat inside a two-hour session with five other people acting.

**Ground role actions matter and should be fast:** Sensor Operator reveals a weakness on a successful Recall Knowledge (one true fact per success). Umbilical Control converts a success into a saved round of internal power. Evacuation Marshal reduces the casualty track. Structural Command changes the terrain. Signals Liaison holds the pilot's Resonance band. **One roll each per round, resolved in a sentence.**

**And the thing is fighting the statue.** Huge, wholly wrong, grappling a bronze gnoll with total sincerity — and when the statue proves unresponsive, it turns and fights the statue's reflection in the water instead.

Play it absolutely straight. Let them laugh. Let somebody ask whether it's losing.

Then it moves an obstacle out of its way, and **a man is killed.** One man, named later in a list they will read.

## 7. Running Notes

**Two hours of real play per session.** Sessions 3, 6, and 8 are single-set-piece nights with nothing else scheduled against them.

**Session 8 is the tightest.** Keep the Claimant simple, keep the statue business to a few sentences, and hold ground role resolution to one roll each per round.

**Split the party in sessions 4 and 5.** Six players in one conversation is slow; two groups of three doing different duties is faster and gives everyone airtime.

**The tone ledger starts in session 1.** How the party handles the mitflits, the thieves, the couriers, and the arrests is the first data the Mirror has. Write it down.

**Write down after each session:** which plants they noticed, which NPC each player gravitated toward, and the theory they left holding. The theory feeds the Watcher.
