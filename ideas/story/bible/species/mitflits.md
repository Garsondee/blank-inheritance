# Mitflits

**Category:** species
**Status:** established — the origin reframe Ingram flagged as "further, not yet written down" on
2026-09-09 was asked directly and answered in full on 2026-09-10 (`bible/LEDGER.md` #6). Still watch
for smaller details surfacing later, but the core divergence from Paizo is now complete and locked.
**Sources:** `ideas/entries/mitflits-and-anabala.json`, `ideas/story/blank-inheritance-v4.md` §9 +
Thread Grid, `ideas/story/blank-inheritance-ch1-v2.md` §4 + Session Four + Open Call #4,
`ideas/story/bible/LEDGER.md` #6 and #7, direct conversation with Ingram, 2026-09-09 and 2026-09-10.

## Read this first

Mitflits are a sentient species undergoing a slow, deliberately sympathetic reveal across this
campaign — not vermin, not comic relief, and not a species this setting is allowed to treat as
background pest control. The single biggest risk with mitflit content is compressing that reveal
or placing anything morally troubling about them too early, before the campaign has done any work
to make them feel like people — that happened once already, on 2026-09-09 (see **Tone and
register**), and it got cut. Ingram has also said plainly that his own personal take on mitflits
diverges from Paizo's presentation *further* than what's recorded below — where this file is
silent, that divergence is real and simply not written down yet. Ask; don't default to Paizo.

## What's established

- Reframed from Paizo's canonical "fey flavor of goblin" into this campaign's own species:
  descendants of a long-lost star-traveling race that crashed on Golarion long ago and reverted to
  a simpler form over the vast time since. *(`ideas/entries/mitflits-and-anabala.json`)*
- That idea entry's status is **accepted** — this reframe is locked campaign canon, not a maybe.
  *(same file)*
- They were meant to be involved in a previous campaign that never got finished, and in that
  campaign this group's shared history was one of **saving** mitflits, not harming them. This
  campaign is explicitly "a chance to bring them in properly." *(same file; confirmed directly by
  Ingram, 2026-09-09, as the reason the harbour-trap content read so wrong — "The players are all
  ones from the previous adventure where Mitflits were the ones being saved.")*
- Two named variants exist, created by Ingram: **"wild"** and **"ascended."** Nothing beyond their
  names and existence is recorded anywhere yet. *(`ideas/entries/mitflits-and-anabala.json`)*
- **Anabala** is a specific, named, already-conceived NPC — a Mitflit/demon hybrid who has lived
  through the hundreds of years between the unfinished prior campaign and this one. Not yet placed
  in any session. *(same file)*
- A named Elder, **Bobbin**, leads a general strike: a mitflit enters a Frame specifically so it
  cannot be used — not berserk, a general strike, indefinite and non-violent. The Directorate
  cannot extract them without killing them. It's aimed squarely at the machines the party needs,
  putting the players in direct opposition to people who are ethically in the right. The bible's
  own instruction: "Do not resolve that discomfort quickly." *(`blank-inheritance-v4.md` §9)*
- The Thread Grid lays out a deliberately gradual, increasingly sympathetic reveal arc across
  chapters: a mitflit glimpsed salvaging something small at a wreckage, chased off, not followed up
  → later, a mitflit captured alive — foul-mouthed, terrified, spiteful — who, walked past an open
  hangar, stops talking entirely and cannot be made to start again, and is seen weeping at the
  sight of a Frame's hand → mitflits arriving in the city in numbers, housed badly, complained
  about in the papers → the sit-in itself (Bobbin's strike, above). *(`blank-inheritance-v4.md`,
  Thread Grid entries)*
- Chapter One, **Session Four** has an authored beat: a harbour patrol finds flood barriers, tide
  marks, and a mitflit caught in a wall trap, furious and refusing help it obviously needs. Open
  Call #4 asks whether anyone frees it. As of 2026-09-09 this is confirmed as **the first mitflit
  any of them meet at all** — Session One no longer involves one (see below).
  *(`blank-inheritance-ch1-v2.md`)*
- The bible's own "Show, Never Tell" texture list (§4) includes, among many unrelated background
  details, one glancing line: "Mitflit traps on the harbour wall with a few coins' bounty, same as
  rat traps." Written as one unexplained detail in a long list — not a scene, not a tracked thread,
  and never intended as the literal first thing the campaign shows the players.
  *(`blank-inheritance-ch1-v2.md` §4)*
- **Confirmed directly by Ingram, 2026-09-09, and this is now settled canon, not just a tone
  preference:** mitflits have never been treated as vermin or exterminated at any point in this
  setting's history — that reading of the §4 trap line was a misstep on my part, not something the
  line itself demanded. Their real social standing: not the city's most beloved citizens, but a
  nuisance and considered rude at worst. Nobody hunts them, bounties them, or treats harming them as
  routine.
- **Population dynamics (confirmed 2026-09-09):** mitflits are extremely rare in the city at the
  start of the campaign. They become steadily more common over its course — not because more are
  being born or migrating in for ordinary reasons, but because they are actively **gathering
  together on purpose, in order to all be in the same place when they leave to go home.** This is
  the in-world engine behind the Thread Grid's "arrive in numbers... housed badly... complained
  about in the papers" beats above — it's a deliberate convergence, not a population boom.
- **Anabala is a founder of the Directorate itself, found 2026-09-09 reading
  `blank-inheritance-master-timeline.md`.** ~200 years ago a human lord enslaved a mitflit clan for
  their "savant intelligence," using it to design weapons. Anabala, "moved by what's being done to
  her own people," recruited Hyuck, Shucks Mordant, and Rupiah to intervene — the same intervention
  that goes wrong at Kortos and produces the Watcher. This is the mitflit-and-Directorate connection
  that was previously only "a chance to bring them in properly" from the unfinished prior campaign —
  it's now also a literal founding fact: **the institution that later treats mitflits as an
  afterthought exists partly because one mitflit's suffering moved someone to found it.** Sharpens
  why "treating mitflits as vermin" was such a bad miss — the irony isn't incidental, it's structural.
- **The shadow-realm generator thread is RESOLVED, not dangling — corrected 2026-09-10
  (`LEDGER.md` #6).** Earlier drafts (this file included, and `dossier-first-impact.md` §2) called it
  "a thread nobody ever followed up on." That was wrong. **A mitflit savant CHILD** — not an adult
  under the lord's direction, curiosity-driven — built the generator. Switching it on let shadow-plane
  creatures overrun the town below the castle; the castle itself then came under siege as Anabala's
  recruited four (Hyuck, Shucks Mordant, Rupiah, and Anabala herself) approached. They fought up
  through the exploiting lord's own manor house as shadow creatures poured in and a "fog wall of
  shadow" closed on them, worked out that destroying the generator was the fix, did it, and the
  shadow-plane incursion retreated. **This is a wholly separate incident from the Kortos exotic-matter
  accident** — same overall intervention, same four people, but its own earlier encounter with its own
  ending, not connected to the Watcher or anything downstream of it. Nothing further to resolve here
  unless a new question surfaces.
- **Mitflits' real origin, confirmed in full 2026-09-10 (`LEDGER.md` #7) — this is Ingram's own
  further divergence from Paizo, previously flagged as existing but not written down:** mitflits are
  the descendants of an **alien robotic crew** whose vessel crashed on Golarion **thousands of years
  ago** — long, long before the Kortos incident, and unconnected to it. Over that vast span their
  biology, magic and cybernetics **muddled together and degraded**: they dispersed, forgot their own
  skills, and became weaker, more primitive, and more violent — which is the real, in-universe origin
  of their current reputation as "the least of races," not an accident of how the wider world happened
  to perceive them. **Small communities did survive intact enough to keep the old ways and older
  memories alive**, and it's specifically that surviving thread of memory that makes "finding a way
  home" a real, achievable goal rather than a fantasy — the gathering-to-leave arc (above) is these
  communities' actual, long-worked-toward plan, not a spontaneous impulse.
- **Anabala is one of these old ones, and far older than the ~200-year Kortos incident.** Hundreds of
  years before Kortos, she became host to a powerful **female demon** — self-interested but not evil —
  who gave her access to great power and enough clarity to search deep into "the mind of the Mitflit"
  (the species' own collective memory/psyche). What the demon found there suggested mitflits could
  become a powerful people again, and the demon wants them to succeed. **Anabala is a High Priestess
  and Prophet of the mitflits** — a specific, real religious/spiritual leadership title within their
  surviving culture, not just "a mitflit who happens to be old."
- **Rupiah is the reason "gathering to go home" is happening at all.** Where others met mitflits with
  violence, **Rupiah used diplomacy** — and started them down the actual path toward getting home.
  This directly connects one of the Directorate's own founders to the campaign's central ongoing
  mitflit thread, not just to the founding/Kortos incident. *(Elf, elf nobility — `LEDGER.md` #8.)*

## Ingram's own take, and how it diverges from any real-world source

**Paizo's default:** mitflits are a fey-flavored subtype of goblin — small, chaotic, comic-relief
adjacent, generally treated by the wider world with the same mix of contempt and casual
nuisance-management goblins usually get.

**This campaign's confirmed divergence:** descendants of a crashed star-traveling species, reverted
to a simpler form over a very long time — not fey, not goblins by origin. Closer to a
lost-civilization / amnesiac-precursor framing than to anything Paizo's flavor text suggests.

**This campaign's *further* divergence — now recorded in full, `LEDGER.md` #7:** alien robotic crew,
not organic star-travelers in the generic sense; a crash thousands of years ago, not merely "long
ago"; biology/magic/cybernetics degrading together into confusion over that span, which is *why* they
declined into "the least of races" rather than that decline being unexplained; small surviving
communities as the actual load-bearing reason a return home is possible at all; and Anabala's real
nature (demon-hosted high priestess/prophet, far older than the other three founders) and Rupiah's
real role (the one who chose diplomacy and started the return-home path) both now settled facts, not
open questions. **Still do not fall back on Paizo flavor** — comic goblin-adjacent pest, casual
contempt from NPCs, played for cheap laughs — for anything a future question doesn't explicitly cover;
the discipline that produced this section stays the same even though this particular gap is closed.

## Tone and register — what NOT to do

**Never write mitflits as unremarked vermin or pests — trapped, bountied, or casually harmed as
background texture. This is not just a tone call, it is factually wrong about the setting.** Tried
once, 2026-09-09, in the opening beat of "The Road In" (a mitflit trap on the harbour wall, glimpsed
on arrival, offered as a passing Encyclopaedia check, bounty "same as rat traps"). Cut entirely
after Ingram's correction: *"Mitflits as vermin is on a level that makes the society that the
players are operating in one that kills sentient creatures casually and even cruelly... We don't
want the players thinking that they are working for the Third Reich."* He then confirmed directly
that this isn't only a matter of framing: **mitflits have never been exterminated or treated as
vermin at any point in this setting's actual history.** They're a nuisance, considered rude at
worst — never hunted, never bountied. The single §4 line this was drawn from was always meant as one
glancing, unexplained detail among many — not a scene, not a tracked thread, and never the literal
first thing the campaign shows. If a future scene needs a mitflit to be unpopular or an annoyance,
that's fine and consistent with canon; a mitflit being trapped, bountied, or killed as routine is
not, ever.

**Session One's first combat used to be a mitflit nest — fixed 2026-09-09, same day as the rest of
this file.** I initially flagged this as an open question rather than fixing it outright, hedging
that the nest might be defensible as an early sign of the gathering-together above. Ingram's
response was correctly blunt: *"Doesn't that scene now massively contradict everything I've been
saying? ... of course we need to replace our Mitflit combat encounter with a different fight
entirely."* **The lesson: a "standard vermin clearance" combat encounter against a sentient species
is the same problem as the trap, not a smaller one, and doesn't get a pass just because it wasn't
raised in the same conversation.** The encounter is now six giant centipedes — same numbers
(party level −2, 120 XP total), same storm-drain/rising-tide setting and "worst job, everyone
comes out filthy" framing, zero mitflit content. See `blank-inheritance-ch1-v2.md` §6.

**Never front-load mitflit content, especially anything morally troubling, before the campaign has
done any work to make them feel like people.** The bible's own pacing (the Thread Grid) is
deliberately gradual: glimpsed and chased off → captured, terrified, then reduced to weeping
silence → arriving in numbers and treated badly by the city → the sit-in. Compressing that arc, or
starting anywhere except its actual beginning, is the mistake that happened.

**The general strike (Bobbin/the Elder) is supposed to be uncomfortable — that's intentional, and
it's a different thing from the mistake above.** The bible explicitly wants the players in
opposition to people who are ethically right about this, and says not to resolve that discomfort
quickly. The difference that matters: that discomfort is earned by chapters of buildup and is about
a specific, named, principled non-violent action — not casual, unremarked cruelty on page one with
no context at all.

**Session Four's trapped-mitflit beat stays in the campaign** — Ingram did not object to it, only
to the opening-scene framing — but it must play as a specific, sympathetic individual encounter:
furious, obviously suffering, worth freeing. It should read as wrong and upsetting, not as routine
pest control.

**When in doubt whether something reads as casual cruelty toward a sentient species, ask before
writing it.** That's exactly the judgment call this file exists to short-circuit next time — read
the whole file first, and if the specific situation isn't covered here, ask rather than extrapolate
from Paizo defaults or from the §4 texture line in isolation.
