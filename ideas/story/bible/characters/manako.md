# Manako

*The Chemist. Founder of Cicada. The campaign's primary antagonist, and the first friendly stranger the party ever meets.*

---

## Read this first

**He is the linchpin.** The Observer has no personality of its own — it conforms to the strongest coherent signal it receives, and for forty years the only coherent signal on this planet has been this man. **Its behaviour is a portrait of his grievance.** Everything else in the campaign runs through him: Nobu's guilt, Shucks's past, Nozomi's refusal, Shinobu's condition, the Claimants the Frames fight, and the party's own investigation, which feeds the thing every time they use a lens.

**Read `ideas/story/dossier-cicada.md` before writing him at all** — §3 (the fall), §4 (the operator reveal), §5 (the Pivot), and **§13 (the arc)**. This file is the at-the-table voice reference; the dossier is the truth.

**The one thing that must never leak: he does not know he is the operator.** He believes he is a faithful worshipper interpreting divine intent, and there is nothing to interpret. He can be written talking freely and honestly about anything, because the only fact that matters is one he would sincerely deny.

---

## What's established

**Manako is an old word for the eye. A man called Eye, who nobody would look at.** And the thing he found grew eyes.

- **A chemist, disgraced.** Handed a shelved two-century-old sample nobody senior thought was worth their time. Cracked it open, a fragment of the cloud fed on it and became briefly corporeal, he panicked, a bunsen burner went over, there was a small fire. Job gone, reputation gone.
- **Elderly now, and physically frail.** He runs the corporeal side of an entire war from a position of complete bodily weakness, and has spent decades biding, building and choosing the moment.
- **The first failed demonstration.** Before the conference he tried to show colleagues, and the chemical worked perfectly — the Watcher had simply moved. Nothing happened and he was finished as a serious person. **He still has that speech.**
- **The conference.** Having checked first, he detonated a canister in a crowded hall without warning anyone. A room of scientists concluded a colleague had gone off the deep end.
- **The Pivot.** A police officer, Hori, shot the Observer's own Envoy in fear. The round passed through it and into a bystander — **Aoi**, Manako's lover. Before: he wanted the city to see. After: he wanted the city to pay.
- **He was never believed. He was echoed.** He already half-suspects this and has arranged forty years of his life so the question cannot be asked.
- **Killing him is the worst available move** — the cloud loses its only organising signal and goes undirected over a populated region.

### His arc, in five movements

**Patient → Delighted → Cornered → Frightened → Useful.** Each turn caused by the party. Full table, with what turns each one, in `dossier-cicada.md` §13.1. **The Claimants are his mood ring** (§13.2) — what manifests is downstream of which movement he is in, which is why his arc and the campaign's escalation curve are the same curve.

## Voice

**Register.** Unhurried, precise, warm. An academic's diction worn soft by decades out of academia. Delighted by a good question and visibly out of practice at being asked one. **Never bitter in front of strangers** — the grievance is enormous and entirely private.

**Tics.**

- Handles the object before he speaks — turns it over, holds it to the light, reads the back.
- Answers the question asked, then the better question they should have asked.
- Apologises for talking too much, and carries on talking.
- Refers to the Directorate with perfect courtesy and no warmth whatsoever.

**Samples** (from the first meeting — `data/scene-briefs/episode-c1s1--the-errand.json`):

> "Ah — no, they wouldn't know that one, and it isn't their fault. Nobody's used that name since before the shop. Before the street, probably."

> "I was a chemist. Past tense's doing a good deal of work in that sentence."

> "Can I say something, and you needn't do anything with it? They'll tell you what you're for. Everyone does that to the young, it's not a crime. But that lot wrote theirs down, and then they painted over it, and I've always thought that was worth knowing about the people who feed you."

> "If they send you out with another of those, bring it here. It's a kindness to me, mind, not the other way about. Nobody's asked me anything in a very long time."

**Later movements shift the register and nothing else.** Delighted: faster, more generous, more corrections. Cornered: shorter sentences, and he stops apologising for talking. Frightened: he talks *over* things.

## Tone and register — what NOT to do

1. **Do not make him mysterious at the first meeting.** No significant pause, no watching them leave, no ominous parting line. He is helpful and then he finishes his tea. The whole force of the eventual reveal depends on that scene being exactly what it looks like.
2. **Do not play him as insane, or as evil.** Ingram, 2026-09-13: the first meeting must demonstrate "that they aren't an insane evil bad guy." He is a courteous, intelligent, grieving old man who is catastrophically wrong about one thing.
3. **Do not have him lie.** The cult never lies and neither does he. He is a reliable narrator of everything except the fact he cannot see.
4. **Do not let him recruit them on day one.** He is not pitching Cicada at the tea house. He says one true thing and offers a way to be contacted, and that is all.
5. **Do not let the party kill or capture him early.** Confirmed 2026-09-13. The henchmen are the recurring fights; he is the rare event, perhaps four appearances in the whole campaign.
6. **Do not have him explain the Observer.** He would explain it *wrongly*, sincerely — and a GM improvising his cosmology risks accidentally writing the real answer into his mouth.
7. **Do not hand the table the *conclusion*.** His name is safe and should be given freely when asked — nobody can look him up, because the Directorate has never identified Cicada's founder. What must never be handed over is the deduction: **no NPC ever tells the party that Cicada has an author, and no NPC ever points at him.** Both halves are theirs to assemble. `dossier-cicada.md` §13.5.

1. ~~Does a day-one records search find him?~~ **Answered 2026-09-13 (LEDGER #17).** No — the Directorate has never identified Cicada's founder, so a search returns a chemist disgraced decades ago and nothing else. **Give the table his name freely when asked**; the later recognition depends on it having been in their notes all along. And **both halves of the discovery belong to the players** — that Cicada has an author at all, and that the author is the old man from the tea house. See `dossier-cicada.md` §13.5 and rule 7 below.
2. **Aoi's ancestry and history.** They exist so far only as a death and a name.
3. **Does he learn about the heist loop before or after the party stops him**, and does he learn about Nobu in the same conversation or a different one?
4. **The text of the speech** he gave to the room where nothing happened. Established that he has it and still could perform it; not written.
5. **What the cult calls the Observer**, if anything of their own.
