# Nozomi

**Category:** character
**Status:** partial — the sequence of what happened to her is now well established; her own voice and
exact current words aren't written yet.
**Sources:** `ideas/story/blank-inheritance-master-timeline.md`, `ideas/story/dossier-cicada.md` §7,
direct conversation, 2026-09-10. Formerly referred to as "Kai" in early drafts — **that name is
banned campaign-wide**, see `feedback_lore_bible_discipline`-adjacent notes; always Nozomi.

## Read this first

Nozomi was slated as the presumptive first pilot — the seat the party's own Day 1 pilot ends up filling
instead. Weeks before Day 1, she went to what she thought was an ordinary private illusionist show
(§ below — this is a real, common thing in this city) and it turned out to be a **Cicada recruitment
drive**. She was shown the Watcher, undeniably, and given a real choice: leave and tell people what she
saw, or stay and learn more. **She stayed.** A senior cultist told her, specifically, that she was
working for the wrong side. The next day she turned off her beeper and phone and avoided the draft.
**The party's own induction happened earlier than it otherwise would have, directly because of this** —
a seat needed filling sooner than planned. She's currently in questioning/temporary detainment: not
talking freely, but not silent either — a begrudging, already-debriefed witness a careful conversation
could get something real out of.

## What's established

- **The magic-show system, as a real setting institution.** Showy, public magic is frowned upon in this
  city except in genuine emergencies — a cultural fact, not just a Cicada quirk. Private illusionist
  shows are consequently a common, mildly guilty pleasure: most residents have been to at least one in
  their life, small audiences, immersive illusion performances. Some are explicitly religious-themed
  and staged openly during high holidays. This is the same cover industry `dossier-cicada.md` §7
  already documents — Nozomi's show was one of these, indistinguishable from the ordinary kind until it
  wasn't.
- **What actually happened at the show.** The performers revealed the Watcher, filling the room, then
  demonstrated — undeniably, not as illusion — that it was real. The audience was then given an actual
  choice: leave and tell people what they'd seen, or stay and learn more. This is a structural addition
  to how Cicada's recruitment shows work generally, not unique to her case — see `dossier-cicada.md` §7
  for the fuller mechanic once folded in there.
- **She stayed, and was told something specific.** A high-level cultist told her directly that she was
  "working for the wrong side." Not vague evangelism — a targeted, personal statement, aimed at a
  presumptive Frame pilot specifically.
- **The very next day, she declined.** Turned off her beeper and phone, avoided being drafted to fight.
  No violence, no scene, no confrontation on record — just absence.
- **Consequence for the party: they were activated early.** "The group goes into action earlier than
  they normally should" is a direct quote from the discussion establishing this — her defection created
  a timing pressure that accelerated Day 1 itself, not just a vacancy the party happens to fill.
- **The first trace of it is on paper (LEDGER #38, 2026-09-26).** Every acceptance letter this year has
  its printed reporting date, "the first Monday of term", ruled through under a red 繰上 BROUGHT
  FORWARD stamp, with "Monday next" typed in. It is a fact, not a lead: nothing on the letter points at
  her, and nobody at the gate explains it. The overheard search at Intake Medicals is the first thing
  that can make sense of it, and still only as a fragment (see below).
- **Her current state: questioning and temporary detainment.** She's been through this once already
  with her actual questioners, which is exactly why a careful visitor might get more than official
  channels would — she's tired of repeating herself to the wrong kind of asking, not unwilling to talk
  at all.
- **The skill is in the asking.** A conversation that doesn't "ruffle her feathers" gets somewhere; one
  that pushes or treats her like a suspect makes her go quiet and stop cooperating. This reads as a real
  social challenge opportunity — a DC and skill set for it isn't written yet.

## Tone and register — what NOT to do

**She is not a traitor, a villain, or a coward.** She was shown something true, was given an honest
choice by people who didn't lie to her, and made a considered decision under real pressure. Play her as
someone who thought this through, not someone who panicked or was tricked.

**Don't make the detainment interview easy or a simple info-dump.** The "don't ruffle her feathers"
framing is explicit: this should function as a real, failable social challenge, with a genuine cost to
getting it wrong (she clams up, the party gets nothing, maybe burns the chance for anyone else to try).

**Her absence is not a mystery to be solved by the party in Chapter One** — it's the reason they exist,
established backstory, not an active investigation thread for Episode One specifically. Whether/when
the party learns any of this is open (see below).

**The Intake Medicals overhear check (`overhear-the-search`) must stay a fragment, even on a critical
success.** Confirmed 2026-09-10: this is meant to be perceptible as "something is happening," not a
resolvable lead. Even the crit-success outcome only half-catches a name and a mismatched word —
enough to make a sharp player go quiet and wonder, never enough to act on. Don't let a future beat
build directly off what a player overheard here without a real, separate scene making it actionable.
