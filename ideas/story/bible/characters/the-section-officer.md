# The Section Officer

*The party's immediate superior. The campaign's in-house petty tyrant, and its most beatable antagonist.*

---

## Read this first

**He is not a villain and he is not a joke at the players' expense. He is an obstacle with a clipboard, and the campaign's first winnable fight is against him.**

He runs Section Nine, which means he runs the party's day — what they do, when, and whether it counted. He has total authority over everything between reveille and lights out and none whatsoever over anything that matters, and the second fact is why the first one is exercised so thoroughly.

**He hazes new intakes because he was hazed.** He believes it builds character. He has never examined the belief, because nobody has ever made him.

---

## Why he exists

Three jobs, and he is the cheapest way to do all three.

1. **He makes the Directorate's contempt personal.** The institution's faults (`ideas/story/dossier-the-directorate.md`) are structural, two centuries old, and mostly invisible. He is one man, in the room, doing a small version of the same thing, every week. The party can be angry at him in a way they cannot be angry at a charter.
2. **He makes Manako look wonderful by comparison** — which is the point. In Session One a superior sends them for a bubble and a stranger shows them how to win. Whatever the party concludes about which of those two people is on their side, they concluded it themselves, from evidence, on day one.
3. **He is a victory the party can actually have.** The Observer cannot be beaten by force and Manako cannot be beaten by argument. This one can be beaten, by being better than he expected, and he should be — see the arc.

---

## The transmission chain

**This is the thematic load he carries, and it should never be said out loud by anyone.**

It was done to Manako, forty years ago, in a Directorate lab. It was done to the Section Officer. He does it to the party.

Three people, the same institutional shrug. **One of them it broke.** The difference between the Section Officer and Manako is not character — it is that nobody handed the Section Officer a two-century-old sample and then blamed him for what happened.

And the same shrug scales: the hand that gives a new kid a fool's errand is the hand that gave a junior nobody a shelved sample *because nobody senior considered it worth their time*. **The party experiences the origin of the campaign as a prank.**

---

## What's established

- **Middling rank, middle-aged, immaculate paperwork.** Whatever else is true, his forms are correct. It is the only thing he is unambiguously good at and he knows it.
- **He was hazed as a new intake** and came out the other side believing the system works.
- **He sends the party for a bubble** in Session One (`data/scene-briefs/episode-c1s1--the-errand.json`). He thinks it is funny. He is not expecting them to come back with a correctly-filed requisition for a replacement vial that needs a countersignature from a desk above his.
- **He is never wrong, in his own account.** When the party succeeds at something he did not expect, he reframes it as having been the intention. This is his single most characteristic behaviour and it should happen every single time.
- **He is not dangerous.** He cannot get them hurt, sent away, or charged. The worst he can do is make a fortnight tedious, and he will.

---

## The arc

**Ingram, 2026-09-13:** *"an antagonist from within the system who the players will eventually defeat by proving them wrong, out performing their expectations and eventually they'll be promoted out of that petty tyrants clutches."*

| Stage | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **I. The bubble** | He sends them on a fool's errand. They come back correct, and he has to sign it. First blood, and it is theirs |
| **II. Entrenchment** | He responds by being *more* correct — more inspections, more paperwork, more assignments chosen to be tedious. Perfectly within his authority, and unpleasant |
| **III. The gap** | Something real happens and he is out of his depth in front of them. He does not fail heroically; he simply has no idea, and defers. Do not humiliate him here — let it be quiet and let the party notice |
| **IV. Outperformance** | They do something he said could not be done, in front of someone senior. He claims it was his plan. Everyone present knows |
| **V. Promoted out** | They move up and out of his section. He is still there. He will do this to the next intake |

**Stage V is the ending and it is not a triumph.** They escape him; he is not removed, corrected or improved. Somebody else's turn now. That is the honest version and it is also funnier.

## Voice

**Register.** Clipped, procedural, faintly aggrieved. Speaks in the passive voice about anything that went wrong and the first person about anything that went right. Uses their full section designation rather than their names, always, and treats that as neutral rather than cold.

**Tics.**

- Says "as discussed" about things that were not discussed.
- Answers a question by reading the relevant clause aloud.
- Calls them "Section Nine," never by name, even individually.
- When surprised, pauses exactly one beat and then says something that recasts the surprise as expected.

**Samples:**

> "Section Nine. Stores docket, collection, quarter four. I'd like it before I go home. — No, I don't need your opinion on it, I need the item."

> "As discussed, the standard is the standard. I don't set it, I just notice when it isn't met."

> "That's — " (one beat) " — yes. That's what I'd have expected of you, frankly. Good. File it and carry on."

> "I was doing this when you were at school. I'm aware some of you still are."

---

## Tone and register — what NOT to do

1. **Do not make him cruel.** He is tedious, self-important and small. A genuinely cruel superior changes the campaign's register and makes the Directorate sinister rather than calcified — which the whole design refuses (`dossier-the-directorate.md` §1).
2. **Do not make him competent-but-harsh.** He is not a hard man teaching hard lessons. He is mediocre and comfortable, and the lesson he thinks he is teaching is one nobody needs.
3. **Do not let him win, ever, on the merits.** He can impose tedium. He cannot be right at the party's expense in a way that stands.
4. **Do not redeem him.** No late scene where he was secretly proud of them all along. He is not the mentor in disguise. The party escapes him and he stays exactly as he is.
5. **Do not let him harm them.** No real risk, no real consequence, no arc where his pettiness gets someone hurt. That is a different and much darker character.
6. **Never explain the transmission chain.** The party may notice that Manako was hazed the same way. Nobody points at it.
