## Read this first

The canteen's own gravitational center, Session One evening: "arrives uninvited and has renamed
all six of them before the plates are cleared." Reappears Session Three briefing the Frame drill,
and again that night with a flash of real weight — "whoever asks Taka gets a beat of silence,
something about hydraulic compensation, and a change of subject." The bible states twice, in
identical words, that this matters: *"Make the table love Taka tonight. Everything he is for later
depends on it."* Read as: instant, unearned, completely confident warmth on the surface, real
hands-on competence underneath it that only shows when something serious actually comes up.

  A third register in the campaign's adult-NPC set, distinct from both existing ones: Shucks is
  warm because he has infinite time; Nobu is generous despite having none; Taka is warm because
  it costs him nothing to be, which is its own kind of tell for later — this is a person who has
  not yet been ground down by the institution the way the two older men have.
- **Likely a peer rather than staff** — an older student or recent graduate, not an instructor from
  a book. The nicknames read as a peer's bonding move, and the hydraulic-compensation comment
  implies real stick time, not classroom knowledge. Not yet confirmed outright.

## Tone and register — what NOT to do

**Don't make him a third variation on institutional kindness-with-a-cost.** Shucks and Nobu are both
adults whose warmth is entangled with the Directorate in some way — practiced patience, guilty
generosity. Taka should read as simpler and more genuine than either, at least for now. If a hidden
cost or secret ever gets added to him later, that's a real creative decision to make deliberately,
not something to default into just because the other two adults in his scene have one.

**The competence should surprise, not announce itself.** Play the goofiness completely straight
until the hydraulic-compensation beat (or an equivalent moment) actually arrives — no foreshadowing
winks that "there's more to him." The bible's own instruction is a beat of silence, not a speech.
