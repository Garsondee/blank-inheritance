## Read this first

The canteen's own gravitational center, Episode One evening — originally written as "arrives
uninvited and has renamed all six of them before the plates are cleared," **revised 2026-09-19** to
claim them as his table rather than invent a nickname for each (see "What's established" below for
why). In the old Episode Three draft (deleted in the 2026-09-13 reset) he reappeared briefing the
Frame drill, and again that night with a flash of real weight — "whoever asks Taka gets a beat of
silence, something about hydraulic compensation, and a change of subject." That draft stated twice,
in identical words, that this matters: *"Make the table
love Taka tonight. Everything he is for later depends on it."* Read as: instant, unearned, completely
confident warmth on the surface, real hands-on competence underneath it that only shows when
something serious actually comes up.

- **REVISED 2026-09-19 — no longer specific invented nicknames.** Originally: Taka renames each of
  the six on sight. Ingram, reviewing the redesign: *"I have no idea about this giving of names
  thing... it feels like something that could easily backfire. Again it also presumes we can guess
  what the PCs will be like, we don't know so it seems like a very risky thing to setup. We can make
  warmth in other ways."* See [[feedback_design_before_characters_exist]] — the same principle as
  Episode One's opening prompts: don't author specific, sticky, personal content for characters that
  don't exist yet. The warmth now comes from total, immediate inclusion (claiming the table, making
  room, seizing on whatever they actually said) rather than labelling. **Worth reconsidering once the
  six real PCs exist** — a nickname aimed at a known character is a different, much lower-risk thing
  than one aimed at an unknown table, and this file's own register (instant, confident, unexplained)
  would still support it if Ingram wants it back later.
- **Likely a peer rather than staff** — an older student or recent graduate, not an instructor from
  a book. The instant-claiming reads as a peer's bonding move, and the hydraulic-compensation comment
  implies real stick time, not classroom knowledge. Not yet confirmed outright.
- **CUT 2026-09-24 — his berserk (`LEDGER.md` #31: "No berserk").** The v4 bible scheduled Taka to go
  berserk in Chapter 3: his Frame dropping its weapon to stand over an evacuation route, his surviving
  it by default, and a debrief where he says there was *someone in there with him* and is not believed
  (`blank-inheritance-v4.md` §2, §17, §19 Chapter Three, §21; §17, §19 and §21's Taka row were
  removed on 2026-09-25). All of that is gone. Mitflit technology
  has no reason to go berserk, and nothing in this campaign takes control of a character away from
  their player. The same bible had him teaching the party "the berserk protocol" by heart, and
  LEDGER #31 proposed turning it into Hyuck's emergency protocol, which he could still have drilled
  into them. **LEDGER #32 moves that protocol to Gen 2 only and leaves it open**, so it is no longer
  something Taka teaches every kid; whether he has any part in it is open with it. Everything else in
  this file stands.
- **He flies Gen 1, which has no living core (`LEDGER.md` #32, 2026-09-24).** Gen 1 Frames are
  Golarion technology, informed by looking at the generation ship's: mechanical clankers, hard to
  work with, with no living core and no seat that shapes itself. (Gen 2, the biomechanical kind, is
  rare and is only offered to the party at the end of Chapter 4.) **There is nobody in there with
  him**, and nothing about Taka should suggest otherwise: the old "someone in there with him" debrief
  came from the berserk, which is cut, and a Gen 1 Frame has no one in it to have been there. Whatever
  sits behind the hydraulic-compensation beat (still open, below), it is not a presence in the machine.

## Tone and register — what NOT to do

**Don't make him a third variation on institutional kindness-with-a-cost.** Shucks and Nobu are both
adults whose warmth is entangled with the Directorate in some way — practiced patience, guilty
generosity. Taka should read as simpler and more genuine than either, at least for now. If a hidden
cost or secret ever gets added to him later, that's a real creative decision to make deliberately,
not something to default into just because the other two adults in his scene have one.

**The competence should surprise, not announce itself.** Play the goofiness completely straight
until the hydraulic-compensation beat (or an equivalent moment) actually arrives — no foreshadowing
winks that "there's more to him." The bible's own instruction is a beat of silence, not a speech.
