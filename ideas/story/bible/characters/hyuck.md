## Read this first

Hyuck is one of the four founders: the gnoll in the old campaign's party (Hyuck Bonebox, a spirit-instinct barbarian
who carried his elder brother's skull and consulted it), played then, and in any flashback now, by Steve. Two hundred
years later he is alive, on the base, and the mad test pilot of the Directorate's worst Frame designs (LEDGER #28). The
players meet him in Chapter 7 without being told who he is, and find out in Chapter 12.

He is alive through **the Carrying**, a gnoll practice. The practice is Ingram's (LEDGER #41). The name and the details
below it are Claude's, marked as proposals.

GM-only. None of this reaches the table before Chapter 12.

## The Carrying

**Ingram's account (LEDGER #41, canon).** Gnolls go in for spirit matters in a big way, and one thing they do is join an
older gnoll's spirit to a younger gnoll's body. Gnolls count it a huge honour, but it carries a risk: every transfer is
a fight, in which the spirit tries to overpower the soul already in the body. A strong young gnoll, wise and strong in
personality, keeps control, and gains a wise older spirit riding along as a source of wisdom and knowledge. A strong
spirit can do the opposite: take over the body and push the gnoll who was there into the background. With particularly
important gnolls this goes on for hundreds of years. **Hyuck has never once lost a transfer, and he is now in a body
with a multitude of other gnoll spirits, which is rather maddening.**

**Claude's details (proposals, veto freely):**

- **The name.** Gnolls call it *the Carrying*, because it grows out of how they already honour their great dead: they
  carry their skulls. A carried skull keeps its spirit close. The Carrying is the next step: the spirit is carried in a
  living body instead of at a living belt.
- **The skull.** Between bodies, the spirit waits in its own skull. When a carried gnoll's body finally dies, the
  skull goes back into the clan's keeping until a new bearer is found.
- **The bearer.** The young gnoll who offers their body. Being asked is the highest honour a clan gives, and nobody is
  made to say yes.
- **The wrestle.** The contest when the spirit comes in. It is fought inside, over one night. Anyone watching sees a
  young gnoll lying very still, and then waking up.
- **Riding and wearing.** If the bearer holds, the elder *rides*: a voice of memory and advice, heard from inside and
  never in charge. If the elder wins, it *wears* the body, and the bearer's own soul goes quiet, pushed to the back but
  not gone.
- **Hyuck's count.** Since Kortos he has been carried five or six times (the GM's choice), and he has worn every body.
  Everyone he has beaten is still in there: the bearers he pushed to the back, and the elders who tried to wear him
  when he was young. That probably includes his elder brother, whose skull he carried in the old campaign, and who on
  this reading rides with him now.
- **Where his bearers come from.** Gnoll clans count it the greatest honour there is to carry a founder, so young
  volunteers come to Kurose for it. The Directorate arranges it quietly, which is part of why it keeps him close. This
  folds in the "well-looked-after asset" thread of Steve's pitch.
- **The statue.** The harbour statue was carved in one of his earlier bodies. It is him, but not the face he wears
  now.

## How to play him

- **Loud, lucky, loyal, and impossible to argue with,** as Steve's pitch has him: too stubborn to change his mind, fast
  and violent in a fight, and with a talent for offending everyone eventually.
- **A crowd, not a split personality.** Now and then another voice answers for him, argues with him mid-sentence, or
  remembers something from a life he didn't live. Play it for comedy first. It is maddening for him.
- **Never lost.** He will tell anyone who asks, and brag about it. It is also the saddest fact about him, for anyone who
  thinks it through: every win pushed somebody to the back.
- *(Proposal)* **Gen 2 seats** reshape themselves to the pilot's true form. With a crowd inside him, a Gen 2 seat can
  never settle on one shape, which is one reason he flies the prototypes nobody else will. Hyuck's protocol (the
  Mystery Ledger's plant 8) is still Gen 2-only and open.
