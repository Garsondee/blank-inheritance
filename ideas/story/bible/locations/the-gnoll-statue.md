# The Gnoll Statue (Hyuck)

**Category:** location
**Status:** partial — physical description, tone, and (almost certainly) Hyuck's identity are locked;
exactly how the statue itself came to exist is still inference, not stated fact.
**Sources:** `ideas/story/blank-inheritance-ch1-v2.md` §4 and §6, `ideas/story/blank-inheritance-master-timeline.md`,
plus direct conversation, 2026-09-09.

## Read this first

A weathered stone gnoll, posed on guard in the harbour square, with a plaque that says "Hyuck"
(legible since LEDGER #27; it was first written as worn past reading). Kids climb it, pigeons sit on it, it's
unremarkable civic furniture, not a hushed monument. Its one genuinely important feature: the
statue is clearly *trying* to be dignified and heroic, and whether it succeeds is a live, unresolved
question — Ingram was explicit that this is for the players to decide, not the GM. Never resolve it
in narration.

## What's established

- Filed originally as one line in the bible's own "Show, Never Tell" list (§4, History): "A
  weathered gnoll statue in the harbour square, plaque worn past reading, pigeons, kids climbing it
  *(Hyuck)*." Planted as unexplained ambient texture among several other unrelated items — never a
  scene, never explained by any NPC. *(`blank-inheritance-ch1-v2.md` §4)*
- **Pose and subject:** a fearsome gnoll barbarian warrior, on guard. *(2026-09-09)*
- **Which body** *(proposal, LEDGER #41)*: Hyuck lives on through the Carrying (`bible/characters/hyuck.md`), so the
  statue shows one of his earlier bodies. It is him, but not the face he wears now.
- **Expression, in detail:** not straightforwardly dignified. If the stone were flesh, it would read
  as foaming at the mouth — tongue lolling between oversized teeth. At the same time, it's clear a
  lot of sculptural effort went into making this feral quality carry a certain dignity anyway — the
  guard stance, the formal presentation, are doing real work against what the face is doing.
  *(2026-09-09)*
- **Whether that effort succeeds is explicitly not decided by this file, or by the GM in play.**
  Ingram's own words: "Whether the statue succeeds at this task or not is up to the players to
  decide." Describe the statue exactly as above and stop there. *(2026-09-09)*
- The plaque is worn past reading under normal circumstances. Someone who makes a real effort to
  read it anyway finds the name **Hyuck**. *(`ch1-v2.md` §4, restated and confirmed 2026-09-09)*
  *(Changed 2026-09-24, LEDGER #27: the plaque is legible now, since a teacher reads it on day one;
  `data/scene-briefs/episode-c1e1--the-errand.json` has it "still reads HYUCK".)*
- Location: the harbour square. *(`ch1-v2.md` §4)*
- **A payoff was once written, and it was deleted in the 2026-09-13 reset.** The old Episode Eight
  climax (`ch1-v2.md` §6, "The Wrong Alarm", found 2026-09-09 while building a session-structure map)
  had the Claimant fighting the statue itself: "the thing is fighting the statue. Huge, wholly wrong,
  grappling a bronze gnoll with total sincerity — and when the statue proves unresponsive, it turns and
  fights the statue's reflection in the water instead." Nothing schedules that now; the image is kept
  here as an idea, not a plan. It is also where "bronze" came from, against the "weathered" stone of
  §4 (see Open questions).
- **Who Hyuck almost certainly was, found 2026-09-09 reading `blank-inheritance-master-timeline.md`:**
  ~200 years ago, "Anabala, moved by what's being done to her own people, recruits Hyuck, Shucks
  Mordant, and Rupiah to intervene" — the founding party. This same group attempts the Kortos
  retrieval that goes wrong and pulverises part of the buried ship into what becomes the Watcher, then
  founds the Directorate itself ~195 years ago. *(2026-09-24, `LEDGER.md` #31: the buried ship is the
  generation ship, and what was pulverised was its navigation and sensor system.)* **Hyuck is named as one of the four founders, and is
  a gnoll.** A weathered/bronze gnoll statue in the harbour square with a plaque reading "Hyuck" is
  therefore almost certainly a monument to this Hyuck — the name match plus a Directorate-founded
  city plus a gnoll subject is about as strong as inference gets without a line of text that says so
  outright. **Treat as near-certain, not yet 100% textually confirmed** — no source explicitly states
  "the statue depicts founder Hyuck," so don't hard-lock it in front of the table without one more
  check. *(`blank-inheritance-master-timeline.md`)*
- If this holds, it also answers "why a gnoll specifically" (below): not a one-off or an unrelated
  species thread, but the actual species of one of the city's four founders — worth checking whether
  gnolls appear anywhere else as a result of that same founding-party fact before assuming they don't.

## Tone and register — what NOT to do

**Never resolve the dignified-vs-undignified question in narration, and never let an NPC resolve it
either.** Describe the guard pose and the feral expression with equal weight and stop. If a player
asks a passing NPC what they think of it, a shrug or a one-line personal opinion is fine — a
setting-wide consensus is not, because the ambiguity is the point.

**Never over-explain who Hyuck was or why a gnoll has a civic statue here.** Nothing about that is
established, and this item's whole role in the bible is "Show, Never Tell" — planted, unglossed
texture, not a hook that demands a lore-dump the first time someone looks at it twice. If pressed,
in-world ignorance ("it's always been there," "there's supposedly a plaque") is more in keeping with
its role than an NPC who happens to know the answer.

## Open questions

- **Is the statue definitely OF founder-Hyuck, and does anyone in the city still know that, or has
  it been forgotten the way the plaque itself has?** Near-certain per the timeline connection above,
  but not yet confirmed as an explicit stated fact, and "does anyone remember" is a separate, open
  question even if the identity is right.
- **Stone or bronze?** §4 calls it "weathered" (read as stone when this file was first written);
  the deleted Episode Eight draft called it "a bronze gnoll" outright. Not yet reconciled — pick one
  before the statue is used in play. **This is now closer to the table than it was:** the statue is spoken about
  on day one in `data/scene-briefs/episode-c1e1--school-first-period.json`. That line deliberately
  says only "weathered", which is true of both and decides nothing — but the first GM who runs it and
  gets asked "stone or metal?" will have to answer, and whatever they say becomes canon at that table.
  Worth Ingram settling before Episode One is run rather than at it.

### `arc-the-four-founders` — who the four were, and whether anyone alive can still say so
**Status:** open · **Last touched:** 2026-09-24 (reconciled with famous founders, LEDGER #27)
**Setup:** Four people were present at First Impact — Anabala (mitflit), Hyuck (gnoll), Shucks Mordant
and Rupiah (`ideas/story/dossier-first-impact.md`). One of them stands weathered in the harbour square
with his name on the plaque. One of them takes the party's blood on their first morning and teaches a
field medicine class on Tuesdays.

**Reconciled 2026-09-24 (LEDGER #26, #27).** The four are famous by name, and every approved picture shows
them as four tall, handsome humans. So the city has not forgotten *who* they were; it has forgotten *what
they looked like* and *how the rift began*. The statue says Hyuck and is a gnoll, so everybody takes it
for somebody's mascot. The school teaches an account of First Impact that names all four as heroes and
never says how the rift opened.

The thread is now plantable at the table in three separate places, none of which resolve it and none of
which connect to each other:
- `data/scene-briefs/episode-c1e1--school-first-period.json`, beat `local-studies` — a teacher reads
  "Hyuck" off the plaque and says cheerfully that it is obviously not Hyuck the founder: Hyuck was a big
  man with a sword, he is in every picture. Certain, and wrong.
- `data/scene-briefs/episode-c1e2--school-period-one.json`, check `read-between-the-lines` — the textbook
  names all four and never says how the rift opened. A critical success reaches an older printing's
  memorial plate showing the four as they were: one very small, one not human. Every later printing
  replaced it with a painting of four tall humans.
- `data/scene-briefs/episode-c1e2--school-period-two.json` — one of the four is teaching in the next
  room, entirely unremarkably, and nothing in the scene points at it.

**Candidates:**
- **A tidying, not a cover-up.** Two centuries of posters and editions made the four taller, more human
  and more heroic with every printing, and nobody decided it. [live — the current default, LEDGER #27]
- **Not forgotten at all by the people who were there.** Rupiah is confirmed still active at Founding
  Day appearances; Shucks is on the island daily. The city remembers the names; the founders remember
  the faces, and the fumble. [live]
- **Partially self-inflicted.** One or more of the four wanted the pictures changed — easier to be a
  hero with a stranger's face than to be recognised in the street as the one who dropped it. [live]
- ~~**Forgotten by everyone.**~~ Retired 2026-09-24: the founders are famous (LEDGER #26).

**Constraint (updated 2026-09-24, LEDGER #28):** Shucks is openly the founder from the first morning. Hyuck is
alive, on the base, test-piloting the worst Frame concepts, and revealed slowly. Nothing at the table may
confirm the connection between the statue, the plate and **Hyuck**. A player may assemble it; a scene may not hand it over. If a player says it out loud, write it
down rather than confirming it — see each scene's own GM-ONLY note.
