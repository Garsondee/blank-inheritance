# Blank Inheritance — Dossier: SHINOBU KUDO (the Witness)

*GM reference. Everything discussed about this character, consolidated. Cross-references to the story
bible (v4), the Cicada dossier, and the Chapter One materials.*

---

## 1. Summary

**Shinobu is the most witnessed person in the city, and nobody involved in that is being cruel.**

She was an ordinary bystander, splashed point-blank by Sap at a demonstration, and has spent the time
since being handled — by the Directorate, by her father, by the crowd that once nearly killed her —
in ways that are individually reasonable and collectively monstrous. She is slowly becoming something.
She knows more than almost anyone alive about what it feels like to be completely seen, and she is
entirely willing to talk about it.

**Her name:** *shinobu* — to endure, and also to hide. Both meanings apply.

---

## 2. The Splash

Splashed point-blank at one of Cicada's demonstrations. Physical result: mild skin irritation, nothing
else.

Full-dose exposure at close range left her carrying a dense population of nanomachines — the colony
(§6) — which is qualitatively different from the ordinary glimpse-and-fade exposure most witnesses get.

---

## 3. Detention

The Directorate held her in military lockdown immediately, and their reasoning was not stupid.
Independent scientists had already confirmed Sap inert and non-reactive. But the Directorate knew the
only place Sap is ever found is inside an alien ship, and knew what it reveals. So they detained a
teenage girl on the working assumption that she might become something.

Months of nothing. No symptoms, no incident, no change anyone could point to.

---

## 4. Release, and Renewed Surveillance

Released under mounting, public pressure from **Kudo (the Lawyer)**, her father — who is very good at
his job and very loud about it. Surveillance continued after release. Confirmed, repeatedly,
uneventful.

---

## 5. The Lorry

A lorry hits her square on, in front of a crowd, and she walks it off.

Picked up within the hour and thrown straight back into a cell — which enraged everyone who had fought
to free her the first time. From outside, the Directorate now looks like an institution that jails
road-accident victims. It makes no sense, and it looks monstrous, and Cicada sympathisers and ordinary
members of the public agree about that for once.

*(Campaign placement: this lands in Chapter 5 — see §9.)*

---

## 6. What She's Becoming — The Colony

She survived the lorry because of what she's carrying. The colony reinforces and repairs her. She is
very slowly becoming something.

She is permanently legible to the Observer. Not glimpsed occasionally, like most exposed people — held
in its attention more or less continuously, because she is steeped in a substance it treats as
important.

---

## 7. The Cell

Glass walls. Cameras on every face. Remote operators watching in shifts, permanently. Deep
underground.

She is the most witnessed person in the city — by the Directorate's own apparatus, around the clock —
and the Observer finds its way in anyway, to look at its favourite person, or at least at someone
steeped in a substance it considers important.

There is no cruelty in this anywhere. The Directorate is being careful. The operators are doing their
jobs. The Observer is only looking. It is the campaign's central question — *what does it mean to be
seen?* — applied at maximum intensity to an innocent teenager, by nobody's malice at all.

She is going nowhere, and she is entirely willing to talk. A point-blank eyewitness the party can
simply visit.

---

## 8. The People Around Her

**Kudo (the Lawyer), her father.** Fought her out of military detention once and will do it louder the
second time. He was once built as live scene data — a Session Five brief in which he confronts a
Directorate information officer in public, unprompted, over her treatment. **That brief was deleted
2026-09-13** along with every drafted beat beyond Session 0/1, so the scene no longer exists anywhere
in the repo. **His voice, his relationship to Shinobu, and the speech below are lore and survive here
unchanged** — they are the character, not the beat, and should be re-staged wherever this thread lands:

> *"My daughter was standing eleven feet from a canister. She has been in a cell for four months. She
> is not charged. She is not ill. Your own physicians say she is not ill. So I'll put it plainly, and
> you can write it down. What do you think is wrong with her? Because you're all behaving as though you
> know, and none of you will say it, and she is seventeen years old and she is very frightened."*

A party that engages him well in that scene earns her name properly, spoken as a person rather than a
case file, rather than just "the girl in the news."

**Satoru (the Advocate).** Has spent years trying to get her released and failing — and that specific,
repeated failure is what radicalised him into believing the kids (and, by extension, people like her)
should simply be told everything. Her case is the origin of his whole position in the campaign's
institutional politics. **Already built and live in the same scene**, cast id `satoru`, `npcRef:
"transparency-advocate"` — confirming Satoru is the Transparency Advocate, one of the Directorate's
three poles (`data/npcs/transparency-advocate.json`).

## 10. Open Questions

1. Does the Directorate actually understand what's happening to her, or are they holding her on
   instinct and precaution alone, with no real comprehension of the colony?
2. What she says, given the chance. She's established as willing to talk, but nothing specific she'd
   say has been scripted — a strong candidate for the "competing accounts" mechanic (story bible §13)
   if the party interviews her directly.
3. Whether "becoming something" has an endpoint that's been decided, or whether it's meant to stay an
   open, unsettling process throughout.
