# Blank Inheritance — Dossier: CICADA
## The cult, its founder, its supply chain, and everything the party can learn about them

*GM reference. Complete as of the current draft. Cross-references to the v4 story bible.*

---

## 1. Summary

**Cicada is a public religious movement with a clandestine operational core, and it is a real SF2E antagonist faction.** In public, at its demonstrations, it does not lie, does not hide, and does not recruit by deception — most of the broad membership is guilty of nothing but having seen something real and believed their own eyes. But it is also a revolutionary, clandestine organisation with a mission and assets it will use force to protect, run by an elderly disgraced chemist who believes he is a servant of something enormous and is in fact its operator.

**Hold both things at once.** The public wing is sympathetic and non-violent by doctrine. The operational core — the Cadre (§2) — will fight the party, sometimes seriously, and some of its inner circle are happy to help Manako with things that are personal to him rather than doctrinal. Not evil for its own sake. Absolutely willing to hurt people for the cause, and for him.

**Capture over kill is a design incentive, not a cult trait.** A live, interrogated Cadre member is worth more to the party than a dead one — names, sites, the revenge list, the funding trail. That is a reason for players to fight carefully. It is not a reason for the cult to fight softly.

## 2. The Organisation

### The name

Behavioural, not symbolic. **Cicada nymphs spend years underground and then emerge everywhere at once, making an enormous amount of noise.** That is the organisation's operating model and it is not a metaphor.

**They call themselves Cicada. So does everybody else.** The movement is entirely open about its identity and its goals — it has to be, since its whole purpose is to make people look. What stays hidden is the column, the funding, and the criminal logistics.

### The demonstrations

Simultaneous releases of Sap in crowded public places. Publicly interpreted as chemical attacks.

**Nobody dies.** The compound is not dangerous. Injuries come from crowd movement, never from the substance — and that anomaly is itself the first clue available to a careful investigator. *A terror campaign with a zero body count is not a terror campaign.*

They are not terrorists. Some of what they do is dangerous and occasionally fatal in its consequences, but terror is never the goal. **Witness is the goal.**

### The stencils

A cicada, sprayed neat, **always at head height, always on a wall people stand in front of** — tram stops, queues, outside picture houses, market corners. Never facing a road. They mark places where crowds gather, which is to say: places worth demonstrating at.

Marine primer, chandler's stock, three suppliers in the city, all harbour-side.

Some walls have been stencilled and painted over repeatedly for a decade or more.

### Liturgy

> *Believe me now?*

That is the whole theology. The conference was attempt one; every manifestation since is the same act at larger scale. Running underneath it is a second and gentler motive Manako rarely says aloud: **it is in agony, and quieting the city is the only relief available.**

### The Faithful, and the Cadre

**Cicada is not one thing. Two very different groups share the name.**

**The Faithful** — the broad public membership, recruited through the magic-show circuit (§7). They believe the mission is witness. At staged public events — demonstrations, ceremonies — their doctrine is deliberate non-resistance. Standing still while arrested, helping a stranger up while being taken into custody, is not weakness. **It is the point.** A martyr who does not fight makes a better argument than a fanatic who does, and the Faithful know it. Most of them have never held a weapon in Cicada's service and never will.

**The Cadre** — the operational wing around Manako. They run the column (§8), coordinate with the criminal supply chain, guard sensitive sites, and carry out the countersurveillance and enforcement the movement needs to survive underground for decades. **They will fight, and some of them will fight to kill.** They do not think of themselves as villains — they see themselves as a vanguard protecting something sacred from an institution that buried the truth once already and would happily do it again. That self-image does not make them safe to be around, and they should be run as a genuine, dangerous SF2E antagonist faction: trained, armed, disciplined, and willing to use lethal force when the mission requires it.

**The inner circle** (§9) sits inside the Cadre, closest to Manako personally, and is where his revenge list gets executed. Cadre loyalty is to the cause; inner-circle loyalty is to *him*, which is a meaningful difference the party can eventually exploit.

**The party's likely mistake:** meeting the Faithful first, learning that cultists don't resist arrest, and generalising it to the whole organisation. **That assumption should get them hurt eventually.** The first time they meet the Cadre — defending the column, moving on a revenge target, guarding a safehouse — should land as a real correction: everything Chapter One taught them about this cult was true, and incomplete.

## 3. MANAKO (the Chemist)

*Manako* is an old word for the eye. **A man called Eye, who nobody would look at.**

Elderly now, no longer fighting fit. He runs the corporeal side of the entire war from a position of complete physical frailty, and has spent decades biding, building, and choosing the moment.

### The fall, in order

Every step small and reasonable. The destination monstrous.

1. **The assignment.** A two-century-old shelved sample handed to a junior with everything to lose, because nobody senior considered it worth their time. Catalogued as noble, non-reactive, useless.
2. **The accident.** He cracks open an alien capsule of the fluid. A weak nearby tentacle takes notice. A little is spilled on it — and it becomes *immediately stronger*, visible and corporeal for the first time in a very long while. He panics. A knocked-over bunsen burner. A small fire.
3. **The disgrace.** Job gone. Reputation gone. Decline begins.
4. **Self-justification.** Desperate to prove it real — **to defend the blunder that ruined him**, not from pure curiosity. This matters. The obsession begins as damage control.
5. **Superstition.** He concludes he is cursed. It follows him everywhere he goes. **It is in fact waiting to see whether more food turns up.** The relationship that eventually kills thousands begins as a man misreading a stray dog.
6. **Collaboration.** A very small corporeal fragment, summoned by the last of a spilled sample, helps him work out how to synthesise more. The first cooperation, and the first time anything had ever confirmed him.
7. **Crime.** Ingredients are expensive. He steals lab supplies — including **the last remaining sample**, never reported missing because it wasn't interesting enough to report — loses that job too, and becomes a petty-crime chemist to make ends meet.
8. **The lens.** He works out how to treat one. Until now he has only ever seen scraps, close up, in tiny quantities.
9. **The sky.** He looks up. It is black with the thing — eyes without number, in every direction, always moving, always listening. **And he asks himself whether this is not a god worth worshipping.**

### The first failed demonstration

Before the conference, he tried to show colleagues. **The chemical worked perfectly. The Watcher had simply moved.** Nothing happened, and he was finished as a serious person.

So he learned to **check first** — to track where its attention was pooling before staging anything. That technique, recovered decades later from discredited papers, is the party's Chapter Four breakthrough. **They will be retracing his exact steps without knowing his name.**

### The conference

Having confirmed it was present, he detonated a canister in a crowded hall **without warning anyone**.

Writhing eyes and tentacles for as long as the dose held. A room full of scientists who concluded they had been attacked by a colleague who had gone off the deep end. Most reassured themselves afterward that it was a mass hallucination event.

**The Watcher was there because it likes gatherings** — dense, efficient, information-rich.

A small minority did not accept the explanation. They became the sceptics (§11 of the bible), and they have never found each other.

## 4. The Operator Reveal

**He is not a servant. He is the operator.**

The Observer conforms to the strongest coherent signal it receives, and for decades **the only coherent signal in the world has been one bitter man.** Read its behaviour backwards and it is a portrait of him:

| Its behaviour | His grievance |
|---|---|
| Grew **eyes and ears** above all else | He wanted to be seen and heard |
| Targets **loud gatherings of people** | He was humiliated in front of a hall full of colleagues |
| **Shows you the reel** — makes certain you know it learned from you | A man who was disbelieved cannot bear to be underestimated twice |

He believes he is a faithful worshipper interpreting divine intent. **There is nothing to interpret.** Every instruction he has ever received was his own grievance handed back to him.

### The sentence that breaks him

**He was never believed. He was echoed.**

Decades of a life built on being the one person in the world with independent corroboration — and the thing that confirmed him never had an opinion. It had a shape, and he was the hands.

**He already suspects this.** He has spent forty years arranging his life so the question cannot be asked, and the cult is partly an elaborate structure for not confirming it. A man whose entire identity is *the one who was believed* cannot survive being told he was only ever agreed with.

**Therefore he cannot be argued out of it.** He can only be **shown** — by watching the Observer take a different shape for someone else, in front of him. He spent his life demonstrating. He should be defeated by a demonstration.

### The trap

**Killing him is the worst available move.** The cloud loses its only organising signal and reverts to decoherent, undirected flailing across a populated region with nobody steering.

He must be **defeated and kept alive** — a tactical necessity, not a moral preference, and the party should discover it by nearly getting it wrong.

### The redemption

Once he understands what he is, he becomes **the only living being with four decades of hands-on experience shaping the cloud**, and therefore the only one who can un-shape it.

Not forgiveness. **Redemption as labour**, performed in front of everyone he hurt.

## 5. The Pivot

*The single most important event in his life after the accident, and the reason the cult stopped being merely evangelical.*

**The Envoy** — the Watcher's own first attempt at contact beyond its worshippers — lurched into a public street. **HORI**, a police officer, fired in fear, at something no reasonable person would not have fired at. The round passed through the Envoy, which did not care, and into a bystander who did.

That bystander was **AOI**, Manako's lover. A cultist, incidentally and unimportantly.

Hori drove them to hospital himself. They died anyway. And he was still standing there when an unremarkable middle-aged chemist arrived and came apart in front of him.

**Before:** Cicada demonstrated. They wanted the city to see.
**After:** Manako wanted the city to pay.

**HORI is the only person alive who knows AOI's name.** The Directorate filed the death as an unnamed cultist casualty in a public-order incident — no inquest, no record. Decades of Manako appearing in every document as a terrorist, and not one line anywhere about what he lost.

**The party's route to understanding their antagonist runs through the man who caused it, and what he gives them is a name.** He has been waiting decades for somebody to ask.

## 6. The Revenge List

Begun after the Pivot. Worked through by the inner circle under cover of "tangential" missions.

- **SHUCKS MORDANT** — the institutional author of his ruin. Supervised the disgrace personally.
- **NOBU** — the personal one. Patron or not (§8).

**Recovering the list turns the mid-campaign from a hunt into a protection scenario**, which suits a table that would rather not solve things by killing.

## 7. Recruitment: The Magic Show

### Why it works

Magic in this city is popular but faintly gauche — **rude to flaunt, wise to conceal.** Illusion shows are a mild guilty pleasure: small crowds, transportive, fantastic stories, and a handful of lifelong adherents.

**Nobody discusses the show they went to.** The city's own manners are Cicada's operational security, and are why the cult stayed buried for decades.

### The show itself

A targeted performance for a hand-picked audience. The tentacles and eyes appear and the audience recoils, assuming a horror-genre piece — unusual but not unheard of. Then they are given **proof that it is real, and has been in the room the entire time.**

Most walk out wanting to know more. Wanting to know more is how you enter the cult.

**The cult never lies.** It shows a true thing and lets the target's own rationality finish the job — which is precisely why it catches lawyers, scientists, and good citizens. The pitch contains no evil and asks for nothing objectionable: *this is here, it must be considered.*

**A PC can attend one and walk out converted. Genuinely. Not as a trap.**

### The lens

Manako's own conversion instrument: ground glass treated with Sap. Look through it and denial is not available. Rational, respectful of the target's intelligence, and exactly what a scientist would build.

**Mechanics:** Perception DC 18, plus a **hidden circumstance bonus of +0 to +8** set by whether the Observer is attending that place and how densely. Crit success = it is *there* and one stalk is oriented toward the viewer. Success = a glimpse. Failure = ordinary air. Crit failure = ordinary air, and then something turns to look at *them*.

**Never show the modifier.** Players will build superstitions about when it works. They will be wrong in exactly the way Manako was wrong, for exactly the same reason.

### The cover industry

Venues, performers, a touring circuit, booking ledgers, ticket receipts. An entire investigation track workable by ground-role characters through theatre paperwork.

## 8. The Supply Chain

### The column

A **secret production facility inside the city** — industrial-scale synthesis of Sap, running continuously, feeding a **black column of nanoparticles** rising from the plant and spreading upward into the cloud. Visible only with treated lenses. A working chimney, pouring food into the sky, day and night.

**Roughly five years old.** (A design decision: if it had run at full scale for the whole forty-year span, the Watcher would already be saturated and the escalation curve would mean nothing.)

Taking it down is a genuine triumph and should be played as one — and it raises more questions than it answers, chiefly *how much of what is overhead is this plant's fault, and will cutting it slow anything down at all?*

### NOBU (the Quartermaster)

**He was Manako's lab assistant.** He was present at the accident. **He saw the tentacle.** And when it mattered he could not make himself say so without proof — so he said nothing, watched a man's life come apart, and kept his job.

He now runs Directorate procurement: materials, permits, budgets, **and the technician-day pool.** Which makes him the person the party argues with about repairs, and the person **UME** must beg for every accommodation. She thinks him unusually kind. He is paying a debt she knows nothing about.

**He funds the column**, laundered through organised crime — a man with a guilty conscience and signing authority is precisely who they recruit. **He believes he is making amends.**

**Manako suspects him but cannot prove it.** Held in reserve as a reveal.

### The heist loop

**Demonstration** → police and civil defence sprint toward an apparent alien attack → **crews across town lift chemical stock in bulk** → precursors feed the synthesis → **more Sap** → more demonstrations.

The stolen goods are unusual but harmless, so thefts are filed low-priority and lost in the general chaos. **Nobody has ever laid the two columns of dates side by side.**

**The cost of doing business.** Both sides profit, both resent it, and **the friction between them is detectable early and exploitable.**

### The humiliation

If Nobu's contacts have learned to anticipate his sacred, spontaneous acts of witness closely enough to plan around them, then **some demonstrations are timetabled by a logistics operation rather than by revelation.**

He believes he is a prophet. He is a subcontractor. **The party should find this out before he does**, and it should hurt worse than almost anything else on offer.

### The investigative chain

Correlate demonstration timings against unsolved warehouse thefts → follow the goods → find the plant → find the column. **Police work, done by seventeen-year-olds, with no Frame involved. This is the ground-role campaign's spine.**

## 9. Personnel

**MANAKO (the Chemist)** — founder, operator, elderly, physically frail, tactically irreplaceable.

**KIRI (the Magician)** — stage name; *mist*. Runs the recruitment shows, which puts them among the Faithful publicly. **Privately, inner circle** — combat-capable, and volunteers for the missions where Manako settles old scores. Has a dull real name in a Directorate file somewhere. **Shares a craft and possibly a teacher with RUPIAH**, who can identify Kiri's technique from a witness description — which makes the Directorate's public face useful and implicates his own past in the same breath. Kiri is the cleanest illustration in the cast that the Faithful/Cadre line runs through individual people, not just through separate groups.

**NAO (the Classmate)** — a kid at the party's own school. **Not a spy, not hostile, never watched anyone, betrayed nobody.** Went to a strange magic show because a stranger in a bar asked. Their dossier is the one that lands.

**The inner circle** — a small core enamoured enough to volunteer for tangential, unethical, or dangerous work. **Clean outer layer, poisoned core, and the outer layer genuinely does not know.**

## 10. The Dossiers (player handouts)

Detained cultists generate files, and the files go in front of the players. **Read in sequence they drift:**

> ex-thugs and drifters → the obviously cult-susceptible → a chemist → a lawyer → a school governor → **a classmate**

The drift must be gradual enough that nobody spots the pattern until it is already personal. **The payoff is the moment a player recognises a name.**

**There is no infiltration plot**, which is far more disturbing than one. Which hands the party a real moral problem: **most cultists are guilty of nothing but having seen something and believed it**, and the Directorate will order them arrested.

## 12. Running Notes

**In Chapter One, the party meets the Faithful and the criminal contractors — not the Cadre.** The pattern they should learn: believers don't resist, contractors do. **This is true and incomplete.** They have not yet met the people who believe entirely *and* fight anyway. Save that meeting for a later chapter — the column raid (Ch6) is the natural home for the party's first real Cadre fight — and let it recontextualise what Chapter One taught them, rather than contradict it.

**Manako is not present until late.** He is a shape behind decades of consequence for most of the campaign.

**Recruitment-level Cicada should read as warm, rational, and sincere — because it is.** The Cadre is a different register entirely: professional, armed, willing to hurt the party to protect the mission and to serve Manako's grudges. Both are true of the same organisation, and the gap between them is deliberate, not a contradiction to smooth over.

**Build Cadre encounters so non-lethal resolution is genuinely available** — enemies who disengage or can be dropped and secured rather than fought to the last — without ever having them pull punches on the way in. The incentive to capture should come from what a live prisoner is worth, not from the enemy going easy.
