## 1. Summary

**The Directorate is not a conspiracy. It is an institution that has never been tested, cannot admit it, and is slowly killing the city it was built to protect — mostly by doing its job exactly as written.**

A century and a half of Frames, a charter, a chain of command, an anthem, a maintenance budget and a conscription statute, and no war. It is a rite of passage, mildly boring, faintly prestigious, and entirely untested. *(Clarified 2026-09-24, `open-questions.json#directorate-extraterritorial-vs-national-service`: there are **two services**. The **kingdom's own national service**, army and navy, is the one everyone's parents did. **The Directorate recruits separately**: candidates apply, or are streamed to it, and it sits outside the kingdom's lines, which is where the rivalry with the army and navy comes from.)*

**Where it came from** *(corrected 2026-09-24, LEDGER #32 item 6, which is canon)*. **It was born on Kortos, about 195 years ago.** First Impact opened up the inner layers of the generation ship. Scientists and magic-users descended on the site, terrified of another rupture; adventurers came for valuables; it became a very dangerous place. **The organisations around Kortos banded together, and the Directorate was born out of the need to keep the site secure, prevent a future rupture, and stop the uncovered technology disrupting the world.** About 150 years ago it moved to Kurose for the prophecy (§1a), and found the search ship there (§2.1). **The technology leaked outwards anyway:** the phones, and anything else too advanced for the era, are ship technology that got out of the Kortos site (§2.7). The full account is in `dossier-first-impact.md`.

**Calcified rather than sinister.** The adults are not competent and do not know it. That is what makes it possible for the tone to stay warm and funny for a long stretch, and it is why nobody knowing what to do when the alarm is finally real is *earned* rather than contrived.

**And it is the reason any of this is happening.** See §2.1.

## 1a. Public standing — how Kurose sees the Directorate (2026-09-24)

Ingram, 2026-09-24, deciding what the campaign's opening may tell the table. New, and authoritative
over anything older here that conflicts.

- **What people believe it is for.** Both at once, and they joke about it: a grand mission to stand
  ready against a foretold rift that nobody expects to see, and a daily heavy-work corps everybody
  relies on — lifting, building, dredging, rescue. The joke in the city is that it was built to save the
  world and mostly moves cargo.
- **It made Kurose.** Kurose was a fishing town until the Directorate arrived about a hundred and fifty
  years ago; its money, harbour works and Frames built the modern city. Kurose's wealth and the
  Directorate are tangled together.
- **Mixed feelings about its people.** They are *future saviours* — sworn to save the world from
  something that has not happened and cannot be predicted precisely in time. So:
  - many see them as **intruders, outsiders, even warmongers**, because they run a military base;
  - some see them as **protectors**;
  - they operate **outside the kingdom's own national lines**, which rubs the kingdom's **army and navy
    up the wrong way all the time**: an extra-territorial splinter in the side of a country that feels
    it can more than defend itself.

- **The police and the Directorate distrust each other** (LEDGER #35). What happens with the police doesn't reach the
  Directorate quickly or clearly, if at all. *(Proposal: jurisdiction is the quarrel. The Directorate takes anything
  alien, the police take everything else, and every incident with both in it is split down the middle and never
  stitched back together.)*

Resolved 2026-09-24 as **two services**: see the note in §1.

*(GM note, 2026-09-24, LEDGER #31: the foretold rift is what the campaign now calls **Second Impact** — a subspace rupture like First Impact at Kortos, torn open by a breached exotic-matter container. It is unavoidable when it comes (item 9), and it arrives together with the need to solve the Watcher (item 5). Everything after it is flexible draft.)*

## 2. The seven faults

### 2.1 They are digging up what draws it in, and they will not stop

*(Rewritten 2026-09-24 to LEDGER #31. This said "the rest of the buried ship is under the city" and "it came home"; both stopped being true once the ships became two.)*

**The hull under Kurose is not the ship the Watcher came from.** It is a second ship — **the search ship**, which arrived about a thousand years after the generation ship to find out what had happened to it, and crashed and was buried in its turn (LEDGER #31 item 1, canon). The Watcher's home is at Kortos: it was the generation ship's navigation and sensor system.

*(Canon since LEDGER #32 item 8; it was a #31 proposal:)* **the search ship's buried systems are still calling for the generation ship, and the Watcher — that ship's navigation system — is drawn to the call.** It crossed the world and fetched up over the one place on the planet that is not noise: the ship that came looking for it. **It cannot get in. There is a city on top and the city is screaming.**

**And the excavation is answering it.** Digging wakes the search ship, and every metre the Directorate digs strengthens the pull. They have been calling it, all campaign and for decades before it, and calling it louder every year.

*(And the ship carries more than a voice. LEDGER #26 with `design/campaign-arc.md`: the exotic-matter fuel that could make Second Impact is the search ship's, under the same city, and one of Ingram's three open candidates for the cause is "Cicada's worst member detonating stolen mitflit spaceship fuel on purpose" — the others are an accident and a tragic misunderstanding. A reading, not decided: whichever it is, the dig is what brings the fuel within reach.)*

This is the single largest fault and the one the party is least equipped to see, because the digging is not secret — it is a public works programme with a budget line.

### 2.2 They conscript children, and they wrote down that they knew

**UNDER REVIEW (LEDGER #36, 2026-09-25).** Ingram on this answer: "it belongs to much older generated ideas and notes that don't really feel sane any more." It also doesn't hold up: the charter predates every Frame and the prophecy, its authors are alive, and the party are volunteers of any age. See `open-questions.json#founding-charter-rethink`.

The founding charter is framed in the lobby with **one paragraph blacked out**, and it has been blacked out since before anyone alive was born. The paragraph is the founders' own honest self-diagnosis: *children with guns.*

They named the problem, in writing, at the founding — and then painted over it and built two centuries of institutional life on top. **The Directorate diagnosed itself and buried the diagnosis, and then became it.**

*(Where the unredacted copy is kept: §8, GM only.)*

### 2.3 They screen the children for something, and tell them not to worry

Every one of the six is told privately at intake that their blood screen came back irregular, that it is very common, and that they should not worry about it. The form has a pre-printed tick-box for it.

Nobody explains what the screen tests for. **Shucks is forbidden from saying, and he thinks the rule is stupid** — which is the tell that the prohibition is institutional rather than personal.

### 2.4 The Gen 2 prototypes are alive, and the institution knows

*(Corrected 2026-09-24, LEDGER #32 item 7 and its consequences, which are canon. This fault was headed "The Frames are alive" and written about every Frame, with a lie a hundred and fifty years old. It now belongs to **Gen 2 only**, and Gen 2 is decades old, not a century and a half.)*

**Gen 1 is not alive.** The fleet, and what the party is issued, is **Golarion technology only**, informed by looking at the ship's godlike technology: **mechanical clankers, hard to work with, not biomechanical, with no living core and no seat that shapes itself.** Whatever the institution is not saying, it is not about the fleet.

**Gen 2 is.** These are **Anabala's machines, built from actual mitflit technology** from the original ship, the First Impact crash site at Kortos (LEDGER #20, #34): built on the real thing, and **more like being inside a giant humanoid** than sitting in a machine. Everything this fault used to say about every Frame now belongs to the **mothballed Gen 2 prototypes**: a living core, *making itself a seat* inside the shell, shaped to its pilot (LEDGER #5, moved to Gen 2 by #32). The institution knows, and does not say so. **The core is biomechanical mitflit technology: robotic parts that are living flesh melded with technology, not a living mitflit** (LEDGER #34), and these machines aren't usable by just anyone. *(Anabala is a free agent rather than part of the Directorate (LEDGER #28). **She works with it on her own terms** (LEDGER #34): building Gen 2 was her price for access to the original ship's technology, which the Directorate keeps highly restricted from the rest of Golarion so it can't destabilise the world, and her own objective is a way home for her people. See `dossier-second-generation-frames.md` §3.)*

**The recurring hull breach**, the same hole in the same place, officially metal fatigue (Mystery Ledger #4), **moves to the Gen 2 prototypes, or is cut** (LEDGER #32; not yet decided).

*(2026-09-24, LEDGER #31 item 14: **no berserk.** A living core is not a loss-of-control risk. Mitflit technology has no reason to go berserk, and nothing in this campaign takes a character away from their player. What the Gen 2 core is was answered in LEDGER #34 (above). Maybe (Ingram's word) a small part of Mother is in every one, as the cerebral glue that makes the technology work: open, `open-questions.json#mother-in-gen2`.)*

### 2.5 They erase people

- **Aoi** — killed by a police round during the Envoy incident. Manako broke down at the scene; the officer who fired rushed her to hospital; in the confusion the circle scattered, Manako with it, and none of them went with her (LEDGER #32 item 15). **The Directorate filed her as one deceased, unidentified**: a fringe group's casualty in a public-order incident. In its file: no record, no name, because nobody thought her worth the paperwork. The cult was largely a non-issue to the Directorate until the adventure properly starts (LEDGER #34). The officer, **Hori**, went through a very strict police process, including a complex court proceeding on whether the killing was lawful, and was found not responsible for an unlawful killing. **That court is where he learned her name** (LEDGER #33). He retired anyway, in self-imposed disgrace, and outside Manako's own circle he is the one who kept her name (LEDGER #31 item 18, #33). *(Proposal, LEDGER #31, extended in #33, for how both are true: the police examined Hori's use of force, and the court cleared him; **the Directorate took jurisdiction over the alien and the scene**, and it was a Directorate report, written at the scene on the day, that filed her as nobody — see the Soldier in `dossier-cicada.md` §9, who helped write it. Nobody went back to correct it.)* **LEDGER #35, canon: the police and the Directorate distrust each other**, so nothing the police's court heard reached the Directorate. Its official line is only that *a cultist was killed during a confrontation in which someone magically summoned a monster.* *(This replaces Claude's #33 proposal of a hearing held behind closed doors.)* *(Corrected 2026-09-24, LEDGER #32 and #33: the older account had Manako come apart at the hospital, and this line then said "No inquest" and left open how Hori knows her name. There was a court proceeding, and that is where he learned it.)*
- **Shinobu Kudo** — an ordinary bystander, detained without charge, released under her father's pressure, re-arrested within the hour.
- **Nozomi** — declined the draft and is in questioning and detainment for it. Her name is still on the roster and her locker is still assigned.

None of these is a cover-up run from the top. Each is a department doing an ordinary thing, and the aggregate is an institution that makes inconvenient people administratively disappear.

### 2.6 Their own alert system feeds the enemy

The Watcher goes for loud places, and **the sirens are a scream directly into its ear.** Every drill escalates the attack it is warning about. Every siren is part of why the next response is worse than the last.

### 2.7 They monitor the children's messages, and the children agreed to it

Trainee communications are read as routine policy. The consent is in the intake paperwork — the same forms, handed over on day one, that ask for mother's maiden name twice and get laughed at.

Nobody is spying on anybody. A clerk is doing a filing job under a policy written decades ago by people who are dead. **Later in the campaign the Directorate acts on those intercepts** — moving on Manako or a cult site at a moment the players did not choose, with information the players themselves generated, wrecking a plan the players had. It is not a betrayal by any individual, which is exactly what makes it one.

**And the Directorate is not the only reader.** *(Added 2026-09-24, LEDGER #31 items 4 and 16; the accepted idea `ideas/entries/watcher-reads-all-comms.json` is now core canon.)* The phones run on the same alien substrate the Watcher is native to, so everything that crosses them is legible to it — all the time, with no clerk, no form and no delay. *(They are ship technology that leaked out of the Kortos site, the very thing the Directorate was founded to stop: §1, LEDGER #32.)* **Two surveillances over the same messages:** one institutional, slow and filed, acting months later through a chain of command; one alien, instant, and learning — and neither knows about the other. The first is a fault of this institution. The second is the campaign's adversary doing what it exists to do, since its objective is to keep observing. Between them they put the campaign's two themes in one place: mass surveillance and its risks, and a runaway agentic system that threatens the structure of society.

**At the table (D14, Ingram's decision).** Players are warned never to use the UI to record anything real or personal. They are **not** told that the messages are read. They are encouraged to use the system, and will need it to reach some things. The warning is about safety — nothing real or personal goes in — and it is not an admission that anyone reads; the in-fiction admission is the consent form nobody reads (above). The second reader is not a metaphor either: the Watcher is played by Claude (LEDGER #31 item 4), and the pipeline that brings the players' messages to Claude between sessions is, in the fiction, the Watcher's own surveillance (`watcher-reads-all-comms`). **And a hard line** (`dossier-observer.md` §2): the Watcher only ever uses the fiction. If a player puts something real or personal into the system anyway, it is never used.

## 3. The mirror — and this is the point of the whole document

**Manako and the Directorate are the same failure at different scales.**

| | Manako | The Directorate |
|---|---|---|
| The buried moment | Panicked in a lab, knocked over a burner, and was ruined | Panicked at Kortos, and a gnoll threw a container an elf failed to catch |
| What they built on it | A religion | A state |
| The admission | He has spent forty years arranging his life so the question cannot be asked | They wrote it down and painted over it *(§2.2, under review)* |
| What they do to the young | Tells them what they are for | Tells them what they are for |
| Are they right about the other? | Yes | Yes |
| Can either see itself? | No | No |

The campaign's thesis — *nobody has ever done anything evil; every catastrophe is an ordinary person flinching and then building the rest of their life on that moment* — is therefore **demonstrated twice rather than stated once**, at two different scales, by two institutions who are each other's best evidence and cannot use it.

The party stands between them and is lied to by both.

## 4. What this is not

- **Not a conspiracy.** There is no cabal. Nobody at the top is coordinating the faults in §2; most of them are separate departments being ordinary.
- **Not malicious.** The adults are, with real exceptions, decent, tired, fond of the children and bad at their jobs in ways they cannot perceive.
- **Not a reason for the party to defect.** The Directorate being badly wrong does not make Cicada right, and the campaign should never resolve into picking a side. Both are wrong in the same way about the same thing.
- **Not to be revealed as a twist.** Every fault above is discoverable by looking. Most of it is on public display — a framed charter, a dig site, a siren, a form. It is hidden the way institutional facts are hidden: in plain sight, boring, and nobody's job to ask about.

## 5. Manako's warning, and why it works

At the first meeting (`data/scene-briefs/episode-c1e1--the-errand.json`, beat `what-he-thinks-of-your-employer`), an old man in a tea house tells six brand-new trainees one true thing about their employer: that the Directorate wrote down what it was doing to children and then painted over it.

**He is right.** He does not know about §2.1, §2.4 or §2.7, and he is right about §2.2 anyway. *(This rests on §2.2, which is under review, LEDGER #36; if the charter changes, his line changes with it.)*

Because he says it before a single attack has happened anywhere in the city, it lands as an old man's opinion rather than a manifesto — and it is the first brick in the party's eventual, correct distrust of the institution feeding them.

**The players may start to believe the Directorate are the actual villains of this story. They will be half right, and they should be allowed to think so for a long time.**

## 6. Running notes

**Keep the people good and the institution bad.** Shucks, Nobu, Taka, the repair chiefs and the bridge staff are warm, funny and on the children's side. The faults in §2 are not any of their doing and mostly not within any of their power. An institution that is failing while being staffed by likeable people is far more uncomfortable than one staffed by villains, and it is the version this campaign's register can actually carry.

**Never have an adult explain §3.** The mirror is the audience's to notice.

**Do not let the party fix it.** Several of these are two centuries old and structural. The campaign's win condition is not institutional reform, and a party that tries should find the institution absorbing the effort politely and changing nothing — which is its own, more interesting outcome.

## 8. GM only — the deepest vaults

*(Added 2026-09-24 from LEDGER #31 item 20, which is canon, plus one proposal from the same entry, marked; updated the same day to LEDGER #32 items 10 and 14. **Secret, and meant to stay secret for a long time.** Nothing in this section goes into play text, a handout, a rumour or an NPC's mouth until Ingram decides it does.)*

**Anabala** — the one founder who is not part of the Directorate (LEDGER #28) — is a very early mitflit who has lived through the ages because of her connection to the knowledge demon **Abraxas**. The demon ruled her for many decades until she turned the tables: the only path to unlimited information runs through her cooperation, not through conquest. Now there is an uneasy alliance. She wants her people home; the demon wants to learn everything there is, and grows by feeding on information. **Nobody mentions the demon openly for a long time.** (`ideas/story/bible/characters/anabala.md`.)

**Abraxas is female** (LEDGER #32 item 10, canon): Anabala's player plays her as a woman with a pact with a demon, and in this version they are all female. Write the demon as *she*.

**Canon:** the demon may emerge from Anabala, unexpectedly, when the party breaks into **the Directorate's deepest vaults** in search of information — and that becomes a new problem. **Anabala must be there for it to happen** (LEDGER #32 item 14). How is open, to solve later: Pippa may choose to keep playing Anabala in this campaign, as one of the students, and either way Anabala can be at the right place at the right time easily enough. *(LEDGER #33: the two don't conflict. Abraxas can emerge from Anabala, and Anabala stays Pippa's to play.)* **Abraxas and the Watcher are a naturally dangerous, aligned pair**: one feeds on information, the other exists to keep observing. Remember it whenever either is on the table.

**Proposal (LEDGER #31):** the deepest vault is where the **unredacted charter** is kept — the paragraph under the paint (§2.2). The chain of redacted documents ends there, so the break-in comes when the party goes for the last black bar. *(On hold with §2.2: the charter is under review, LEDGER #36.)*

**Running rule.** The vaults themselves can be mentioned; the redacted-document chain points at them. What must not surface in play, for a long time, is anything about the demon.

## 9. GM only — the founders' old enemy

*(Added 2026-09-24 from LEDGER #32 item 1, which is canon, plus proposals from the same entry, marked. **Secret, kept until the right moment.** Nothing here goes into play text, a handout, a rumour or an NPC's mouth until Ingram decides it does.)*

**William Charles Baldwin is alive.** The old campaign's mitflit-hunting lord, who blamed the four for the deaths of members of his family in the shadow siege at Baldwin Manor, though the four did not cause them, was insanely rich, and wealth bought him two hundred years. He is now **a criminal lord who arms criminal gangs with incredible weapons, still made by essentially enslaved mitflits.** Mitflits are extremely helpful when given the right tasks, and fascinated by the production of technology; that is what makes them so easy to use (LEDGER #32 item 2). **The four founders are his natural enemies.** Full file: `bible/characters/william-charles-baldwin.md`.

**Proposals (LEDGER #32, veto freely), and where they touch this institution:**

- **He looted the site the Directorate was founded to protect.** He was on Kortos when First Impact opened the generation ship, with hunters, money and a weapons works on the island, so he was among the first in. Ship technology keeps him alive, and his mitflit slaves keep it running (`open-questions.json#baldwin-longevity`). On that reading, some of the leak in §1 went straight into his hands.
- **Directorate money reaches him.** Nobu's laundering runs through Baldwin's organisation (`dossier-cicada.md` §8), so procurement money from the institution the four founded passes through their oldest enemy's hands on its way to Cicada's column.
- **His front may trade as Croglang**, the old name of the castle that became Baldwin Manor: a name the four founders would recognise.

**Running rule.** His organisation can be felt long before he is. His name, his face and his survival cannot. When and how he is revealed is open.
