## 1. Summary

**The party's own Frames start as Gen 1 — the classic, century-and-a-half-old cargo-cult fleet
(`dossier-first-impact.md` §8) — configured for utility work, not combat, and chosen from a real range
of different utility mechs at the point Frame service actually begins.** A Gen 1 Frame is **Golarion
technology only**, built by people who had looked at the generation ship's godlike technology and done
their level best in riveted plate and boiler technology: **a mechanical clanker, hard to work with**, with
no living core and nothing in it that shapes itself to anybody (LEDGER #32 item 7). Utility work is the
unglamorous end of an internal status ladder within Frame service itself: the base's real combat-Frame
corps is the prestige track, and utility duty (construction, disaster response, heavy lifting) is where
candidates not yet considered skilled enough for "harder work" get sent. **This is a second, Frame-scale
version of the exact same undervaluing Episode One already runs at personnel scale** — different job,
different scene, same shape.

**What actually pulls the party into real Frame combat isn't a promotion. It's an emergency.** The
Watcher begins probing the city's defences, and the Directorate — never enough combat-configured
Frames or pilots to spare — sends out whatever's ready. Utility mechs included. Ordinary institutional
logic under pressure, exactly the same "nobody decided this, the system just did what it does" shape
as everything else this campaign's institutions get wrong. This is the real engine behind the
campaign's own already-agreed Episode Two shape (`ch1-v2.md` §8): a training exercise interrupted by a
real call-out, before they're ready.

**Shucks's DNA discovery doesn't change any of that.** It's a long-burn plant (Mystery Ledger #1,
**payoff chapter deliberately unassigned** — this line claimed "Chapter 6 payoff" until 2026-09-22,
which was an inherited claim written after `ch1-v2.md` §5's whole payoff column had already been
cleared on 2026-09-13. Ingram resolved it 2026-09-22 in favour of the table, and the thread now lives
on the `mystery-ledger-1` Node) that pays off much later, after real Frame-combat experience on Gen 1 —
not an immediate reward, not why they get deployed at all.

**Gen 2, when it's finally offered, is a real choice rather than an upgrade, and a different kind of
machine.** **Anabala built it from actual mitflit technology** (LEDGER #32 item 7). Gen 2 is built from
the generation ship's technology: the original ship, the First Impact crash site at Kortos, which the
Directorate holds and keeps restricted from the rest of Golarion (LEDGER #20, #34). Building Gen 2 for the
Directorate was her price for access to that technology (§3). *(Corrected 2026-09-24, LEDGER #34: this
said the search ship under Kurose. That was Claude's inference; Ingram's own words put Gen 2's technology
at the First Impact crash site (#20) and in "the original ship" (#34).)* It is **biomechanical**: built on
the real thing, so that flying
one is, in Ingram's words, *"more like being inside a giant humanoid"*. It is gated on "the right DNA or
the right something", extremely promising, and, early on, worse in real ways than a Gen 1 Frame the
player has spent levels investing in (Frame Sickness, §3). Both paths have to actually work,
deliberately, because some players will get attached to the machine they started with. **It is offered
at the end of Chapter 4** (LEDGER #31 item 13), adjusted by how the table takes to Frames and vehicles.

## 3. Why Mitflits, and Why It Isn't "Mitflits Are Mechs"

Ingram was explicit, back on 2026-09-13 (`bible/LEDGER.md` #15, question 1), that she didn't want a
solution where *"you can also ride into battle inside them"* — where piloting cheapens the species by
turning them into components. **That line still holds, and #32 makes it matter more.** A Gen 2 Frame is
biomechanical and "more like being inside a giant humanoid" (LEDGER #32 item 7), so a Gen 2 pilot now
*is* riding inside something alive. What that living thing is was the open `arc-mitflit-frame-core-link`
Node in `bible/species/mitflits.md`, and Ingram has answered it (LEDGER #34, Casebook D13): **it is
biomechanical mitflit technology, robotic parts that are living flesh melded with technology, and it is not
a living mitflit.** So a Gen 2 pilot rides inside living technology, never inside a mitflit, which was the
worry. **A mitflit is always a person in the story, never a vehicle or a component**, and nothing in a
Gen 2 Frame takes control from its pilot (below). *(Corrected 2026-09-24, LEDGER #34: this said the Node
was open, and set the rule above under "Until Ingram answers it".)*
*(Corrected 2026-09-24, LEDGER #32: this section used to open "Nobody is riding inside anybody. A
second-generation Frame is still an ordinary cockpit with an ordinary pilot seat. What's biological is
the compatibility requirement, not the machine's guts." #32 made Gen 2 biomechanical, so the machine's
guts are alive now, and that old answer to Ingram's worry no longer works on its own.)*

**Why a mitflit can fly Gen 2 natively: it is their own people's technology.** Gen 2 is built from
*actual* mitflit technology (LEDGER #32 item 7), recovered from the generation ship their ancestors
crewed: the original ship, the First Impact crash site at Kortos, which the Directorate holds and keeps
restricted from the rest of Golarion (LEDGER #20, #34). *(Corrected 2026-09-24, LEDGER #34: this said the
search ship that came looking for the generation ship. That was Claude's inference; Ingram's own words put
Gen 2's technology at the First Impact crash site (#20) and in "the original ship" (#34).)* The interface
reads as genuinely alien to a human nervous system unless something in the pilot is close enough to the
source to parse it, and mitflits parse it natively.
Most mitflits don't know any of this; see the tiered-knowledge rule in `bible/species/mitflits.md`.
**Mitflits don't instantly understand where Gen 2 comes from, and wild mitflits are uncomfortable around
Gen 2 Frames** (LEDGER #34). *(Proposal, LEDGER #34, veto freely: they feel something of their ancestors'
ship in a machine made of living flesh, and can't say what.)*
**A small number of humans carry enough of the same genetic marker, diluted over generations, to parse it
too** — without that marker meaning anything else about who they are. It's a compatibility, not a
species, and not always a gene (below). For a human, this is the actual, textured version of "you have
Mitflit DNA": a fact about ancestry many generations back, not a secret alien nature the party is hiding.

**Why all six of the party have it: coincidence (LEDGER #31 item 10, Casebook D6).** Ingram:
*"Coincidence, they just happen to have the right stuff. We might have robots and androids joining the
group, we might have aliens composed entirely of light or something even stranger, but they all have
the right DNA or the right something that means they can be Gen 2 pilots."* For most humans the right
stuff is that diluted mitflit marker. A robot, an android or a being of light can have it too, as some
right *something* that isn't DNA at all. **There is no hidden cause behind the six**, so don't write
one. (This answers what §8 used to carry as open question 5.)

**Anabala built Gen 2, and flew it first** *(rewritten 2026-09-24, LEDGER #32 item 7)*. **Gen 2 is
Anabala building mechs from actual mitflit technology.** She is its builder, not only its test pilot, and
the one person who could sit in what she built and have it answer her. Her success is what kept the
programme alive past its first failed pilot rounds, until almost nobody else could fly one and it was
mothballed (LEDGER #20). It is not public knowledge that she flew one; the Directorate's official record
of the early trials is thin on exactly who succeeded, for the same "ordinary institutional caution"
reasons everything else in this campaign gets filed vaguely.
*(Corrected 2026-09-24: this used to say only that she "was involved in the recovery and reconstruction
effort". LEDGER #20 already had her "involved in producing them"; #32 makes Gen 2 hers. Today she is a
free agent, not Directorate staff (LEDGER #28), and Pippa may choose to keep playing her in the new

**How her machines became the Directorate's programme: a bargain (LEDGER #34, Casebook N25).** Anabala
works with the Directorate, and has her own ultimate objective: using mitflit technology to get a way home
for her people. Building Gen 2 for the Directorate was her price for access to the original ship's
technology, which the Directorate keeps highly restricted from the rest of Golarion so it can't destabilise
the world (LEDGER #34). Ingram: *"Anabala works with the Directorate but also has her own ultimate
objective, using Mitflit technology to get a way home for her people. Making Gen 2 Frames for the
Directorate gave her access to the tech from the original ship, something which is highly restricted to

it. *(Answered 2026-09-24, LEDGER #34: this paragraph said how her machines became the Directorate's

**Confirmed 2026-09-20 (resolves `open-questions.json#gen2-tradeoff-mechanism`): even Anabala suffered
the sync problem.** Compatibility is not instant fluency. A pilot who switches into an unsynced Gen 2
Frame takes **Slowed 1** until they've synced with it — a real, inherent property of the pilot/machine
bond, not an invented penalty. This is exactly why treating Gen 2 Frames as freely interchangeable, or
swapping on a whim, is a genuinely bad idea rather than a strictly-better upgrade: even the person who
built the thing and proved it could work had to earn fluency with her own machine.

**Named, 2026-09-20 (resolves `open-questions.json#gen2-sync-duration-mechanism`): "Frame Sickness."**
A well-known, in-fiction phenomenon AMONG THE PEOPLE WHO HAVE MET IT — pilots are warned about it in
advance, it is not a hidden gotcha.

**Who actually knows, and when the players find out (Ingram, correcting a leak into Episode Two).** Only
Anabala and senior Directorate are aware Frame Sickness exists at all. Gen 1 machines do not cause it; it
is a consequence of the Gen 2 Frames being biomechanical (LEDGER #23), which #32 makes the whole
difference between the two generations. The players are told when they are offered Gen 2 Frames, at the
end of Chapter 4, and not before. *(Corrected 2026-09-24: this said "around Chapter Four or Five"; LEDGER

warn about — see LEDGER #23, which is the scene this line was misread into.
Slowed 1 for the entire first fight in a given Gen 2 Frame; gone from the second fight in that same
Frame onward. Ingram's own words: *"being slowed one for a whole fight is already a very brutal penalty
to operate under."* Per-Frame rather than a one-time character trait — climbing into a *different* Gen 2
unit re-triggers it — which is what keeps swapping between Gen 2 Frames a bad idea even after a pilot
has fully synced with one of them. (Confirmed per-machine by Ingram the same day; the full text,

**No berserk, and the machine never takes over (LEDGER #31 item 14, Casebook D11).** Ingram: *"That's
the EVA idea but I don't think taking control away from the players makes sense in this adventure.

bond.** The Frame never acts on its own, never overrides its pilot, and never goes berserk. Hold every

describes what a Gen 2 is like to sit in, not a machine with a will.

**Hyuck's protocol: Gen 2 only, and open (LEDGER #32 item 11; Ingram, Casebook N8).** *"Hyuck absolutely

**true form** (LEDGER #5, Gen 2 only since #32). Ingram: *"When they enter the machine they find it uncomfortable to do so except in their true form."* So a disguised pilot can climb in and
fly; it is uncomfortable, because the seat is made for the body they are hiding. **Nothing forces a reveal**: the reveal
still belongs to the player (*"The system may know; the table finds out when the player decides"*, `design/induction.md`
§2). The first time it can happen is the first Gen 2, at the end of Chapter 4 at the earliest.

*(Proposals, LEDGER #33, veto freely.)* **The empty seat.** The bible has every cavity keep its current pilot's shape,

## 4. Shucks's Testing Programme

**He doesn't review blood samples. He tests them, personally, and has for a while.**

Shucks was already positioned to notice this before anyone thought to ask him to look: he ran medical
clearance for the original prototype trial pilots, years ago, and quietly kept better notes than
anyone else involved, because that is simply how he works. When the programme nearly folded, he was
the one who went back through those notes and found the pattern nobody else had correlated — the
handful of successful pilots all shared a marker on a screen most of the Directorate's medical staff
would have filed as noise.

**He now runs the same screen on every candidate who passes through intake, as a matter of course**,
under general Directorate authorisation to flag "irregularities" — the same field, the same tick-box,
the same flat, bored delivery the party already gets in Episode One. He is not being sinister when he
deflects; **he is genuinely forbidden from explaining what irregular means, for a completely ordinary
institutional reason: the Directorate does not want anyone to know who its compatible pilots are
before it does.** Operational security, not conspiracy. *(2026-09-24, LEDGER #34: this said "anyone —
Cicada included —". The cult is largely a non-issue for the Directorate until the adventure properly
starts, and the Directorate has only a bit of information about it, so the rule names no enemy.)* He thinks the rule is
stupid because he's watched it upset kids for years and never once seen it leak anything worth
protecting.

**Episode One's six are unusual, and he knows it the moment the results come back — all six, one
section, the same morning.** That's a real statistical surprise to him, not a shrug. He does not say

makes this a good `## Arcs` Node rather than a settled fact — see `mystery-ledger-1` in
`blank-inheritance-ch1-v2.md`. *(Clarified 2026-09-24: this pointed at §7, which isn't about it. And
LEDGER #31 item 10 settles the truth behind his surprise: coincidence. His surprise is real, and there
is nothing behind it for him or anyone else to find.)*

## 6. Why Cicada Gets It Backwards

Cicada's entire theology is already built on reading the Watcher's attention as meaningful — Manako's
whole life is organised around *"believe me now?"*, around proving that what the Watcher fixates on
matters. **Give them a genuinely new, sustained fixation and they will do exactly what their own
doctrine trains them to do: conclude it's significant, then conclude significant means dangerous.**

This isn't Cicada being irrational by the story's standards — it's Cicada being exactly as rational as
their founder's own worldview allows, which is the same "ordinary flinch, not malice" shape every other
institutional failure in this campaign already takes. They watch the thing they revere reach for its
makers' technology, read it as divine alarm, and conclude the new mechs must be blasphemous,
dangerous, or a rival power's doing — never landing anywhere near the actual truth. *(Adjusted
2026-09-24: this used to say "a starving, homesick instrument reach for a fragment of itself" and "the
actual, much sadder truth". Under LEDGER #31 the pull is recognition of a sister ship's technology, a
proposal, and the Watcher is the adversary rather than a sad instrument.)* *(2026-09-24, LEDGER #34: Gen
2 is built from the generation ship, so what the Watcher reaches for is its own ship's technology, not a
sister ship's; see §5b. It is still the adversary, not a sad instrument.)*

**Consequence worth keeping deliberately light for now:** this gives Cicada a plausible reason to take
a real interest in the new Frames specifically, later in the campaign, without inventing a second
antagonist motive from nothing. Not written any further than that here — a hook, not a plotted beat.

for the Gen 1 half of this** — the fiction now explains the economy that was already there.
*(Corrected 2026-09-24. The runway is Chapters 1–4: Gen 2 is offered at the end of Chapter 4 (LEDGER
