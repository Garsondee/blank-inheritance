# Self-play transcript — Intake Medicals

Personas: **Priya** (Caster), **Oskar** (Brute), **Devi** (Rules-lawyer), **Fen** (Lateral thinker),
**Ren** (Quiet one), **Sable** (Chaos agent). Playing GM + all six.

---

## The queue

**GM:** Section Nine, one queue, eleven minutes and it hasn't moved. Say one thing.

**Oskar:** *(to the queue in general)* "If this is the army, why is the army a queue."

**Priya:** *(to the person twice her age in front)* "First time too?" — genuinely trying to make a
friend before anything else happens.

**Devi:** *(not looking up from the sign)* "'Forms first, no exceptions' is taped over 'medicals
first.' That's not a policy change, that's somebody losing an argument."

**Fen:** *(peering at the older sign underneath)* "Whoever wrote the second one has worse
handwriting than the first. That's not authority, that's just whoever grabbed the tape first."

**Ren:** Says nothing. Watches Fen work out the handwriting thing and files it without comment.

**Sable:** *(loudly, to nobody)* "Twenty quid says the queue is the test."

*GM note: this beat did what it says on the tin — six lines, thirty seconds, everyone's already
distinct. No finding here.*

## Form 12-B

**GM (as clerk):** *flat, hands out forms* "Box four is mother's maiden name. Box nineteen is also
mother's maiden name, but in capitals."

**Devi:** "Why."

**GM (as clerk):** "No, I don't know why."

**Priya:** *laughs, actually charmed* "That's the best answer anyone's given me all morning."

**Oskar, Fen, Ren, Sable:** fill in the form and move on. Nobody else engages the clerk at all.

*Finding, matching prediction 4: the joke landed for exactly one persona (Priya, whose whole thing
is engaging NPCs) and was invisible to the other five, who had a form to fill in and nothing pulling
them toward the bit. It's a good line delivered to an audience of one. See §Findings.*

## Shucks — six private conversations

**GM:** One at a time. *(Turning to each player in turn, quietly, while the others keep queueing.)*

**Priya.** Shucks: "Name. Not the one on the form." She gives both anyway, unprompted, and asks his
in return. He tells her — actually tells her — and she's visibly delighted to be the first person
all day who asked. *No mechanical content. Pure warmth, which is exactly what her persona is for.*

**Oskar.** Shucks: "Arm." Oskar holds it out like it's a dare. Shucks, unbothered: "Not a
competition." Oskar tries to end the conversation in one line; Shucks asks the emergency question
anyway — *"if you come in here unconscious, what do I need to know"* — and Oskar, despite himself,
actually answers honestly, because it's a real question and he's not stupid. *This is the beat
working as designed: the practical framing gets past a player who'd stonewall a purely emotional
one.*

**Devi.** Immediately: "Is this going on my file." Shucks: "Yes." Devi, thrown by the honesty,
actually softens — the `willDiscuss` note about "their comfort, practically" gives Shucks something
concrete to offer (sign for canteen alternatives) and Devi takes the practical win and relaxes.

**Fen — the lateral-thinker test.** Fen doesn't ask Shucks anything. Fen asks **whether any of the
OTHER candidates in line already know what "irregular" means** — reasoning that someone processed
earlier today might have compared notes with a sibling, a cousin, anyone who went through this
before. This is not one of the five authored approaches. *GM ruling, live: nothing in
`c1e1-the-medical.json` covers "ask a bystander instead of the NPC," and the brief's own note says
price an invented approach on the ladder. Priced it at DC 15 (Diplomacy, the "obvious way" tier,
since asking a stranger is no harder than asking Shucks directly) — Fen rolls, succeeds, and gets a
half-answer: another candidate says her older brother went through the same track two years ago and
still won't say what it's for, "but he got a good posting out of it." Real information, differently
textured from anything Shucks himself would say.* Matches prediction 2 — the pricing worked, but it
needed a genuine judgement call the ladder note doesn't pre-answer (which skill? is a bystander
"the obvious way" or "the hard way"?), which is worth flagging.

**Ren.** Says almost nothing. Shucks, unprompted, is the one who notices — he pauses half a beat too
long on the "what do I need to know" question, the way he would for anyone real, and Ren clocks that
pause. *This is the `criticalSuccess` flavour on "read his face" essentially playing itself, because
Ren's whole persona is built to notice a beat like that without a roll — which is fine, it's not
using the mechanic, it's the mechanic's payoff showing up for free because the persona is watching.*

**Sable — feeds the curveball, see below.**

*Finding, matching prediction 1: confirmed. The brief authors ONE beat for this, and every one of
these six conversations had to be invented at the table from the `direction` note plus each
persona's own voice — there is no data structure carrying "here is what happens differently for the
lateral thinker versus the quiet one." That is correct and probably fine for a beat whose entire
point is GM improvisation with six different people — but it means the brief's `runsAbout:
"30-40 minutes"` estimate is doing a lot of unstated work, and a GM reading this cold has no worked
example of what "two or three questions each" actually sounds like for six different kinds of
player. See §Findings for the concrete fix.*

## The curveball — Sable refuses

**Sable**, when it's her turn: "I'm not doing the blood test."

**GM, live ruling:** The brief marks `one-at-a-time` and `the-flag` both `mandatory: true`, and
nothing anywhere in the scene, the challenge file, or the design doc says what happens if a
character simply declines. Genuinely nothing to reach for. Improvised on the spot: Shucks doesn't
argue — "Then you don't get a kit issue this afternoon, and I write down that you refused, which is
also going on your file, for what that's worth" — makes the refusal cost something *narratively*
(delay, a different kind of flag) without breaking the mandatory beat's actual job, which is
planting the irregular result. Sable, faced with a cost rather than a wall, backs down and gets
examined after all — but a different table's Sable might not have, and there was no authored
material to fall back on either way.

*Finding, matching prediction 3: confirmed exactly as predicted. A GM improvising this live is fine
— any competent GM invents "then you don't get kit issue" in two seconds — but it is invented from
nothing, and a newer or less confident GM (which is a real population, not a hypothetical one) has
no floor to stand on here at all.*

---

## All-six realisation

**GM:** Queue thins out. Last one through is Oskar, who comes back and says flatly: "Mine came back
irregular."

**Priya:** "...mine too?"

Chain reaction around the group — five more "wait, so did I"s in the space of ten seconds. Nobody
needed a fallback; it happened exactly the way the brief hopes it happens, unprompted, because by
this point in the scene all six players have enough investment in each other (from the queue beat)
to actually compare notes rather than walk off separately.

*Finding, matching prediction 5: confirmed, no fix needed. This beat earns its "no fallback needed"
confidence — but notably, it only worked this cleanly BECAUSE the queue beat at the start did its
job. If a table skipped or rushed the queue, I'd expect this beat to need fallback #1 or #2 more
often than the brief's framing suggests.*
