# Re-read: An Early Night

version 1 · re-read against canon/reread.md version 2 · 2026-09-29 · by Claude · state: awaiting Ingram's read

Built on the approved idea in `canon/plan/chapter-01.md` ("The night's rest", number 2): the bunk in the overflow block
where half the furniture hasn't arrived, half HP, and a deck of dormitory life (a snorer, a homesick roommate, a
lights-out inspection, the radiator). All four promised things are in: the snorer is Grenna's event, the homesick
roommate is Haru's, the inspection is "Lights, please", and the radiator is in the place itself, said on every visit.
The rest is half HP and nothing else, as the idea has it; the deck carries one modest buff of its own (Haru's plums).

## The questions

**Does every part earn its place?**

- **R1, earns its place.** The place (`haunt`) is read on every arrival and sets the jokes the deck pays off (plywood
  walls, crates, radiator, Wash House). Each regular is someone a player can talk to, with a want. Each event gives a
  player something to roll, say or decide: five rolls (Performance, Stealth, Crafting, Diplomacy, Athletics), one buff,
  two role-play prompts, one dilemma and one informant. The held-in-reserve pencil map is a thing a player can find and
  keep. Changed: the radiator was going to be its own buff event; it was only texture, so it moved into `everyVisit`.
- **R2, worse without it.** Each event is a different idea and none repeats another haunt's. Cutting the dilemma would
  lose the one event that mirrors the city changing; cutting the informant would lose the one glimpse of how the island
  works.
- **R16, directions.** Two kept, both about delivery that matters: "Roll it for the player in secret" on the Stealth
  check (Conceal an Object is a secret check), and "Take a moment" in the role-play prompts, which is Ingram's own
  instruction to give players time to think. Nothing else tells her how to deliver a line.
- **R17, negatives.** None in the GM text. Mrs Morishita's rules ("no food in the rooms") are her lines, not
  directions.
- **R18, prepared for what can't happen.** Nothing. There is no speech anyone must sit through; every regular can be
  talked to at any time.

**Is it clean?**

- **R3, clean.** No decision numbers, dates, tools, files or other scenes in anything the GM reads. The warden's
  jokes about the roof, and the pencilled ladder in Row E, are things in the world, not references to another haunt.
  Changed: Haru's answer "the bit with the tunnel was good" named the night's duty from inside the scene before it,
  and assumed he had been there; it is now "some of it was good".

**Does it give away too much?**

- **R12, early reveals.** Nothing the table shouldn't hear. No founders, no rift, no screening results, nothing about
  anyone's blood or nature. Read for the uncited: the informant says the warden's card goes to the section officers
  and "the office reads all of them". That is ordinary office life on the island, and it names no watcher and suspects
  nothing. Changed: the first draft said "somebody, somewhere, reads all of them", which leaned toward a mystery this
  scene has no allowance for; it is now plainly the office.
- **R19, answers or questions.** The scene gives small answers only (which dryer eats coins, when the real timetable
  goes up), which is what an informant is for. The larger things stay questions: why the overflow block has been
  "temporary" for six years, and what the section officers do with the card. Neither is answered here.
- **R20, telling the players what it means.** No line does. The dilemma's note tells the GM that either choice is fair
  and that the change comes back; nothing tells the players what the crates stand for.

**Do the players roll?**

- **R4, a roll where they act.** Yes: whistling, hiding food, mending the bunk, talking Haru round, the pillow fight,
  and saving the names in the dilemma. The two role-play prompts have no roll on purpose: they are for talking.
- **R5, the dilemma through rolls.** The choice itself is a choice, but a player who wants a third way (save the
  names and take the lockers) gets one through Society, Crafting or Thievery, with four outcomes.
- **R6, all four degrees.** Every roll has all four. Each critical success gives something extra (the block's
  whistler, "tidiest room", Grenna's approval, Haru's seat at breakfast, prisoners of war, the porters signing the lid).
  Each failure moves the night on. Each critical failure escalates: the block wakes, the warden confiscates everything,
  the bunk folds, Haru knocks on the warden's door, feathers, or nobody gets lockers.

**Who's in it?**

- **R7, free, on a check, never.** Mrs Morishita gives the rules, the furniture saga, the Wash House and her own
  history freely; on a Diplomacy check at her Will DC (friendly or better) she says what the card says; she never
  discusses the card's contents otherwise ("Heads. Counted.") or her biscuit tin. Haru talks freely about his village,
  his grandmother and the day; he never says why he came, and deflects with "It's a good opportunity. Everyone says."
  Grenna talks about home, mending and the block; she never discusses the snoring and deflects by offering to fix
  something. Sachi, the informant, is a one-off and gives everything freely.
- **R8, likely questions.** Five or six per regular, answered in their voices.
- **R9, stat blocks.** **Missing.** Each regular carries a proposed level and archetype (all level 1, non-combat) to
  be made with Ingram once the NPC generator exists. Checks cite each regular's Will or Perception DC with the
  level-based DC (15 at level 1) as the fallback until then. Sachi and the porters have none; they never need to roll.
- **R10, if they do something else.** `notes` covers sneaking out (Stealth against her Perception DC, and the card),
  lying (Deception), stealing or raiding the biscuit tin (Thievery), starting a real fight, splitting across rooms, and
  walking out.

**Is it well said?**

- **R15, read aloud.** Read through out loud: six sentences, each one breath or two, ending on the radiator knocking
  like somebody at the door, which is the joke the table will hear all night.
- **R21, fragments.** None found in the GM text. Voice samples are short because they are speech.
- **R22, coined words.** None needed. "Candidate" is what the table has been called all day; "Frame" is not said.
  "Second-termer" explains itself in the informant's own line ("I was you last term").
- **R23, length.** A downtime menu, the same length as the other haunt: ten events, three regulars, four short beats.

**Does it hold up?**

- **R11, one thing to retell.** Grenna's mother's three pages of snoring remedies, ending "stop blaming the friend,
  Grenna"; or the bunk folding like a deckchair and the warden being delighted.
- **R13, facts agree and are cited.** **Not cited.** The scene tells no fact from `canon/world/`, and the beats' fact
  lists are empty, as in the other haunt. It does state things about the place that are mine and not yet canon: Row C,
  Block 3 as first-term overflow at the landing end, packing crates for nightstands, the Wash House next door and
  Thursday washing, Block 7 as second-term housing further down the row, a ladder in Row E, the last train on the
  causeway (which agrees with the provisional causeway-and-railway fact), the warden's card, and six years of
  "temporary". They should become provisional facts if Ingram keeps them. The place itself is not in canon yet (the
  check notes `row-c-block-3`).
- **R14, story time.** The plan puts this on Day 1, at night, after the night's duty. "Tonight" and "from tomorrow"
  agree with that. Changed: the warden's line about lights at half past ten now says the group are already late, since
  they arrive after the duty; her inspection event is her last round; the morning bell no longer has a fixed hour, so
  it cannot clash with the next morning's scenes.
- **R24, places.** Checked against the map: Block 3, the Wash House and Row E, Block 9 are all at the landing end and
  close together. Changed: the first draft had the second-termer talking "across the path" from Block 7, which is about
  a hundred and fifty metres away; she now stops at the window on her way back from the Wash House.
- **R25, all her detail.** Ingram's words on haunts are in: a menu, not a script; skill rolls that produce results;
  a fun buff; chances to role-play, with "tell us about your character's day" asked in full and time to think; a
  dilemma that mirrors the city changing (the steel lockers replacing the crates with every intake's names in them);
  an informant with an unconsidered line of information, shared willingly (how the first week really goes, and who
  reads the warden's card); regulars whose attitudes move with the events, including by accident (the pillow that
  bursts). Allowance respected: no plot event and no danger event.
