# Re-read: The Bathhouse

version 1 · re-read against canon/reread.md version 2 · 2026-09-29 · by Claude · state: awaiting Ingram's read

Built on the approved idea in `canon/plan/chapter-01.md` ("The night's rest", number 3): half HP, and automatically
temporary HP equal to the character's level until the next rest; a deck of regulars (dockworkers, a retired Frame
fitter with stories, the attendant who runs the place like a ship). All of that is kept. The shape, length and voice
follow The Crew Mess.

## Does every part earn its place?

- **R1** Every part is something the GM runs or a player does. The read-aloud sets the room; `your-bath` gives each
  player a turn to speak in character; the deck gives rolls (five), buffs (two), a role-play prompt, a dilemma and an
  informant; the closing beat applies the rest. The letter under the brass weight (`holdInReserve`) is there for a
  player who asks, and seeds the dilemma. The milk at closing is something to do and laugh at (hand on hip, facing the
  mural). Nothing was cut; nothing was found idle.
- **R2** Each event is a different kind of fun: a custom to get right, an endurance contest, a picture to search, a
  farce, a board game, a kindness, a small gift, an argument, a gossip and a story. Taking any one out loses its idea
  from the haunt.
- **R16** Directions kept only where delivery matters: "Give them a moment to picture it" in `your-bath`, "Take a
  moment first" in the scars prompt (Ingram's own ask: time to think before speaking), and the dilemma's note on which
  attitude buttons to press, which the Runner can't tell on its own.
- **R17** No negatives to the GM. The notes say what happens, not what to avoid. Two NPC lines ("never repeats what
  anyone said about a candidate by name", Kazue "never turns it over while anyone is looking") describe the people, not
  the GM.
- **R18** Nothing prepares for what the shape rules out. It is all interruptible conversation.

## Is it clean?

- **R3** Nothing the GM reads mentions how it was made: no decision numbers, dates, tools, files or other scenes. The
  one reference outward is "the overflow block" in Mori's gossip, which is a place in the world the characters sleep
  in, not a scene. The check found no path outside `canon/`.

## Does it give away too much?

- **R12** Nothing about the founders, the rift, why the Directorate came, other ships, the screening or the player
  characters' nature. Mori's gossip is about beds and surveyors, and he explicitly never repeats anything said about a
  candidate by name. **Deliberately left out: glamours.** A bath is where a disguise would matter most, and any rule
  or joke about glamours in the water could press on a player's private choice about their character's disguise, so the
  scene never raises them.
- **R19** The answers the scene gives are small and ordinary (the rules, the regulars' lives, the offer). The offer is
  held back twice: behind the weight, then behind a Diplomacy check at Kazue's Will DC, and the informant tells it from
  a second angle (the surveyors), so it can be found two ways.
- **R20** No line tells the players what anything means. The dilemma's note says both answers are fair, for the GM.

## Do the players roll?

- **R4** Five roll events, a check on the dilemma, and checks for every "something else" in the notes. The two buffs
  and the role-play event have no roll on purpose: they are the fun buffs and the chance to speak that Ingram asked for.
  The informant has none: Mori shares willingly.
- **R5** Yes, for the harder answer. Keep or sell is a spoken choice with attitude costs either way; arguing for a third
  way is a Diplomacy roll against Kazue's Will DC with four outcomes.
- **R6** Every roll has all four. Critical successes give something extra and lasting (a numbered bucket, the champion's
  peg, finding young Hachi and Kazue in the mural, a friend in the locker deck, a rematch forever, Kazue writing the
  idea down). Failures move the night on. Critical failures escalate (the pool drained, a week's ban from the hot pool,
  paint off the mural, forty dockworkers in one heap, a nickname, both regulars turning on the character).

## Who's in it?

- **R7** Three regulars, each with `willDiscuss`, `willNotDiscuss` with the deflecting line in their voice, and what a
  check gets: Kazue's offer (Diplomacy at her Will DC, friendly or better), Hachi's advice on turning a Frame on the
  ball of the foot (Diplomacy at his Will DC, friendly or better). Sigrun has nothing behind a check; her soft spot (her
  brother) she guards with "Next year. Get in the pool." Mori the stoker and the stevedore are walk-ons inside events.
- **R8** Five or six likely questions each, answered in voice.
- **R9** **Missing: stat blocks.** Each regular has a proposed level and archetype only (Kazue level 2, Hachi level 1,
  Sigrun level 1), to be made with Ingram once the NPC generator exists. Until then the Will and Perception DCs the
  events name have no numbers; the level-based DC (15 at level 1) stands in where the event offers it. Mori and the
  stevedore need none unless a fight starts.
- **R10** The notes cover fighting, lying, stealing from the baskets, sneaking to the boiler room, crossing to the other
  side, splitting up across the wall, and leaving before bathing (half HP and no temporary HP). Following a regular
  home was missing and is added: Stealth against their Perception DC, where each one lives, and what happens to a
  follower who is caught.

## Is it well said?

- **R15** The read-aloud was read aloud: one breath per sentence, the whistle as the joke, and the story in the steam as
  the hand-off to Hachi.
- **R21** Full sentences throughout; no fragment lists.
- **R22** Shogi is explained where it appears (Minkai's chess, wedge-shaped pieces, captured pieces come back). A
  boatswain is explained in Kazue's history. The speaking pipe is explained in its event and in Kazue's topics. "Frame"
  and "candidate" are the campaign's words the table already has by nightfall.
- **R23** The length of The Crew Mess, for a 10 to 20 minute visit with a deck to draw from.

## Does it hold up?

- **R11** The candidate who wakes up on the tiles with a crane driver fanning them with a washbowl, or the one whose name
  hangs on the champion's peg; or the whole bathhouse sitting on stools while the big pool is refilled.
- **R13** Canon it leans on: the Directorate's base is its own island in the harbour with barracks and workshops
  (directorate.md); Frames are tall, clumsy, purely mechanical and mostly seen lifting girders and crates of cabbage
  (frames.md), which Hachi's cabbage-crate story fits. Everything else (the regulars, the mural, the offer, the speaking
  pipe) is the place's own and new, and cites no fact, as in The Crew Mess. The quays, the east-quay crane and the dry
  dock the dockworkers come from are assumed to be on the island; Ingram should confirm the island has working quays.
- **R14** Day 1, night, after Flood Tunnel Clearance, as the plan's table has it. The scene says "tonight" and "today"
  only, and the flood tunnel's mud is the one link to the day.
- **R24** The bathhouse (`the-bathhouse-447`) is on the map in the middle of the island, and the scene puts it there.
  The mural shows the old town low along the waterfront, which agrees with the map. The check notes the location is not
  in canon yet.
- **R25** All of the plan's promises are kept: half HP, automatic temporary HP equal to level, and the three kinds of
  regular. All of the task's events are here: etiquette, the hottest pool (Fortitude), the mural and its painter, the
  basket muddle, a board game on the bench, a back to scrub, old-timers arguing about the bathhouse being sold for
  Directorate housing (the dilemma that mirrors the city), and the one who hears everything (Mori, through the pipe).
  From D050 and D051: rolls with four outcomes, buffs, prompts with time to think, a dilemma that mirrors the city, an
  informant sharing willingly, and attitude buttons on the events. No plot or danger events, as allowed.
