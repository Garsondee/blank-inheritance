# Re-read: The General Hospital

version 1 · re-read against canon/reread.md version 2 · 2026-09-29 · by Claude · state: awaiting Ingram's read

## Does every part earn its place?

**R1** Each part gives the GM something to run and a player something to do. The ward read-aloud sets the room; the
admission card gives every player a turn to say what happened to them; the deck gives rolls (Society, Medicine,
Diplomacy, Perception) and in-character prompts; the three regulars carry attitudes the next visit reads from. The
postcard drawer (hold in reserve) is a reward for kindness, not a plot hook. Changed: nothing cut.

**R2** Each part would be missed. Without the admission card, the one full-rest haunt would have no guaranteed turn for
each player; without the regulars, a return visit would read the same. Changed: nothing.

**R16** Directions kept only where critical: the rest field says plainly that nothing on the ward adds to or takes
from the full recovery, because D050 makes the hospital the sober choice with no buff, and a GM improvising a critical
success would otherwise be tempted to hand one out. Changed: no delivery directions in the beats beyond "give them a
moment", which is Ingram's own ask (D051).

**R17** No negative directions to the GM. The one "never" in the deck is the cleaner's firm's rule and the soldier's
note says what is held back, which is critical for a plot event. Changed: nothing.

**R18** The scene's shape is a quiet ward with interruptible conversations; nothing is prepared that the shape rules
out. Changed: nothing.

## Is it clean?

**R3** Nothing the GM reads mentions decisions, dates, tools, other scenes or files. The statblock lines ("Made with
Ingram once the NPC generator exists") follow the finished Crew Mess and are the one place a tool is named, because
the stat blocks genuinely wait on it. No path outside `canon/` appears. Changed: nothing.

## Does it give away too much?

**R12** Nothing forbidden is said or hinted: no founders, no Watcher, no screening, no blood, nothing about the
characters' nature. The facts cited are all `told: start` (`directorate.base`, `directorate.gate`,
`minkai.own-forces`). The spring drill Gennai mentions rests on what the table already hears in the opening: the
Directorate drills for a disaster. The twenty extra beds point at "something big on the island this week" and say
nothing about what; Ingram may want to check that against Episode Two's exercise before it is run. Changed: Gennai
knows how many beds and when, never why.

**R19** The soldier's event gives no answers, only observations a skill earns: the knuckles (Medicine), the lie
(Sense Motive), and on a critical success "Everyone's agreed I fell", which raises the hush-up as a question.
The nurse's line "I didn't write his" is a second, independent route behind a Diplomacy check at her Will DC, so the
question can be found from two angles. Who he hit, and why, stays held. Changed: the gate is named only as where he
was brought from.

**R20** No line says what anything means. The cleaner's dilemma shows the new city firm's rule and lets the choice
speak; the soldier's "You're theirs, then" shows the friction without explaining it. Changed: nothing.

## Do the players roll?

**R4** Wherever a character acts, there is a check: helping on rounds (Medicine), the crossword (Society), the new
father (Diplomacy, or Stealth/Deception), finding the way back (Perception or Survival), the cut hand (Medicine plus
Stealth/Deception), the soldier (Sense Motive, Medicine or Diplomacy). The three role-play prompts and the tea
trolley are talk, with nothing to roll by design. The rest itself has no roll, as the approved idea promises.

**R5** The dilemma resolves through rolls if they choose the quiet way (Medicine, then Stealth or Deception against
the nurse's Perception DC); telling the nurse is a choice with a cost, and needs none.

**R6** Every roll pays on all four degrees. Critical failures escalate socially (the ward woken, the nurse down a
step, the soldier shouting) and never touch the full recovery. Critical successes give something extra that is not a
mechanical benefit: a finished crossword in the drawer, a name in the ward book, the harbour lit up below, the
baby's name, the soldier's "Everyone's agreed I fell".

## Who's in it?

**R7** The three regulars each have what they say freely, what a check gets (only the nurse is coercible: Diplomacy
at her Will DC for the soldier's card) and what they never say, with the deflecting line. The soldier, the porter,
the cleaner and the new father live inside their events, each with their lines written there.

**R8** Five or six likely questions per regular, answered in their voices. The soldier's likely questions ("What
happened?", "Who brought you?") are covered by the event's outcomes rather than a separate map.

**R9** Not yet. Every regular and the soldier has a proposed level and archetype; the actual stat blocks, with
Perception, Will and a Deception DC, wait for the NPC generator and Ingram. The checks already point at those DCs by
name (the nurse's Will and Perception, the soldier's Deception).

**R10** In the notes: a fight, lying to the nurse, the medicine cabinet, sneaking out, splitting up and discharging
themselves, each with its roll and consequence. There is no danger event, as the scene's allowance says.

## Is it well said?

**R15** The read-aloud was read aloud: it runs as one person describing a room, with the joke of the Gentlemen's
Outfitting sign and the coin-bounce sheets to end on.

**R21** Full sentences throughout; no clipped fragments in the read-aloud, beats or events. Changed: the outro line
was rewritten so it describes what happens rather than what anyone thinks of breakfast.

**R22** No coined word needs explaining here beyond what the table already has. "Directorate kit" and "candidate" are
heard from the first scenes; "harbour pilot" is explained by Mr Aucourt when asked.

**R23** Ten to twenty minutes of play, the same length as the other rests: a short read-aloud, one turn each, and a
deck of ten.

## Does it hold up?

**R11** The retellable moment is likely the crossword finished for the first time in twenty years, or the new father
on the back stairs with his chrysanthemums.

**R13** Cited: `directorate.base` (the island) and `directorate.gate` and `minkai.own-forces` (the soldier and where
he was brought from). New and uncited, and Claude's own, for Ingram to accept or change: Ward Four and its Gentlemen's
Outfitting sign, the maternity ward in the old toy department, the three regulars, Gennai the porter, the city
cleaning firm and its rule, the spring drill, and Private Kuroki and his sister. None of these is yet a fact in
`canon/world/`; the soldier's truth sits only in his event note.

**R14** The plan puts this on day 1, at night, after Flood Tunnel Clearance. "Today" in the prompts means that first
day, and the soldier "came in this afternoon", which agrees.

**R24** The hospital is on the island, and the soldier is brought over the causeway from the gate at its land end,
which agrees with the base as described. Its map footprint is the island-centre site; the location is not yet in
canon, which the check notes.

**R25** Everything the approved idea promised is here: full HP, no buff, no roll for the rest, the soldier with a
wrist he won't explain, the crossword aloud, a patient talking in her sleep. Ingram's D050 reasoning (the sober
choice against the gamble) is honoured by making sure no event on the ward can add a benefit.
