# Re-read: The Roof

version 1 · re-read against canon/reread.md version 2 · 2026-09-29 · by Claude · state: awaiting Ingram's read

Built on the approved idea in `canon/plan/chapter-01.md` ("The night's rest", number 8): half HP, Perception to learn
the city by its lights, +1 status bonus to Will saves on a success, and a deck that is "who else is up there". All
four promises are kept. Shape, length and voice follow `canon/scenes/rest--crew-mess.json`.

## What the re-read changed

- **Clipped lines rewritten as full sentences.** Faruq's answers ("Katapesh. Hot. Loud. Far.", "Yes. Slowly.", "It
  ends there."), Sayo's "Up the coast. Three houses and a boat.", Isamu's "Four years. Thirty on the tugs…", and
  several `willDiscuss` notes and outcomes that began with a fragment. Faruq is still a man of few words; he now says
  them in whole sentences.
- **The read-aloud's stargazer is "a candidate in a borrowed greatcoat"**, not a girl: the table hasn't been told the
  candidates' ages, and it lets Sayo introduce herself.
- **The rest's critical failure was a flat "nothing"**; it now escalates (something drops into the yard and Isamu has
  to decide whether he saw it).
- **The paper lantern's slip** is the one physical risk on the roof, and it escalates into the warden rather than into
  damage, so the rest stays a rest and nothing on the roof is violent.
- **The padlock dilemma's rolls** apply only to a third way (keeping the second key); the two plain answers are a
  choice with attitude buttons, as in the Crew Mess's petition.

## The questions

**Does every part earn its place?**

- **R1, earns its place.** Each beat gives the GM one job: set the roof; give each player a turn and the rest's roll
  (with what a success can pick out, so the GM has the districts to hand); draw; apply the rest. Every event gives a
  player something to roll, decide, say or laugh at. `holdInReserve` (Isamu's initials by the tank) is there for the
  player who pokes about, and pays off his `willNotDiscuss`.
- **R2, worse without it.** The district list in the second beat is the one long part; without it a success on the
  rest's roll has nothing to tell the player. Each event carries a different idea (stars, direction, the warden, a
  slip, a Frame, a wish, a day, home, a rule, the harbour), and none repeats another haunt's.
- **R16, directions.** Only three, all practical: "Give them a moment to picture it", "Take a moment first" in the
  prompts (Ingram's own ask, D051), and the note to go straight to the warden's torch on the lantern's critical
  failure.
- **R17, negatives.** None aimed at the GM. The "nevers" in the text are description ("the factories never go out")
  or the Runner's deck rule, copied from the model.
- **R18, prepared for what can't happen.** No. Isamu's questions are asked at the foot of the ladder or when he's
  caught someone, both of which the deck makes possible.

**Is it clean?**

- **R3, clean.** No decision numbers, dates, file paths, tools or other scenes in anything the GM reads. The padlock's
  note says "This comes back as the city keeps changing", which is an instruction about the haunt itself.

**Does it give away too much?**

- **R12, early reveals.** Nothing about the founders, the rift, why the Directorate came to Kurose, any ship, the
  screening or the characters' nature. The informant's tell (tugs moved and kitchens lit early the night before a
  Frame goes out) is ordinary and new, and its note says so. The lantern's wish ("Please let my big sister be sent for
  this year") rests on `directorate.sends-for-trainees`, told at the start.
- **R19, answers or questions.** The scene's answers are all small and ordinary: which light is which district, what
  the Frame is doing tonight, when the ferry runs. The bigger ones are held behind checks (Sayo's hidden star,
  Faruq's tune, the inspection date) or never said (Isamu's initials).
- **R20, meaning.** No line says what the roof, the padlock or the lantern means. The padlock's note to the GM says
  either answer is fair; it tells the players nothing.

**Do the players roll?**

- **R4, rolls.** Every character rolls Perception for the rest. Five events roll: Nature or Lore (stars), Stealth
  (Hide, the torch), Athletics or Acrobatics (the lantern), Perception or Piloting (the Frame), and Thievery or
  Diplomacy (the padlock's third way). The notes give rolls for lying, stealing and jumping roofs. The roleplay events
  and the buff have no roll by design; the informant is freely given.
- **R5, dilemma through rolls.** The padlock is first a choice, because its cost is the point; its third way resolves
  through Thievery or Diplomacy with all four degrees.
- **R6, all four degrees.** Every roll has four real outcomes. Critical failures escalate: the crane lamp becomes a
  standing joke, the flask brings Isamu up with his book, the slide booms the tank and starts the torch, the shout
  closes Faruq's harmonica for the night, the dropped cap lands in the yard, the caught key costs Isamu's goodwill.

**Who's in it?**

- **R7, free, on a check, never.** All three regulars have `willDiscuss`, `coercible` (Diplomacy at their Will DC,
  friendly or better) and `willNotDiscuss` with a deflecting line: Sayo's fear of heights ("I like the middle"),
  Faruq's lost tune ("That's where it ends."), Isamu's roof and his initials ("I don't know anything about any roof.").
- **R8, likely questions.** Six for Sayo, six for Faruq, five for Isamu, each in their own voice.
- **R9, stat blocks.** Missing. Each has a proposed level and archetype (Sayo and Faruq level 1 candidates, Isamu
  level 2 working adult, all non-combat); Perception, Will and a Deception DC wait for the NPC generator. The events
  already call on Isamu's Perception and Will DCs and Sayo's and Faruq's Will DCs, with the level-based DC as the
  stand-in.
- **R10, attack, lie, steal, sneak, follow, split, walk out.** In `notes`: noise, lying to Isamu, taking the chart,
  jumping to the next roof, picking a fight (nobody fights; the regulars leave), splitting, and going down early.
  Sneaking is the warden's torch itself. Following someone doesn't arise: nobody leaves the roof but the group.

**Is it well said?**

- **R15, read aloud.** Read through as speech: one breath per sentence, the view before the people, and it ends on
  the harmonica so the GM can hand straight to Sayo.
- **R21, fragments.** Fixed throughout (above). The remaining short lines are spoken dialogue in character, in full
  sentences.
- **R22, coined words.** "Frame" is glossed where it is heard ("one of the great walking machines"). "Candidate" is
  the table's own word by now. Katapesh is a real Golarion nation, placed in Faruq's role as far across the sea.
  The constellation names are explained in the event that uses them.
- **R23, length.** A haunt runs ten to twenty minutes and most of it lives in the deck, so the beats are short; the
  one long beat carries the city's lights, which is what this rest is for.

**Does it hold up?**

- **R11, the retold thing.** The candidate who pointed confidently at a crane lamp and got it named on the chart as
  the Candidate's Mistake, with a tune to go with it. Or the water tank booming like a gong.
- **R13, facts agree with canon.** Checked against `canon/world/kurose.md` (old town on the waterfront, factories
  along the shore, river and trams inland, hills behind, electric light, trams and factories working through the
  night), `canon/world/frames.md` (walking machines with a pilot inside, clumsy, run by the Directorate) and
  `canon/world/directorate.md` (the island base with hangars and workshops, sending for trainees). Like the Crew Mess,
  the scene cites no facts in `facts[]`; its new ordinary details (the regulars, the ladder, the tugs-and-kitchens
  tell, the constellation names) are Claude's and would become `provisional` facts if Ingram keeps them.
- **R14, story time.** Day 1, night, after Flood Tunnel Clearance, as the plan has it. The scene says "tonight" and
  "tomorrow" only, which agree.
- **R24, places.** The block is on the island near where the causeway comes ashore, so the city is across the
  harbour, as the read-aloud has it. The factories, old town, river and hills agree with canon. **To confirm:** the
  Upper Wards, the Container Ground, the campus on the cliff top, the hangars' berths and the dry dock are taken from
  the map's older entries and the Crew Mess, not from canon; the campus in particular is named only by the block's own
  map note, and whose campus it is is not said.
- **R25, all of Ingram's detail.** The approved idea (half HP, Perception, the city by its lights, +1 status to Will,
  who else is up there) and the task's list are all here: the stargazer with a homemade chart, the harmonica player,
  the warden who pretends not to know, Golarion's constellations with invented names, the districts by their lights,
  the shooting star and a wish, the torch on the ladder (Stealth), the paper lantern, a Frame moved at night, a
  dilemma, the harbour informant, and a prompt about the character's day and home. D051's asks are met: rolls with
  results, a buff, chances to speak in character with time to think, a dilemma that mirrors the city changing (the
  office's new lock), and a chance-met informant with a glimpse behind the scenes.
