# Re-read: The Card Game

version 1 · re-read against canon/reread.md version 2 · 2026-09-29 · by Claude · state: awaiting Ingram's read

## What the scene is

A haunt (`design/haunts.md`) built on the approved idea in `canon/plan/chapter-01.md`, "The night's rest", item 6, and
the place's own note: a block-wide card game running longer than anyone living there has been a candidate, stakes of
chores and favours, and a debt board behind the common-room door taken with total seriousness. A quarter of maximum HP;
no automatic buff, because the game is the gamble. The plan's "pot that grows with level" became the board itself:
the stakes are chores and favours, never money, and the roll is at the level-based DC, so it stays as live at level 10
as at level 1. The plan's "a critical failure means you owe someone" is kept as the critical-failure result, and "the
regulars know every candidate on the island" is kept as the informant.

The game invented for it is **Last Ferry**: four cards each; every round you pass one to your left; the first to hold
four of one suit slaps the table and calls "Last ferry!"; whoever holds the Ferryman (the one joker left in) when
somebody calls pays double. Michiru says it in one breath.

## For you, while reading

These are mine, not yet yours:
- **The three regulars**: Michiru Arai (keeper), Lucan Farrow (the cheat, from Absalom), Chiyo Nakamura (never wins).
  Their three secrets are tied together: Lucan cheats to make Chiyo win, Chiyo drops every card he slips her, and
  Michiru is saving forty-one favours to wipe Chiyo's list on the day she leaves.
- **The Ninth Pier**, a harbour gang, and its two collectors, Mister Kanda and Goro, who work days in the base's coal
  yard. This assumes city men work on the island by day; it is how a city gang reaches a fenced base.
- **Candidates have leave days in the city.** Lucan's debt comes from one.
- **Warden Ogata**, Row F's block warden, who sleeps by the telephone and grows tomatoes.
- **Blue Hat**, the painted oldest debt, and the board being an old door with a brass number 3.
- **"Third-termer" and "second-termer"**: this assumes candidates stay for several terms.

## The questions

- **R1, earns its place.** Every event gives a player something to roll, decide or say, and each regular gives the GM a
  want and a secret to play. The four beats are the frame: room, names, the night, the last hand. The coal-yard detail
  and the warden's tomatoes are there because events use them.
- **R2, worse without it.** Each event does a different job: a dilemma (the cheat), a custom (Blue Hat), a raise
  (something that isn't a chore), a character prompt (the tell), an informant (the exercise books), danger (the Ninth
  Pier), a buff (watch the hands), and two light rolls (the tomatoes, the cat). None repeats another haunt's idea.
- **R16, directions.** Two kept: "Give them a moment before they answer" on the name beat and "Take a moment" in two
  prompts, because Ingram asked for thinking time before speaking (D051). Nothing else tells her how to deliver.
- **R17, negatives.** None in the GM text. "Chiyo mustn't know" is the secret itself, not a direction.
- **R18, prepared for what can't happen.** No. Nothing is a speech; every answer can be asked for.
- **R3, clean.** No decision numbers, dates, tools, files or other scenes in anything the GM reads. Checked by eye;
  `story.mjs check` raises nothing for this file except that the `data/` copy is not built yet.
- **R12, early reveals.** Nothing about the founders, the rift, the screening, anyone's blood, or the characters' own
  nature. Organised crime is `told: start`. The Ninth Pier is an ordinary gang with an ordinary grudge.
- **R19, answers or questions.** The one answer given freely, that the base runs on favours as much as on orders, is an
  informant's gift and harmless. Michiru's column is a question with its answer held behind a Diplomacy check, and
  Blue Hat stays a question for good.
- **R20, meaning.** No line says what anything means. The favour economy is said by Michiru as gossip, and the
  cheat dilemma's note says either answer is fair rather than which one is right.
- **R4, rolls.** Every hand is a roll; seven of nine events carry a check. The tell and the exercise books are pure
  role-play, as the deck's mix asks.
- **R5, dilemma through rolls.** Calling out the cheat is Society or Diplomacy at the level-based DC; staying quiet
  needs no roll and has its own cost (a private favour from Lucan, and complicity).
- **R6, four degrees.** Every roll pays on all four. A failure always moves the night on (the hand plays on, the night
  goes on, the hand is void, one more try). Critical failures escalate: red chalk, a debt written up for the week, a
  six o'clock tomato lecture, a fight.
- **R7, what each NPC says.** Each regular has willDiscuss, willNotDiscuss with the deflecting line, and coercible where
  there's something to earn: Michiru's column (Diplomacy at her Will DC, friendly or better), Lucan's debt (Diplomacy or
  Intimidation at his Will DC). Chiyo has nothing to force; her secret comes out only if she chooses.
- **R8, likely questions.** Five or six each, in their own voices.
- **R9, stat blocks.** Still missing. All five people (three regulars, Kanda, Goro) have a proposed level and
  archetype only, waiting for the NPC generator. The scene uses Michiru's Will and Perception DCs, Lucan's Will DC,
  Kanda's Perception and Will DCs and Goro's Perception DC, none of which exist yet.
- **R10, if they do something else.** The notes cover walking out, leaving in debt, rubbing a chore off, lying,
  attacking, sneaking upstairs, following Kanda, and splitting up, each with its roll.
- **R15, read aloud.** Read it through out loud: one breath per sentence, and it ends on Michiru's question to the table.
- **R21, fragments.** None found; the samples are spoken lines, and "Lovely sea." is Lucan dodging, which is how he
  talks.
- **R22, coined words.** Last Ferry, the Ferryman, the debt board's colours, Blue Hat and the Ninth Pier are each
  explained where they first come up. No Frame is mentioned.
- **R23, length.** Matches the Crew Mess: three regulars, four beats, nine events. A quick scene for ten to twenty
  minutes.
- **R11, the retold thing.** Chiyo winning her first hand, or the Admiral in a lap all night, or the tomatoes at six.
- **R13, facts.** Leans on `kurose.organised-crime` (decided). Everything else is new and ordinary, listed above as
  mine. Like the Crew Mess, the beats carry no fact citations; the new facts are not yet in `canon/world/`.
- **R14, story time.** Day 1, night, after the flood tunnel, as the plan has it. "Tonight" is the only time word, and
  "in the morning" for the tomatoes.
- **R24, place.** Row F, Block 5 is a candidates' block on the island, as its map entry has it; the party walk back
  "across the rows" to their own bunks. The coal yard and the causeway are on the island, as Scene 0 describes the
  base. `row-f-block-5` is not in `canon/` yet (the check notes it).
- **R25, her detail.** Used all of it: the rotating players, "longer than anyone living there has been a candidate",
  chores not money, the board behind the common-room door and its total seriousness (the game starts when the door
  shuts), Deception, Thievery or Society, owing someone on a critical failure, and the regulars knowing every
  candidate.
