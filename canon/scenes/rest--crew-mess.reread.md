# Re-read: The Crew Mess

version 1 · re-read against `canon/reread.md` version 2 · 2026-09-29 · by Claude · state: awaiting Ingram's read

The first haunt (`D051`), written as the model for the other nine. Built on the approved idea in the plan ("The bar:
where the repair crews drink when a refit finishes") and on Ingram's example, word for word in D051: "Did the group
trash the bar by accident by failing a skill roll? Whoops! That lowered the attitude..." That is `the-keg`.

**Does every part earn its place?**
- **R1** Every event gives a player something to roll, say, decide or keep; every regular has a want and a line per
  attitude. The slate is the room's memory and pays for the attitude system.
- **R2** Cutting any event leaves the deck thinner, not broken. That is the point of a deck.
- **R16** One direction survives, in `howToPlay` and `the-night`: how many events to draw. Everything else is a prompt
  or a result.
- **R17** No "don't". `ren`'s deflection ("Doesn't matter.") is a line, not a direction.
- **R18** No preparation for something the shape rules out.

**Is it clean?**
- **R3** Nothing mentions how it was made. One GM note names canon facts by id in the plot event, which is how canon
  notes point at the truth.

**Does it give away too much?**
- **R12** The plot event (`the-one-who-paid`) plants a question: a founder once came in and paid the slate, and
  looked like a picture older than the fitter. It names nobody and confirms nothing. It leans on `founders.alive` and
  the founders' look, both held for later; the table only hears an old man's story. **For Ingram:** whether even that
  story is too early is her call; drawing it can simply wait for a later episode (`episodes`).
- **R19** The crews' insight (the fitness list, which Frames are unsound) is given as talk, never as fact about the
  plot.
- **R20** Nothing tells the players what the petition means.

**Do the players roll?**
- **R4, R5, R6** Five events roll (`stand-a-round`, `the-keg`, `name-the-part`, `the-one-who-paid`, `two-crews`), each
  with all four degrees: a critical success gives something extra (a name on the slate, a free drink, a round from the
  tug crew), a failure moves the night on, a critical failure escalates (the bar shut, a brawl aimed at them). The
  dilemma (`the-petition`) resolves by choice, not by roll, on purpose: both answers cost something.

**Who's in it?**
- **R7, R8** Tomoe, Petr and Ren each have a conversation map: what they give freely, what they won't say and why,
  what leaning on them gets, and five or six likely questions answered in their voice.
- **R9** **Missing:** stat blocks. Each regular carries a proposed level and archetype, and the tug-hands in
  `two-crews` need brawler stat blocks. All wait for the NPC generator.
- **R10** In `notes`: leaving without paying, stealing, lying about being pilots, searching, splitting.

**Is it well said?**
- **R15** The read-aloud was read aloud; it is five full sentences, ending on the singer.
- **R21** No clipped fragments.
- **R22** "The slate" and "refit" are explained in the read-aloud and Tomoe's lines. "Frame" is known from the start.
- **R23** A haunt is long in deck and short in beats; this is about 2,500 words, most of it optional.

**Does it hold up?**
- **R11** The keg going through the mirror, or the toast to the wrong crew.
- **R13** Facts agree with canon: Frames are old and hard to drive (told from the start); the founders' Founding Day
  print is in supporters' windows (`founders.painting`); no founder is named or gendered.
- **R14** Day 1, night, in the plan. No time words that disagree.
- **R24** The Crew Mess is behind the dry dock, as the map has it.
- **R25** D050 and D051 read in full: the quarter HP, the bar-versus-hospital choice (the morning Fortitude save), the
  attitude system, the trashed bar, a dilemma mirroring the city (the petition), an informant, one plot event, one
  danger event with ways out.
