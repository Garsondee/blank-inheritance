# Re-read: The Shrine Gate

version 1 · re-read against canon/reread.md version 2 · 2026-09-29 · by Claude · state: awaiting Ingram's read

A night's rest and a haunt, built on the approved idea in `canon/plan/chapter-01.md` ("The night's rest", item 4): a
quarter of maximum HP, and each character draws a fortune (great blessing: a Hero Point; blessing: a +1 status bonus to
one check before their next rest; ordinary: a line to keep; curse: tied to the rope and left behind). The fortune is a
d10 table with ten slips, in the rest's `buff`. Two regulars, ten events, one `danger`, no `plot`.

## For you, while reading

These are mine, not yet yours, and none is in `canon/world/` yet:
- **The shrine** belongs to the Net-Mender, a kami (a small local spirit) of the old town's fishermen who mends what
  is torn. The keeper says the fishermen left offerings on the rock before there was an island, and the builders laid
  the streets round the shrine rather than move it. That is his story, told in his voice; the rock itself is canon
  (`directorate.base`: the island was "built up around a rock").
- **Tokuji Tsuruta**, the keeper, seventy-eight, a retired old-town fisherman who crosses on the last train every night;
  his son works on the island and thinks the shrine is nonsense; he can't write well.
- **Mira Kostel**, a switchboard operator on the island's night exchange, from a mountain country far to the west,
  who prays to her own "lady of stars and roads, with the butterflies" at home (Golarion's Desna, not named) and to
  the Net-Mender on her way home. She wishes every night for her brother's travel papers.
- **The works office** plans to widen the street and move the shrine to a plinth in the office gardens.
- **Linesmen switch off and swing aside street lamps** on a Frame's route before it walks through the island at night.
- **Kaito**, a crane hand laid off when new cranes came, in debt to a lender in the city, who has been taking coins
  from the offering box.

## The questions

- **R1, earns its place.** The rest gives HP and the fortune; the regulars give two people to talk to with wants and
  soft spots; every event gives a player something to do, say, roll, decide or keep (a wish, a lap cat, a stolen glove,
  a festival debt, a letter, a way to spot a Frame moving). The holdInReserve stone is for a table that lingers.
- **R2, worse without it.** Each event carries a different idea: speaking a wish, doing the rite right, an animal, the
  city's wishes in writing, a festival, the city squeezing the shrine, a behind-the-scenes trick, a wish made for
  someone else, a quiet buff, and a danger. None repeats the Crew Mess (no drink, no brawl, no petition to sign).
- **R16, directions.** Two kept as critical: "Give them a moment to picture it" and "Take a moment" in prompts, which
  are Ingram's own instruction for role-play prompts; and the danger's "This happens on the walk home, after the
  fortunes", because a drawn event would otherwise land at the wrong moment.
- **R17, negatives.** None in the GM's directions. The "don't" and "won't" that remain are in characters' mouths or
  describe what an NPC does ("won't chase far").
- **R18, prepared for what can't happen.** No. Nothing is uninterruptible, and every question has someone to answer it.
- **R3, clean.** No decision numbers, dates, files, tools or other scenes in anything the GM reads. The Crew Mess is
  never mentioned. The bathhouse nearby on the map is left out on purpose, so no other haunt is referenced.
- **R12, early reveals.** Nothing from the forbidden list: no founders, no rift, no other ship, no screening, nothing
  about the candidates' blood or nature. Every fact the scene states is known to everyone in Kurose and told at the
  start (the old town, newcomers, organised crime, change making winners and losers), or is new and ordinary to this
  place. The one fortune I rewrote: a draft great blessing about the sea bringing everything home again read as a
  hint about lost founders or far-off homes, so it became "The Net-Mender has mended your net already."
- **R19, answers or questions.** The scene answers only small, local things. The keeper's letter, his son and his own
  wish board are held behind attitude and rolls (Diplomacy at his Will DC, Perception critical success), so the table
  finds them rather than being told. The linesman's trick is the one piece of behind-the-scenes knowledge, and it
  is ordinary.
- **R20, meaning.** No line says what the shrine or the letter means. The dilemma's note lays out the cost of each
  answer for the GM only, and says either is fair.
- **R4, rolls.** Religion (the rite), Nature (the cat), Perception (the wish boards; spotting the men on the road),
  Crafting or Performance (the festival), Society (the letter), Diplomacy, Intimidation and Deception (the road), and
  Thievery and Deception in the notes. The wish, Mira's wish and the broom are role-play with no roll, by design.
- **R5, dilemma through rolls.** Yes: the letter is decided by what the player writes, and how the office receives it
  is a Society check with four results.
- **R6, degrees.** Every roll has all four. Critical successes give something extra (the rock story, the Abbot's hoard
  and a ring to return, the keeper's own wish board, a place in the festival, a meeting with the works office,
  Kaito's confession). Failures move on. Critical failures escalate (the bell off its hook and lights on across the
  street, a stolen glove, forty boards on the ground, a festival debt, the move brought forward, a fight aimed at the
  speaker and a purse gone).
- **R7, NPC maps.** Both regulars have willDiscuss, willNotDiscuss with the line they deflect with, and the keeper's
  letter is coercible (friendly or better, Diplomacy at his Will DC). Mira's one secret, what she hears on the lines,
  is never coercible, and says why. The linesman and Kaito are walk-ons inside events, with what they say written in.
- **R8, likely questions.** Six each, in their voices.
- **R9, stat blocks.** Missing, honestly: the keeper and Mira (proposed level 0, non-combat), the lender's men
  (proposed level 1 thugs with clubs), and Kaito (proposed level -1) all wait for the NPC generator. Their Perception,
  Will and Deception DCs are referenced but not yet numbers.
- **R10, if the group does something else.** In `notes`: attack, lie, steal from the offering box, go round the back,
  follow Mira, split, walk out early. Sneaking round the back is covered by what's there.
- **R15, read-aloud.** Read it aloud: it runs as one person describing a corner at midnight, and ends by explaining the
  kami in a single sentence.
- **R21, fragments.** Fortune slips are traditionally terse; every slip is written in full sentences instead. The read-
  aloud and event texts have no clipped lists of nouns. The rite in the Religion event is a list of steps, which the
  keeper would say that way.
- **R22, coined words.** "Kami" is explained where the players first hear it, in the read-aloud. "Net-Mender" is
  explained in the same sentence. "Frame" appears only in the linesman's event, and the
  players have heard it explained before this night.
- **R23, length.** About the Crew Mess's length: a menu of ten events for a ten-to-twenty-minute rest.
- **R11, the retold moment.** The Abbot stealing a candidate's glove and having it waiting on the next visit, or the
  player who wishes for Mira's brother.
- **R13, canon.** Agrees with `directorate.base` (island built around a rock, fence, offices, billets),
  `kurose.old-town` and `kurose.first-families-stayed` (the keeper), `directorate.newcomers` and
  `directorate.newcomers-built` (Mira and her own god), `kurose.left-behind` and `kurose.winners-and-losers` (Kaito and
  the new cranes), `kurose.organised-crime` (the lender), `kurose.everyday` (electric light, trams). The facts are not
  yet cited in the beats' `facts` arrays, which stay empty as in the Crew Mess; the new facts above wait for Ingram.
- **R14, story time.** Day 1, night, after Flood Tunnel Clearance, matching the plan. The scene says "tonight",
  "midnight" and "a very long first day", which agree.
- **R24, places.** The map puts the shrine in the island's centre, among offices and billets, which is what the scene
  describes. `the-shrine-gate-444` is not in `canon/` yet (the check notes it). Whether the island's original rock
  stands at this corner is the keeper's claim, not stated as fact; if Ingram wants it true, the map should agree.
- **R25, all her detail.** From D050 and D051: a quarter of HP, the fortune exactly as approved, roll-heavy with buffs
  and role-play, a dilemma that mirrors the changing city (the road widening), an informant with an unconsidered line
  (the linesman), an unannounced danger with ways out that aren't a fight and a theatre-of-the-mind fight if it comes,
  regulars whose attitudes move with events. The brief's "who else comes to a shrine at night" is the keeper, Mira,
  the linesman and Kaito.
