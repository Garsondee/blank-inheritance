# Re-read: The Magic Show

version 1 · re-read against `canon/reread.md` version 2 · 2026-09-29 · by Claude · state: awaiting Ingram's read

The first discovery (`D052`, settled points 6 and 8): what a player finds on the first night if they choose somewhere
new instead of a rest they know. Written as a haunt that plays once (`haunt.discovery`, `haunt.oneTime`), in the shape
of the Crew Mess. Ingram's brief, word for word: "the chance to go to a 'Magic Show' which is the very low key magical
illusion 'like magical theatre / IMAX' show which will be good and fun and carry some fun buffs, you can offer that as
the unknown location on the first night."

The show also follows what Ingram said about magic shows in `D002`: showy magic is frowned upon except in an emergency,
so people go to private back-street shows where illusionists cast spells around them, "the equivalent of a 3D immersive
film", a guilty pleasure most people in the city have been to at least once. The blankets over the windows, the
knock at the side door and "it isn't secret, it's private" all come from that.

**Does every part earn its place?**
- **R1** Every event gives a player something to say, roll, decide or keep. Each regular has a want, a line per
  attitude and two to four doors. The book of names and the ticket halves exist so that each spectator leaves with
  something that makes a later invitation possible (`D052` point 9); they are innocent, and nothing says who sends the
  next one.
- **R2** Cutting any event thins the deck without breaking it. The finale beat is the one part the scene can't lose:
  it is the gamble that makes a discovery riskier and stronger than an ordinary rest.
- **R16** Two directions survive: how many events to draw, and who volunteers if nobody does (the finale's
  `direction`). Both answer something a GM would otherwise stop to decide.
- **R17** One negative, in Daisuke's line "Don't touch it", which is dialogue, not a direction.
- **R18** No preparation for anything the shape rules out.

**Is it clean?**
- **R3** Nothing mentions how the scene was made, the later return of the show, or what any other scene does. The
  notes say what the show is and what the book is for, in the present tense.

**Does it give away too much?**
- **R12** Nothing about the later show, and none of the held-back names or topics, appears anywhere. The one glimpse
  behind the scenes (`the-watchman`) is that Directorate officers come to these shows in civilian coats; the officer is
  nobody the group has met and is gone if they look. **For Ingram:** the scene tells the table the `D002` facts about
  magic shows (frowned upon except in an emergency, private, a guilty pleasure, most people have been to one), and
  there is no canon world fact for them yet, so no beat can cite them. They want a world file of their own, from `D002`,
  told `start` or `in:discovery--the-magic-show`. The recruitment shows in `D002` (the "leave or stay" choice) are
  deliberately absent: nothing here asks the audience to stay behind.
- **R19** Tamsin's age and her husband, Daisuke's family and Mrs Handa's wish are all held as questions, each with a
  deflecting line; a Diplomacy check opens two of them.
- **R20** Nothing tells the players what the glamour dilemma means, or what the ticket half is for beyond Tamsin's own
  words.

**Do the players roll?**
- **R4, R5, R6** Four events roll (`the-cloud`, `how-is-it-done`, `the-dragon`, `backstage`), and the finale's volunteer
  rolls the gamble; each has all four degrees. A critical success gives something extra (the volunteer slot, a wink,
  the dragon's bow, the lantern up close); a failure moves the night on; a critical failure escalates (the hall goes
  dark, the front row goes over, the second act starts late, the volunteer is Stupefied 1). The dilemma
  (`the-glamour-song`) resolves by choice, on purpose: each answer costs a step with somebody.

**Who's in it?**
- **R7, R8** Tamsin, Daisuke and Mrs Handa each have a conversation map: what they give freely, what they won't say
  and why, what leaning on them gets, and five or six likely questions answered in their voice. Kenta, Mr Ikeda, the
  man without his glamour and the officer are walk-ons inside single events.
- **R9** **Missing:** stat blocks. Each regular has a proposed level and archetype, waiting for the NPC generator.
- **R10** In `notes`: casting a spell during the show, reading or taking the book, heckling, leaving early,
  splitting up. Walking out is covered by Daisuke's hostile door and the notes.

**Is it well said?**
- **R15** The read-aloud was read aloud: three units, sound first (music behind blankets), then the alley and the
  knock, then Daisuke at the door. The two beat read-alouds (the lights going out, and the finale) are three sentences
  or fewer.
- **R21** No clipped fragments.
- **R22** "Glamour" is known from the start. "Private show" is explained by Daisuke at the door, and why it is private
  by Tamsin and Mrs Handa when asked. "The lantern" is shown before it is named.
- **R23** About 5,000 words, most of it optional deck and conversation maps; the beats are five short lines.

**Does it hold up?**
- **R11** The whale looking at you; the cloud raining on one character; the dragon bowing, or the whole front row
  going over; Tamsin letting her stage self go out like a candle and turning out to be a gnome three feet tall.
- **R13** Facts agree with canon and are cited on the show beat: glamours and the argument about them
  (`glamour.debate`), Kurose as a fishing village with one road in (`kurose.fishing-village`, told by Tamsin), magic
  small and everyday (`era.engines`). The `D002` facts are the gap named under R12.
- **R14** Day 1, night, in the plan; `story time` finds "tonight", which agrees.
- **R24** **For Ingram: the siting is her call.** Proposed: the Market Hall (`the-market-hall-456`), a covered produce
  market in Island Centre, "loud in the morning and empty by early afternoon", so empty and shuttered at night, a short
  walk from the other rests on the island. Nothing on the map is a theatre; the nearest alternative is the Civic Hall
  (`the-civic-hall-451`), also in Island Centre, but it is an administration building, where a private show would sit
  oddly.
- **R25** `D052` read in full. Low-key illusion, like theatre you sit inside: the sea, the whale, the lantern city, the
  dragon. Good and fun: no danger or plot event. Fun buffs: +1 status to Perception for everyone, temporary HP for a
  wish. Riskier and more powerful: the volunteer can win twice their level in temporary HP and +1 to all saves, or come
  away Stupefied 1 until their next rest. The unknown place on the first night: the players choose it blind and find it
  by its music. Attitude as a promise: every regular has doors, and both company members share the faction
  `the-oddwater-company`. `D050` and `D051`: a quarter HP, the gamble-or-hospital choice, a dilemma that mirrors the
  city (the glamour song), an informant, and roles for the players to speak in character.

**Choices worth Ingram's eye**
- **The buffs.** Perception is the one bonus no ordinary rest gives. The volunteer's win beats every ordinary rest
  (the bathhouse gives temporary HP equal to level; this gives twice that, and a critical success adds +1 to all saves),
  and its loss (Stupefied 1 for a whole day) is worse than any. A Hero Point was left out on purpose: Hero Points are
  lost at the end of a session (Player Core p. 405), and a night's rest may well end one.
- **No upgrades or setbacks.** The show plays once, so there is nothing to upgrade or wear down.
- **The faction.** Tamsin starts friendly and Daisuke indifferent. If a faction's attitude starts anywhere below
  friendly, Tamsin will play at the worse of the two (the schema's rule), so the company's starting attitude wants
  setting to friendly, or Tamsin's to match Daisuke's.
- **The ticket half could be a Papers handout** (`personal: true`, with the character's name in Tamsin's hand), so the
  invitation is something the players physically keep. Not added: it needs the handout pipeline's own checks.
- **The glamour dilemma's man** has grey-green scales and gold eyes, and no ancestry is named.
