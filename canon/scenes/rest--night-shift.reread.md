# Re-read: The Night Shift

version 1 · re-read against canon/reread.md version 2 · 2026-09-29 · by Claude · state: awaiting Ingram's read

A haunt, built on the approved idea for rest 10 in `canon/plan/chapter-01.md` and on the Crew Mess as the model. What
the plan promised is all kept: a quarter of maximum HP, Earn Income with Crafting on SF2E's own table, the night crew
explaining what they're taking apart, a critical failure leaving the character Fatigued until their next rest, and room
for three.

## For you, while reading

These are mine, not yet yours, and none is cited as a fact yet:

- **The regulars**: Kaede Ishimori (night foreman, works nights to be home for her daughter, keeps a notebook drawing
  every joint she has rebuilt), Joji Mabuchi (the old pit hand, a jar of every broken bolt, due to retire and not
  wanting to), Nkiru Oduya (the gantry-crane driver from the Mwangi, who sings the lifts).
- **Seven**: a Frame in the back bay that hasn't walked in years and is stripped for parts to keep the others walking,
  while the office book says it is in for a refit. It is the informant event, and it touches nothing held back.
- **The tram depot opening next week**, where a Frame will walk in front of half the city, and the office has given its
  paint more technician-days than the knee. It drives the sheet-on-the-door dilemma. Nothing founder-related is said
  about it.
- **The office asking twice how the shop would manage without a night shift** (Kaede's coercible line).
- **The Earn Income rules.** The mirror holds GM Core's rule for setting the task level and its DC, and Player Core's
  note that Crafting is one of the most reliable skills for it, but not the Earn Income activity itself or its income
  table. The scene cites the rule and the table by name and gives no figures; the paid-nothing result on a critical
  failure is the rule's own, from the Player Core text, to confirm against the book.

## The questions

- **R1, earns its place.** Each regular runs one part of the shop (the clipboard, the pit, the crane), so the three jobs
  offered in the second beat each have a person attached. Each event gives a player something to roll, decide, say or
  keep. `holdInReserve` (the tally on the pit wall) is a laugh and pays off on the pit's critical failure.
- **R2, worse without it.** The two dilemmas were checked against each other: the shortcut is the old hands' knowledge
  against the manual; the sheet is what the city sees against what keeps the machines working. Different costs,
  different people pulling each way (Nkiru tempts in one, opposes in the other).
- **R16, directions.** Three kept: "give them a moment to picture it" (Ingram's own ask), the break prompt's "take a
  moment", and the note that the pit damage comes off before the rest is applied, which the GM needs for the order of
  presses.
- **R17, negatives.** None in anything read aloud. Kaede's "your feet out of the pit unless Joji says so" is her
  character talking. The tool's note was rewritten from two negatives into "the bonus keeps until they use it".
- **R18, prepared for what can't happen.** Nothing. There is no speech; every question the regulars answer is one a
  player can ask while working.
- **R3, clean.** No decision numbers, dates, tools, files or other scenes in anything the GM reads. The walk-out note
  sends characters to their own bunks rather than naming the other rest.
- **R12, early reveals.** Checked against the held-back list: no founder is named or mentioned, nothing about why the
  Directorate came, other ships, a second rift, the Watcher, Cicada, screening or blood. Frames stay purely mechanical:
  bronze bearings, pins, cable pulleys, paraffin crack tests. The tram depot opening deliberately has no ribbon-cutter.
  Seven raises "why does the office keep it on the books?" and Joji says he doesn't know.
- **R19, answers or questions.** Seven is an answer given freely (the crews know the machines' real state), which is
  what an informant is for; the question it leaves is why the office keeps a stripped Frame on its books as "in for a
  refit". Kaede's reason for working nights and Joji's retirement stay behind attitude and deflection.
- **R20, telling them what it means.** The dilemmas' notes say "both are fair answers" for the GM only; nothing tells
  the table what the sheet or the shortcut means.
- **R4, rolls.** Every job a character does has a check: Crafting at the bench, Athletics in the pit, Perception on the
  crack, Crafting (Recall Knowledge) or Engineering Lore on the fault, Athletics or Crafting for the shortcut, Society
  for the sheet, and Crafting to Earn Income at dawn. The break, the song and the tool are chances to speak, on purpose.
- **R5, dilemmas through rolls.** Yes: the shortcut resolves on Athletics or Crafting, the sheet on Society. Refusing
  either resolves without a roll and costs the knee time.
- **R6, all four degrees.** Every roll has all four. Each failure moves the night on (Joji finishes it, the crew find it
  the long way, the manual's way anyway). Each critical failure escalates: a scrapped part on the docket, 1d6 damage and
  a reset tally, an hour lost to a pencil mark, the sheet landing on Kaede, and at dawn no pay and Fatigued.
- **R7, what they say.** Each regular has `willDiscuss`, `willNotDiscuss` with the deflecting line ("Somebody has to.",
  "Pass me that bar.", "Ask me in the spring.") and, for Kaede, a coercible topic with its check (Diplomacy at her Will
  DC, friendly or better).
- **R8, likely questions.** Five or six each, in voice, including "What's a technician-day?" and "Do we get paid?".
- **R9, stat blocks.** Missing, honestly: each regular has a proposed level and archetype, waiting for the NPC generator.
  Kaede's Will DC and Perception DC are named in checks and don't exist yet.
- **R10, if they misbehave.** In `notes`: four or more arrive, walk out, pocket a tool (Thievery against Kaede's
  Perception DC), lie about their skill (Deception against her Perception DC), look under Seven's canvas, start a fight,
  split up.
- **R15, out loud.** The read-aloud is five sentences and Kaede's line, under a minute, and reads as a person
  describing a room.
- **R21, clipped lines.** None in the read-aloud or the prompts. Short lines survive only in NPC speech, where people do
  talk that way ("Seven gives, and the others keep walking.").
- **R22, coined words.** Frame is explained in the read-aloud ("the kind of walking machine that is taller than the
  warehouses"). Technician-day is explained in `everyVisit` and in Kaede's answer; the snag book is explained where it
  appears; the paraffin crack test is explained in the event.
- **R23, length.** Matched to the Crew Mess: three regulars, four beats, ten events. It runs 10 to 20 minutes.
- **R11, retold.** The pilot's complaint about the sad goose, and "Is that a goose?" for the rest of the night; or the
  line a player sang on the big lift.
- **R13, facts agree.** Frames as walking machines taller than warehouses, purely mechanical, built, crewed and kept
  running by the Directorate, and seen by the city lifting cabbages: all as canon has them (`frames.look`,
  `frames.mechanical`, `frames.directorate-runs-them`, `frames.what-people-see`). No facts are cited in the beats yet,
  as in the model.
- **R14, time.** Day 1, night, as the plan has it: they arrive about midnight straight from the flood tunnel, break at
  two, and leave at dawn. Nothing says "yesterday" or "this morning".
- **R24, places.** No. 1 Machine Shop is on the base (the garrison region), with gantries, the pit and the Monday sheet
  as the location describes. The transition's train on the causeway follows the railway beside the causeway.
- **R25, all her detail.** Everything in the plan's line and the location's note is used: the gantries, the pit deep
  enough for a Frame's limb, the Monday allocation argued about all week, Earn Income on SF2E's table, the crew
  explaining what they take apart, Fatigued on a critical failure, room for three. From D050 and D051: quarter HP, rolls
  on all four degrees, a role-play prompt about the character's day with time to think, a dilemma that mirrors the
  city's change, an informant, attitude buttons on every event that should move someone, no plot and no danger.
