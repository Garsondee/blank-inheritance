## manako.the-sample-and-the-spill
About forty-four years ago Manako, a junior Directorate chemist, was given a shelved sample nobody senior wanted, catalogued as Inert 12. It was Sap. He cracked the capsule and spilled some on a tentacle of the Watcher that was nearby: the Sap fed it and made it visible, and Manako panicked. He knocked over a Bunsen burner and started a small fire in the lab. After the spill he lost his career for telling the truth: he was disgraced, with Shucks supervising.

## manako.what-he-wants
Manako wanted one thing, to be believed, and he was right. His need to prove the thing was real began as damage control after his disgrace: he wanted to defend the blunder that had ruined him, more than to understand it. Before Aoi died he wanted the city to see; after, he wanted the city to pay, and he stopped being particular about who helped. His tragedy is not that he's wicked. It is that he's right about the sky and wrong about what it is; that he's feeding the thing whose first hello got his wife killed; and that the party are the first people with any authority to believe him, and the ones who have to stop him.

## manako.arms-length-work
Manako works for criminals at arm's length so he never has to see what he's part of. He has never met whoever is at the top of the crime lords he works for, and doesn't know who it is. At first the crime lords' problems reach him on paper and leave as solutions, and whatever they become happens somewhere he never goes. Each job sits a little closer to what it is for, and each time he finds a reason not to look. When he finally sees it, he accepts it without enjoying it. ## manako.voice
Unhurried, precise, warm: an academic's diction worn soft by decades out of academia. Delighted by a good question, and visibly out of practice at being asked one. He talks freely about his past and never about the Watcher. He is openly bitter about the Directorate and fatherly to recruits (D045), which is part of why he makes an effective cult leader. He handles the object before he speaks: turns it over, holds it to the light, reads the back. He answers the question asked, then the better question they should have asked. He apologises for talking too much, and carries on talking. He speaks of the Directorate with perfect courtesy and no warmth whatsoever.

## manako.its-steering
The Watcher wants what it wants on its own account, but it had no personality to want it with. Drowning in a world it couldn't take in, it took the shape of the strongest coherent signal it could find, and for decades that has been one bitter man. Manako's grievance steers how it pursues its objective; the objective was never his. He is its steering, and its supply. Read backwards, its behaviour is a portrait of Manako. It grew eyes and ears above all else because he wanted to be seen and heard; it goes to loud gatherings because he was humiliated in front of a hall of colleagues; it shows you the Reel, so you know it learned from you, because a man who was disbelieved can't bear to be underestimated twice. What each of its children is doing answers to a fact about him, so the party assembles his portrait by fighting them, without knowing it.

## manako.the-echo
Manako believes he is a faithful worshipper interpreting divine intent. There is nothing to interpret: every instruction he has received was his own grievance handed back to him, wrapped round an appetite that was never his. The sentence that breaks him: he was never believed, he was echoed. The thing that confirmed him never had an opinion of him. It had an objective, and he was useful to it, as its shape and as its supply. He can't be argued out of his belief. He can only be shown, by watching the Watcher take a different shape for someone else, in front of him. He spent his life demonstrating, and a demonstration is what defeats him.

Manako's escalation is desperation, not ambition, and the Directorate winning causes it. ## manako.five-movements
Manako's arc runs in movements, each turned by something the party does. The Patient Man: serene, kind, in no hurry; he wants to be believed, and it turns when somebody finally takes him seriously. The Delighted Man: energised, generous, dangerously flattered, helping them and correcting their mistakes; he wants to be understood, and it turns when they start winning and his assets fall. The Cornered Man: brittle, out of time; he wants to finish before he dies, and it turns when he learns he was a subcontractor, not a prophet. The Frightened Man: he has begun to suspect the echo; he wants it not to be true, and only being shown turns it.

## How to play him
from: cb:faces.0

Play him with no villain beats: no gloating, and no cruelty for its own sake. He never meant any of it. Every scene with him needs careful handling, so it leaks no secret and explains nothing early (D044).

## manako.voice-by-movement
His later movements change his register and nothing else. Delighted: faster, more generous, more corrections. Cornered: shorter sentences, and he stops apologising for talking. Frightened: he talks over things.

## manako.what-not-to-do
What not to do. Don't make him mysterious at the first meeting: no significant pause, no watching them leave, no ominous parting line; he helps, and then he finishes his tea. Don't play him as insane or evil (D016); once he has accepted what he is becoming he does monstrous things, still never with relish. Don't have him lie. Don't let him recruit them on day one: he says one true thing and offers a way to reach him, and that is all. Don't have him explain the Watcher: he would explain it wrongly and sincerely, and an improvised cosmology risks putting the real answer in his mouth. Don't hand the table the conclusion: his name is free, but no one ever tells the party that Cicada has an author, or points at him (D017). Don't let his lines sound written: real speech hedges, trails off, repeats itself and reaches for the plain word, and a clever, symmetrical, self-aware turn of phrase is the failure to look for.

## manako.arc-is-the-spine
Manako's arc is the spine of the Cicada half of the campaign, and his state shapes what manifests. Defeating him, alive, doesn't end the threat: the Watcher's objective is its own. What it buys is a Watcher that stays guidable. Once Manako understands what he is, he becomes the only living being with four decades of hands-on experience shaping the swarm, and so the only one who can un-shape it. What he can change is how it pursues its objective, never the objective itself: he can keep it steerable on the road to the mitflit home world, but can't talk it out of wanting to multiply. If it happens it is redemption as labour, done in front of everyone he hurt, not forgiveness.

## manako.slide-and-movements
The slide and the movements fit together. Before play, fifteen years of arm's-length work for the crime lords let him believe he is not so bad, which is why the kindness at the tea house is real: he still thinks he is the man he used to be. In the Patient and Delighted movements the self-image holds: being taken seriously, then understood, is the best evidence in fifteen years that he is still that man. In the Cornered movement the distance goes, and he takes on being a monster as a role, without relish. In the Frightened movement he meets the one thing he can't accept. In the Useful movement he is past the role.
