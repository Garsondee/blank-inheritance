## hori.after-the-shot
After he shot the Envoy and one of his rounds hit Aoi, Hori rushed her to hospital, and couldn't save her. A court cleared Hori, and that is where he learned Aoi's name. Outside Manako's own circle, Hori is the one person who carried Aoi's name out of the court and kept it. Of the three ways to her name (Hori, the police record, a survivor of the old circle) he is the easiest, and the only one who will simply tell the party. He has been waiting ever since for somebody to ask.

## hori.dutiful
Hori is a dutiful, honourable and regretful man. The party meets him when they are ready to learn a great deal about the inciting incident. Hori's colleagues feel he went above and beyond the call, did the right thing, and is a man of great honour.

## hori.carried-alone
Hori doesn't agree with his colleagues. He has carried that day alone ever since. He is not a villain and is never played as one: an ordinary person who did an ordinary human thing, and built the rest of his life around that moment.

## hori.how-to-be-believed
What Hori gives the party, besides Aoi's name and what the Envoy was, is a way to be believed: a decorated officer telling the truth about his worst day. What saves him is simply being asked, and being believed.

## hori.knew-the-man
Whether Hori ever learned who the broken man in the street was. Probably not: he was keeping Aoi alive, and in the confusion the man was gone. If Manako was arrested as part of the court proceeding, Hori may have seen him there. Either way, no one points the party at Manako.
