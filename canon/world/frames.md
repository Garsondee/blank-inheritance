# Frames
kind: thing

## frames.what-a-frame-is
A Frame is a mech: a walking machine with a pilot inside it.
status: decided · source: D048 · known: everyone · told: start

## frames.look
A Frame is shaped roughly like a person and is taller than the warehouses it works beside. The pilot sits inside and works its arms and legs. Frames are patched, riveted and loud.
status: provisional · source: episode-c1e1--from-up-here · known: everyone · told: start

## frames.mechanical
Frames are purely mechanical: clanking machines, hard work to drive.
status: decided · source: D032 · known: everyone · told: start

## frames.directorate-runs-them
The Directorate builds its Frames, crews them and keeps them running.
status: provisional · source: episode-c1e1--from-up-here · known: everyone · told: start

## frames.clumsy
Frames are clumsy. One trying to step over a tram doesn't quite manage it.
status: provisional · source: episode-c1e1--from-up-here · known: everyone · told: start

## frames.what-people-see
Every child in Kurose can tell you the Directorate was built to save the world, but the only thing anyone has ever seen a Frame do is lift girders at a building site or help lift a crate of cabbage.
status: decided · source: D048 · known: everyone · told: start

## frames.catalogue
The catalogue the party picks its machines from is visibly built for construction work, not war: converted diggers, a crane rig, decommissioned army trucks, one inexplicable sports car and a helicopter. Every pilot may take a Frame and may take a vehicle; the two do different jobs and neither is the better choice.

## frames.repair-crews
Each pilot has their own repair crew chief, met for the first time at Familiarisation. The crews are always working round the clock, so a baseline of repair happens whatever the pilot does; a pilot who works alongside the crew gets more, and one who treats them well, going out drinking with them, may get an unasked-for night shift.

## frames.gen-1-clank-grace
The party's idea of what a Frame is comes from Gen 1: clanky, horrible-to-drive machines where you can see the pilot working, every movement a correction of the one before. That baseline is what lets a first sight of a Gen 2's grace land; soften the clank and the contrast is lost.

## frames.status-ladder
Frame service has a status ladder of its own. The base's combat-Frame corps is the prestige track; utility duty (construction, disaster response, heavy lifting) is where candidates not yet thought skilled enough for harder work are sent. It is the same undervaluing the party meets on foot in Episode One, in a machine.

## frames.sent-because-ready
The Directorate has never had enough combat-configured Frames or pilots to spare, so when the Watcher begins probing the city's defences it sends whatever is ready, utility machines included. The party is pulled into real Frame combat because their machines happened to be on hand, not because they are special: nobody decided it, the system just did what it does.

## frames.gen-2-core
Gen 2 is built from the generation ship's own technology, taken from the First Impact crash site at Kortos, which the Directorate holds and keeps restricted. It is not built from the search ship under Kurose, whose job is its call. The core of a Gen 2 is biomechanical mitflit technology: robotic parts that are living flesh melded with technology. It is not a living mitflit. The end result is that these machines aren't usable by just anyone.

## frames.mitflit-a-person
A mitflit is always a person in the story, never a vehicle or a component. A Gen 2 pilot rides inside living technology, never inside a mitflit, and nothing in a Gen 2 takes control from its pilot.

## frames.compatibility
A Gen 2 forms a seat only for a pilot who can link with it, which needs a real, specific compatibility: "the right DNA or the right something". Most humans don't have it. Mitflits parse it natively, because it is their own people's technology, and a small number of humans carry enough of the same marker, diluted over generations, to parse it too. It is a compatibility, not a species, and not always a gene: a robot, an android or a being of light can have the right something that isn't DNA. Almost nobody the Directorate could issue a Gen 2 to can fly one, which is why it stayed a small, struggling programme, mothballed once, and not a fleet-wide replacement.

## frames.sickness-per-machine
Frame Sickness comes back with each Gen 2: climbing into a different Gen 2 unit starts Slowed 1 for the first fight again, which is what keeps swapping between Gen 2 machines a bad idea even for a fully synced pilot. A Gen 1 never causes it.

## frames.never-takes-control
A Gen 2 is demanding, not wilful. It never acts on its own, never overrides its pilot and never goes berserk: Frame Sickness is the whole cost of the bond. "Like being inside a giant humanoid" describes what one is like to sit in, not a machine with a will.
status: sketch · by: ingram · source: D074, D031 · from: d-gen2:3-why-mitflits-and-why-it-isn-t-mitflits-are-mechs · known: nobody · told: later

## frames.the-empty-seat
Every Gen 2's cavity is shaped like its current pilot, and keeps that shape even in the hangar, so a crew could see a hollow shaped to a disguised pilot's true body. The crew may know; the GM never describes the hollow's shape to the table until the player reveals it. When a pilot dies, the Frame sits in the hangar holding a hollow the exact shape of a dead teenager, until it slowly re-forms for the next one.

## frames.disguise-not-sickness
The discomfort of a disguised pilot in a Gen 2 seat is not Frame Sickness: it is a borrowed body in a seat made for the real one, while Frame Sickness is the sync problem any pilot has in a Gen 2 new to them. Whether the discomfort ever has a mechanical cost is open; if it does, it must raise risk in one moment and never shrink the pilot's options.

## frames.look-of-gen-1
Gen 1 Frames look like an army of the 1950s got hold of something and did its level best. Riveted plate and boiler technology; catwalks and ladders, because human crews have to get up there; a radar dish, because they saw a sensory organ they could not comprehend and rendered it as the only remote-sensing device they knew how to build; roundels and a flag on a pole, because these are state assets and procurement insisted. The original they copied can look like anything: blue, organic, wrong, nothing like its children.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D078, D032 · from: v4:gen-1-golarion-machines · known: some · told: later

## frames.mitflit-fits-the-seat
A mitflit in a Gen 2 seat is not overriding anything: they are simply sitting in a seat built for them. That is how the city learns it may not need the children at all, and it will not concede for one second that what they gave was ever worth anything.
