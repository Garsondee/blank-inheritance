## hyuck.the-carrying
Gnolls honour their great dead by carrying their skulls. In the Carrying an elder's spirit is joined to a younger gnoll's body, a great honour for both, and the two wrestle for the body: if the young gnoll holds, the elder rides along as a voice of memory and advice; if the elder wins, it takes the body, and the young gnoll's own soul is pushed into the background.

## hyuck.never-lost
Hyuck is alive through the Carrying. He has been carried from body to body for two hundred years and has never lost the contest for a body, so he now shares his body with a crowd of other gnolls' spirits, which is maddening.

## hyuck.how-to-play
Hyuck is spiritual without being religious, a gnoll who stumbles through life and simply never quite falls down: too dim to be argued out of a belief, loyal to friends, committed to everything he does, fast and violent when action is called for, and lucky in a pinch. His old player's own list of traits that make him himself. Hyuck is loud, lucky, loyal and impossible to argue with: too stubborn to change his mind, fast and violent in a fight, with a talent for offending everyone eventually. He is a crowd, not a split personality: now and then another voice answers for him, argues with him mid-sentence, or remembers something from a life he didn't live. He brags that he has never lost, which is also the saddest fact about him, since every win pushed somebody to the back.

## hyuck.statue-as-described
The harbour statue is a fearsome gnoll barbarian, posed on guard. If the stone were flesh, its face would be foaming at the mouth, tongue lolling between oversized teeth; yet a great deal of sculptural effort has gone into giving that feral face some dignity, and the guard stance and the formal presentation are doing real work against what the face is doing. Whether the statue succeeds at being dignified is for the players to decide. It is described with the guard pose and the feral face given equal weight, and no narration or NPC settles it; a passer-by may shrug or give a personal opinion, never the city's. The statue stands in the harbour square as ordinary civic furniture, not a hushed monument: kids climb it and pigeons sit on it. Asked about it, most people say it has always been there.

## hyuck.old-campaign-portrait
In the old campaign Hyuck's constant quest was to find his father by scent, with intentions best described as unclear. He was convinced he could follow the trail alone, and found how many strange places his father had gone, always one step ahead. His trunk of ancestral bones held no mementoes of his father. Hyuck was superstitious even for a gnoll: he muttered to unseen ancestral spirits, especially his grandmother, left a space beside him when he sat, and kept a trunk of his ancestors' bones and five tiny white skulls on a cord. He paused before entering rooms and sniffed the air as if afraid, and his fur never grew properly round his right shoulder.
