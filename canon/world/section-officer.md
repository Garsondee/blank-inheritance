# The Section Officer
kind: person

## section-officer.vetch
Section Officer Vetch runs Section Nine. He is never wrong in his own account.

## section-officer.hazing
Vetch sends every new section on a fool's errand because it was done to him, and he believes it builds character. The same was done to Manako, decades ago.

## section-officer.voice
Vetch is clipped, procedural and faintly aggrieved. He uses the passive voice about anything that went wrong and the first person about anything that went right. He says "as discussed" about things that were not discussed, answers a question by reading the relevant clause aloud, and calls the party "Section Nine", never by their names, not even individually, treating that as neutral rather than cold. His sample line: "I was doing this when you were at school. I'm aware some of you still are."

## section-officer.takes-the-credit
Vetch takes the credit when the party wins his errand, and buries them in a fortnight of legitimate, miserable work.

## section-officer.unredeemed
Vetch is tedious, self-important and small, mediocre and comfortable. He isn't cruel or dangerous, and he is not a hard man teaching hard lessons. He never wins on the merits, never harms them, and is never redeemed: no late scene where he was secretly proud of them all along. He is still there at the end of the campaign, doing the same to the next intake, unredeemed.

## section-officer.middling-and-never-wrong
Vetch is middling in rank and middle-aged, with immaculate paperwork: the only thing he is unambiguously good at, and he knows it. He has total authority over everything between reveille and lights out, and none over anything that matters; the second fact is why the first is exercised so thoroughly. Vetch is never wrong in his own account. When the party does something he didn't expect, he pauses exactly one beat and then recasts it as the intention all along. A player who spots the pattern can get him to claim a plan in front of somebody, and he is then committed to it.

## section-officer.not-dangerous
Vetch cannot get the party hurt, sent away or charged. The worst he can do is make a fortnight tedious, and he will. His weapons are technically permitted duties and awful-but-real army tasks: never sabotage, never a crime, never cruelty.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D075, D018 · from: b-char:the-section-officer.what-s-established, b-char:the-section-officer.the-arc, npc:section-officer · known: some · told: later

## section-officer.the-hazing
Vetch knows he was foiled over the vial, and has it in for the party however gracefully they handled it, even if they try to save his face. His punishment is personal rather than procedural cover: he keeps ramping up the horrible duties because he doesn't like them. The hazing is a repeated exchange, not one clean win: every fresh assignment is also a chance to score a point back by malicious compliance, by doing the awful task so well it turns on him, or by using his own procedure against him. It is always full-party work, so that six strangers become a cohesive unit and nobody sits idle.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D075, D018, D082 · from: b-char:the-section-officer.the-arc, npc:section-officer · known: few · told: later

## section-officer.ongoing-problem
Vetch stays an ongoing annoyance for the start of the adventure while being the party's primary point of contact with the Directorate for many early events. He turns up in person at the end of tasks to collect, assess and push the party toward what is next. There is no plan to defeat him yet.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D075 · from: b-char:the-section-officer.the-arc, b-char:the-section-officer.appearances-so-far · known: some · told: later

## section-officer.promoted-out
The party is eventually promoted out of Vetch's section by proving him wrong and outperforming his expectations: they do something he said couldn't be done in front of someone senior, and he claims it was his plan. He is not removed, corrected or improved. Shucks's quiet word is why the win sticks.

## section-officer.out-of-his-depth
Something real happens and Vetch is out of his depth in front of the party. He doesn't fail heroically; he has no idea and defers. It is quiet, and the party notices.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D075 · from: b-char:the-section-officer.the-arc · known: nobody · told: later

## section-officer.the-transmission-chain
It was done to Manako forty years ago, in a Directorate lab; it was done to Vetch; he does it to the party. Three people, the same institutional shrug, and it broke one of them. The difference is not character: nobody handed Vetch a century-old sample and then blamed him for what happened. Nobody says it aloud; the party may notice Manako was hazed the same way, and nobody points at it.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D075, D018 · from: b-char:the-section-officer.why-he-exists, b-char:the-section-officer.the-transmission-chain, npc:section-officer · known: nobody · told: later

## section-officer.has-done-every-job
Vetch has done every job he hands out, properly, and knows what a bad one looks like. He tests a surface with a thumbnail rather than looking at it. He cannot let a wrong statement about procedure stand, and an appeal made correctly in writing gets further with him than one made out loud.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D075 · from: npc:section-officer · known: few · told: later

## section-officer.watched-from-above
Somebody well above Vetch has taken an interest in his section and he doesn't know who; he is frightened of exactly what the party is curious about. He has never heard of Rand, which is why the override humiliates him.
