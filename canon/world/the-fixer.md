## the-fixer.his-dead-crew
The Fixer's crew died in an industrial accident on the waterfront, signed off and filed by the Directorate within a fortnight, and nobody read the file. He spent years seeking an inquest through the correct channels, with no reply. Then a shabby old chemist read all of it, start to finish, in one sitting, and asked three questions about the third page: "He's the first man who ever read my paperwork." That man was Manako. The Fixer has been quietly diverting Cicada's money to the families of his dead crew for years, through routes he built and audits himself. Manako doesn't know. It will look appalling when it surfaces, probably at the worst moment, to people inclined to sympathy until they see the numbers.

## the-fixer.audit
The way to beat the Fixer is an audit: the one fight he can't win. Beating him with bookkeeping unravels his whole network without a fight, and that is a legitimate way to win: tell the players so. An audit that unravels his network would also start a paper trail into the crime lords' organisation.

## the-fixer.what-he-wants
The Fixer is the reason the movement works: venues, crates, permits, the crime partners, the timing. He takes no pleasure in it and is exceptionally good at it. What he wants is for it to keep working. His want, beyond the movement, is the district the Directorate wrote off. He will explain it to a kid once, calmly.

## the-fixer.how-he-fights
The Fixer is twenty-five years of moving heavy things, and fights like it: a cargo hook, a length of chain, a crowbar, and a working knowledge of how much everything in the room weighs. Shoves, trips, grapples; he puts people into the floor and the floor into people, on ground he has worked since he was sixteen, so the winch, shutter, stacked crates and gantry are his to reach. He changes the battlefield while hitting people.

## the-fixer.voice
The Fixer is unfailingly polite, faintly apologetic and unhurried even mid-fight. He knows everybody's name, asks after their families, and means it. He is the only person in the campaign who has read the regulations, all of them. His line, about anything he has arranged, is "It's already been signed for", and it is usually true.
