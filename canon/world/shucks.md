## shucks.who-rand-is
Rand is Shucks's detached right hand: a free agent, like Anabala, that doesn't speak, flies on a kite and trusts nobody. Its name is Rand, from Ingram's "Right hand... Rand... Get it?" She also offered "Randy"; the paperwork says Rand. It can't talk normally: it writes, points and taps. It keeps the only honest paper archive on the base. It writes in Shucks's old handwriting, works in ink and chalk, never types and never goes through the phones, so nothing it has found has passed through the technology the Watcher reads: it is the one investigator in Kurose the Watcher has never read.

## shucks.rand-the-override
Rand did what Shucks wouldn't. It countersigned the party's requisition and their roster, wrote the Medical order that put the six on Frame and vehicle familiarisation (a fitness familiarisation, which a medical department can order and nobody can refuse), and chalked "Same six. Tomorrow." on the board to keep them together. The names on the override are in the order the six went through Intake Medicals, which is the order their files sit in Medical's cabinet; a player who remembers the queue can notice.

## shucks.rand-conspiracy
Rand is a conspiracy theorist, investigating what is actually happening in the city entirely on its own initiative. Its theory is right as far as it goes: something in the city is wrong and getting worse. It doesn't know what and doesn't suspect the Watcher; nobody on the base does. It has been collecting the evidence on paper for years. Rand hurries because of its own paper trail: something in the city is wrong and getting worse, and Gen 2 carriers are the most valuable people on the base. Shucks would do nothing, because a founder openly steering six trainees draws the attention the marker mustn't get, and the hand has no position to protect.

## shucks.found-the-marker
Shucks ran the medical clearance for the original Gen 2 trial pilots, years ago, and kept better notes than anyone else involved, because that is how he works. When the programme nearly folded he went back through them and found what nobody had correlated: the few pilots who succeeded all shared a marker on a screen most medical staff would have filed as noise.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D074, D020, D021 · from: d-gen2:4-shucks-s-testing-programme · known: nobody · told: later

## shucks.screen-and-silence
Shucks now runs the blood screen on every candidate who comes through intake, as a matter of course, under the Directorate's general authority to flag "irregularities": the same field, the same tick-box, the same flat delivery every candidate gets. He is forbidden to say what "irregular" means, for an ordinary reason: the Directorate doesn't want anyone to know who its compatible pilots are before it does. He thinks the rule is stupid, because he has watched it upset young people for years and never once seen it protect anything; that he is forbidden at all is the sign the silence is the institution's, not his.

## shucks.skeleton-and-voice
Shucks is literally an animated skeleton in a white coat and stethoscope, not a figure of speech. He started as a rogue with real medical skill and became Dr Shucks Mordant on the strength of it. His "two hundred years old" is not an idiom either. Shucks is warm, unhurried and deadpan. He gives instructions as single words ("Arm. Light. Breathe."), asks a question and actually waits, gives practical instructions before kind ones and means both, and compares candidates to relatives he processed decades ago. He is kind about things that are dangerous to know and honest that he is forbidden to say more: he won't explain what the blood flag means, thinks the rule is stupid, and shows both. Being a skeleton doesn't change the register: a kind, unsettling-looking mentor, never a horror element.

## shucks.hand-detached
Some while ago Shucks put his hand into a beam of powerful necromantic energy. The hand survived by becoming its own thing: small, independent, commandable and, as an emergent property of whatever that beam was, able to fly using a kite. It doesn't know it's supposed to be his. He is genuinely unworried: it always comes back eventually, once hungry, the way a stroppy cat does.

## shucks.rand-ways-to-find-out
Who Rand is can be learned four ways, any one enough: noticing Shucks's prosthetic and then reading the name "Rand"; putting the countersignature beside anything a founder signed two hundred years ago (the handwriting is Shucks's); catching it at work in Hangar Two at night or coming home to Medical when hungry (a hand on a kite string); or showing Shucks the signature, when he says "That's my hand", meaning it literally, and will not explain further. No roll reveals it in Episode Two: the checks give the name on a success and the department on a critical success. No NPC explains it at the table.

## shucks.processed-grandparents
Shucks has processed intakes for long enough to have taken blood from a candidate's grandparents, and says so without making anything of it. Pressed on how long, he answers honestly and immediately, which is more disconcerting than evasion. He talks about students across a span of years that doesn't add up without noticing: he isn't hiding his age, it has never occurred to him that it is remarkable.

## shucks.why-he-works
Shucks is the campaign's proof that an institution two centuries old still contains people: the first adult the party meets who treats them as people rather than intake, forbidden from telling them the one thing they most want to know, and saying so rather than lying. Everything unsettling about him is cosmetic and everything reassuring is real, the inverse of the Directorate he helped found.
