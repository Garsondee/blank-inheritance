# Sap
kind: thing

## Pencil

## sap.what-it-is
Sap is the generation ship's stock of raw material for its repair nanites. It isn't fuel, and it has nothing to do with exotic matter. The Watcher's children are made of Sap.

## sap.grows-the-watcher
Every use of Sap makes the Watcher permanently bigger: the party's own included, every demonstration, and every day the column runs.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D071 · from: cb:words.24, cb:words.27 · known: nobody · told: later

## sap.inert-12
The Directorate catalogued a shelved sample of Sap as "Inert 12": noble, non-reactive, useless. The sample is about a century old.

## sap.makes-it-visible
Sap is the chemical that makes the Watcher visible: on a treated lens, or splashed onto it.

## sap.released-where-children-form
Each Cicada demonstration releases Sap into a crowd, and in Book I the Watcher's children can form only where Sap has been released.

## sap.catalogued-at-kortos
The first trace of Sap was catalogued at the Kortos site about 120 years ago as Inert 12, and shelved, until a junior chemist named Manako was handed it about 45 years ago. It was the generation ship's own stock of building material for its repair nanites.

## sap.cost-of-seeing
Exposure to Sap makes a person permanently easier for the Watcher to see. Every revelation is paid for by being watched more closely.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D072 · from: d-observer:11-sap · known: nobody · told: later

## sap.the-name
Cicada calls the chemical Sap, and eventually everyone does. Cicada nymphs drink sap for years underground before they emerge; Manako chose the name deliberately and has never explained the joke.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D078 · from: v4:8-the-chemical · known: some · told: later

## sap.last-sample
Sap is extraordinarily rare, and the Directorate knows it. The last remaining sample walked out of the lab in Manako's pocket the day he lost his job, and nobody bothered reporting it stolen because it wasn't interesting enough. That single sample is what he reverse-engineered everything from.
