The Faithful's non-resistance is doctrine, not weakness. ## cicada.public-face-and-hidden-core
Cicada is a public religious movement with a clandestine core, and a real antagonist faction. Its people call it Cicada, and it never lies. In public it doesn't hide and doesn't recruit by deception, and when asked it doesn't lie about what it wants: its whole purpose is to make people look. It shows a true thing and lets the viewer's own reason finish the job, which is why it catches lawyers, scientists and good citizens; the pitch asks for nothing objectionable: this is here, and it must be considered. Most of its members are guilty of nothing but having seen something real and believed their own eyes. Behind that it is a revolutionary, clandestine organisation with a mission, and assets it will use force to protect; what it keeps hidden is the column, the money and the criminal logistics.

## cicada.demonstrations
A Cicada demonstration is a release of Sap in several crowded public places at once, and the public reads it as a chemical attack. But nobody dies at a demonstration: Sap does no harm, and the only injuries come from the crowd moving. That is the first clue a careful investigator can find: a terror campaign with no dead isn't a terror campaign.

## cicada.the-show
A Cicada show is a performance for a hand-picked audience. The eyes and tentacles appear and the audience recoils, taking it for a horror piece, which is unusual but not unheard of. Then they are given proof that it is real, and has been in the room the whole time. After every show the audience is given a real choice: leave and tell people what they saw, or stay and learn more. Nobody is trapped. Most walk out wanting to know more, and wanting to know more is how you enter the cult; most of those who stay do so because they were persuaded, not because they couldn't leave.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D073, D081 · from: d-cicada:the-show-itself · known: few · told: in:chapter-06

## cicada.recurring-four
The four lieutenants carry the contact frequency, since Manako appears in person perhaps four times: they arrive, announce themselves, wreck something, fight a little and leave. Each has one gimmick, one want, an escape the party watches happen, and a counter that is fixed before the first appearance, so each is a locked door with a keyhole and not an authorial veto. The party defeats them when the story is ready, and not before. The Soldier is the worthy opponent and the Fixer the aide with the real complaint; the Young One is the escalation, and works because the other three have a code and he has none.

## cicada.not-the-first-fight
In Chapter 1 the party meets the Faithful and the criminal contractors, not the Cadre. The pattern they should learn is that believers don't resist and contractors do: true, and incomplete. The first real Cadre fight (the column raid) recontextualises it, and doesn't contradict it. Cadre fights should offer a real non-lethal resolution (enemies who disengage or can be dropped and secured) without the Cadre pulling punches on the way in.

## cicada.misreads-gen-2
Cicada's theology reads the Watcher's attention as meaningful, so when the Watcher fixes on the new Gen 2 Frames it concludes the fixation is significant and then dangerous. It watches the thing it reveres reach for its makers' technology, reads divine alarm, and decides the new machines are blasphemous, dangerous or a rival power's doing, never landing near the truth. It is exactly as rational as its founder's worldview allows.
