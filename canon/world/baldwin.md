## baldwin.the-hunters
In the old campaign Baldwin led the hunters who captured mitflit savants for their minds, and ran the Baldwin family's magical-weapons manufactory at Baldwin Manor, once Croglang Castle, at Volkheed on Kortos. His note, carried by the mage Regina Key and signed "WCB", read: "Mitflits are there in force now, including many 'savants' ripe for the taking. I am gathering forces for a procurement operation." The last the old table saw of Baldwin was an army prepared on the Gauntlight map, hunters, infantry, archers and mobs, set against the mitflits in the keep.

## baldwin.the-grudge
At Baldwin Manor an Inicrium battery pulled the Shadow Plane into the town and a shadow siege followed, in which members of Baldwin's own family were killed by accident, not by the four. He blamed the four anyway: "These criminals broke into my ancestral home... That's my fucking horse's leather on that Gnoll." (Hyuck had paid a thief to steal a hunter's horse and had its hide made into armour.)

## baldwin.the-organisation-in-kurose
The crime lord Manako works for, at arm's length, is Baldwin's organisation: an arms trade can keep a gifted chemist solving problems on paper for years without his ever seeing a weapon. Baldwin is the crime lord Manako works for, neither fully knowing the other. Nobu's laundering, the Fixer's crime partners and the heist crews all run through his organisation. The mitflits arriving in Kurose to go home together are poor, badly housed and eager to be useful, which makes them the easiest prey his workshops have ever had. The weapons are built from ship technology that leaked out of the Kortos site.

## baldwin.hunter-band
The hunters William Charles Baldwin led were Aubert "Golden", Kirkham, Thompkins, McGuire, Connor, Campbell, Hunniford and Shackley, with hired lackeys and a mage, Regina Key, in his pay. They were rough outdoors types who went from tavern to tavern in Otari making loud pronouncements about Gauntlight, and Baldwin himself struck the killing blow on a gnoll in the woods.
