## era.engines
Magic is still part of everyday life, small and cheap, but the great works of this age are built with engines: steam and oil, then electricity; railways across continents, steamships that don't wait for the wind, and wires carrying voices between cities.

## Pencil

## era.golarion-1950s
The setting is Golarion in its own 1950s-style retrofuturistic branch, with light, specific touches of real Golarion (temples of Pharasma, real ancestry terms, real place names such as Kortos and Absalom) where they serve the story, and no obligation to follow the rest.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D077 · from: ie:golarion-grounding-touches · known: everyone · told: later

## era.dug-up-not-invented
Ordinary society stayed at roughly a 1950s level while giant machines and out-of-era devices exist because the advanced technology was dug out of the two crashed ships, not invented: it is narrow and scarce, only partly understood even by the Directorate, and kept gated, which is why only trained pilots touch it. The phones run on the same ship-derived substrate, which is the only way anything with a phone's capability could be built in this era. The Directorate keeps the original ship's technology highly restricted to stop it destabilising the world, but it still leaks outwards from Kortos.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D077, D032, D034 · from: ie:gap-derived-tech-disparity · known: few · told: later

## era.kortos-cataclysm
Where the old campaign's Kortos Cataclysm fits, if anywhere, is not decided. In the old campaign's lore it was an Inicrium explosion about a thousand years before 4721 AR, so roughly twelve hundred years before the present, that left part of Kortos uninhabitable: a different event from both the Baldwin Manor battery and First Impact. (The search ship's "thousand years after the generation ship" is a different span, not the same thousand years.)

## era.fifties-feel
The setting reads as the 1950s, specifically of Japan: red-roofed old-town streets terraced up a cliff on one side, smokestacks and a more industrial waterfront on the other, aqueduct-like structures threading the city, and a sea-fort out in the channel, joined to the shore by a long causeway at the point through which the city's seaborne wealth passes. It is magic and fey beside industrial capitalism, giant machines and teenage drama, all at once. Ordinary society runs on roughly 1950s industrial capitalism, with tall sailing ships beside modern harbour craft, while Frame technology is narrow, scarce and institutionally controlled, derived from what was dug up. The two sit together in one harbour, and a Frame is far more advanced than the street it works in.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D074, D081 · from: arc:setting-tone-1950s-historical-divergence, arc:corrections-to-the-design-bible-recorded-here-rather-than-edited-into-it · known: everyone · told: later

## era.loosely-golarion
This world started as Golarion, but Ingram changed the mitflits, then Kortos island, then Abadar, so it is loosely based on Golarion and not a slave to it. The passage of time has changed much, and one of the most important events is First Impact.
status: sketch · by: ingram · source: D078, D004 · from: ledger:4-this-world-s-relationship-to-golarion · known: nobody · told: later

## era.magic-draining
Magic is slowly draining from Golarion, and industry has grown into the vacuum. That is the whole reason this world went from medieval to smokestacks in two centuries, and every long-lived character (Anabala, Shucks, Rupiah) has personally watched it happen. It explains the age, not the rift.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D078, D040 · from: v4:the-sit-in · known: few · told: later
