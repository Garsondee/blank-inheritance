## shinobu.the-lorry
Shinobu Kudo, the girl in the news, is hit by a lorry in front of a crowd and walks away. By evening she's in custody again, jailed in a glass cell. After the lorry, being jailed again within the hour enrages everyone who fought to free her. From outside the Directorate now looks like an institution that jails road-accident victims: it makes no sense, and looks monstrous, and Cicada sympathisers and ordinary members of the public agree about that for once.

## shinobu.most-watched
Shinobu is the most witnessed person in the city, and nobody involved in that is being cruel. The Watcher finds its way into her glass cell deep underground to watch her. She is the most-watched person in the city. She was an ordinary bystander, handled by the Directorate, her father and the crowd that once nearly killed her in ways that are individually reasonable and collectively monstrous. She knows more than almost anyone alive about what it feels like to be completely seen, and can say something about it that nobody else can; she is entirely willing to talk. Her name means to endure, and also to hide. She belongs to the Observed thread, alongside the stray and the Mass Clock: the throughline about what it costs to be seen.

## shinobu.the-colony
Splashed point-blank with Sap, Shinobu carries a colony of the Watcher's nanites, and she is its favourite subject. She survived the lorry because of what she carries: the colony reinforces and repairs her, and she is very slowly becoming something. Part of the Watcher lives in her, so she is held in its attention more or less continuously, not glimpsed occasionally.

## shinobu.the-splash
Shinobu was splashed point-blank by Sap at one of Cicada's demonstrations. The physical result was mild skin irritation and nothing else. At that range the swarm had enough of it on her and in her to build a colony there, and the colony stayed: a dense population of the Watcher's own nanomachines, qualitatively different from the glimpse-and-fade exposure most witnesses get.

## shinobu.detained
The Directorate held Shinobu in military lockdown at once, and their reasoning wasn't stupid: independent scientists had confirmed Sap inert, but the Directorate knew the only place Sap is found is inside an alien ship, and knew what it reveals. They detained a teenage girl on the working assumption that she might become something. Months of nothing followed: no symptoms, no incident. She was released under mounting public pressure from her father Kudo, who is very good at his job and very loud about it. Surveillance continued after release, and confirmed, repeatedly, nothing.
status: sketch · by: claude · source: D075, D081 · from: d-shinobu:3-detention, d-shinobu:4-release-and-renewed-surveillance · known: some · told: later

## shinobu.the-cell
Shinobu is held in a deep underground cell with glass walls, cameras on every face, and remote operators watching in shifts, permanently. The Watcher finds its way in anyway, to look at its favourite subject: its visits are surveillance, since nothing in the city is more worth watching than a colony of its own growing inside a person. Two watchers on one cell: the institution's cameras and the Watcher's attention. There is no cruelty in it anywhere: the Directorate is being careful, the operators are doing their jobs, the Watcher is only looking. She is going nowhere and is entirely willing to talk: a point-blank eyewitness the party can simply visit. What she would say is not scripted.

## shinobu.peril
Shinobu is not scheduled to die. The jeopardy is that the colony is mishandled, by the Directorate or by well-meaning rescuers; she is saved by the party understanding what she actually is before acting.
