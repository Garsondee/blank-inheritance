# D061 · 2026-10-05 · A check is a pill in the scene's words that opens the check console; the Runner's check card is gone

asked: Ingram sent a screenshot of a beat's check card in the Scene Runner (the check's name, its skill and DC, Rolled blind, Post to chat, four outcome rows) and proposed a new approach: the check as a pill in the prose that opens a dedicated window for the GM, with a window of their own for each player.

## Ingram

> "Suggested new approach for the Scene Runner: When a scene includes a skill check we put it all into the same pill shaped cartouche with the dice symbol. The idea is that I'm reading about something and then I find a section of text which might say "... it's at that moment you realise you could bluff or intimidate your way out of here..." and the bluff or intimidate is a hyperlink in the pill which opens up a new dialogue which controls the DC setting and collecting area. We create a dedicated UI that opens up when a skill check is required, we can use it to alter the DC, change the skills that could be asked for, create new random rolls up on the fly and most importantly of all it contains wonderful little gem LED lights which represent the players rolls and as they come in the LED changes to different colors for the result (crit fail, fail, succeed, crit succeed). When this dialogue appears the players will see their own version of it which allows them to pick which thing to roll and doesn't give them the GM level controls but allows them to also see the results as they come in. This is an important component, it needs to look like the north star UI visually and it means we can remove these clunky things from the scene runner which means the scene runner becomes visually a lot cleaner. This new UI is also perfect for me to be able to give the players the 'result' text for each roll so once we get all the results in I can then see what I need to tell the players then a button to close it down. This records everything automatically."
>
> "Notice I said "bluff and intimidate" so the dialogue is per challenge, but allows a range of different solutions to that challenge."
>
> "Don't reinvent the wheel. We've worked out a lot of the digetic UI stuff in the main UI. Follow the recipe, but make the result LEDs really pretty please."

## Settled

1. **A check is a pill in the beat's own words**, with a die, the ways it can be met, its DC (an eye struck through when it is rolled blind) and a small gem for each result in. Pressing it opens the check console. A person's conversation check is the same pill on their card.
2. **The check console is one window per challenge, offering every way it can be met.** On the GM's screen: the DC on a knob with a printed scale (a click on either half, the wheel or the arrow keys turn it, and one key puts back the scene's own DC), Blind and Show DC on rockers, the ways as pill keys (each can be taken off, and any other skill added from a second knob), a gem for each player with what they rolled and four keys to set a result by hand, who is asked, and what each degree means with who it landed on.
3. **Each player sees their own version**: the ways as their character's own skill keys, each carrying the number they would roll with it, and everyone's gems as the results come in. No controls of the GM's, and nothing that names the check or says what a degree means.
4. **The gems are cut stones in brass collars, lit from inside**: ruby for a critical failure, topaz for a failure, emerald for a success, a twinkling diamond for a critical success, smoked quartz while a player is still to roll, and a smooth pearl for a roll only the GM may read.
5. **A check can be made up on the spot** from the Runner's Check key or the Palette, with any ways and any DC.
6. **Close and record** closes every window; the log keeps one line saying the check, its DC and how each player did. Each result is kept on the beat as it lands, as a roll from chat always was.
7. **The Runner's check card is gone**: its post key, outcome buttons, per-player rows, list of rolls, by-skill notes and tally all live in the console now, along with the row's DC chip.

## Claude's readings, marked

- **Where the pill goes when the words do not say**: at the words that ask for it ("offer the check", "post the check"), at an authored `<chk>words</chk>` in a beat's line, or at the end of the line. Episode One's lines have no `<chk>` yet; its checks say "the check".
- **Who is asked**: every player with a character and anyone else who is here, the same rule as the choice prompt; the GM takes anyone off with their Asked pill. A player who is not asked sees the check and cannot roll it.
- **A blind check** (sf2e's secret trait, D058) shows its players neither its DC nor any result: their gems light as pearls. An open check shows its DC by default, and the GM can hide it.
- **What the GM marks as the outcome** is the beat's one recorded degree, which its branches read; the first result in sets it, as the first roll from chat did, and a press on any degree's key changes it.
- **One check is open at a time**: opening another closes and records the first.
- **A made-up check starts at DC 15** (SF2E's simple DC for a trained task) with no ways until the GM adds one.
- **Post to chat stays** as a key in the console, for anyone without their window: a roll from that card still lights its gem.
- **How often each degree has come out before** (the tally Ingram asked for on 2026-09-19) is printed on each degree's row.

No fact of the world changes.
