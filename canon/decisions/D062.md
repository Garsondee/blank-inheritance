## Settled

1. **A way is added from a dropdown, not a knob.** "Add a way…" lists Perception, every skill and the three saves, in those groups; picking one puts its pill among the ways.
2. **A pill's label on black has a dark shadow under it.** The pale offset shadow belongs to a pill on the light plate, where it seats the word; on black it was the ghost that blurred "Perception".
3. **The deck's keys have tactile hardware**: a raised nub in each lower corner, either side of the pips, and a ribbed grip down each side beside the number, on every key of the main deck (and the same keys in a player's check window). Nothing sits on the legend's line, so a long one ("Intimidation", "Performance") reads in full.
4. **Blind is a switch that says which way it is set.** "Roll: Open | Blind", each half with its own lamp; Blind lights purple, and a purple Blind tag stands in the window's head (the player's too). Beside it, "DC: Shown | Hidden", held at Hidden while the roll is blind.
5. **The DC dial shows how hard the DC is for the party.** GM Core's DC by level and its difficulty steps (incredibly easy −10, very easy −5, easy −2, standard, hard +2, very hard +5, incredibly hard +10, from `reference/tables/dc-and-check-math.json`) are coloured bands round the knob's scale, with a notch at the level's standard DC. The readout under the knob gives the DC, its step and the level it is measured against: "13 · Easy −2 · Lv 1". The level is the party's, read from the players' characters; today everyone is level 1.
6. **"What it means" is now "Results", and a result can differ per way and per degree.** A way's entry in a check's `bySkill` is either one line (as before, shown as a note over the rows) or its own result for any degree; a degree a way leaves out is the check's own. The schema, the Results tabs (one per way, when the check says anything per way) and the scene edit sheet (a field per degree) all carry it. **No check has results per way and per degree written yet.**
7. **The results lights are a bank of six tall rectangular lights across the bottom of the window**, dark until a result comes in, then lit with a flare: red for a critical failure, orange for a failure, blue for a success, green for a critical success, and purple for a hidden roll. Every player's window has the same bank, so each result is seen by the table as it lands. D061's gems are gone, from the console and from the pill, whose small lights use the same five colours.

This replaces D061's settled points 2 (the second knob that added a way, and the rockers) and 4 (the gems).

## Claude's readings, marked

- **The GM sees a hidden roll's true colour**, with a purple "Hidden" tag on its light; only the players see it purple. The purple is there to conceal the result from the table, and the GM has to know it to say what happens.
- **The party's level** is the highest level among the players' characters; with none assigned, the party's members; with neither, 1.
- **A DC between two steps** reads as the nearer one, and a tie reads toward standard (DC 16 at level 1 is standard, not hard). Past the last step it is that step.
- **The six lights go to the first six people** asked or rolling. A seventh would have no light; their result is still recorded and named under Results.
- **"(the same for every way)"** is printed under a degree's result only when some way has results of its own per degree; a check whose ways each have one line has nothing to set it apart from.
- **The flare plays once**, when a light first comes on, not on every redraw, and never for anyone whose system asks for reduced motion.
- **The switches sit in the Ways cell** and the DC shares one readout with its difficulty, so the GM's window fits a laptop screen; on a shorter screen the window scrolls.
- **The purple is the light's own** (#8a3dff), not the Claimant ring's violet.
