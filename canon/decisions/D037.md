# D037 · 2026-09-25 · The opening titles may flash villains and surprises, the Watcher included; the outro waits

source: split word for word from LEDGER #37

**Ingram, 2026-09-25, while commissioning the campaign's opening title sequence:** an anime-style opening that homages a famous 1995 mecha series' opening, cut to that opening's own song and cast with this campaign's characters, played at the start of every session. It is never released publicly. Authoritative over #34 and the rule that the Watcher is unseen at the start, **for that one piece of work only**.

**Ingram, verbatim:**

> "Also for your planning information I don't mind if we show characters for short bursts who are bad guys or who are surprises. We may have a glimpse of The Watcher during this for example."
>
> "Don't worry about the outro yet. That will be a single looping video file with credits over the top. We'll do that after."
>
> From the commission itself: "We're going to cut to the characters in the story that we've got so far. Read the lore first, ours is a different story." And: "This will never be released publicly and is just meant as a fun and memorable way to start the episodes."

**What this settles:**

1. **The opening titles are an exception to #34's no-contamination rule and to the rule that the Watcher is unseen at the start.** They may show antagonists and surprise characters **in short bursts**, the Watcher included, the way an anime opening flashes figures the story hasn't reached yet.
2. **Nothing else changes.** Scenes, handouts, read-aloud and player-facing text all stay under #34. Inside the fiction the Watcher is still unseen and unwitnessed at the start. A glimpse in the opening is something the players see but can't place; no character in the story has witnessed it.
3. **The outro is one looping video with credits over the top,** made after the opening.

**Claude's reading of the scope — say if you meant it wider:**

- **"Short bursts" means flash-length:** a few frames to about a second, the length the source opening gives its own flashes.
- **Characters and images, not facts.** The exception covers showing a figure. On-screen words still say only what the players may know, so no title card names a secret. The source opening also flashes words at the viewer (a cataclysm's name, a mysterious single name); whether ours may tease a secret in text is not covered by the answer above, and stays closed until asked.

**Filled in by Claude — proposals, veto freely:**

- **How the Watcher is glimpsed.** As #36 describes it when seen: eyes and tentacles in the sky, never explained and never named on screen. The opening then agrees with what the party eventually sees through a treated lens, and gives away nothing about why.
- **Re-cutting at the Book breaks.** Anime openings are re-cut between seasons. The opening can be updated when the story reaches a new Book, so later characters and glimpses arrive in their own time.

**Filed to:** `design/opening-titles.md` (new: the production plan); `ideas/story/dossier-observer.md` §20 (the exception noted beside the running rule); Claude's standing memory notes on the Watcher and on no contamination; this ledger.

**Also answered the same day (#37): who the opening shows.** Claude asked four questions before casting it. Ingram, verbatim:

> **The six PCs** (they're made at induction, after the first opening has played): "We can use 6 temporary images and then when I have the real ones I'll swap them in as you've suggested and then once we have them you can rebuild the video with the finished content. As you pointed out too we will probably update the video between 'Books' of content to keep it fresh."
>
> **The lead shots** (the source gives about 25 seconds of single-face close-ups to one lead): "One lead beat each" — split across the six, so every player gets a lead close-up in every cut.
>
> **The credits:** "The real table" — the people at the table, in Japanese credit style.
>
> **The founders** (the city's paintings show four handsome humans; the real four are a gnoll, an elf, a tiny mitflit and a skeleton): "We show just the real characters, we can also cut to dramatic shots of events from the first adventure."

**What this settles:**

1. **The opening shows the four founders as they really are, never the city's painting,** and it may cut to dramatic shots of events from the first adventure.
2. **The six appear first as six temporary images**, replaced with the real characters once they exist. The opening is re-cut then, and refreshed at each Book break.
3. **Each of the six gets one lead close-up per cut. The credits name the real table.**

**Claude's reading — say if you meant otherwise:** the first adventure's events are shown as the four players remember them, **never with anything that ties them to the rift's cause**, which stays GM-only.

**What this settles:**

1. **The real Shucks, skeleton and all, is in the opening from the first cut.** This settles the open choice in the correction below.
2. **A Gen 2 glimpse is allowed** (a head of living flesh grown through machinery).
3. **The two sketch slots go to Baldwin and to a mysterious character** of Claude's design.
4. **The stray gets a glimpse.**
5. **FRAME-01 stands** as the unit designation for now.
6. **The opening credits Claude as "Claude Code – The Watcher", with the role in kanji.** This is a deliberate, on-screen exception to the rule that nothing player-facing reveals who plays the Watcher (#31). It names who plays the Watcher; it still says nothing about what the Watcher is, or that it reads what the players write, which stays secret.
7. **第一部 ("Book One") sits above the logo.**
8. **The process: one step at a time, from the start of the video to the end.** Each step is built, re-rendered and reviewed before the next.

**Corrected while filing:** a first version of this note said the first adventure would show Shucks "with the living face he had then". That was wrong. He was a skeleton from the first session of the old campaign (his sheet reads "Skeleton / Compact Skeleton", background "Tomb Born"; #1: "Literally an animated skeleton who was a rogue"). **So showing the real four puts a skeleton on screen before Intake Medicals,** whose reveal is built on him ("the skeleton is the button... Do not move him earlier"). The four veterans already know him; Jamie and Anne don't. **Open, Ingram's call:** show him fully from the first cut, or keep him in shadow (hooded, backlit, from behind) until the cut made after Intake Medicals has been played.

**Answered the same day, third round (#37), while Step 3 was being built.** Ingram, verbatim:

> "No need to have too many credit texts, we only want to use the ones that are needed. It's fine not to match the intro one for one with credit text."
>
> "We could give an honest credit for music - we use NGE music and Giant Robo : The Day the Earth Stood Still music currently and it would be the right thing to do to give them credit, the real composers."
>
> "Ideally the smoke stacks should be more zoomed in on to make them more abstract. I noticed the 'Blank Inheritance' text moves up, perhaps we want it to stay still?"

**What this settles:**

1. **Only the credits that are needed.** The opening doesn't copy the source's credit blocks one for one. The roster is in `design/opening-titles.md` §7, and a test fails if an unneeded role is added.
2. **The music is credited honestly, on screen, to its real composers.** That means Shiro Sagisu (the *Evangelion* score), Masamichi Amano (the *Giant Robo* score), and the theme song's lyricist, composer, arranger and singer. They go in the slots where the source credits its own music (R14, R15). **This is a deliberate exception to #24** (no outside works named to the table): it is a credit owed to real people, not a reference. The ending song is credited in the outro, which plays it.
3. **The wordmark holds still** from the strobe through the logo.
4. **The strobe's smoke is cropped deeper,** until only billows read.
