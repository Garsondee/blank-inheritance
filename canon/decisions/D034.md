**What this settles:**

1. **The cult is largely peaceful, and largely a non-issue for the Directorate until the adventure properly starts.** The Directorate has a bit of information about it: not enough to dismantle it, not enough to understand its aims, and certainly not enough to know the Watcher is active alongside it. Everything that described the Directorate fighting Cicada for years, filing it as terrorism, or keeping "a cabinet of detained believers" is rewritten. **This refines #17**, whose rule stands: the Directorate has never identified Cicada's founder, and both halves of that discovery belong to the players.
2. **Manako may be known to the Directorate, and that is fine.** He is the scientist it employed, and disgraced, decades ago. It may even suspect that the Manako who runs the tea house is that same man. That doesn't alarm anyone, and it may salve the Directorate's conscience to see him still about and running a successful small business. **What it has never had is any link between the kindly old man in the tea house and the cult.**
3. **N20 is settled: accept the risk.** Files can be stored anywhere; the table won't go digging. **The real rule is about the scenes: they must never be contaminated with information the players shouldn't know yet.** GitHub #201 is closed on that basis, and the Sign-Off checklist gains a check for it (v6, Q18).
4. **D8 is delegated to Claude**, to decide and explain in detail for Ingram to read. **Ingram named the Hand: Rand** ("Right hand... Rand"). Claude's answer is below, and the full explanation is in the Casebook (B17) and `ideas/story/bible/characters/rand.md`.
5. **D13: the Gen 2 core is biomechanical mitflit technology.** It is not a living mitflit: it is robotic parts that are living flesh melded with technology. **Wild mitflits are uncomfortable around Gen 2 Frames**, and **mitflits don't instantly understand where Gen 2 comes from.** The end result: these mechs aren't usable by just anyone.
6. **Maybe a small part of Mother is in every Gen 2**, the "cerebral glue" that makes the technology work. Ingram's own "maybe": open (`open-questions.json#mother-in-gen2`).
7. **N25: Anabala works with the Directorate, and has her own ultimate objective: using mitflit technology to get a way home for her people.** Building Gen 2 for the Directorate gave her access to technology from **the original ship**, which the Directorate keeps highly restricted from the rest of Golarion so it can't destabilise the world.
8. **N26: the six draft kid sheets become test-only PCs**, marked for removal before Session 0/1.

**Corrected while filing — flagged so nothing changes silently: Gen 2 comes from the generation ship, not the search ship.** Ingram's own words in #20: the Directorate *"has been producing new mechs which use technology found from the crash site of the spaceship during 'First Impact'"*, which is the generation ship at Kortos; and here, *"the tech from the original ship"*. From #31 onwards Claude had written that Gen 2 was built from the search ship under Kurose. That was Claude's inference, never Ingram's, and it is corrected everywhere it appeared. The search ship keeps its own jobs: its call is why the Watcher is at Kurose (#32 item 8), and it is the second excavation in the chapter draft.

**D8 — Claude's answer, at Ingram's request, awaiting her read:**

- **Who:** **Rand**, Shucks's detached right hand: the nonverbal, kite-flying familiar that investigates the city on its own as "a conspiracy theorist" (#1). It countersigned the party's vial requisition (Episode One), countersigned the Section Officer's punishment roster, wrote the override order, and chalked *"Same six. Tomorrow."* in Hangar Two. No senior officer spent four minutes on Section Nine. A hand did.
- **What the party reads:** the signature says **Rand**, with no rank and no first name, over the **Medical** department's stamp, borrowed from Shucks's desk. Nobody called Rand works in Medical, or anywhere else on the base.
- **The handwriting:** Rand writes in Shucks's own handwriting from before the hand came off. Shucks writes with his left hand now, so nobody alive recognises that handwriting. But anything the four founders signed two hundred years ago shows it, the founding charter's signature page included.
- **Why:** its theory about the city is right, and it has been collecting the evidence on paper for years. The party's vial requisition was exactly its sense of humour: an agent of chaos, as in the old campaign. So it signed, then looked the six up in Shucks's intake files. It found that all six carry the marker, and put them together and onto the machines, because its theory says the city will need pilots soon.
- **Who knows:** nobody. Shucks doesn't know, but would recognise the handwriting at once. The Section Officer doesn't know, which is still the point of the override (the Node's constraint). **The Watcher has never read a word of Rand's:** it works in ink and chalk, never through the phones.
- **How the party can find out:** through the prosthetic at Intake Medicals plus the name Rand; through the handwriting against the founders' charter; by catching it at work in Hangar Two at night; or by asking Shucks, who will look at the signature and say "That's my hand", meaning it literally. No single roll reveals it in Episode Two.
- **What it doesn't do:** Shucks is still not their guardian angel, and `arc-what-shucks-said` stays open. Rand only ever moves paper and never rescues anyone. It never points at Manako (`dossier-cicada.md` §13.5).

**Filled in by Claude — proposals, veto freely:**

- **The Directorate's file on the cult, before the adventure:** a thin file on a fringe group, with private showings, a stencil or two and the odd arrest for a public nuisance, released without charge. No leader is named or assumed, which keeps #17's rule that the party must discover the movement has an author at all. Nobody is assigned to it. It starts to matter when the first attacks come, each one at a place where Sap was released.
- **Aoi's file** now reads as indifference rather than erasure: a fringe group's casualty in a public-order incident, and nobody thought her worth the paperwork.
- **Shucks and Manako:** Shucks supervised Manako's disgrace (`dossier-cicada.md` §6). He is the likeliest person in the Directorate to suspect that the tea-house owner is the man he once ruined. If he does, it is Shucks's conscience that feels salved, while Manako keeps Shucks on his revenge list.
- **If a piece of Mother is in every Gen 2:** Anabala took the pieces from the generation ship's reasoning core, under the restricted access she bargained for, and the museum fragment is one more piece nobody else understands. It would explain why the Watcher's children are drawn hard to Gen 2: each one carries a piece of the mind the Watcher used to report to. And it would explain why the machines aren't usable by just anyone.
- **Why wild mitflits are uncomfortable around Gen 2:** they feel something of their ancestors' ship in a machine made of living flesh, and they can't say what. The captured mitflit who weeps at a Frame's hand becomes a mitflit who won't go near a Gen 2 and can't say why.
- **Flashback Two** ("the founders burying what the core is") moves from two hundred years ago to when Anabala built Gen 2, decades ago: the day she decides what the Directorate will be told about the core.
- **Anabala's way home:** perhaps the search ship under Kurose, which lifts at Chapter 15 in the draft; perhaps the shuttle under Kortos (N18). Not decided.
- **The test PCs** are already level-1 SF2E sheets. They still carry Anchors and ground-role history (both cut) and two past-Gap lines; as test fixtures those don't matter, and they go before Session 0/1.

**Still open:** a piece of Mother in every Gen 2 (Ingram's "maybe"); whether Manako was arrested; the shuttle (N18); Mother's voice (N21); what Shucks said behind the door; and whether Ingram accepts Rand.
