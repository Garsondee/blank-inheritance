## Settled

1. **A scene may be an exception to a general rule, and an author can say so where the scene is.** The first scene of an adventure may be exposition with no rolls and no cast. A relax beat may have no skill check. Other exceptions will follow, and they must be easy to make.
2. **A scene may have no real location.** The Location Editor's Story tab already records that (`offMap` in `scene-set.json`); it is a decision, not a gap.
3. **Temptations are twists**: fun, organic ways a scene might mutate, which the GM can reach for to evolve it without breaking the scene's objective. The thin lines written so far ("Manako looks like a man who'd enjoy being asked a question") are not that.
4. **Nobody in the party worked for the Directorate before the adventure.** Any of them may have lived beside it all their lives and know a lot about it. Character creation should offer **Directorate Lore**.
5. **The violet mark deliberately echoes the rift**, and the table is meant to feel that the mystery connects to how the previous adventure ended.
6. **Getting onto the island**: by train, which becomes a tram at the causeway; or by two one-way car bridges that run under a huge bunker. The island has an airport, and small boats dock at several places. Security is patchy at the start because the Directorate cannot afford to upset the people of Minkai or Kurose.
7. **The material has to survive the table doing something odd.** Ingram offers the players the chance to make problems outside plot-critical moments and works through them in play; she may ask a player why a result was worth remembering. Scenes are written to hold up to that, and do not try to predict it.
