# D024 · 2026-09-24 · The city is Kurose, and what everybody in it already knows

source: split word for word from LEDGER #24

**Asked 2026-09-24**, while drafting a new Scene 0 (a spoken opening before The Road In): what is
the city called, and how much of the setting may the GM tell the table at the start?

**The name.** Chosen from a shortlist: **Kurose (黒瀬)** — roughly "black shallows" or "dark current".
It was already the map project's working name, so it is now canon everywhere. The open question
`city-name` is resolved.

**Common knowledge, told up front.** Ingram: *"We need to fill in the sort of knowledge that needs to
be common knowledge."* The standing rule that players learn the setting at their characters' speed
still holds for everything that is not common knowledge. What any adult in Kurose knows, and the GM
may therefore say in Scene 0:

- About two hundred years ago something tore open on Kortos, near Absalom, on the far side of the
  world. Nobody agreed on what it was. Many brave people gathered to undo it, and it eventually
  receded. No what, no why.
- The Directorate was founded afterwards so that nobody would be caught unprepared again, and moved
  to Kurose about a hundred and fifty years ago. What it is, why it brings people in, who usually
  comes, and what happens to them next.
- Frames: the ordinary working machines everyone has seen.
- Aliens are rare enough to draw stares; widely available fey magic and devices let many disguise
  themselves; some argue that easy disguise has pushed unusual-looking people into hiding when they
  have done nothing wrong.
- In the last year, strange incidents in crowded places — what the papers call chemical attacks —
  and a cicada painted on walls. The public only started noticing this year (the decades of smaller
  demonstrations in the master timeline stay undiscovered history).

**Not said.** The Watcher is unknown and stays unknown until well into the campaign.

**Two standing rules for anything spoken to the table, from the same conversation.**
1. *No outside references.* "The table won't all know the same references. Also talking about real
   life things is something we should do as little as possible. We can talk about things without
   having to say exactly where the inspiration came from." Describe the qualities; never name the
   source work, the real country or the real decade.
2. *Don't name what isn't there.* Drafting "as far as anyone knows there's only one kind [of Frame]"
   was rejected: *"I tell you there isn't a pink elephant in the room so don't say pink elephant."*
   If something is to stay unmentioned, leave it out entirely — including denials, hints and
   "as far as anyone knows".
