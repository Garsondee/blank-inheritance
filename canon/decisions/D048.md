## Settled

1. **Scene 0 builds mystery, not answers.** The four founders are not named; the speech may speak of four founding members. Where the rift stopped (inside Absalom's walls, in Pharasma's temple) is held back. The founders' names come out later in play, in a history class or another event, with a skill roll, perhaps in two places that each sell it from a different angle.
2. **Rupiah asking the gods, and the gods' answer, are not common knowledge.** They are a drop for around Episode 3 or 4, and they help answer why the Directorate is in Kurose instead of Kortos. Scene 0 asks that question instead of answering it. The public does not know the details of the foretelling.
3. **Directions are dropped unless something about delivery is truly critical.** No coaching on delivery, no negatives unless critical, nothing about other scenes.
4. **The ledger is not complete.** Ingram described more of this history than the scene used. See "What Claude found" below.
5. **Style:** fuller sentences that listeners can follow, not clipped fragments that compress the meaning. When the prophecy is revealed, Ingram's own wording is the model: "And the gods said yes, but in hundreds of years of time and thousands of miles of distance. In the tiny fishing village of Kurose in (Golarion's equivalent of Japan)."
6. **Scene 0 is about twice as long: a big expositional speech,** unusually. It sets up the kind of world this is, the two hundred years since the PF2e era, where Golarion's Japan is, Kurose growing from a tiny fishing village into a great city within a few generations, and the unrest between the families who were there first and the hundreds of people from across Golarion who came for the Directorate.
7. **The old town is on the waterfront**, where the fishing village was, not on cliffs.
8. **The Frame line is Ingram's:** "Every child in Kurose can tell you the Directorate was built to save the world, but the only thing anyone has ever seen a Frame do is lift girders at a building site or help lift a crate of cabbage."
9. **The players are told what a Frame is:** the campaign's word for a piloted mech. No secrets, just the concept.
10. **Founding Day, as Ingram wrote it:** a painting of the four in some supporters' shop windows; at least one of the founders might turn up to cut the ribbon at a major event; not everyone trusts the Directorate, but few can argue with the wealth and technology it has brought in so little time. Plus electricity and rapid, destabilising modernisation: winners and losers, wounded national pride, and isolationist groups who see the Directorate as a foreign thorn in their country's side.
11. **Cicada is not in Scene 0.** It has operated quietly for a long time, below most radars, because it isn't violent or threatening. The party learns about it piecemeal, as they look into it. Scene 0 may hint at unrest among groups in the city, and at organised crime, which is a serious problem.
12. **No "promise… somebody is going to have to keep it" line.** The players get their heroes' call, but not like this.
13. **The trainees' line grows:** what most people expect of the Directorate is a faff, a bother, a duty that's done once it's done; some volunteer, some are encouraged into it, some are pushed.
14. **Scene 0 has no skill rolls and no questions.** Dice start in The Road In. The optional answers and their checks are gone.
