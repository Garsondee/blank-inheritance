# D083 · 2026-10-12 · Cards: the pencil is grouped by topic, and nothing in it changes

asked: After the first two merge waves (D081, D082) Claude measured what was left: the pencil is about 35,500 words of mostly distinct claims (only 52 of 688 facts had a close neighbour in wording, 3 restated an ink fact), so further merging of text would lose information. Claude proposed grouping the claims into titled cards instead, with every claim keeping its own words, author, who-knows, when-told and sources, and asked whether to build it.

## Ingram

> "Have a think about how we can both merge and retain critical information. The goal is to bring the number down to a more manageable amount overall without losing information."

> "Please proceed in the manner you think is best. Then report."

## Settled

1. **Pencil is grouped into cards.** A card is a titled group of pencil facts about one thing, written as a `## Cards` section at the end of an entity's world file (`### Title`, then `- fact-id` lines). A card changes nothing about the facts in it: ids, words, `by`, `known`, `told`, `when`, `payoff`, sources and their order are exactly as they were, so grouping cannot lose information and undoing it is deleting the section. A title is a label, never a claim.
2. **First grouping.** Every entity with four or more pencil facts was grouped: 146 cards hold 608 of the 688 pencil facts; the 80 facts of the 33 smaller entities stay loose. What a person holds is therefore about 226 things instead of 688. Claude chose the groupings by reading; she can regroup any claim.
3. **Merging stays for true duplicates only.** Text is merged only where pieces say one thing (D080, D081, D082); cards are for everything else.

## Claude's readings, marked

- Inking or cutting a whole card in one step is **not** built: each claim is still decided on its own, and the card is for reading and navigation. Whether a card may be inked as a unit is hers to say.
- Card titles were written by Claude and are hers to rename. Ideas (107) are not grouped yet.
