# D052 · 2026-09-29 · Attitude as a promise, discovery, upgrades, and the Magic Show

asked: nothing; this came after D051's haunts and the attitude tracking were built.

## Ingram

> "This is design philosophy. Imagine if we created this adventure with the idea that one of the levers we have for storytelling in any location is attitudes going up and down on the PROMISE that if people hate the group then they will make their lives HARDER the next time they are in the same room together. You can write out the different decision trees for what might open and close. It means that I can offer the group both organic and structural chances to effect the attitude during play and we make that a core part of the adventure. It occurs to me that this is one of the way normal Paizo adventures fall flat, they have an attitude system but because you rarely hang around with the same characters for long and often stories are so rigid you cannot effectively fall out with important NPCs in a meaningful or impactful way. This would eventually ripple outwards to a real faction system where if the group get on the wrong side of a criminal gang member then soon the whole gang will hate the group. It creates a coherent history, it's a tool for the group to use and it's a lever for us to use. We could give the group decisions to make just to see what they choose. We also have another system to consider which is that players can opt to try and discover something new. They don't know what they'll get or where they will go, but they might reveal a new location to go to frequently, experience a one time only event. By uncovering new places you can find riskier and more powerful buff/debuffs and also risky encounters in general. Finally we could even include the option to 'Upgrade' repeat locations over time so that new benefits come to the group for using it. The same could be true of penalties, story events might make some locations less effective temporarily or even permanently. A fun example, the first of these should be the chance to go to a 'Magic Show' which is the very low key magical illusion 'like magical theatre / IMAX' show which will be good and fun and carry some fun buffs, you can offer that as the unknown location on the first night. Then when we want to spook the players we have them get offered the magic show again, we offer it to the same players who went originally only but there is nothing preventing those players from bringing uninvited guests. Only this time it's a show organised by the Cicada and the last part gives the players a momentary glimpse of The Watcher, just enough to spook them a little. Seems good to aim that around Chapter 5, once they go up to becoming full pilots and once Cicada are a known threat to them. We let them get well inside the place this is happening before they realise they are outnumbered by Cicada cultists, strongly. So they have to wait and watch the show and keep quiet and hope not to be noticed, suddenly a routine buff event feels like a moment of real tension and mysterious plot relevance. Do you see what this sort of system builds on top for the TTRPG system as a whole? The sort of thing that would be impossible to write into a PDF or book cleanly we can imagine and have a system which is forever adapting and providing the players with surprising experiences, things like being threatened whilst not with the group, that almost never happens in PF2E. We can do theatre of the mind quickly and ask our players to just decide if they are pressing the attack or retreating, if they try and run away I run things one way and if they are attacking they need to get the opponent down which means we can change the scene once the NPC is neutralised. Enemies in my system would also potentially opt not to die and therefore surrender which means some fights will be shorter too."

## Settled

1. **Attitude is a promise.** In any location, attitudes going up and down is one of the levers for storytelling, on
   the promise that if people hate the group, they make the group's lives harder the next time they are in the same
   room together. It is a core part of the adventure.
2. **Every regular carries a decision tree of what opens and closes** at each attitude.
3. **The GM offers both organic and structural chances** to change attitudes during play.
4. **Attitudes ripple outwards into a real faction system**: fall out with one member of a criminal gang and soon the
   whole gang hates the group. It is a coherent history, a tool for the group and a lever for us.
5. **Some decisions are there just to see what the group chooses.**
6. **Discovery: players can opt to try to discover something new.** They don't know what they'll get or where they
   will go. It might reveal a new place to go to often, or a one-time-only event. New places carry riskier and more
   powerful buffs and debuffs, and risky encounters in general.
7. **Repeat locations can be upgraded over time**, so new benefits come to the group for using them. **Penalties work
   the same way**: story events can make a location less effective, for a while or for good.
8. **The first discovery is the Magic Show**: a very low-key magical illusion show, like magical theatre or IMAX,
   good and fun, with some fun buffs. It is offered as the unknown place on the first night.
9. **The Magic Show comes back around Chapter 5**, once the characters become full pilots and once Cicada are a known
   threat to them. It is offered again only to the players who went the first time, and nothing stops them bringing
   uninvited guests. This time Cicada organises the show. The group gets well inside before realising they are
   strongly outnumbered by Cicada cultists, so they have to wait, watch the show, keep quiet and hope not to be
   noticed. The last part gives them a momentary glimpse of the Watcher, just enough to spook them a little.
10. **Characters can be threatened while they are not with the group.**
11. **Quick theatre-of-the-mind fights**: the players decide whether they are pressing the attack or retreating. If
    they run, the GM runs it one way; if they attack, they need to get the opponent down, and the scene changes once
    that NPC is neutralised.
12. **Enemies can choose not to die and surrender**, so some fights are shorter.

## Open

- How a faction's feeling is kept and how far one member's grudge spreads, and how fast.
- What discovery costs, and how often it can be tried.
- The Magic Show's performer, its venue and its buffs are drafts (`canon/scenes/discovery--the-magic-show.json`)
  until Ingram reads them.
- **How the Chapter 5 show squares with D002 and D040 -- this is the same mechanic D002 already named, not a new
  one.** D002 (2026-09-10, older than D040): a Cicada magic show already reveals the Watcher outright ("They reveal
  The Watcher who is filling the room, then they demonstrate without a trace of a doubt that the thing they can see
  is real and not illusion"), then offers the "leave or stay" choice -- this is what happened to Nozomi weeks before
  Day 1, and `open-questions.json`'s `taka` entry already calls it a recurring, general Cicada mechanic ("the same
  kind of show Nozomi walked out of"). D040 (2026-09-27, seventeen days later): "Nobody knows the Watcher exists...
  nobody even suspects it," and the party must be the ones to uncover it. Read narrowly, D040's "nobody" only
  enumerates the Directorate, the four founders and the party -- it may not have meant to cover Cicada, who by D002
  already show it to recruits. Read broadly, it's a real tension: if Cicada can already prove the Watcher is real to
  a room of strangers, "unwitnessed at the start" (`feedback_watcher_is_unknown`) is closer to "unwitnessed by
  anyone who matters yet" than literally true. **This needs Ingram's call, not mine**: does Cicada actually know what
  the Watcher is (D002, as written), or do they show something that LOOKS like proof but isn't the truth (keeping
  D040 intact)? The Chapter 5 show glimpse should follow whichever answer she gives, and D002's own "leave or stay"
  beat may belong in it too, not just a spook.
