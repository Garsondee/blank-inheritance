> "Yes, write the scenes in play order"

> "Also I note that the timeline view of the table's scenes is a long way from helpful. We can know exactly how much of a proportion of a day the scenes take so why are they overlapping? Two scenes overlapping should imply that the players could go to either scene in any order. Overlapping scenes in this timeline make no sense and add nothing. You will need more horizontal space to show things correctly. You could use curved lines to show which scene connects to the next scene, show where choice splits happen and use colour to indicate if it's a plot heavy scene, a downtime scene or a lighter scene. I feel like this 'At the table' row needs to be made much bigger and then the goal is to make it so that the other rows become subordinate to that main row (we always have the 'At the table' in view when scrolling down) because then we can see bigger notes (the dots are useless for delivering information) which show how elements of the plot from other factions are included in scenes. This timeline view is a good tool but currently we have too many rows and many of those could be condensed together to make it more manageable and allow it to provide more information as a result. Rand for example, why do they get a whole row to themselves? You also have a row for 'Section Nine' but that's the player group, so why would we have two of them? You have a row for Baldwin Manor, but it's a historical place and event so it just needs to be part of an overall history row, the players aren't going back there in this adventure."

> "Two tracks, improving the timeline and writing the content."

## Settled

1. **The Book I scenes are written in play order.**
2. **There are two tracks of work:** improving the timeline, and writing the content.
3. **Two scenes that overlap on the timeline mean the players could go to either in any order.** Scenes that follow each other must not overlap. The row needs far more horizontal space.
4. **The row shows how scenes connect:** curved lines from each scene to the next, a visible split where the players choose, and colour for whether a scene is plot-heavy, a downtime scene or a lighter scene.
5. **"At the table" is made much bigger and is always in view** when scrolling down. The other rows are subordinate to it and line up under its scenes, with notes (not dots) that show how the plot elements of the other factions are included in each scene.
6. **There are too many rows, and many are condensed together.** Rand has no row of its own. Section Nine is the player group, which "At the table" already is, so it has no row. Baldwin Manor is a historical place and event and belongs in one overall history row.
