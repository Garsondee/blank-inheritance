**What this settles:**

1. **Two ships, not one.** The **generation ship** was sent tens of thousands of years ago and crashed in a hidden jungle valley on Kortos. It is the black obelisk the founders took for an overgrown temple, the "ruin of the first spaceship" of #29. About **a thousand years later a second ship, a search ship**, came to find out what had happened to it, and crashed too. Its hull lies under what is now Kurose. Both were buried by volcanic eruptions and earthquakes. **The mitflits are descended from the generation ship's crew.**
2. **Why ships fall on Golarion** (Ingram's own "perhaps", so a working hypothesis, not a lock): something about Golarion is both attractive to the mitflits and critically dangerous to exploring ships. Perhaps it is the gods, jealous of their domain, who foresaw that mitflits landing and spreading freely would change the world in ways they did not want. The mitflits were not conquerors. They saw a world they could live on without understanding gods, or even the idea of a territorial domain.
3. **The Watcher is the generation ship's navigation and sensor system**, torn apart at First Impact into a swarm of nanotech that isn't bound by anything.
4. **The Watcher is the campaign's adversary, and Claude plays it.** Its objective is **to multiply, to expand, and to keep observing.** It learns how to defeat the party if they get in its way. **The campaign's two over-arching themes are mass surveillance and its risks, and runaway agentic systems that threaten the structure of society.** The accepted `adaptive-antagonist-combat-ai` and `watcher-reads-all-comms` ideas are now core canon, not side features.
5. **Solving the Watcher needs the mitflit home world**, just as Second Impact does. Normal technology and magic cannot bind an unbound swarm. The two crises arrive together.
6. **Manako may be killed; that is the players' choice, and it is the wrong one.** Without him the Watcher is a dangerous, unguidable problem.
7. **Sap is nanotech feedstock**: concentrated raw building material for nanobots, used to give the Watcher's children manifestation. **It is not fuel.** The **exotic matter** containers are fuel, and a breached one causes a **subspace rupture**: First Impact at Kortos and **Second Impact**, the name for the rupture in Kurose.
8. **The runaway begins when the Watcher's own children, given new purposes, start producing their own Sap.** "The Watcher learns."
9. **Second Impact is unavoidable**, a beat that puts the whole world at risk and starts the campaign's next part. Everything not yet played is a flexible draft.
10. **All six carry the right stuff by coincidence.** Future party members (robots, androids, beings of light) can have "the right DNA or the right something" too.
11. **The mitflit home world is unique and custom, and high-level content**: the journey there, and a quest to solve an extreme problem on it.
12. **The table:** Liam (Rupiah), Pippa (Anabala), Craig (Shucks) and Steve (Hyuck) play their founders in flashbacks. Jamie and Anne need flashback characters, to be made later. The same six are the campaign's players.
13. **Gen 2 is offered at the end of Chapter 4**, adjusted by how the table takes to Frames and vehicles.
14. **No berserk.** Mitflit technology has no reason to go berserk, and nothing in this campaign takes control of a character away from their player.
15. **Manako is a Heisenberg.** Not a bad person at the start. After Aoi's death, angry ambition needs money. At first his criminal work is picked so he never sees what he is part of, and he tells himself he isn't so bad. It slides until he accepts he is becoming a monster. He doesn't enjoy it; he accepts it as a role.
16. **The phones and the UI:** players are warned never to record anything real or personal in the UI, are not told the messages are read, are encouraged to use the system, and will need it to reach some things.
17. **No spaceship combat.** Combat is personnel scale or Frame scale.
18. **Hori** shot the Envoy dead in fear and, by accident, killed Aoi. He tried everything to save her and failed. Police HQ put him through a very strict process and found him not responsible for an unlawful killing. He retired anyway, in self-imposed disgrace, and his colleagues regard him as a man of great honour who went above and beyond. He is dutiful, honourable and regretful. The party meets him when they are ready to learn a great deal about the inciting incident.
19. **Wild mitflits** (goblinoid) are the only mitflits on Golarion. **High mitflits** (blue-skinned, elegant, unblemished, aloof, high-fantasy elves) are how they looked on the home world. "Ascended" is retired as a name.
20. **Anabala** is a very early mitflit, and lives through the ages because of the knowledge demon **Abraxas**. The demon ruled her for many decades until she turned the tables: the only path to unlimited information runs through her cooperation. Now there is an uneasy alliance: she wants her people home; it wants to learn everything, and grows by feeding on information. **It is not mentioned openly for a long time.** It may emerge when the party breaks into the Directorate's deepest vaults in search of information. **Abraxas and the Watcher are a naturally dangerous, aligned pair.**
21. **The lord is William Charles Baldwin** ("WCB") of the old campaign.
22. **Baldwin Manor and First Impact are two events, months apart in play.** At Baldwin Manor the four rescued mitflits forced to make weapons; an Inicrium battery that summoned shadows had been causing chaos in the nearby town; a shadow siege followed, in which members of Baldwin's own family were killed by accident, not by the four; Baldwin blamed the four anyway. First Impact happened many miles away, in a hidden jungle valley, at the ruined ship the four first took for an overgrown temple, **while they were trying to get exotic fuel for the mitflits who lived there.**
23. **The deep-buried machine at Kortos** was perhaps a teleportation accident, ending up inside the rock and impossible to extract. **Flagged: needs clarification.**
24. **Baldwin's line in the present day** is a beat the players will eventually come across.

**Filled in by Claude to make it cohere — proposals, veto freely:**

- **Why the Watcher went to Kurose:** the search ship came looking for the generation ship, and its buried systems are still calling for it. The Watcher is drawn to the ship that came to find it, and the Directorate's digging wakes that ship and strengthens the pull. This replaces "it came home" (its home is at Kortos).
- **Mother** (the old campaign's level-15 incorporeal construct, met in the mitflit village and on the cargo deck) was the generation ship's **reasoning core**: the mind the Watcher's reports went to. First Impact tore the navigation and sensor system away from her, which is the "organising layer" the swarm lost. Where Mother is now is open.
- **The Cargo Deck breach was First Impact.** The throw and the catch happened on the generation ship's cargo deck. The split container opened the rupture, and the planar breach flung the four across the planes to Nirvana, where time ran differently. They came back to find it grown and carving towards Absalom, and stood in front of it at Pharasma's temple. (#30 floated this as a reading; this treats it as the working account.)
- **The Watcher's arc runs from instrument to runaway:** a drowning instrument that took the shape of the strongest signal it could find (Manako's grief); then a learner (the Envoy, then the party); then self-supplying (its children making Sap); then runaway, and unguided if Manako dies. **It is never evil.** It is an objective pursued without limit: to observe more, it must be more, and anything in the way is an obstacle. That keeps the thesis ("nobody has ever done anything evil") and is exactly the runaway-agent fear.
- **Its supply chain grows more autonomous over the campaign:** first a human it taught to make Sap (Manako, canon step 6 of his fall), then an industrial plant (the column), then its own children. The runaway threshold is the moment it no longer needs any human to feed it. **The Mass Clock's final threshold is now that moment**, replacing the older "self-organisation".
- **The Mirror** still works, but on **method, not objective**: how the party fights teaches it how to pursue its goal (probe or weapon). It will always pursue the goal.
- **"Claimant" fits the new objective:** its children stake claims to ground for it to watch. The older property-dispute and escheat framing now has somewhere real to point: the ships belong to the mitflits, so there *is* an heir. (A blank inheritance is one whose heir hasn't been named.)
- **Hori's file:** the police examined Hori's use of force and cleared him. The Directorate took jurisdiction over the alien and the scene, and filed Aoi as one deceased, unidentified. **Outside Manako himself, Hori is the only person alive who knows her name.** *(Narrowed the same day: Aoi was part of Manako's circle, so its surviving members know her name too. Read it as: outside Manako's own circle, Hori is the only one.)*
- **Where Abraxas emerges:** the deepest vault holds the unredacted charter. The redacted-document chain ends there, so the break-in comes when the party goes for the last black bar.
- **The "berserk protocol"** (Mystery Ledger #8) no longer has a berserk to be about. Proposal: it becomes Hyuck's emergency protocol for machines that fail under their pilots, written by a test pilot who has crashed everything, and it still opens with the line about brothers. Otherwise cut it.
- **The Baldwin line:** Baldwin House as the arms concern, or the crime lords, that Manako works with, carrying a two-hundred-year grudge against the four. Only "a beat they eventually come across" is decided.
