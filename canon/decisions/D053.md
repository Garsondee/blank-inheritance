## Settled

**On the Watcher's secrecy (corrects the practical reading of D040, not D040's own quoted text, which stays as the
historical record of what was said 2026-09-27):**

1. **The rule was never "nothing in the fiction has ever shown anyone the Watcher."** D040 itself already had three
   sightings before the party (Manako's lab spill, the convention canister, the Envoy) and D002 already has Cicada
   showing it to recruits. The rule is about writing DISCIPLINE: nothing about the Watcher, Cicada, or anything
   crucially plot-related gets written into any scene without careful thought first.
2. **The specific failure to guard against, named directly: writing the conspicuous absence.** Not just avoiding a
   mention of the secret, but avoiding the tell of pointedly asserting there is nothing to see — the "pink elephant"
   problem. A location's text should read as straightforwardly about itself, neither leaking the secret nor
   performing the avoidance of it.
3. **LLMs are bad at pacing a drip-fed mystery on their own**, so this needs active care, not a rule that can run on
   autopilot once written down.
4. **The chemical that reveals the Watcher (already named in D040) is short-lived**: the visibility it gives lasts
   under an hour, and it evaporates quickly, leaving only a hard-to-find residue that carries little information.
5. **Players don't need to personally see the Watcher.** They can't attack it directly, so a sighting is not a
   required beat.
6. **A future scene, much later, not yet placed on the chapter plan:** the Directorate's senior leadership argues
   over how to act against something invisible, intangible, and above the city itself. The party may be present to
   make their own case to the room.
7. **The group cannot defeat the problem on Golarion, and by the end of the adventure everyone will know the
   Watcher existed, whether they've personally seen it or not -- stated as how Ingram sees it going ("potentially"),
   not yet locked the way points 1-6 are.** It confirms, rather than changes, LEDGER #31/#41 (Second Impact and the
   Watcher both need the mitflit home world; the Watcher's own final fate stays open, un-touched by this).

**On the Directorate's discipline scale (new):**

8. **Assembly's speech states the Directorate's own rules, in character**: disobeying a direct order is a serious
   crime; breaking curfew is a very minor one. This builds the in-universe understanding of the Directorate as
   school and military at once — defenders and bureaucrats together.
9. **The party gets real chances to disobey a direct order.** Choosing to do so puts them in front of someone
   higher up, to explain themselves and fight their case: a story moment with real stakes.
10. **Disobedience is not immediately rewarded.** It can cost something for a while. But a character who disobeyed
    for the right reason can be proven right, and the reward for doing the ethical thing is always greater than the
    reward for simple compliance.
11. **This is deliberately "a bit of EVA"**: institutional friction, a real chance to stand apart, to do the
    difficult right thing, to stand up to an authority figure who is wrong. It is scoped to structure and stakes,
    not to the Evangelion shelf's quarantined register (`design/research/evangelion/tone-guardrail.md`): the
    higher-up in the hearing is a warm, competent adult who may be genuinely correct, may be genuinely wrong, and
    can be shown to have been wrong without the scene reading as despair or institutional failure across the board.

## Changes

Corrects the practical application of D040's "nobody knows the Watcher exists... nobody even suspects it" and
