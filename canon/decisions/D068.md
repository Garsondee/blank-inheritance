# D068 · 2026-10-07 · The mayor of Kurose, the police and the Directorate forced to work together on raids, the party as a magical armed-response team, and where the crime and cult threads lead

asked: nothing. Ingram gave this lore unprompted, as one block in the chat.

## Ingram

> "Lore: The directorate have a large number of people under arms and training. The city of Kurose has an organized crime problem. The mayor of the town is a big fan of the directorate because of the commercial impact it has on the city, so much so that the directorate and the cities police are forced into uneasy cooperation in the event that the raid or activity is being carried out against the organised criminals or dangerous cultists. The idea is that this gives a new character for the plot that the group can meet, the mayor, who can act as both a blessing and a curse for the group, someone who is their own agent and who protects the city and their own position equally. This also is our reasoning for allowing our players to be a sort of 'Magical SWAT Team' brought in for certain missions against criminals and cultists which are our two main branches of personnel level combat. The organised crime thread eventually leads to Baldwin and Manako's underworld dealings. The cultist thread leads to Manako directly but also leads to the players uncovering the non-violent origins, the event with Aio and Hiro, the tragic history of a man who tried to upset the order of things to warn about something invisible in our midst but who was ostracized for it whilst powerful people looked into and started to suspect he was right."

Claude said in the chat that "Aio and Hiro" looked like canon's "Hori and Aoi" with the letters swapped. She answered the same turn:

> "You are correct, my spelling was incorrect."

## Settled

1. **The Directorate has a large number of people under arms and training.**
2. **Kurose has an organised-crime problem.** Already decided (D048, `kurose.organised-crime`).
3. **The mayor of Kurose is a big fan of the Directorate, because of the commercial impact it has on the city.**
4. **So much so that the Directorate and the city's police are forced into uneasy cooperation** whenever a raid or other activity is carried out against organised criminals or dangerous cultists.
5. **The mayor is a new character for the plot, whom the group can meet.** The mayor can act as both a blessing and a curse for the group: their own agent, who protects the city and their own position equally.
6. **This is the reasoning for the party being a sort of "Magical SWAT Team"**, brought in for certain missions against criminals and cultists. Criminals and cultists are **the campaign's two main branches of personnel-level combat.**
7. **The organised-crime thread eventually leads to Baldwin and to Manako's underworld dealings.**
8. **The cultist thread leads to Manako directly.** It also leads to the players uncovering Cicada's **non-violent origins**: the event with Aoi and Hori (the Envoy incident), and the tragic history of a man who tried to upset the order of things to warn about something invisible in our midst, was ostracised for it, and whose warning powerful people looked into and started to suspect was right.

## Claude's readings, marked

- **"Aio and Hiro" are Aoi and Hori**, the Envoy incident of D028 and D031 to D035. Her spelling slip, confirmed in chat. Her words above are kept as typed.
- **The man who tried to upset the order of things is Manako**, and "something invisible in our midst" is the Watcher: the science-conference disruption of D040 and D045 (the canister, the "breakdown", the expulsion). She named neither, so this is Claude's reading, and it is the only one canon allows. If she means someone else, `manako.was-warning`, `manako.ostracised` and `manako.suspected-right` change.
- **"Powerful people started to suspect he was right" is suspicion, not knowledge.** `watcher.unknown` stands: nobody has named or understood the Watcher. Who the powerful people were is open (`manako.who-suspected`); the candidates listed there are Claude's, not hers.
- **"The town" is Kurose, the city.** The mayor has no name yet and no gender: the canon file says "the mayor" and "they". The name is a naming placeholder (`mayor.name`, `open-questions.json#kurose-mayor-name`).
- **"Dangerous cultists" are, in the main, Cicada once the attacks begin.** Cicada is still largely peaceful at the start (D034, D048, `cicada.quietly`). The police already meet it on the street (the clash on the harbour road, D043); the Directorate stays out until the attacks start (D034). From then on, a raid on cultists is where the mayor's arrangement puts the two in the same room. Nothing here says Cicada is the only cult in Kurose, and nothing rules out other cults.
- **The distrust stands.** D035's two plain facts are now in canon (`kurose-police.distrust-directorate`, `kurose-police.news-travels-badly`); the mayor's pressure is why they work together *uneasily* on raids and nowhere else. The induction agreement's Article 105.4 (a recruit refers any police officer to the Liaison Office, open on alternate Tuesdays) still fits: it is the Directorate's paper wall against the police, and a raid is where the mayor makes it give. Nothing in the agreement changes.
- **It fits what is already canon.** The Directorate is "a school and a service at once" (`directorate.school-and-service`), so trainees on a mission is not new. Scene 0's "the only thing anyone has seen a Frame do is lift girders" still holds, because these missions are personnel-scale and use no Frames. A large armed force is also part of why many in Kurose call it "a foreign army in the harbour" (`directorate.warmongers`), which stays false as a claim of warmongering.
- **"SWAT" is her shorthand and stays out of in-world text** (no real-world references at the table). The canon fact says "a magical armed-response team"; her phrase is in the file's notes.
- **Episode Two is not settled by this.** `open-questions.json#episode-two-shape` is a Frame/vehicle-scale call-out; these missions are personnel-scale. Where the first raid goes is a plan decision for her.
- **Not decided, and not invented:** the mayor's name; where and when the party first meets the mayor (`open-questions.json#mayor-first-appearance`); what the mayor asks of them; how the mayor holds office. Nothing is placed in the plan. Baldwin, Hori, Aoi and the Envoy incident still live only in decision records (D028 to D035), because nothing in play cites them yet; they get world files when a scene needs them, or on her word.
