# D084 · 2026-10-12 · Book I rethought: Episode Two's first real fight, the three combat tracks, the mayor's crime initiative, and what Book I is for

asked: Claude told her what canon's notes hold for Book I (what happens, the mystery, the fights) and named its thin spots. She asked how Claude would rethink Book I with carte blanche, Episode One's ink included, and gave her own thoughts to integrate.

## Ingram

> "If I gave you carte blanche to rethink this book how would you do it? Even the stuff which is inked for Episode 1. Below are my thoughts for you to integrate into your thinking."

> "Try to imagine what would be going through the players minds as they read the scenes written or the ones you are imagining. Can you make your ideas responsive to the reactions that players are likely to have?"

> "1.) By the end of Episode 2 I need the players to be in their frames/vehicles taking part in an exercise which turns into a real  combat, that feels like pretty solid ground. The players are training and then a real emergency occurs and they get sent in early. We want this first combat to have a bit of a feel of EVA, three alien completely unknown things appear from out of thin air and start attacking things in the city. The combat should be tuned towards being very hard, not easy, but we provide ways for Directorate forces to appear and tip the balance if we think the players might completely fail. The players will still get in trouble with Vetch for acting so we need them to be told not to act by Vetch and then put them in a position where the only sensible or heroic thing to do would be act."

> "2.) The way that I see the combat is that we have a few 'tracks' and we'll keep returning to the same tracks but the combats themselves will change each time whilst still keeping a sort of theme."

> "a.) The players in vehicles and frames fighting the children of the Watcher at frame scale combat. These children are huge building sized enemies and can only be effectively fought by using the special rules set for this sort of combat."

> "b.) Some of this plot stuff will be about adding flavour to the city, the players will deal with the different gangs which we'll have to give different fun thematic flavours to give them a memorable edge. At first this won't seem like it connects to the overall plot, but as the players win these fights they will be given a chance to investigate the scenes where the fights happened and they'll start uncovering hints that the criminals are working with the cult."

> "c.) Cultists: This is the other major branch of combat. Manako's cultists who will be responding to the group's attempts to investigate by attacking them. Sometimes the players will be raiding something they think is a criminal operation and it turns out to be a cultist operation. The thing about the cultists is that once defeated the group will discover they are upstanding citizens, not mind controlled, drugged or coerced. They are smart and motivated people who are often"

> "The goal of the first book has to be to bring the group up to speed with the world they are inhabiting, the directorate and the city itself (they need positive scenes with nicer characters to be motivated to defend the city) as well as the various threats to the city. Ideally after the first two episodes we start to quickly give the players options about how they approach things."

> "A good way to make the whole story work better would involve working out what the antagonists and other factions are doing at each stage, if we can make reasonable assumptions about their efforts then we can have the players work to thwart the antagonists at the same time the antagonists are looking to sabotage the efforts of the players."

> "There are lots of good ideas but I don't think at the moment the threads come together to tell a fun story. Have a think about how you would reorganize things, you don't HAVE to remove anything but I'm giving you the option to reconsider everything."

## Settled

1. **Episode Two ends in a real fight.** By its end the players are in their Frames and vehicles taking part in an exercise that turns into real combat, on ground that feels solid: they are training, a real emergency occurs, and they are sent in early.
2. **The first combat feels a little like Evangelion:** three alien, completely unknown things appear out of thin air and start attacking things in the city.
3. **It is tuned to be very hard, not easy,** with ways for Directorate forces to appear and tip the balance if the players might completely fail.
4. **Vetch tells them not to act,** and they are put where the only sensible or heroic thing to do is act. They still get in trouble with Vetch for acting.
5. **Combat runs on a few tracks** the campaign keeps returning to. Each fight changes while keeping a sort of theme.
6. **Track (a), Frame scale:** the players in vehicles and Frames fight the Watcher's children: huge, building-sized enemies that can only be fought effectively with the special rules for that kind of combat.
7. **Track (b), criminals:** the party is called in as a sort of magical armed-response team, brought in by the mayor (they won't know that for a long time) as part of an initiative to use the Directorate's resources to fix the rampant organised crime in Kurose. Part of this plot adds flavour to the city: the party deals with different gangs, each given a different, fun thematic flavour and a memorable edge. At first it won't seem to connect to the overall plot; as the party wins these fights, it gets the chance to investigate the scenes and starts uncovering hints that the criminals are working with the cult.
8. **Track (c), cultists, the other major branch of combat:** Manako's cultists respond to the party's attempts to investigate by attacking them. Sometimes the party raids what it thinks is a criminal operation and it turns out to be a cultist one. Once defeated, the cultists turn out to be upstanding citizens, not mind-controlled, drugged or coerced: smart and motivated people.
9. **What Book I is for:** bringing the group up to speed with the world, the Directorate and the city itself (with positive scenes and nicer characters, so they are motivated to defend the city) and with the threats to the city. After the first two episodes, the players are quickly given options about how they approach things.
10. **The method:** work out what the antagonists and the other factions are doing at each stage, so the players work to thwart them while they work to sabotage the players.
11. **Her view of the notes:** there are lots of good ideas, but the threads don't yet come together to tell a fun story. Claude may reconsider everything in Book I, Episode One's ink included; nothing has to be removed.
