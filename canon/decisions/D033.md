**What this settles:**

1. **Hori learned Aoi's name in court.** He did not know it until a complex court proceeding to decide whether the killing was lawful. That proceeding is part of the "very strict process" of #31, and it found him not responsible for an unlawful killing. The Casebook's three guesses (she told him in the car, her papers at the hospital, he found out later on his own) are all withdrawn.
2. **He was on his own.** On the day he was just dealing with a local disturbance, with no backup, and it escalated.
3. **Manako may have been arrested, but not imprisoned, as part of it. Or he may not.** Ingram's own "maybe": open (`open-questions.json#manako-arrested-after-the-envoy`).
4. **Ingram sees no contradiction here.** The fill-ins below show how the older facts all stay true.
5. **Abraxas can emerge from Anabala, and Anabala stays Pippa's to play.** The worry in N23 was that a demon emerging from a player character takes the character away from her player. It doesn't: the demon comes out, and Anabala is still Pippa's. "Not an issue to worry too much about." Whether Pippa does play Anabala as one of the students is still her choice, and open (`open-questions.json#anabala-as-student`).
6. **The Gap is an event after the adventure.** It is not Second Impact. Perhaps it is a result of the adventure. Perhaps the only way to undo Second Impact is to create the Gap. "Maybe"; to be resolved later. This narrows the 2026-09-20 ruling that the Gap is a future event (`open-questions.json#the-gap-is-future`).
7. **So nothing in play is "Gap-derived".** The out-of-era technology that leaked from Kortos is ship technology (#32 item 6). The old idea entry `gap-derived-tech-disparity` keeps its id so links don't break, and its wording is corrected.
8. **A Gen 2 is uncomfortable to enter except in your true form.** A disguised pilot can climb in and fly it; it is uncomfortable. Nothing forces a reveal, and the reveal stays the player's (`design/induction.md` §2). `open-questions.json#gen2-seat-and-disguise` is resolved.
9. **How Baldwin stays alive (N16) and when he steps out (N17) stay open.** "We'll work this out later."

**Filled in by Claude — proposals, veto freely:**

- **Why the Directorate's file still says "one deceased, unidentified".** Its report was written at the scene on the day, when the circle had scattered and nobody had named her. A young Directorate officer on the cordon, later the Soldier, helped write it (`dossier-cicada.md` §9). The court proceeding came later, on the police side: the Directorate had taken jurisdiction over the alien and the scene, not over the question of whether an officer had killed lawfully. Nobody went back to correct the Directorate's file.
- **Why her name never got out.** The hearing was held behind closed doors, because an alien was part of the evidence, and its record is not public. So, outside Manako's own circle, **Hori is the one person who carried her name out of that room and kept it.** It also gives the party a second, harder way to her name besides Hori: the closed court record. A third is any survivor of the old circle. That is the Three Clue Rule (`design/ttrpg-campaign-and-session-craft.md`).
- **What the local disturbance was.** The circle's meeting itself: people gathering in a public street to meet something nobody else could see, and drawing a crowd. A lone officer came to it.
- **If Manako was arrested, #17 still holds.** He would have been arrested by the police as a man at a public disturbance, before Cicada existed, and released without charge. That does not identify him as Cicada's founder, and the Directorate has never connected the two, so #17 ("the Directorate has never identified Cicada's founder") stands. Two knock-ons if it did happen. First, Hori may have seen him, or heard his name, during the proceeding, which bears on whether Hori knows who the broken man in the street was (`hori.md`, open question 4). Second, a fifteen-year-old police record with his name in it exists. The players can find it themselves; no NPC may point them at it (`dossier-cicada.md` §13.5).
- **What the emergence costs Anabala.** She lived through the ages because of Abraxas (#31). With the demon out of her she may begin to age, or weaken. That makes letting Abraxas out, or taking her back, a real price, and Anabala's (and Pippa's) to pay or refuse. It is never something done to Pippa's character without her. (This is the Casebook's N23 first option.)
- **The empty Gen 2 seat.** The v4 bible has a Gen 2 cavity keep its pilot's shape even when the cockpit is empty ("The cavity conforms"). So a hangar crew could see a hollow shaped to a disguised pilot's true body. The induction rule already covers it: *"The system may know; the table finds out when the player decides."* The crew may know. The GM never describes the hollow's shape to the table until the player reveals.
- **The discomfort is not Frame Sickness.** The discomfort is a borrowed body not fitting a seat made for the real one. Frame Sickness (#23) is the sync problem any pilot has in a Gen 2 new to them, disguised or not, and only Anabala and senior Directorate know it exists. Keep the two separate.
- **The Gap and the published setting.** In Starfinder's own setting the Gap is "amnesiac": a stretch of history that every mind and record lost at once, after which Golarion was gone ("Lost Golarion", `reference/srd/gm-core/1219-timeshifted-adventures.json`). GM Core even offers it as "a plot device that supernaturally alters or censors sensitive information" (`1234-archaic-adventures.json`). A Gap made to undo Second Impact fits that exactly: the cost of undoing the rupture is that nobody remembers it. Nothing decided; it is written down so it is to hand when N24 is resolved.
