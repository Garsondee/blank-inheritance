## Settled

1. **All ten rests are approved** as proposed in `plan/chapter-01.md`, to be written out in detail as a foundation;
   they will get more work later.
2. **A new type of scene: a haunt**, a place the group often comes back to.
3. **The rumour mill runs on sets of rumours.** Each episode has three sets. A player names any skill they can
   justify and rolls blind. Critical failure: three wrong. Failure: two wrong, one true. Success: two true, one wrong.
   Critical success: three true. A rumour is short, usually one sentence. Some rumours can invite a guess at what
   else might be true.
4. **At most one rumour in a set touches the actual plot**, to reveal it or mislead about it. The rest need not be
   plot-relevant.
5. **A few events are plot-relevant.** They fall back into the ordinary Scene Runner way of running a scene, and the
   Runner's header alerts the GM that the event is more than it seems.
6. **Some events are dangerous**, and the players are never warned which. Theatre-of-the-mind fights are possible.
7. **Most events are skill rolls that produce results, or fun buffs, and a chance to role-play.** Some are a
   deliberate challenge: narrate something, put the table in the world for a few minutes, ask a player to tell us
   about their character's day, what they do and how, with time to think before they have to speak.
8. **A haunt is a menu, not a script**: fun things to do to or with the players, which get them speaking in
   character and narrating what their characters do.
9. **Dilemmas are good**, and dilemmas that come to mirror the way the city is changing are storytelling.
10. **People the group meet by chance can have an unconsidered line of information** and share it willingly: a
    little insight into what might be going on behind the scenes.
11. **The NPCs at each location have an attitude that is tracked**: events can knock it up or down in one press, and
    the GM can shift it up or down by hand.
12. **The attitude changes how the scene reads next time**, and comes back to Claude as context: which NPCs are at
    each location, who they are, and how they feel about the group, to build new ideas from.
