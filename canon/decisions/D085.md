## Settled

1. **She loves almost all of D084's rethink.** The exceptions are the ones she names below.
2. **The Errand must expect its players to guess the prank within seconds.** Her players will guess what is happening with the bubble almost immediately, within seconds of being told what they are doing; the scene has to anticipate that. She likes introducing the players to locations, but not to too many places where they already know the answer is unlikely to help.
3. **The Errand is not a tour.** The players won't want a tour, because they would guess they are being messed with very early on.
4. **The jam in the flood tunnel is smuggled crates.** It gives a hint of the criminal side of things, without revealing too much too soon.
5. **The Comms Officer is unsure and disagrees with Vetch, but cannot get a better answer quickly.** That makes her a better ally and makes her seem more caring.
6. **A sentence that means nothing is a defect.** The sentence "Contacts are plotted before the alert that reports them" meant nothing to her. Descriptions of a scene's purpose and elements must be plain and unambiguous; the mystery lives in the prose delivered to the players.
7. **Book I is rewritten scene by scene,** with more characters, strong routing (why the players go from one scene to the next), choices where the players can investigate in any order, and many more scenes than were planned.
8. **The timeline shows the new Book I, and Claude talks her through it** in enough detail to understand what is going on.
9. **The cultists are good, competent, well-educated people,** who are assumed to be the opposite. The group learns this slowly, because anyone taken alive gives up a small amount of it in debrief.
10. **The Watcher's children evoke the same feeling of the unknown as the angels in Evangelion:** strange, flowing creatures that move in unusual ways. The players eventually learn this is because they are entirely composed of nanomachines.
11. **The Road In is split into smaller scenes aimed at individual players.** Each player character's route into the city is assumed in advance, some of them nailed down, and changed only if the players ask for a different way in. A scene without the whole group is short, because only one player is fully engaged in it. The players learn a lot from these mini challenges.

## Claude's readings, marked

- **"Almost all" is read as adoption.** D084's ideas in `canon/ideas/book-i.md` become the plan for Book I, except the Errand tour (rejected; its wording is retired), the sentence she found meaningless (replaced), and the Comms Officer's role (changed as she says). Everything that follows from them is pencil until she goes through it; none of it is ink.
- **What the rejected sentence was reaching for.** It came from an old Episode Two draft. Claude reads the intent as a clue planted early: the instruments register something at a place before the children appear there. The plain version, which replaces it, is: the plotting floor's instruments register a disturbance at the harbour road eight minutes before the three things appear, and the operators put it down to a fault. The eight minutes is Claude's.
- **"Seat" means the player's place at the table, not a character.** The six assumed routes are assigned by seat, and each is one of the arrival modes the game already has (ferry, train, tram, bicycle, coast road, on foot). Bus and teleportation stay open as swaps. Which seat gets which route is Claude's proposal.
- **"The mini challenges" are read as one check each,** per way in, with different information on different skills and degrees.
- **"Hint of a criminal side" is read narrowly:** the crates show that someone is smuggling through the Directorate's drains and say nothing about Cicada. The violet paint that links crime to cult does not appear in the tunnel.
- **"Unsure" is read as unsure what the three things are and whether Vetch's order is right,** not unsure of her job. She gives the party everything she has, orders nothing, and keeps trying for a better answer.
- **"Eventually learn" the children are nanomachines:** when is not set. Claude's proposal is Book III.
- **Not decided and not invented:** which characters are named (every new person in this rewrite is a Claude proposal, pencil, and hers to rename or cut); what happens if the players obey Vetch's order; whether any civilian dies on the harbour road; the mayor's name; the Frame-scale rules the first fight needs (`open-questions.json#vehicle-and-frame-rules-model`).
- **Scenes are planned, not written.** The plan for every Book I scene is in `canon/plan/`; the written scenes (read-aloud, checks, cast cards) follow in play order, each through the re-read and her read, as D048 and the scene rules require.
