**The collision.** #26 made the four founders famous. Episodes One and Two were built on the founders being
*nameless*: a teacher who cannot place "Hyuck" on the statue plaque, a page-forty history "in the passive
voice, naming nobody", and an older printing whose memorial plate had its naming caption reset.

**The fill-in.** The city remembers the four by **name**. What two centuries of posters, paintings and
textbook editions tidied away is **what they looked like** and **how the rift began**:
- Every approved picture of the four, from the textbook frontispiece to the Founding Day posters, shows
  four tall, handsome humans. The real four were a gnoll, an elf, a very small mitflit, and a man who is
  now a skeleton.
- The approved history names all four as heroes who closed the rift and swore to be in Kurose. It goes from
  nothing to catastrophe in a single sentence and never says how the rift opened. (They opened it, with a
  fumbled catch — `dossier-first-impact.md` §3. That stays GM-only.)
- Half the city is named after the four. So a gnoll statue with HYUCK on the plaque reads as somebody's
  mascot, and a skeleton doctor called Shucks reads as a namesake or a Directorate in-joke. Nobody finds
  either worth asking about.
- Nobody decided any of this. It is a tidying, not a cover-up — which keeps `dossier-the-directorate.md`'s
  "calcified, not sinister" register intact.

**The three plants now read:** (1) day one, the teacher says cheerfully that the statue is obviously not
Hyuck the founder, because Hyuck was a big man with a sword; (2) page forty names the four and omits the
cause, a success finds the seams (no cause, margin dates that do not reconcile), a critical success finds
the older printing's plate showing them as they were; (3) Shucks teaches on a Tuesday, unremarked. The
discipline is unchanged: nothing at the table may connect them.
