# D020 · 2026-09-19 · The second-generation Frames — utility mechs, Mitflit-DNA piloting, and why it wakes the Observer

source: split word for word from LEDGER #20

**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Asked because:** reviewing the Episode One redesign surfaced a real, previously-unnoticed continuity
problem — the party are pilot candidates urgently needed (Nozomi's defection is why this induction
happened two weeks early), yet Episode One hands them the base's single worst job. Nothing on record
explained why. Put to Ingram as an open dilemma; he answered with a full, unprompted pitch that solves
it and connects several previously-separate threads at once.

**Ingram, unprompted, verbatim:**

> This brings up an interesting dilemma. The group are mech pilot candidates but they are being picked
> on and are considered the worst of the worst? They are given a shit job cleaning a sewer. I think
> there has to be a good reason for this, after all these characters would normally be considered the
> bases hotshots. We've already determined in the plot that there aren't enough pilots because one of
> them isn't cooperating.
>
> So here's the pitch and it changes a lot. We make working a mech the uncomfortable, sweaty and hard
> job because mechs aren't machines of war and cool they are instead more akin to diggers and
> bulldozers and things like that. The technology is bad and dangerous. We need to change some other
> things. Shucks needs to take their blood samples, not review them, he's going to test the group and
> discover that all of them have Mitflit DNA for some reason. The purpose for this test is that the
> Directorate has been producing new mechs which use technology found from the crash site of the
> spaceship during 'First Impact'. These mechs looked extremely promising but they soon discovered that
> very few people could use them. A brilliant scientist (Anabala) was involved in producing them (she
> could pilot because she's a Mitflit) but when they tried to give them to other pilots many found the
> mechs unusable. The Directorate almost gave up on the project and the mechs were moth balled. Shucks
> has a theory about DNA and starts testing students and starts finding ones that can actually pilot
> the mechs. It's the piloting of these new mechs which helps awaken The Watcher and also we'll try and
> make the new mechs in some way provocative to the cult (perhaps simply The Watcher pays special
> attention to things based on their tech level and when it sees obvious Mitflit technology it
> scrutinises it closely. The cult think that the things The Watcher fixates on must be potentially bad
> or dangerous and mistakenly thinks that the new mechs are themselves a bad thing.
>
> This changes our initial Mech designs too, they become utility mechs drafted into combat and then the
> players will modify their mechs from utility things into war machines over the course of the
> adventure. This makes me question the whole level 1 and level 12 versions of personnel and frame
> scale combat, perhaps we can make the gap smaller and have frame scale combat start at level 4
> because I can see much more room for growth from the mechs.
>
> This also pays off when the players initially think they've been given the worst roles on base, but
> when Shuck's medical blood DNA screening comes back it identifies them all as potential pilots for
> the enhanced mechs it means they instantly become the most useful and powerful pilots on the base
> which drags them from the worst jobs to the EVA treatment.
>
> You need to record all of this as ideas that I'm really happy with. I think this adds a bit of depth
> to the mechs and the Mitflits.

**What this settles:**

- **The party's own six Frames are second-generation — built from the true hull, not the Kortos
  fragment — and are issued as utility machines**, not war machines. The players convert them into war
  machines over the campaign, giving the existing upgrade-point economy a reason to exist.
- **Shucks actively tests blood for a genetic piloting-compatibility marker, personally, as a running
  programme** — not a passive form-review. All six flagging "irregular" the same morning, in one
  section, is a genuine statistical surprise to him, GM-only.
- **Mitflits pilot the second-generation Frames natively; a small number of humans carry enough of the
  same marker, diluted over generations, to also qualify.** Not "mitflits are mechs" — a compatibility
  gene, not a species, and Ingram's own 2026-09-13 worry about cheapening the species (LEDGER #15,
  question 1) is the deliberate constraint this was built against.
- **Piloting the new Frames is what accelerates the Observer's comprehension curve toward "Frames
  specifically matter"** (`dossier-observer.md` §14, Chapter 3) — mechanism: the new Frames are made of
  real fragments of the Observer's own ship, and it is already straining toward anything that feels
  like home (§9). Not a threat-response — recognition. Stays benign, per that dossier's own tone rules.
- **Cicada misreads the Observer's fixation on the new tech as a divine danger signal**, consistent
  with their own theology (the Observer's attention already means everything to them) — an "ordinary
  flinch," not malice, same shape as every other institutional failure in this campaign.
- **The worst-job-to-most-valuable-pilots reversal is a held payoff, not a same-session one.** Mystery
  Ledger plant #1 already pays off Chapter 6; this doesn't move that, it explains it.
- **Genuinely open, not decided here: whether Frame-scale combat should start at level 4 instead of
  level 12.** Flagged, not acted on — too foundational (touches `scale-and-chassis.md`, the whole
  Resonance action-economy, and every XP budget) for a same-conversation call.
