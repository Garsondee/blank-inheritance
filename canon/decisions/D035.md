# D035 · 2026-09-24 · The police and the Directorate distrust each other; the Envoy incident's official line

source: split word for word from LEDGER #35

**Ingram, 2026-09-24, unprompted, alongside the brief for the Casebook's sixth version.** Authoritative over #33 and #34 where they differ.

**Ingram, verbatim:**

> "I had a thought about Manako and the police. The police and the Directorate aren't really good allies, they distrust each other. So things that happen with the police won't immediate or clearly translate into knowledge that the Directorate has. This means it's possible for the Aoi shooting to happen and for the Directorate to end up with very little useful information except that a cultist was killed during a confrontation in which someone magically summoned a monster, that's the official line anyways."

**What this settles:**

1. **The police and the Directorate aren't good allies. They distrust each other.** What happens with the police doesn't reach the Directorate quickly or clearly, if at all.
2. **The Envoy incident's official line**, which is all the Directorate has: *a cultist was killed during a confrontation in which someone magically summoned a monster.*
3. **So the Directorate never learned Aoi's name,** and never learned anything else from the police's side: the proceeding over Hori's shooting, or an arrest of Manako, if there was one. That record stays with the police.
4. **This replaces Claude's #33 proposal** that the hearing was held behind closed doors because an alien was part of the evidence. That proposal is withdrawn. The distrust is the reason.

**Filled in by Claude — proposals, veto freely:**

- **Why they distrust each other: jurisdiction.** The Directorate works outside the kingdom's own national lines, which already rubs the army and navy the wrong way (`dossier-the-directorate.md` §1a). It takes anything alien, and the police take everything else. Every incident with both in it gets split down the middle and never stitched back together. The Envoy incident was the classic case: the Directorate took the body, and the police took the shooting.
- **The official line suits everyone.** "Magically summoned" is an ordinary thing to write on Golarion, so nobody asks further. The police keep their officer's inquiry to themselves. And the Directorate files the cult as dabblers whose summoning went wrong once. That is part of why the cult is a non-issue to it (#34).
- **The official line is a clue and a false trail at once.** The party can find it in Directorate records. It points at the cult summoning monsters, which is exactly what the first attacks will seem to show, since each one comes where Sap was released. It is wrong: the Envoy wasn't summoned. It came to a meeting (`hori.md`).
- **The police's record is a real route to Aoi's name**, and a harder one because of the distrust: Directorate trainees asking the police for anything get nowhere quickly.
