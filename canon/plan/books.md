# The five Books

The campaign in five Books of chapters. Each Book's answer is the next Book's question. Three things climb together, Book by Book: the party's rank, how open their jobs are, and what the Watcher knows; Manako's mood moves with them.

## Book I · Grunt Work
- The question: What is attacking the city?
- The party: Recruits in the section nobody wants, driving converted diggers.
- Rank: Recruits to Duty Section (Clearance 0 to 2).
- Their jobs: Assigned in Episodes One and Two. From Chapter 2 the party chooses the order of its leads from a board, and the Plot Room calls it in whatever it chooses.
- Against them: Vetch's paperwork. The Watcher's first children, which can only form where Cicada has released Sap, so every attack looks like the cult's work. Five gangs, each selling the cult one service. The cult's own answers to the investigation: Kiri's card, the Fixer, and the Soldier's squad, which doesn't kill.
- What the Watcher knows: Things that move are alive; Frames matter.
- Manako: Patient: kind, for real. A patient man's children are aimless.
- The breather: The night off, and school.
- It ends: The party is at the square before the creatures form, the official story cracks, they are moved out from under Vetch, and Gen 2 is offered.
Section Nine: the worst jobs, Gen 1 clankers, and a petty superior.

## Book II · The Big Leagues
- The question: Who is doing it?
- The party: Duty Section, then Officers: the Directorate's sharp end.
- Rank: Duty Section to Officers (Clearance 2 to 3).
- Their jobs: Assigned, but the method is theirs.
- Against them: The Watcher seeking out their machines, then their people. Their own Directorate, which reads their mail. Cicada going public.
- What the Watcher knows: Frames have pilots; pilots have people.
- Manako: Patient, then Delighted: someone finally takes him seriously. A delighted man's children are showy.
- The breather: Founding Day: the painting of the four, Rupiah cutting the ribbon, and the anthem about the harbour.
- It ends: Down in the dig, with the search ship and the thing coiled around it.
The Directorate's hotshots, flying Anabala's Gen 2.

## Book III · The Reel
- The question: What is it?
- The party: Officers, then Command.
- Rank: Officers to Command (Clearance 3 to 4).
- Their jobs: Only the problem is given. The goal is theirs to set.
- Against them: The Watcher going for the Directorate itself, then for the party's own tactics. The column. The crime lords' money. A city in panic.
- What the Watcher knows: There are institutions; the party's own tactics.
- Manako: Delighted, then Cornered: he learns he was only ever a subcontractor, and accepts becoming a monster. A cornered man's children are wasteful.
- The breather: A dinner with the sceptics: the first room in the campaign where everyone was right.
- It ends: With the accusation, which is the players' to make, and with knowing what the Watcher is.
A unit with clearance and its own operations.

## Book IV · The Promise Comes Due
- The question: Can it be stopped?
- The party: Command, then Prodigies: the city does what they say, and the Directorate's resources are theirs. They earn it by showing extraordinary ability and judgement.
- Rank: Command to Prodigies (Clearance 4 to 5): the whole Directorate.
- Their jobs: Many at once, with finite means. They choose which.
- Against them: People-shaped children, then children that feed themselves. Abraxas beside the Watcher. A weapon it doesn't understand.
- What the Watcher knows: There are people; it can feed itself; it can build what its makers built.
- Manako: Decided closer to the time. As drafted: Frightened, then broken, and a frightened man's children are enormous and incoherent.
- The breather: Graduation, played straight, under a sky full of eyes for anyone holding a lens.
- It ends: With the rift open over the city, and a ship lifting.
The last pilots, the ones the city looks to.

## Book V · The Long Way Home
- The question: What do we do with it?
- The party: A ship and its crew of thousands. Each player commands their own part of it (a Frame wing, a department, a deck), so nobody's turn goes into a shared token.
- Rank: Past the ranks: a starship.
- Their jobs: Theirs to set.
- Against them: Distance, and the clock at home: the rift growing, and the Watcher still probing the city's defences.
- What the Watcher knows: Whatever the party taught it.
- Manako: Decided closer to the time.
- Deliberately loose: The home world is Ingram's, and so is the ending. No spaceship combat: every fight is at personnel scale or Frame scale.
Command staff of a starship, with nobody above them.
