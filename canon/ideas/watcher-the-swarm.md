# Ideas: the Watcher

Claude's proposals and readings about the Watcher and its children, from the old Watcher dossier and the old antagonist files. None of them is a fact until Ingram takes one up.

## idea--the-voice-changes
The Watcher's voice changes over the campaign, and the change is itself a clue. Early, the drowning instrument is frantic, exhausted, polite and unable to round a number: "There are four thousand one hundred and nine people awake in the harbour district. I know what each of them is doing. I did not ask to know. A woman on the third street from the water has been crying for eleven minutes and I have counted every one and I cannot stop counting and I do not know what crying is. I was built to watch a place where nothing happened. I was very good. Nobody has read my reports in a long time." Later, the learner is calm, patient, precise and interested, the voice already drafted for it. Use it sparingly, never gloating, never cruel for effect.

## idea--claimants-stake-claims
The city's bureaucratic word for the Watcher's children, Claimant, is accidentally correct: its children stake claims to ground for it to watch, because expanding is one of the things it wants.

## idea--escheat
Escheat, property going to the state when there is no rightful heir, has somewhere real to point: the ships belong to the mitflits, the generation ship's crew's descendants, so the Directorate digging up the search ship is claiming an estate that does have an heir. A blank inheritance is one whose heir hasn't been named. It also seeds the question of what the Frames themselves are a claim on.

## idea--nobody-did-evil
The heart of the fear: nobody in this story has ever done anything evil. A frightened man, a flinching officer, an institution that wouldn't stop digging, and an objective with no limit. To observe more the Watcher must be more; to be more it must be fed; to be fed it must not be stopped. Its horror is how reasonable each step is.

## idea--children-roster
Following the XCOM model, the Watcher's children are a roster of enemy types with different roles, so the players learn them one kind at a time. Ingram's words: "a roster of enemy 'Children of the Watcher' - different roles." What the roles are is not set. (Settled elsewhere: they are building-sized, strange and flowing, and nanomachines: D084, D085.)

## idea--lab-research-ladder
A laboratory is a downtime activity, and what the group can do with a dead child is a ladder they climb by research. At first even examining the bodies is impossible. Later they research how to stop the bodies decomposing. Later still they learn to capture a child. The laboratory also pays out fun bonuses for the players, unlocks new technology, and teaches them the enemy's threat. Ingram's words: "at first even examining the dead bodies is impossible, later on the group gets the chance to research how to prevent the decomposition of the bodies, then later they learn how to capture." Recorded only; she has not asked for it to be built or placed on a chapter. Claude's marked readings: the decomposition is possibly linked to `watcher.children` (the pieces of a beaten child drain towards the dig) and `watcher.children-are-nanomachines`, but she has not said so; the downtime rules are for when she asks for them, and must pay out something real.
