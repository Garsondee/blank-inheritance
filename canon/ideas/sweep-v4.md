# Ideas: what the old story bible kept
Themes, images and moments from the older whole-campaign bible that no built scene or planned beat carries. None is scheduled; each waits for a scene that wants it.

## idea--everything-becomes-what-it-was-mistaken-for
Everything in this setting becomes what it was mistaken for. The Watcher was mistaken for a monster, so it inferred that being a monster is its function. Manako was mistaken for a madman, so he became one. The mitflits were mistaken for tools, so they were used as tools. Shinobu was mistaken for a contaminant, caged for it, and is slowly becoming something. A Gen 2 is mistaken for a weapon, and it is alive. The kids are mistaken for pilots, a substitute part, and will become pilots regardless. Being seen wrongly is worse than not being seen at all, which inverts the apparent moral: Cicada says hide, the Watcher wants to be seen, and the evidence says neither is the answer. See accurately.

## idea--a-thing-in-the-wrong-container
Everything here is a thing in the wrong container, and the story is about whether anything ever gets out: a biomechanical core in a riveted steel shell that does not fit it (a Gen 2); mitflits reverted from a star-faring form into small frightened bodies (the wild ones); Anabala, two beings sharing one body through the ages; the Hand, detached and living independently; Rupiah, forced back into a role his family chose; Hyuck, a person made into a weapon; the Watcher, an awareness with no hull. Nothing in the setting needs to state any of this. It only needs to keep being true.

## idea--the-three-modes
Each Gen 2 is a biomechanical core inside a steel exoskeleton. The Directorate has tried many shell geometries, some fail catastrophically, and all develop the same hole in the same position, which they call metal fatigue; it is the core making a seat. A Gen 2 could be run three ways. Autonomous mode exists but is unusable, because the communication it needs is a language only the mitflits have. Direct control, overriding the core and driving the body, is the only mode humanity has, and it is a violation. The third mode is a mitflit standing outside a Frame talking to the core while a human rides the cavity: cooperation, which nobody has tried because nobody believed there was anyone in there to cooperate with. All of it rests on a piece of Mother being in every Gen 2. If there is, this stands and cooperation is how a mitflit talks to her; if there isn't, "violation" and "anyone in there" both need rethinking, since the core is not a living mitflit.

## idea--nobody-has-to-die
Nobody has to die is a first-class ending, not a hidden option. The Watcher's children are condensations, so there are no corpses on the enemy side and every death in the campaign is one a human caused. Capturing Manako is the tactically correct choice, not merely the merciful one. Every major confrontation offers kill, capture, destroy and protect as visible, supported options, and the adventure adjusts. The content of the scene is the choice, not the outcome. A character trained toward a lethal specialism is later offered a side encounter in which they are asked to take a shot at Manako, non-lethally.

## idea--five-trails
Five trails through the mystery, any of which a character can pick up. The sky: where attention pools is where things appear. The power: industrial synthesis needs enormous power, so municipal load going somewhere unaccounted for finds the column. The timing: where every response unit goes, and when, gives away the demonstration and heist correlation. The ground: apertures open where the ground has been watched. The word: a mitflit says "the Observer" in a flat technical way, and nobody in the room is frightened. The last two predate the Watcher's present shape and need reworking.

## idea--contradictory-testimony
The conference is not run as a flashback. It is reconstructed through contradictory testimony, as everything in the campaign should be: Rika, Nobu, Hori, Shinobu, retired Directorate staff, cultists in cells and mitflit elders each hold a piece and colour it with their own stake, and the party is told plainly that everything anyone has to say might matter. It is the theme run as a game mechanic: a story about who gets believed, played by deciding who to believe.

## idea--six-threads
Six recurring lines run in parallel through every chapter, each with a question and a currency. The War: contact and Frame combat, tactical stakes; can the city be defended? The Observed: personal surveillance, the stray, Shinobu, treated lenses, dread and intimacy; what does it mean to be seen? The Cult: demonstrations, dossiers, the magic show, the column, the heist loop, investigation and moral complexity; who deserves to be believed? The Mitflits: arrival, Anabala, the sit-in, generational politics, found family and ethical conflict with allies; what do you owe someone you are using to save yourself? The Founders: the old party in flashback and institutional secrets, glory curdling into complicity; were they right, and does it matter now? School: the downtime economy and ordinary teenage life under pressure, small stakes that matter enormously; what does the city owe them back? School is live in every chapter; it is the thread most likely to quietly atrophy under table pressure.

## idea--the-kindest-payoff
The Watcher's early incompetence, fighting a statue and losing to a reflection, is not a joke at the audience's expense and not a trap. It is the setup for the kindest payoff available: it was never stupid. It was new, and it was drowning, and nobody had ever spoken to it.

## idea--the-asking
The mitflits enter Frames only to defend, and being asked to attack is the deepest violation of that. At the end the party has to ask them: the battle is the epilogue, and the scene is the asking. Whichever of home or here the mitflits choose, both endings are honest. Still open: who does the asking, and what it costs them.
