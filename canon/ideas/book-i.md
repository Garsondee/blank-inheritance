# Ideas: Book I, rethought

Claude's proposals for Book I around Ingram's three combat tracks (D084). She loved almost all of them (D085); the adopted ones now live in the plan, the facts and the new people.

## idea--book-i-braided
Book I is one braided case. The three combat tracks are tied together by Cicada's chemical: the Faithful release it at demonstrations; the gangs move the cult's paint, venues, permits, crowds and crates and steal chemical stock under cover of the demonstrations; and the Watcher's children can only form where the chemical was released. Every fight on every track hands the party one piece of one picture, and the official line (cultists summon the things) fits everything they see until they can test it. Book I ends when they prove it wrong with their own eyes.

## idea--episode-one-retuned
Episode One keeps its shape and gains three changes. The Road In splits into six short solo scenes, one per player, each a way into Kurose with one check, something to learn and something planted. The Errand expects the players to guess that the bubble docket is a prank within seconds, says so out loud, and lets them choose how to beat it, with short optional stops that each have their own reward and none that sends them on. The jam in the flood tunnel becomes a stack of smuggled crates, a hint of the criminal side and nothing more.

## idea--errand-tour
The Errand as a tour: each shopkeeper cheerfully sends the recruits on to the next warm place in turn, so the afternoon becomes a walk through the places they will defend. Her players would guess they were being messed with, and a chain of places that cannot help them makes the bubble worse.

## idea--episode-two-lead-in
Episode Two's lead-in. The Override is the mayor's initiative arriving without the mayor's name on it: an order from above Vetch's head naming the six. The Night Off puts them in the Paper Lantern while a gang leans on its keeper. Slack Water finds an old violet stencil under the sea wall's growth. Every scene before the exercise gives them someone in the city to like.

## idea--first-contact-dilemma
The first real fight is built so that acting is the only sensible or heroic choice, and the players know the price. Vetch orders them to hold. The Comms Officer is unsure what the three things are, disagrees with him, tries the duty officer, the Deputy Director's office and the Plot Room supervisor, and cannot get a better answer quickly. She gives the party everything she has and orders nothing. A tram with forty people aboard has stopped between the three things and the old town, and the duty lance is twelve minutes out. Afterwards Vetch charges them, the Deputy Director's room is frightening, Doctor Mordant asks for a private word, and the episode ends in Hangar Two with their names still chalked on the board.

## idea--three-unknown-things
The three things of Episode Two are puzzles, very hard together, each with its own way of moving and one weakness, and all made of nanomachines, which nobody knows yet. The Loom is a tall lattice of dark threads that hangs above the roofs and weaves a web between buildings; whatever touches a thread is bound; its knot is the weak point. The Bell is a smooth dome that drifts at head height, sings one low tone and rolls towards noise; engines and sirens make its shockwaves worse, so silence is the answer. The Tide is a low sheet of dark liquid that spreads along the road, then stands up into a wave that sweeps the tram; it flows round obstacles and is fought from high ground. Help is a dial: the harbour battery, Vermilion Block casters on the roofs, the duty lance, and last the Young One's combat Frame.

## idea--g1-movers
The Watcher's next children, in Chapter 2, hunt whatever moves: trams, running crowds, machines on the move. Stillness hides you. The Faithful at the site stand still by doctrine and are spared, which looks like proof that the cult controls the creatures, so the official line gets stronger before it breaks. The fight is red light, green light at building scale.

## idea--five-gangs
Five gangs, each with a flavour, a fight of its own and one service it sells the cult, so each raid yields a piece of one supply chain and every trail ends at the Hanabusa Booking Office. The Salt Dogs run the harbour by whistle and move the cult's crates. The Brass Choir fights to the tempo of a band that never stops, and rents Kiri her back rooms. The Hanko Men are polite forgers who burn their ledgers when raided, and sell permits for the marked squares. The Rust Saints are scrap salvagers in loader rigs welded from Frame parts, and repair weapons that no Golarion workshop could have made. The Sakura are hired crowds, in which paid civilians and real ones look alike, and fill the marked squares before a demonstration.

## idea--cult-answers
How the cult answers the party's investigation, softest first. Persuade: Kiri's card goes to whichever of them Mrs. Ando saw on the way in. Obstruct: the Fixer has evidence disappear. Then fight: the Soldier's squad ambushes them to take evidence back, non-lethally, and calls them "Section Nine". Those the party takes alive are educated, competent and kind, the opposite of what the city assumes, and each gives up a small amount in debrief.

## idea--book-i-last-day
Book I's last day. The violet marks, the booking office's calendar and the discredited papers let the party predict a demonstration at several squares at once, with a chemical-warehouse heist across town under its cover. Vetch will not hear it, so they choose where to be. At the square they are there first: the dogs leave, the barometer twitches, the attention gathers, and only then do the Faithful release the chemical and the children form. The official line cracks in front of witnesses, and they are moved out from under Vetch.

## idea--clues-in-the-haunts-and-manako
Each rest from Episode One carries one road to a Book I clue, so whichever the players choose, the clues reach them: the night observer's barometer twitches before each contact; the bathhouse's retired fitter knows exactly where a Gen 1's joints fail; the card game's regulars know the Hanko Men; a Ward Four nurse is one of the Faithful. Manako is their kindest friend in the city, and once tells one of them to stay off a certain square on a certain afternoon, which turns out to be the afternoon of a demonstration: one odd clue for the player who suspects him, and nothing more.

## idea--faction-plans-book-i
What each side is doing while the party acts, chapter by chapter, so they thwart each other. The Watcher: alien things (Episode Two), then movers (2), then machine-breakers built against their refits (3), then it learns it is being tracked (4). Cicada: quiet demonstrations and recruiting shows; notices Section Nine on its supply chain (2); persuades, obstructs, then ambushes (3); its biggest demonstration yet, covering a heist (4). The crime lords: a turf war on the docks (2); a price on Section Nine, felt at the party's favourite haunt, and a forged order if the Counting House was skipped (3); the heist (4). The mayor: the order naming the six (Episode Two); public praise for "our Directorate cadets" on the radio (2); a raid wanted for the papers (3 and 4). The Directorate: Vetch's grunt work and the official line throughout; the hearing (Episode Two); Doctor Mordant watching the six; the move out from under Vetch (4).
