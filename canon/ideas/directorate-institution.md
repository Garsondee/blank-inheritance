# Ideas: the Directorate as an institution

## idea--the-mirror-of-two-institutions
Manako and the Directorate are the same failure at different scales. Each is built on one buried moment of panic (Manako's burner in a lab; the Directorate's container at Kortos), each built a faith on it (a religion; a state), each hides it (he has arranged his life so the question can't be asked; it locks what it digs up in deep storage), each tells the young what they are for, each is right about the other, and neither can see itself. The campaign's thesis, that nobody ever did anything evil and every catastrophe is an ordinary person flinching and then building a life on the moment, is demonstrated twice instead of stated once. Never have an adult explain it: the mirror is the audience's to notice, and the party stands between the two and is lied to by both.

## idea--the-institution-absorbs-reform
A party that tries to reform the Directorate finds it absorbing the effort politely and changing nothing, which is its own, more interesting outcome. Several of its faults are two centuries old and structural, and the campaign's win condition is not institutional reform.

## idea--nobody-to-unmask
Every institution in the campaign is sincere, calcified and wrong about its own history. The history teacher believes his book; a civil defence instructor has applied four times to have her drill reinstated and been refused on cost by people who aren't wrong that nothing has ever happened; the Transparency Advocate can't see what is in the vaults; the Deputy Director tells the party they were wrong, would give the same order again, is glad they are alive, and means all three. So antagonist pressure has to come from outside the Directorate: there is no one inside it to unmask. The Directorate being badly wrong doesn't make Cicada right, and the campaign should never resolve into picking a side: both are wrong in the same way about the same thing. Nothing about the Directorate is a twist; every fault is discoverable by looking, hidden the way institutional facts are, in plain sight, boring, and nobody's job to ask about.

## idea--mitflits-feel-the-ship
Wild mitflits feel something of their ancestors' ship in a machine made of living flesh, and can't say what, which is why they are uncomfortable around a Gen 2. Uncomfortable isn't unable: a wild mitflit can still fly one, fits it, and hates it.

## idea--true-forms-hidden
Player characters with a visibly non-human ancestry present as ordinary humans, and the player chooses whether and when to reveal their true nature. The reason it works in the fiction is not decided, and it could connect individual backstories to the wider mystery.

## idea--enduring-not-restoring
The campaign's trajectory is dire, not utopian: the Directorate takes damage, the city takes real visible scars, and nobody wins their way back to how things were. The throughline is enduring, not restoring, so even clean tactical victories should cost something real.

## idea--the-core-theme
The campaign's throughline is a group that struggles, and then finally succeeds, in defining its own objectives, its own process and its own independence, against a system that demands great sacrifice from them while imposing rigid inflexibility and a constant critique of their results that never fails to find some small failure. The Directorate and the school should be that specific kind of institution: one that extracts everything from these young people and never concedes that it was enough.

## idea--themes-on-both-sides
Mass surveillance and the runaway run on both sides of the fence. The Directorate reads its trainees' messages as routine policy while the Watcher reads the same traffic natively; the runaway is the Watcher's children learning to make their own Sap, and it is also an institution that won't stop digging. Nobody in the chain is evil, which is what makes it frightening.

## idea--the-watchers-early-voice
Written before the Watcher became the generation ship's lost eyes, its first-person answer to "who are you" is a bureaucratic, legalistic voice echoing the Claimants' property-dispute framing, vast and patient and non-human: "A name is a claim. I have spent this whole war on the other side of that word: I file claims, I do not answer to them." It has "a jurisdiction, shaped like your city", never fights directly, sends its chosen and takes notes behind their eyes, replays the party's own past actions back to them ("I'll show you the reel"), and treats the party's victory as its own defeat: "I am the sum of everything you survive. Survive all of it, and I'm done." It is the voice of the Watcher later in the campaign, once it has learned.

## idea--rupiahs-nemesis
A pitch from the old campaign, never played: Rupiah has a camp, chaotic neutral nemesis from his performing past, once a friend and rival, who fell out with him and now leads a search party of fake travelling performers (a swashbuckler, a fire breather, a mime, a musician and a mitflit), disguised, hired to find him, and likely to run or surrender rather than die. The hunters might capture the nemesis, thinking him Rupiah, and force him to perform for the lords on the hill.
