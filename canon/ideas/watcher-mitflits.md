# Ideas: the mitflits

Proposals about the mitflits from the old species notes, kept so a scene that wants them can find them. The rule under all of them stands: the reveal is slow and sympathetic, and nothing morally troubling about the mitflits comes before the campaign has made them feel like people.

## idea--the-slow-reveal
A gradual, increasingly sympathetic run of mitflit moments across the chapters: a mitflit glimpsed salvaging something small at a wreck, chased off, not followed up; later a captured mitflit, foul-mouthed, terrified and spiteful, who goes quiet near a Frame and can't say why (once, weeping at the sight of a Frame's hand; now better as discomfort near a Gen 2); then mitflits arriving in the city in numbers, housed badly, complained about in the papers; then the sit-in. Compressing it, or starting anywhere but its beginning, is the mistake to avoid.

## idea--the-first-mitflit
The party's first mitflit, whenever it comes: on a harbour patrol, among flood barriers and tide marks, a mitflit caught in a wall trap, furious and refusing help it obviously needs, and the choice whether anyone frees it. It must play as one specific, sympathetic, suffering person worth freeing, and read as wrong and upsetting, never as pest control.

## idea--rapid-adaptation
Perhaps the mitflits' special ability is rapid evolutionary adaptation: they revert into whatever form is likeliest to survive their circumstances, and keep the capacity to regrow their original features when required. If so, they didn't merely degrade; they adapted downward to survive, and the High form is what they could regrow, not something new.

## idea--why-gen-2-unsettles
Why wild mitflits are uncomfortable around Gen 2: they feel something of their ancestors' ship in a machine made of living flesh, and can't say what. Uncomfortable isn't unable: a wild mitflit can still fly a Gen 2, fit it, and hate it.

## idea--mother-in-the-cores
If a small part of Mother is in every Gen 2, Anabala took the pieces from the generation ship's reasoning core under the access she bargained for, so each Gen 2 carries a piece of the mind the Watcher used to report to. That would be why the Seekers are drawn hard to Gen 2, and why the machines aren't usable by just anyone.
