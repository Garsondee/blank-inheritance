# The re-read

version 3 · 2026-10-01

Read the scene as a GM who has never seen this repo. Answer every question, fix what fails, and record the answers in
`scenes/<id>.reread.md`. When Ingram's reading of a scene finds something these questions missed, add a question here
and raise the version. A question keeps its number for good; new ones go at the end of their group.

Version 2 added R16 to R25, and widened R3 and R12, from Ingram's read of Scene 0 (D048). Version 3 added R26 to R28
from her reading of The Road In in the Scene Runner (D056); the house style they check is
`design/gm-facing-prose-style.md` §0.

**Does every part earn its place?**
- **R1** For each part: what does the GM do with it at the table, and what does a player get from it (something to
  do, decide, roll, learn, keep or laugh at)? If neither, cut it, or move it to the scene's `notes`.
- **R2** Would the scene be worse without it?
- **R16** Does each direction tell the GM something about delivery that is truly critical? Ingram knows how to run a
  table and how to deliver prose, and she skips a direction to get to the words, so anything less than critical goes.
- **R17** Does anything tell the GM not to do something? Keep a negative only if it's critical.
- **R18** Does the scene prepare for something its own shape rules out, such as answers to questions during a speech
  nobody can interrupt?

**Is it clean?**
- **R3** Does anything the GM reads mention how the scene was made: decision numbers, names, dates of changes,
  "no longer", ideas that were cut, the state of the tools, or what another scene does? If so, take it out, and say
  what to do instead.

**Does it give away too much?**
- **R12** Does anything tell the players something they shouldn't know yet? Something everyone in Kurose knows can
  still be held back from the table, so check `told`, not only `known`. The check catches cited facts; read for
  anything uncited.
- **R19** For each answer the scene gives: could it be a question instead? Is this the moment for it, or could it come
  later, found with a skill roll, perhaps in two places that each sell it from a different angle?
- **R20** Does any line tell the players what the story means or what they're meant to do? Let play show it.

**Do the players roll?**
- **R4** Would the scene be better with a skill roll here? Wherever a player character acts, is there a check?
  (Scene 0 is the one scene with no rolls, D048.)
- **R5** Does a dilemma the group faces resolve through skill rolls?
- **R6** On a critical failure, is there an escalation? On a failure, a way forward? On a critical success, something
  extra?

**Who's in it?**
- **R7** For each NPC the party can talk to: what do they say freely, what on a check (the skill, the DC, the result),
  and what never, with the line they deflect with?
- **R8** What will the group probably ask, and what's the answer, in the speaker's own voice?
- **R9** Does every NPC present have a stat block, with at least Perception, Will and a Deception DC?
- **R10** If the group attacks, lies, steals, sneaks, follows someone, splits up or walks out, what happens, and what
  does the GM roll?

**Is it well said?**
- **R15** Read the read-aloud out loud. Does it sound like a person talking to a table?
- **R21** Are there clipped fragments, or pithy lines that squeeze the meaning ("The base. The fence. The gate.")? Say
  it in full sentences a listener can follow the first time. Ingram's own rhythm is the model: "And the gods said yes,
  but in hundreds of years of time and thousands of miles of distance."
- **R22** Is every word this campaign coined (Frame, glamour) explained the first time the players hear it?
- **R23** Is the scene the length its job needs? An opening that sets up a whole world needs room; a quick scene
  doesn't.
- **R26** Is the most important thing first: in the scene, in each beat, in each field and in each sentence? A beat
  line starts with what happens or what the GM does; a note's first line is the one that breaks the scene if missed.
- **R27** Is each thing said once, in the place it's used? Count the places a rule or a detail appears, Runner and prep
  notes together, and cut every copy but the one where the GM needs it.
- **R28** Is every field inside its budget (§0 of the style guide)? If a field won't fit, it's two beats, a list, or
  prep notes.

**Does it hold up?**
- **R11** Is there one thing a player would retell?
- **R13** Does every fact the scene states agree with canon, and is it cited?
- **R14** Is the scene's rough day and time in the plan, and do "yesterday", "this morning" and the like agree with it?
  (`story time`)
- **R24** Does every place agree with the map and with the other scenes? (The old town is on the waterfront.)
- **R25** Did the scene use all the detail Ingram gave on what it covers? Read the decisions and source records its
  facts cite, not only the facts, and bring back anything the scene lost.
