# Opening Titles — the campaign's anime opening

**Status: drafted, 2026-09-25; finished on 2026-09-26: all 112 source shots covered from Ingram's four batches, no slates, a five-second tail, and an episode title card to follow it. Lead beats 4–6 hold temporary faces until the group's own character art comes.** A production plan, built one step at a time. Step 1 (R00–R05, the sung opening), Step 2 (R06–R10, the title), Step 3 (R11–R13, the verse and the sunset), Step 4 (R14–R17, the pre-chorus) and Step 5 (R18–R31, the sortie build and the founders) are rendered. Steps 6–8 (R32–R111, chorus one, the flashes, the last chorus and the end card) play to the end of the song, part art and effects, part slates. Claude directs and produces; Ingram
makes the Midjourney art. The decisions in §2 are Ingram's (LEDGER #37). Everything marked *proposal* is Claude's
and can be vetoed.

**In short:** a 90-second anime opening that plays at the start of every session. It is a close, shot-for-shot
homage to the opening titles of *Neon Genesis Evangelion* (1995), cut to that opening's own TV-length song and
recast with this campaign: the four founders as they really are, the Gen 1 Frames, Kurose, the Directorate's
people, glimpses of the enemies, and one close-up for each of the six. **The timing is the source's own, to the
frame**; the pictures are Midjourney's; the typography, the animation and the edit are built here.

This document names the source because it is GM-facing. Nothing on screen names it, or any real place.

**Seventh round, on the rooftop and the tape:**

- **Heat haze on the mitflit's rooftop** ("a soft, subtle heat haze to it, as if it were high summer... a slight pan
  might help make it feel more alive"). The ground and the rooftop shimmer, the high sky doesn't, the credit stays

  have so far"), from the pre-roll to the last built shot. Claude tells her where the file is rather than sending
  it.
- **Softer, fainter tape lines** ("soften and lower the opacity of the VHS horizontal lines"). The scan-line noise
  is lower and blurred across neighbouring lines. Dropouts are pale, tapered streaks at about half strength instead
  of solid white scratches.
- **A pre-roll of three seconds before frame 0** ("start with a 'tracking issue' and add some blank space, just a
  few seconds"; "do the VHS text on the screen too... as if it were changing channel"). In order:
  1. the set flicks through two empty channels of static, CH 01 and CH 02, with a frame of black between them
  2. it lands on CH 03, the VCR's blue screen
  3. PLAY ▶ comes up and the tape starts with its tracking off, bands of white dashes rolling up the black
  4. the TRACKING meter hunts down, overshoots and settles, and the picture locks
  5. half a second of clean black, then the programme

  The captions are the VCR's own: chunky green blocks with a hard shadow, laid over the tape after its noise, so
  none of it touches them. The static hisses under the two empty channels and the tape hisses faintly until it
  locks. The song itself still starts where it did.

## 3. How it gets made

### 3.1 Who does what

- **Ingram:** runs the prompts in `design/opening-titles-prompts.md`, a few at a time, and saves each result into
  `artwork/in-tray/video/` named by its code. Watches the drafts and gives notes by shot ID ("R35 feels slow").
- **Claude:** writes the prompts; processes every drop (cut-outs, upscaling, colour, conforming video); animates the
  stills (slides, push-ins, parallax between separated layers); builds all typography and effects; cuts to the
  music; renders drafts; does the finishing pass.

### 3.2 Folders

All of these are under the gitignored `artwork/`, because they hold third-party and generated material.

- `artwork/title-sequences/reference/source-op/`: the source, analysed. Every distinct frame, the time map, the beat
  grid, the separated stems, the continuity (`analysis/reference-continuity.json`) and the reference video.
- `artwork/title-sequences/opening/style-refs/`: 18 clean frames from the source, for Midjourney's style reference.
  `_contact-sheet.png` shows them all.
- `artwork/in-tray/video/`: where Ingram saves Midjourney results, **named by their prompt code** (`R02-sky.png`). Claude files them into
  `artwork/title-sequences/opening/art/` (moved, never copied, as with all in-tray art), and the timeline points there.
- `artwork/title-sequences/opening/work/` and `renders/`: processed assets and drafts, made by the tool.
- `artwork/title-sequences/typography-tests/`: type studies.

### 3.3 The tool

`tools/title-sequence/` in Python (numpy, Pillow, OpenCV, ffmpeg), with tests in `test/title-sequence/` in the
same stdlib `unittest` style as `test/city-map/`. See its `README.md` for how to run it.

**Built so far (2026-09-25):**

- **The renderer's three modes.** `draft` is the whole opening with the music; shots still waiting on their art show
  the source, greyed and tagged REF, so every draft plays end to end. `compare` puts the source and ours side by
  side, optionally slowed down. `stills` renders single frames.
- **The timeline,** `data/title-sequences/opening.json`, with its schema. It holds the 112-shot cut list and the
  clips. Keys may be given in frames; they hold like cel drawings, or glide per parameter.
- **Layers:**
  - Ingram's art as image layers: pans, tilts, push-ins, turns, fades, and normal or screen blends. A layer can
    crop its art, and grade it with fill (a flat silhouette colour), levels, gamma, tint, brightness and lift.
  - **Cut-outs** (`titleseq/matte.py`), for art Midjourney paints on a plain backdrop:
    - The backdrop is fitted as a smooth surface to the image's border.
    - Opacity follows each pixel's distance from it ("flat"), or its darkness for ink on paper ("dark").
    - At the edge, opacity is solved against the art's own colour, sampled just inside, so no rim of old backdrop
      survives on the new sky.
    - The anime background remover once planned for this isn't needed.
  - **Eased moves:** a clip's keys can ease in, out or both, so a move keyed at its two ends is one smooth curve.
  - **Exposures:** a clip's `on` (2 or 3) holds each drawing for that many frames. Its moves and its own clock
    step; its fades don't. Since the eighth round only drawn effects use it: every camera move over art is on ones.
  - **Slates** (`slate`): the text-only card holding a shot's place until its art comes: its code, what belongs
    there, and the prompt that makes it, over a field in the shot's colour, or over an effect with no field. Since
    the ninth round, each is set as a title card: a justified block of heavy capitals, an amber lead-in, the code
    huge and faint behind it, corner ticks, and dark ink on pale fields.
  - **Film type:** a text line can be fitted to an exact box and thickened. A text layer can be softened,
    mottled, sprayed like a stencil, given a halo, and made to weave and flicker from frame to frame.
  - **More effects:**
    - `field`: a cel-painted background
    - `orb`: a cel-shaded glowing sphere
    - `ray_fan`: rays of light fanning out and unfurling
    - `arcs`: lightning
    - `radar`: a phosphor scope with an afterglow and contacts
    - `data_bars`: a readout panel
    - `streaks`: speed lines
    - halation in the tape finish
  - **The column of light** (`light_column`): the orange field of rising streaks and sparks the Frame stands in,
    re-drawn on twos.
  - **Art with its own alpha** (`keyed`), such as the Directorate's approved seal, and **upright type** (`vertical`)
    for the director card.
  - **The tape finish:**
    - over every frame: colour smeared sideways, soft picture with an edge ring, soft scan-line noise, a wobble,
      the torn head-switching lines at the bottom, lifted blacks and the odd pale dropout
    - `vhs_damage` layers at chosen moments: a tracking band tearing the picture, with pale dropout streaks
    - `heat_haze`: a high-summer shimmer over everything beneath it, rising, strongest at the bottom of the frame
  - **The pre-roll** (the timeline's `preroll`, frames -72 to -1): `tape_blank` layers (empty-channel static, the
    tape playing with its tracking off) and `osd` layers (the VCR's captions, drawn after the tape finish), with
    static and hiss laid under them in the soundtrack. A draft without `--from` opens on it; `--no-preroll` leaves
    it off.
  - **Hand-drawn speed lines:** tapered wedges with uneven edges, re-inked on twos. They fire from a point keyed
    to follow the camera's subject, their flight can speed up smoothly, and they can curve.
  - **Several shapes in one polygon layer** (hazard stripes, tunnel lamps), placed to a sixteenth of a pixel.
  - **Every text layer says whether it is a credit or text in the world** (a stencil, a counter), so the credit
    roster test counts only credits.
  - **Video layers:** a Midjourney clip, cut out frame by frame against one fitted backdrop so its edge never
    shimmers, or a folder of PNGs Ingram has keyed herself, which play with their own alpha. It can play on twos or
    threes, once, looped or back and forth.
  - Typeset credits in the heavy mincho.
  - Drifting dust.
  - The measured effects: R01's raindrop ring, and R05's burst and wash.

### 3.4 The process, and versions

**Step by step, from the start of the video to the end** (Ingram's instruction):

1. Claude names the next few things to generate.

## 4. The source, measured

- **The time map.** The source video's audio runs 0.15% fast against the track file, a transfer quirk, which adds up
  to about three frames by the end. Every cut time is mapped with that drift corrected (video time = 2.026 s +
  0.998482 × track time), with a median error of 3 ms.
- **Tempo.** 128.45 BPM and steady from the band's entry. Bar 1 is at 12.333 s; a bar lasts 1.868 s. The sung
  opening before it (0–12.3 s) keeps a loose pulse.
- **The song's map,** from the separated vocal stem:

  | Section | From |
  |---|---|
  | Sung opening | 0 s |
  | Band entry | bar 1 (12.33 s) |
  | Title | bars 1–5 |
  | Verse | bar 6 (vocal from 22.1 s) |
  | Pre-chorus | bar 14 (36.6 s) |
  | The long build | bars 19–24 |
  | Chorus | bar 25 (vocal from 59.0 s) |
  | Last phrase | bar 33 (73.9 s), sung to 88.8 s, rings out to 89.6 s |

- **How the source cuts,** which we keep:
  - The big transitions land on the "and" of beat 4, half a beat before the downbeat, following the vocal's
    anticipations.
  - The black title cards land just after the beat.
  - The four flash portraits land one per beat, on the off-beats.
  - **Our cut points are the source's own, to the frame.**
- **Sync is confirmed** at two hard hits: the white flash at the band's entry (12.76 s, against a hit at 12.751 s) and
  the crossed-beam flash (21.44 s, against 21.452 s).
- **The frames.** The source file is a 60 fps upscale of 24 fps animation. Its 1,586 distinct frames are extracted
  and every one was viewed. The continuity (112 shot units, R00–R111) is in `analysis/reference-continuity.json`.
- **The reference video,** `reference/source-op/reference-synced.mp4`: the source re-timed onto our track, with the
  shot ID, the bar and beat, and a vocal light in the side bars. Audio track 2 adds a beat click. **Use its shot IDs
  for notes.**

## 6. The Midjourney method

### 6.1 Matching the style

- **Name the show.** Ingram found that naming it outright is what gets the iconic look: "1995 TV anime screenshot,
  Neon Genesis Evangelion, Gainax, character design by Yoshiyuki Sadamoto 1995 TV anime cel animation". That
  reverses this section's first rule, which said never to name the series or its artists.
  `design/opening-titles-prompts.md` opens with that anchor and a list of additions to try. The additions include
  `original character`, to keep the show's own cast out.
- **No Midjourney parameters and no Niji** (Ingram): set 4:3 in the web interface, and use whichever model is giving
  the best anime results. *(Loosened 2026-09-26, while starting the propaganda manga: `--ar`, `--no` and similar
  modifiers are now welcome in any prompt. Still no Niji, never a version number, and no reference parameters,
  because Ingram attaches references by hand. See `design/propaganda-manga.md` §8.)*
- **Everything is generated new.** Existing portraits are never used in the video. The founders' opening stills may
  be attached as character references, to keep the likeness.

### 6.2 Layers

- **Characters on a plain flat pale-grey background,** so they can be cut out cleanly and slid over their own
  backgrounds.
- **Backgrounds without characters.** Wide plates (`--ar 3:1`) for pans, tall plates (`--ar 1:2`) for tilts.
  Midjourney's Pan and Zoom Out extend a plate that comes out too tight.
- **Light, steam and sparks on black,** so they can be added over a shot as a separate layer.

### 6.3 Motion

- **Midjourney video:** 5 s at 24 fps, extendable to about 21 s. It renders at 480p, or 720p in HD mode, which
  needs the Standard plan or above, in Fast mode.
- **Manual motion, Low motion** for anything subtle. **Start and end frames** for a controlled change, such as eyes
  closed to open. **Loop** for ambient plates.
- **Most shots last under a second,** so one 5 s clip usually feeds several shots; Claude cuts the best moment.

### 6.4 Codes and drops

Every prompt has a code (B0-02, B1-14 and so on). Save the chosen results into `drop/<code>/`, under any filenames:
PNG for stills, MP4 for video. Upscaling in Midjourney first is welcome but not needed, since everything is
upscaled here anyway.

### 6.5 The six temporary images

- **Deliberately non-specific:** trainees in plain new uniforms, faces lit hard or half in shadow, so nobody takes
  them for NPCs.
- **Each lead beat's composition stays fixed** when the real character replaces the temporary one.
- **Before the real-six cut, each player sees their own lead shot,** expression included. The character is
  theirs, and the opening must not decide what they feel.

## 7. Typography

- **Fonts.** All free (OFL or the GUST licence), kept in the gitignored `third-party/fonts/` and logged in
  `CREDITS.md`:

  - Noto Serif CJK JP Black: the wordmark.

- **The wordmark: BLΛNK INHERITΛNCE.** The source draws its A's without crossbars, so ours does too. A wide,
  heavy serif, white on black.
- **The logo: 空白相続, built 2026-09-25** (`tools/title-sequence/titleseq/logo.py`).
  - **Why hand-built:** the source's katakana logo isn't a font. Every stroke is a straight blade with hard
    angled cuts, and some are thrown out as long spikes.
  - **How:** each of the four kanji is drawn stroke by stroke as blades, following its real strokes. The blades
    are fattened, their cuts slanted, their horizontals raised to the right and their spikes thrown further.

    whole block, and edged with a thin pale outline.
  - **The composition:** the kanji cross the wordmark's lower edge, never bury it, with a few spikes piercing up
    through the letters. 第一部 sits above and pops in right after the second flare, where the source's small line
    appears (Ingram: "Book 1" in that slot). **Redrawn at the Step 3 review** (Ingram: slightly bigger, and the
    outline was losing its clarity). It is now 96 px, about the size of the source's own small line, in the bold
    weight with a red fill and the kanji's own thin pale outline, drawn outside the strokes only. The first version
