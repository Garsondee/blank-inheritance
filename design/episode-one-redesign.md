# Episode One, reimagined

*Written 2026-09-19, at Ingram's request, after running the scene audit (`design/scene-audit.md`)
against the whole of `episode-c1e1-mothers-maiden-name` and then reading what the bible and the
dossiers actually lock. Originally a proposal, paused pending `design/node-registry.md`, then resumed
in `/goal` mode the same day with a dense set of scene-by-scene notes.*

**STATUS, same day: built, not just proposed.** Every scene below is now real, played content under
`data/scene-briefs/episode-c1e1--*.json` (eight scenes total — the original five, enriched, plus three
new: The Tram Back, The Walk Down, and the previously-unwritten Flood Tunnel Clearance; the count has
grown since, and the scene-brief files are the current list), wired into
`data/episodes/episode-c1e1-mothers-maiden-name.json`, and backed by real new canon
(`quarantine/ideas/story/dossier-second-generation-frames.md`, `bible/LEDGER.md` #20). This document is now the
**design record** of what changed and why — read the real scene-brief files for what a GM actually
runs at the table; read this for the reasoning behind each change. Sections below are left as
originally written where the real files still match them, and annotated where Ingram's review changed
the plan. One thing this document raised was explicitly **not** decided here: when Frame-scale combat
should start (`open-questions.json#frame-scale-starting-level`) — too foundational to act on inside
this pass, flagged prominently instead. *(Decided 2026-09-20: lockstep. Pilot, Frame and vehicle share
one level from level 1, and Frame combat begins within the party's first two levels.)*

---

## 1. The diagnosis

**Episode One is well-written and almost entirely passive.**

Count what the players actually do, in order: they narrate a journey, queue, are examined, are told
something, are equipped, are given a gift, are sent on an errand, are laughed at, are rescued, are
renamed, and are assigned a job with no choice offered. Then, in the sixth scene, they finally act.

Five scenes of being processed, one scene of agency.

That is thematically *correct* — processing people is exactly what the Directorate does, and the
institution's characterisation depends on it. But as a first session it teaches the table that this
is a game where things happen to you, and it runs straight into the standing requirement from
2026-09-13: **every player must have real agency over their own role and actions.**

The fix is not to cut the processing. The processing is the best writing in the episode. The fix is
that inside each of those scenes the players need something to **take, decide, notice, or work out**
— and the episode needs one genuine decision whose consequence reaches Episode Two.

**Second diagnosis: the episode is under-planted.** `blank-inheritance-ch1-v2.md` §5 lists thirteen
mystery-ledger plants. Episode One lands three. At least four more have obvious homes in scenes that
already exist, at almost no cost — and the chapter's own stated job (§2) is *"plant mysteries, none
explained here."*

**Third: two characters with enormous later payoffs are currently disposable walk-ons**, in the one
scene where the wider trainee body appears.

## 2. What stays, untouched

Stated first and explicitly, because most of this episode is good and the point of a redesign is not
to prove otherwise.

- **Shucks Mordant, entire.** Want, voice, the single-word instructions, the deflection that is itself
  the information. The best-written NPC in the repo.
- **Form 12-B**, including the pre-printed IRREGULAR tick-box and the two departments that have not
  spoken since the flood. Textbook evidence-not-exposition; do not touch a word.
- **The flag, and the all-six realisation**, with its three ranked fallbacks — and it stays in the
  morning, where `ch1-v2` puts it.
- **Manako exactly as specified**, including his conspiratorial help with the requisition (LEDGER #18
  is explicit that this is the intended shape), the throwaway delivery of *"They did this to me, you
  know"*, and the absolute rule that nothing in the scene suggests what he is.
- **Taka's nicknames**, delivered as settled fact, not offered.
- **The no-choice assignment**, read flat and last. *(Revised 2026-09-20: read out in person by the Section Officer, who comes down to the canteen to do it himself. The tannoy is gone — he is a real, present minor antagonist now, met face to face and dealt with directly.)*
- **The Road In's montage structure** — six separate spotlights, no shared beat, they do not meet.
  That was decided and defended on 2026-09-09 and it is right.
- **Personnel scale throughout.** No Frames, no vehicles.
- **The fight's build** — six giant centipedes at party level −2, Moderate/120, difficult terrain,
  poor light, optional compactor hazard.

### Scene 1 — The Road In

**What changes: one question.**

Players still each describe their own arrival, alone, in a montage. But the prompt changes from *"how
do you get here, and what's the last thing that happens before you arrive?"* — which generates travel
colour — to something that generates a hook the campaign can actually schedule:

> **What did you bring that you were not supposed to, and who did you not say goodbye to?**

The letter already says *bring nothing you cannot carry, and nothing you cannot leave.* LEDGER #10
settled that this is practical military packing in house-style officialese, not a hidden key — so the
question is not fishing for a clue, it is asking each player to hand the GM a person and an object.
Both are schedulable. "I took the early train" is not.

**Also fix:** the cicada sighting is currently offered on *"a wall, a crate, a train window."*
`dossier-cicada.md` §2 is specific: the stencils are **always at head height, always on a wall people
stand in front of, never facing a road.** A train window is the one siting that never happens. Move
every Episode One sighting to somewhere a person *waits* — a tram stop, a ticket queue, a ferry
waiting room. This costs nothing and it makes the pattern real, because every mark the party sees is
then somewhere they were standing still.

**Also fix:** the beat's note says this plants Mystery Ledger #6, *"pays off Ch5."* It does not.
`ch1-v2` §5 cleared the entire payoff column on purpose. The Road In inherited a schedule that no
longer exists; `the-errand` already says "currently unassigned." Make them agree.

### Scene 2 — Intake Medicals

**What the players do that they did not before:** they walk past the founding charter, and one of
them can notice that the doctor's hand is not a hand.

Everything else about this scene stays exactly as written.

**Add — the blacked-out paragraph (Ledger #5).** The founding charter hangs framed in the lobby, one
paragraph painted out since before anyone alive was born (`ch1-v2` §4). The queue goes past it.
Nobody comments on it, nobody explains it, no check is offered and none is needed.

This is the single cheapest high-value change in the whole redesign. Manako's one true thing, that
afternoon, is *"that lot wrote theirs down, and then they painted over it."* Right now the party has
no referent for that line and it floats past as an old man's opinion. If they queued past the frame
at eight in the morning, it detonates instead.

*(Cut 2026-09-27, LEDGER #41: the charter has no painted-out paragraph any more, Manako's line in The Errand is
rewritten, and the charter check in Intake Medicals studies its four signatures instead.)*

**Add — the hand (optional, and it is canon we are currently not using).** LEDGER #1 establishes that
Shucks is a **literal animated skeleton**, that his right hand is a detached, flying, nonverbal
familiar currently off pursuing its own conspiracy theories, and that in its place he wears a very
convincing prosthetic — and that **noticing the prosthetic is a real Perception or Medicine check,
not GM narration.**

None of this is in the scene brief. A GM running that file cold does not know the doctor is a
skeleton. That is a straightforward bible-to-brief gap and it should be closed regardless of anything
else in this document — but it also hands the scene exactly what the audit says it lacks: an optional
beat with a real, strange, delightful payoff for a player who was paying attention.

**Add — a refusal floor.** `one-at-a-time` and `the-flag` are both `mandatory: true` and nothing
answers a character who refuses to be examined. The 2026-09-09 self-play run found this live and had
to invent a consequence at the table. Two lines of authored fallback closes it.

**Add — a `landmark`.** The `intro` block never sets one. A queue is inherently spatial and nothing
anchors it. "The doctor's door, second from the end" is sufficient.

### Scene 3 — Kit Issue

**What the players do that they did not before:** they see a locker that already has a name on it.

**Add — Nozomi's locker (Ledger #2).** Her name is still on the roster and her locker is still
assigned. The party is being issued lockers. One of them is spoken for.

Nobu is not evasive and never should be, so when somebody asks — and somebody will — he simply
answers, at his own speed, and it helps nobody:

> *"That one's spoken for. Someone didn't turn up. Right — sign there, and there —"*

Completely honest, completely useless, and the party is now standing in the space left by the girl
whose refusal is the reason they are here two weeks early. Nothing is explained. `nozomi.md` requires
that she stay backstory rather than a Day 1 investigation thread, and a name on a locker is an object,
not a thread — but the GM note should say so explicitly so nobody turns it into one.

**Proposed, and needs your call — the extra item becomes responsive.** Nobu's free gift is currently
identical for everyone and handed over without engagement. The bible locks the *distribution* (all
six, no exceptions, presented as mundane) but explicitly does **not** lock the mechanism.

The version I would build: Nobu half-registers something the player just said or did, grabs something
off a shelf without breaking stride, and does not explain. Still all six. Still mundane. Still
impulsive rather than considered — which is his characterisation, not a deviation from it.

**Two hard guards if you want this:** it stays all six without exception, and it never becomes Nobu
*deciding* anything for a player. That second one is the line the dropped ground-role-assignment beat
crossed, and it should not be re-crossed by accident.

### Scene 4 — The Errand

This is where most of the episode's agency problem lives, and where most of the fix goes.

**What the players do that they did not before:** they find Manako themselves, and they decide what
to do with what he gives them.

**Change 1 — the search becomes the party's own work.** Currently the party is stuck, offers one
`ask-around` check that succeeds on any result, and arrives. That is three pointers in a row — go to
the store, store says no, go find someone, someone tells you the answer — which is the breadcrumb
chain named as a failure mode in `quests-objectives-and-threads.md`.

Instead: several lanes, run split, per `ch1-v2` §7's own standing note that *"two groups of three
doing different duties is faster and gives everyone airtime."* The chandler's, the tea house, the
harbour office, a tram stop, an old hand on a bench. Each lane returns something true and partial.
Pooling them finds him.

They still find him on any result — that stays. What changes is that finding him is something the six
of them did together, which is the ensemble beat this episode does not currently have anywhere.

**Change 2 — the ending becomes a decision.** Manako's conspiratorial help stays exactly as Ingram
specified it. He tells them how to requisition the vial, correctly, in a way that crosses a desk above
the man who sent them. Then he finishes his tea.

What the party does with it is theirs. And the interesting question is not *whether* they use it —
they will — but **how they file it.** The Section Officer has gone home; he is offscreen all
afternoon and `the-section-officer.md` says that is deliberate. So the choice is:

- **Quietly, through the correct channel.** Perfectly proper. Deniable. He cannot prove it was
  pointed. He will still know.
- **Loudly** — routed so it crosses the maximum number of desks, or left where the morning shift
  reads it first. Enormously satisfying, witnessed, and he will know exactly who and exactly why.

Both sides are things the table actually wants: being right, versus not yet having a superior's
focused attention. **Episode Two opens differently depending on which they picked**, which is already
flagged as pending — LEDGER #18: *"STILL OPEN: what actually happens when the vial requisition lands
— Episode Two's business."* Making it a choice does not resolve that; it gives it an input.

**Add — bulk reagent theft (Ledger #7).** The general store *(moved to the instrument makers' workshop, LEDGER #45)*. The storekeeper grumbles that dull stock
keeps walking — nothing valuable, nothing anyone would bother with, which is exactly why nobody has
looked into it. One line, no check, and the heist loop is planted.

**Add — dogs and birds (Ledger #9).** City texture on the walk across. A street the dogs won't use.
Free.

**Fill `outro.tracks`.** It is currently empty on a scene where the party's first act as a unit is
insubordination.

### Scene 5 — Canteen

**What the players do that they did not before:** they meet two specific people, and they are given
something to resent.

**Change 1 — the pilot-track walk-on is The Young One.** `dossier-cicada.md` §9 describes him as *"a
pilot-track student a year or two ahead of Section Nine. They eat in the same canteen. He has an
opinion about their assignment."* The Canteen brief already contains exactly that character, saying
exactly that, currently marked *"flexible texture, not canon fixtures."*

He is unnamed, and no file says when the party first meets him. This is where they should. The
requirement that *"the party should be able to recognise the voice before they are told"* only works
if they have heard it — and a peer rival the party's own age is the largest single gap the Giant Robo
sweep identified, which the Evangelion shelf exists to fill.

Nothing about the scene changes at the table. He is still a slightly snotty older student with better
assignments than theirs. He just stops being disposable.

**Change 2 — Section Nine is the bottom of the pecking order, and somebody says so out loud.** The
dismissiveness is already seeded ("You're Section Nine? Rough. We got harbour patrol."). Make it land
as a standing, visible fact rather than one passing line.

This does real work. The storm drain assignment currently deflates; with a pecking order behind it,
it becomes **a slight with an answer available**, and the answer is the fight. That gives the night's
combat emotional stakes it does not currently have, and it bonds six strangers faster than any amount
of NPC charm, because a shared grievance is what actually makes a party.

**Add — the girl in the news (Ledger #10).** A radio in a canteen. A father on it, talking about a
daughter held without charge. Nobody in Section Nine has any idea who that is. Plants Shinobu whole,
for the price of ambient noise.

**Fill `outro.tracks`.** Bonds moves here or it moves nowhere — this is the scene built entirely
around the episode's stated purpose (*make the players like each other's characters*) and it currently
rewards it with an empty array.

### Scene 6 — The walk down *(new)*

**What the players do:** kit up, walk to Dock C, and are alone together for the first time all day.

Ten minutes. No checks. No NPCs required. Waders, torches, the smell coming up off the water, and six
people who met this morning with a job ahead of them and nothing to do but talk.

This scene exists for three reasons and it fixes all three at once:

- **The pacing.** The episode currently runs four scenes up, one deliberate cold turn, then straight
  into combat with nothing between. That is the missing scheduled relax beat named in
  `pacing-and-the-responsive-campaign.md` as the specific cause of tone drift.
- **The ensemble.** Every scene so far is either six private spotlights or one NPC working the whole
  group. This is the first time the six of them are the only people present.
- **The nicknames.** Taka's names were given two hours ago. This is where the players use them on
  each other for the first time, which is the moment they stop being six characters and start being a
  section.

**Optional, and it needs your call:** Taka sees them off. *"Wear the boots. I told you."* Nothing
else — no information, no warning, so it cannot step on the canteen's drain question, which is his
only coercible topic and should stay there. Purely the warmth landing a second time. `taka.md` does
not forbid him appearing elsewhere on Day 1; it is established practice rather than a rule.

### Scene 7 — Flood tunnel clearance

**Built 2026-09-09** (`data/scene-briefs/episode-c1e1--flood-tunnel.json`, commit `f843579`), then
extended in this same `/goal` pass with refusal floors, a real-reason path for the optional beats, a
`choicePoint` on the fight, and morale/surrender grounding cited from GM Core/Alien Core. This section
is left as originally written, per this document's own stated convention (§ banner, top) — the real
file matches the proposal below. `ch1-v2` already specified the build, and one thing in it needed to
be honoured rather than quietly dropped when the brief was written:

> **"What matters: whether they kill them, drive them off, or find another way. Record it."**

Three genuinely viable resolutions, not one plus two that don't work. The compactor hazard is what
makes that real: a clock closing from the centre outward turns the fight from *how much damage can we
output* into *how long can we afford to be in here*, which means somebody is working the machinery
instead of swinging — and a fight where not everyone is attacking is the only real test of ensemble
play this episode contains.

**Two fixes carried in, both verified applied:**

### The close — the inference, not the fact again

**Proposed, and it needs your call.**

The morning gave them a fact: *all six of us came back irregular.* Repeating it at the water is the
bug above. But the flood-tunnel notes already permit the alternative — *"Don't re-run the discovery"*,
while a later conversation about what it means **is** allowed.

So the night gives them the conclusion instead. Soaked, in the accommodation, somebody says the thing
nobody said this morning:

> We were all flagged. And then we were all put in the same section.

That is not a repeat, it is an escalation — a fact in the morning, an inference at night, drawn by the
players themselves after they have been in a drain together, which is *why* they say it out loud. It
is also exactly the pattern `narrative-theory-foundations.md` describes: give a fact, never a
conclusion, and let retrospective coherence do the work.

Nobody answers it. The episode ends there.

## 7. What I deliberately did not touch

The tone. Every change above is structural — where agency sits, what gets planted, who is in the
room. None of it makes the episode darker, sadder, or more serious, and the one place I add a
grievance (the pecking order) exists so the fight can *answer* it rather than to make the day hurt
more. It is still a warm, funny, faintly ridiculous first day at a calcified institution where
nothing has ever happened.
