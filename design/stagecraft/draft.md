# The Draft: drawing cards in an action scene

is one of the fun ones.*

## 0. In short

During an action scene the GM has a **draft** along the bottom of the screen: a row of **draft cards**. The GM draws a
series of them, face up or face down. The players pick a face-down one. It turns face up, the group takes the result, and
the table plays what that card says. An encounter, an action sequence or a problem becomes a situation the GM can draw
cards for, instead of a list to run.

**A word to keep apart.** On the stage a **card** is a person (her word, `README.md`). The draft's cards are a different
thing, so every document calls them **draft cards**, and the row of them the **draft**. If she prefers another word for
them, this is the one place to change it.

## 2. How it would play

1. **The scene is an action scene.** The draft exists only there. A scene that is not one has no draft, the same way a
   serious scene has no box of chalk (`chalk.md` §3).
2. **The GM draws a series.** Some face up, some face down, as the moment needs. Face up is a choice with the results in
   view. Face down is a gamble.
3. **The players pick.** The table chooses a face-down draft card; it turns face up.
4. **The group takes the result.** There is no appeal and no re-draw: the card is what happens next.
5. **The table plays it.** The result is a thing to play out, with the stage, the cards and the dice already in front of
   them.

The point of it, as I read her: a fight or a chase or a problem stops being something the GM has to hold in their head
and becomes something the whole table does with its hands. The pick is the drama.

## 3. Where it would sit

- **On the GM's screen, at the bottom.** The Deck is already the strip along the bottom of every player's screen
  (`README.md`, Words). The draft would be a GM-only row on it, shown only in an action scene.
- **On the stage, for everyone.** The players have to see the drawn series to pick from it, so it has to appear on their
  screens as well. Where (a row on the stage, or on the Deck) is not decided (§5).
- **In the stage's own manner.** Draft cards are things to pick up and turn over, so they should be as lively as the
  cards: a stick, a lift, a turn with some weight to it (`feel.md`). That is a suggestion, not a requirement.
- **Written the way situations are written.** A draft is what `situation-scenes.md` calls the likely moves and the valves,
  made into cards. Claude would write a scene's draft cards as part of the scene, in the same pass, and the GM would
  see them with the rest of the prep.
- **One scene flag.** The way chalk and the stage are switched on per scene, the draft would be a scene's own flag, off
  unless a scene says otherwise. (A sketch, mine.)

## 4. Checks against what is already settled

These are mine, not hers. They are the things this idea has to be built to satisfy.

| Rule | What it means for the draft |
|---|---|
| **Law 11: removal, not hiding** (`README.md`, What does not change) | A face-down draft card's result must not be in a player's browser until it is turned. What is sent is the back of a card. This is the same rule as a person not yet revealed |
| **Real agency over their own role and actions** (the standing requirement in `CLAUDE.md`) | The result is a situation to play, not a ruling that takes a character out of a player's hands. The choosing of the card and the playing of its result are theirs |
| **Penalties cost risk, never choice** (`design/campaign-arc.md`) | A bad draft card raises the stakes. It does not delete options from a character's menu |
| **A beat guarantees the outcome and never scripts the approach** | A card says what has changed in the scene, not how the table must answer it |
| **SF2E first** (`design/design-layers.md`) | The real dice and rules stay. How a draft card meets them is open (§5) |
| **Nothing reaches the table before its reveal** | The same as Law 11, and it covers the card's art and sound as well as its words |

## 5. Open, and hers

Recorded so they are not lost. None of these is asked yet.

1. **Where do the players see the drawn series?** A row across the stage, or on the Deck, or on the GM's screen only with
   the pick made by pointing?
2. **Does the GM choose the series, or draw it blind off a shuffled draft deck?** Or either, as the scene needs?
3. **What is on a draft card?** A complication, a boon, a twist, a change of place, an event? One kind per draft or a mix?
   How many cards in a draft?
4. **How does a card meet the dice?** It replaces a roll, sets the stakes of one, or follows one?
5. **One pick for the group, or one each?** She wrote "the group must take the result", which reads as one pick. Who makes it?
6. **Can a series mix face up and face down?** She wrote "face up or face down", which could mean either.
7. **Is a spent draft kept?** Cards turned over, for the session record, or gone?
