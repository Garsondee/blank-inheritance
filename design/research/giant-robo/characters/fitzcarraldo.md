# Fitzcarraldo the Splendid (素晴らしきヒィッツカラルド)

*Big Fire, Magnificent Ten · the delighted butcher with one perfect gimmick. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 素晴らしきヒィッツカラルド — "the Splendid Fitzcarraldo". English releases render the name Fitzgerald or Fitzkerald |
| **Role** | The Ten's hitman; part of the assault on the heroes' fortress |
| **Powers** | **He snaps his fingers and a vacuum blade cuts whatever he was pointing at.** Anything. He dances between targets as he does it |
| **Fate** | Fused into rubble by a mass teleport, then murdered by his own colleague Mask the Red; his final blade is deflected back and severs his own hands |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Red hair, a white tuxedo, and an unfailingly delighted manner. Production notes describe him as happy-go-lucky, and he is described in the Japanese material as taking pleasure in destruction and slaughter, with no mercy shown to women or children.
- The technique is called 真空破, a vacuum-break — a *kamaitachi*, the folkloric cutting whirlwind — released by a finger snap. He kills the heroes' guard captain by cutting him in half with it.
- **He had a second, hidden ability, which the series never revealed because he ran out of episodes.**
- He was originally written to fight Chief Chujo. The sequence was cut for length.
- **[S] The origin of the gimmick, per the Japanese production notes:** Imagawa was practising finger snaps in a convenience store between storyboard sessions, and an automatic door slid open at exactly the moment he snapped. He immediately thought "a finger snap that cuts things in half", and then spent the several years before the character reached the screen afraid that somebody else would use the idea first.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He arrives as one of a three-member assault and moves through defenders in a dance, killing casually. He does not leave; he is caught by a teleport that moves an entire building, ends up half-inside a rock, and is finished off by a colleague.

**[R]** His death is the single best-constructed defeat in the series, because **he is not beaten by a counter to his power.** Nobody blocks the vacuum blade. Someone changes where the building is, and geometry kills him. A villain whose gimmick has no counter can still be defeated by a solution aimed at something else entirely — which is exactly the shape of solution a party of players invents.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **One gimmick, perfectly clear.** The audience understands his entire threat model in one gesture: if he can point at it, it is cut. No explanation, no build-up, no technobabble. The most memorable villains in this series are the ones whose power can be demonstrated in a second and a half.
- **Delight is more frightening than menace.** He is not grim. He is having a lovely time, and that is what makes the killing land.
- **He is killed by his own side**, which does more for the faction's characterisation than anything he does himself. See [mask-the-red.md](mask-the-red.md).
- **[R] The hidden second ability is an accidental lesson.** It never mattered. Nothing was lost by his having exactly one trick, and the one trick is why anyone remembers him.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The One-Gimmick Killer.* A villain who does exactly one thing, instantly legible, with obvious joy.

**The one rule:** **The gimmick must be demonstrable in a single sentence and a single gesture, and it must never be upgraded.** If the players work out how to handle him, let them handle him. His job is to be solved.

**Voice:** cheerful, chatty, complimentary. Treats a fight as a social occasion and the dead as part of the entertainment. Never angry — irritation would make him ordinary.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "Oh, *good*. Stand a little further apart, the pair of you — I want to see which of you moves."
- *To a child:* "You are very small. I am going to save you for last, as a treat."
- *On leaving:* "No, no, keep the room. I have taken what I wanted out of it."
- *When cornered:* "Well. That was clever of somebody. Which of you was it? I would like to know before we continue."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the single-gesture power that a table understands immediately; cheerfulness as the register of cruelty; being defeated by a solution aimed at the environment rather than at him, which rewards the kind of lateral play a group actually produces.

**Worth avoiding:** hoarding a second ability for a later reveal. Design the villain to be fully known and still frightening.

**Where it might attach:** a one-gimmick killer is the easiest villain in this whole shelf to run at a table, and the easiest to stat, because the gimmick is the stat block. If our own antagonist roster ever needs a mid-tier recurring threat that a party can actually beat, this is the shape — the counterpart to an implacable Minder, not a replacement for one. See [../the-recurring-antagonist-engine.md](../the-recurring-antagonist-engine.md) §8 on the unhittable-villain failure mode.
