# Professor Go (智多星の呉用)

*Experts of Justice · the staff officer who was there when it happened and has never said so. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- His eyes are shut almost all the time. Almost.
- **He is a survivor of the atrocity that underlies the whole series**, and one of very few people alive who knows what actually happened. In his youth, under a different name, **he was Dr. von Vogler's first assistant.**
- He also knows the secret of the two Vogler children's identities — including that the organisation's most trusted field agent is one of them.
- He carries the guilt of not having stopped his old chief, and of having failed to save the man's son. **[S]** The series is careful to note that he was missing crucial context, and that his guilt is therefore built on an incomplete account.
- **His one great moment is an outburst.** When the chief mentions that a new disaster resembles the old one, Go's eyes snap open and he shouts that the Shizuma Drive is perfect — *it has to be*. It is the first crack in the story's official history, and it is delivered by the calmest man in the room.
- By temperament he is composed and analytical, and when events go outside the plan he loses his composure completely and laments at length.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He is at the briefing, behind the chief, saying little.

**[R]** He is staged as furniture so that the one moment he stops being furniture will register. **The entire design of the character is a held breath.**

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **The tell is physical and cheap.** His eyes are closed; when they open, something is wrong. A GM can run this NPC with one gesture and the table will learn to read it within two sessions.
- **He is the party's ally and he is withholding the central secret.** Not maliciously — out of guilt and a defensible institutional judgement. That makes the eventual reveal a betrayal that nobody has to be a villain for.
- **He panics.** A strategist who is unflappable is a plot device; one who unravels when reality departs from the plan is a person, and his unravelling is itself information.
- **[R] His guilt is wrong.** He has spent a decade blaming himself for something he did not understand. That is the series' central engine — misinformation rather than malice — running in a minor key inside a supporting character.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Assistant Who Was There.* A friendly expert NPC who was present at the campaign's founding disaster, knows more than he says, and is wrong about his own part in it.

**The one rule:** **He must be useful to the party constantly, so that the withholding costs them something when it surfaces.** If he is only a mystery, he is an obstacle. If he is the person who answers their technical questions every week, the reveal has weight.

**Voice:** precise, formal, faintly academic. Answers the question asked, exactly, and not the one implied. Comes apart audibly when the situation leaves the plan — the register shifts from lecture to lament.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "I can give you three answers, and only the third is any use to you, so I will begin with the first."
- *To a child:* "You are asking a good question. I am going to give you an incomplete answer, and I want you to notice that I have done that."
- *At the crack:* "It is *perfect*. It was checked. It was checked by me — it has to be perfect."
- *After:* "Yes. I was there. Nobody has asked me that in eleven years, and I have been waiting for someone to."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the trusted expert who was present at the founding disaster; the single physical tell; the collapse under unexpected news; guilt built on an incomplete account, which lets a reveal free an ally rather than condemn him.

**Worth avoiding:** making the withholding malicious. His silence should read as shame and procedure, not conspiracy.

**Where it might attach:** the campaign has its own founding disaster material (`quarantine/ideas/story/dossier-first-impact.md`) and a redaction system in design (`ideas/entries/redacted-document-chain.json`, `design/harvest-inference.md`). An NPC who is a redaction in human form — helpful, verifiable, and missing the same paragraph the documents are missing — is a natural fit for those systems. **Whether one exists is not decided here.**
