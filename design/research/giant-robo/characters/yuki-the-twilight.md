# Yuki of the Gathering Dusk (暮れなずむ幽鬼)

*Big Fire, Magnificent Ten · the telepath who read everyone and stopped liking people. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 暮れなずむ幽鬼 — "the lingering ghost of gathering dusk", the light that will not quite go |
| **Role** | Member of the Magnificent Ten; works in a standing pair with Old Man Kawarazaki |
| **Borrowed from** | Yuki of *Iga no Kagemaru* |
| **Powers** | Telepathy strong enough to control any living thing — animals, insects, plants. Carries a swarm of insects inside his body that devour by sheer number. Can take insect form himself |
| **Fate** | Survives |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Pale skin, unkempt hair. The insects he keeps in his body are called 群雲虫, "cloud-swarm insects"; released, they cover an enemy and eat. He can also become one of them, a miniature of himself with gossamer wings.
- **He could read minds from early childhood.** Seeing the difference between what people showed and what they were made him a misanthrope. He was brought out of it by Kawarazaki, the oldest member of the cabal, and the two have worked together ever since.
- Their standing assignment is on Hanzui's behalf: find out what the strategist Koumei is actually doing. **Neither Yuki's telepathy nor Kawarazaki's power can read him**, which is the only proof the audience gets that Koumei's protection is genuine.
- **[S]** Fan reception lists him among the series' most popular characters.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He is usually already present, usually alongside Kawarazaki, and usually knows what is about to be said.

**[R]** A telepath makes an awkward antagonist because he removes the party's secrets. This one is written around that problem: his ability is total *and* has failed on the single person he needs it for. **The interesting telepath is not the one who reads everything, but the one who has met something he cannot read** — and who is therefore as curious and off-balance as the party.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **His misanthropy is earned, specifically.** He does not dislike people in the abstract; he dislikes them because he has read them, all of them, since he was small. That is a complete character in one line, and it is sympathetic without being excused.
- **He was rescued by a colleague**, which is why he is loyal to a faction rather than to a cause. The oldest man in the cabal talked him out of hating everyone; that debt explains everything he does afterwards, including why the two of them investigate their own strategist together.
- **The swarm is a body-horror image attached to a gentle backstory.** The contrast is what makes him memorable rather than merely disgusting.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Reader Who Stopped Liking People.* A telepath whose ability is the reason for his isolation, loyal to one person who reached him anyway.

**The one rule:** **Give him something he cannot read, early.** It protects the party's secrets, it gives him a want the players can interact with, and it turns his power from a GM problem into a plot.

**Voice:** quiet, tired, slightly ahead of the conversation. Answers the question the speaker was about to ask. Only warms up when talking to or about the one person who rescued him — and that warmth is the crack in him.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "You were going to ask what I want. Nothing from you. You are exactly what you look like, and that is restful."
- *To a child:* "You are frightened, and you have decided not to show it. Good. Keep deciding that."
- *On his partner:* "He is the only person I have ever met who meant what he said out loud. I stayed for that."
- *On leaving:* "I have what I came for. It was in the room before I was."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the telepath with a declared blind spot; a villain whose loyalty is a personal debt rather than an ideology; the pairing of a young misanthrope and an old patron, which gives two characters one subplot.

**Worth avoiding:** running him without the blind spot. A mind-reading NPC in a campaign built on hidden facts (`design/npc-social-intel.md` Facts, `ideas/entries/redacted-document-chain.json`) would strip the investigation layer if he worked as advertised.

**Where it might attach:** nowhere mechanical; the campaign's resonance material was cut on 2026-09-20. **This shelf makes no proposal** — it only notes that if a mind-reading NPC is ever written, the blind spot is what makes them playable.
