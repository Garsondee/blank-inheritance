# Taisou the Magic Traveller (神行太保・戴宗)

*Experts of Justice, the Big Nine · the mentor who dies on schedule, and makes the death land. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 神行太保・戴宗 — "Taisou, the Magic Traveller" |
| **Age / role** | 27. **The youngest member of the Big Nine**, the IPO's nine strongest |
| **Borrowed from** | Dai Zong 戴宗 of the *Water Margin*, whose talisman let him travel 800 *li* in a day. Epithet kept exactly |
| **Powers** | Jet-fists — shockwaves from palms and soles, which also give him superhuman travel speed. He is additionally a living generator, able to produce electricity from his own body |
| **Fate** | Takes a fatal blow from Lord Alberto to buy the others' escape, and goes down with the Shanghai oil field |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Brave, open-handed, boisterous, and devoted to his colleagues. **Married to Youshi**, another Expert, and the two work as a pair.
- **A father figure to the twelve-year-old protagonist, and a sworn elder brother to Tetsugyu**, who still calls him "brother" in the older sense.
- He once fought Lord Alberto of the Magnificent Ten, took his right eye, and killed his sworn friend Cervantes. Alberto has been waiting for the rematch ever since. **Each regards the other as the only worthwhile opponent he has.**
- His most quietly impressive scene is not a fight: with an enemy field shutting down every power source in the building, he plugs *himself* in and brings mission control back up.
- He dies deliberately. Already mortally wounded, he holds a position to let the others get clear, and Alberto — realising he was fighting a dying man who chose the terms — shouts at him not to die.
- **[S]** He is one of the two characters Imagawa specifically wanted to use, and the reason the whole Yokoyama crossover exists at all.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He arrives fast, usually first, usually alone, because arriving fast is literally his power. He leaves by staying behind.

**[R]** The mentor death is the oldest beat available, and the series makes it work with three specific supports rather than with sentiment: it is **chosen** (he was dying anyway and picked the ground), it is **witnessed by his rival** rather than by his students, and it **vacates a role** that two other characters then compete to fill. The grief is distributed.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **His death does four jobs at once.** It saves the escape, it breaks Tetsugyu open, it hands Alberto the grief that will drive his final act, and it leaves the protagonist without a father figure for the second time. **[R] That is the test worth taking from him: before killing an NPC, count how many living characters are changed by it. Four is a good death; zero is decoration.**
- **The rivalry is mutual and mostly offstage.** The audience is told about the eye and the dead friend and never sees it happen. Two veterans with a shared history that predates the story is one of the cheapest ways to make a setting feel old.
- **The generator scene.** A small, non-combat use of a combat power, solving an infrastructure problem. It is the single best demonstration in the series that a superpower is a *property of a person* rather than a move list.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Elder Brother Who Spends Himself.* A senior, warm, enormously capable ally with one rival on the other side and a death already written.

**The one rule:** **His death must vacate a job.** Not a gap in the party's power — a *role*: who protects the youngest, who keeps the peace, who is allowed to tell the loud one to be quiet. A vacancy is playable; a hole is only sad.

**Voice:** hearty, warm, direct. Gives orders as though they are favours. Talks about his wife constantly and without embarrassment. Speaks to the youngest party member as an adult and to his own peers as family.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "Go. I will be four minutes behind you, and I have never once been late."
- *To a child:* "You are not too young. You are too new. Those wear off at different speeds."
- *To his rival:* "You have been carrying that eye around for eleven years. Put it down or come and get the other one."
- *At the end:* "Do not turn round. If you turn round you will come back, and then it will all have been for nothing."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the four-job death; the rival who mourns him; the non-combat use of a combat power as characterisation; the married pair of veterans who work together, which gives a campaign two NPCs for the price of one relationship.

**Worth avoiding:** killing the mentor before the party knows what he does for them socially. The series spends four episodes establishing him as the person who manages Tetsugyu, which is precisely why his absence is felt as a *job* nobody is doing.

**Where it might attach:** the campaign's NPC roster is thin on warm authority (`data/npcs/` has three Directorate poles and little else), and the `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default` policy says stubs are the correct first version. A stub for this archetype needs only four lines: name, what he is best at, who he is married to, and whose job he will leave vacant.
