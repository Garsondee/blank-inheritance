# Giant Robo (ジャイアントロボ)

*Experts of Justice · the machine as a character: obedient, wilful, and haunted by its builder. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this machine does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | ジャイアントロボ, GR-1 |
| **Role** | The IPO's trump card. Built under a Big Fire project by the protagonist's father, who then betrayed them and was killed for it |
| **Powers** | Enough ordnance to level cities; booster rockets; a nuclear reactor instead of the world's clean Shizuma Drive |
| **Voice** | A growl. No speech |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- **It runs on a nuclear reactor**, which in this world is archaic and dangerous — and which makes it the one machine on Earth immune to the enemy's weapon that shuts down every Shizuma Drive. That is why it decides the finale.
- The same reactor is its weakness: **it cannot regulate its own output**, so sustained overexertion risks meltdown.
- It has an **Auto-Guard Circuit**. If the wristwatch senses its wearer's life is in danger, the robot activates itself, breaks out of its maintenance dock, and goes — at fifty times normal power, with no control over output.
- It follows the *gist* of a command rather than the letter. Told to shoot, it fires everything it has, including one of its own booster rockets as a missile.
- It is repeatedly hinted to carry the will or spirit of the man who built it, and the final episode makes that explicit.
- Visually: a barrel chest roughly three times the width of its head, stovepipe arms, exposed rivets, a unicorn mark on the forehead, and a permanent glare. Production material calls it high technology meeting old-school engineering — a weapon of mass destruction rather than a robot superhero.
- Its own side seals it away for a while when the reactor is discovered, and repairs it later as a deliberate gesture of good faith.

## 2. How it enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** It arrives late, loudly, and usually because the boy is in danger rather than because anyone ordered it. It leaves under tow, or on foot, slowly.

**[R]** The series never lets it be convenient. It is heavy, it is slow, it breaks, it has to be repaired, and it is a political object — an international police organisation has to have opinions about a private nuclear weapon operated by a child. **The robot's inconvenience is what makes it feel real**, and the inconvenience is mostly administrative rather than mechanical.

## 3. Why it works **[R]**

- **Power at a stated price.** Immune to the thing that defeats everyone else; liable to melt down if pushed. One sentence, and every deployment becomes a decision.
- **It disobeys usefully.** A machine that interprets rather than executes is a character. It is also a mechanism for giving the party outcomes they did not ask for without the GM simply overruling them — the robot did more than it was told, and that is in the specification.
- **Its protectiveness is a circuit, not a feeling.** The Auto-Guard is written as hardware, which lets the audience read it as love without the series ever claiming it. That restraint is why the last episode lands.
- **[R] It is not a friend, and that is deliberate.** The emotional content is inherited — the builder's will, the father's promise — rather than native to the machine. Compare almost every other mecha series, where the robot is either a vehicle or a soul.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Inherited Weapon.* A machine that is too powerful, slightly wilful, expensive to maintain, politically radioactive, and carries a dead person's intentions.

**The one rule:** **Deploying it must cost something every single time** — fuel, damage, political capital, meltdown risk, or the simple fact that using it tells the enemy where the party is. A weapon with no cost stops being an event by its third appearance.

**Presence at the table:** describe it through its effects on the environment and on bystanders rather than through its own actions. Weight, noise, the reaction of people in the street, the time it takes to arrive.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *Arrival:* something in the distance is knocked over, twice, at a regular interval, getting closer.
- *Obedience:* it is told to stop a vehicle, and it stops the vehicle, the bridge and the road.
- *The circuit:* nobody gave an order. It is already moving.

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the stated power-at-a-price; the auto-guard as hardware rather than sentiment; the maintenance and politics of owning the thing; the machine that interprets orders generously.

**Worth avoiding:** frequency. See [../what-makes-it-tick.md](../what-makes-it-tick.md) §2 — this robot is scarce, and scarcity is the whole design.

**Where it might attach:** our Frames are PCs with their own sheets (`design/scale-and-chassis.md`, `data/characters/frames/`), not NPC weapons, so the archetype does not transfer directly. What does transfer is the **cost-of-deployment** framing and the idea that a Frame is a political object other people have opinions about. `design/strategy-layer.md` is where that would live if it lives anywhere; this shelf does not put it there.
