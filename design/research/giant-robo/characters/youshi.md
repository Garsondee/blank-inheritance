# Youshi, the Blue-faced Beast (青面獣の楊志)

*Experts of Justice · the veteran wife, the instructor, the one who tries the trade. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 青面獣の楊志 — "Youshi of the Blue-faced Beast" |
| **Role** | Peking branch Expert; **the Experts' combat instructor**; Taisou's wife |
| **Borrowed from** | Yang Zhi 楊志, the *Water Margin*'s Blue Faced Beast — **gender-flipped**, and the blue face taken literally as blue skin |
| **Powers** | Mastery of the staff, a six-*shaku* pole that extends at will |
| **Fate** | Attempts to destroy herself and an unkillable enemy together, to cover the protagonist's escape. He survives; the escape works |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Blue skin, which the series never explains — although the Japanese material suggests she acquired it in the same past operation that cost Alberto his eye.
- Bold, expansive, and the dominant "big sister" presence among the Experts. She trains them.
- She and Taisou are married, and they fight as a pair. She has something close to maternal feeling for the twelve-year-old protagonist.
- **Her death is a trade she offers and loses.** During a field operation, to get the boy clear, she tries to take Ko-Enshaku with her. Ko-Enshaku walks it off. The boy gets away.
- **[S]** She and her husband die in the same stretch of the story.

## 2. How she enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** She arrives with her husband or with the monk Issei, and she leaves by making a decision on the spot.

**[R]** The series treats her as a fixture until it needs a sacrifice, which is worth naming as a flaw rather than describing as a design. **[R]** What it does well is the shape of the sacrifice itself: she is not overwhelmed, she *offers a trade*, and the trade is refused by an enemy who is simply too durable. That is meaningfully different from being killed, and it keeps her death an act rather than an event.

## 3. Why she works **[R]**

- **She is the instructor.** One line of role — she taught these people to fight — makes every other Expert's competence hers as well, and gives her authority over people more powerful than she is.
- **She is half of a marriage that works.** Two veterans who are good at their jobs and fond of each other is rare in this genre, and their scenes cost the series almost nothing while making the organisation feel like a place people live.
- **The literalised epithet.** A fourteenth-century nickname about a blue-marked face becomes a woman with blue skin, unexplained and unremarked. See [../legendary-sources-china-and-japan.md](../legendary-sources-china-and-japan.md) §4.
- **[R] The honest criticism.** She is the veteran woman in an ensemble who dies to advance a boy's story, and the trope is old enough to have a name. If the archetype is used, the fix is not to spare her — it is to give her decisions earlier, so that the last one is the fourth she has made rather than the first.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Instructor Who Trades.* A senior combat trainer, respected by everyone she taught, whose exit is an offered exchange rather than a defeat.

**The one rule:** **She must be seen teaching, on screen, before she is spent.** One short scene of her correcting somebody's form in the middle of a crisis buys the entire archetype.

**Voice:** brisk, physical, unsentimental, funny. Corrects people mid-conversation. Uses the imperative constantly and nobody resents it. Affection expressed as criticism.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "Your guard has been wrong for nine years and you have been lucky for nine years. One of those is about to run out."
- *To a child:* "Elbows. — No. *Elbows.* There. Now you are only in a little danger."
- *On her husband:* "He is going to arrive four minutes late and be pleased with himself. Plan around it."
- *At the trade:* "You are worth more than me to them, and I am worth more than nothing to you. Take the deal."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the instructor role as instant authority; the working marriage between two veterans; the sacrifice framed as an offered trade; the unexplained physical marker that nobody comments on.

**Worth avoiding:** the timing. Give her decisions before her last one. See §3.

**Where it might attach:** an instructor NPC has an obvious structural home in a campaign where the PCs are children being trained (`design/induction.md`, `design/character-creation-and-progression.md`). Whether one exists, and who they are, is a story-bible matter and is not decided here.
