# Dazzling Cervantes (眩惑のセルバンテス)

*Big Fire, Magnificent Ten (deceased) · the dead enemy who was at the birthday party. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 眩惑のセルバンテス — "Cervantes the Bewildering" |
| **Role** | Member of the Magnificent Ten, and their leader before Hanzui. Dead before the series begins |
| **Borrowed from** | Chen, the whip-user under Yomi in *Babel II* |
| **Powers** | Illusion and mind control — he can enter a person's mind and steer it. Heat beams from the hands strong enough to melt steel. Neither is shown in the OVA proper |
| **Fate** | Killed by Taisou during a past operation, taking Alberto's right eye with him into the story's backstory |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- White keffiyeh, red goggles, a long thin moustache, and markings on both cheeks that may be paint or may be scars.
- **The only member of the Magnificent Ten with a public face.** He operated openly as an oil magnate — "Oil Dollar" — and ran operations from behind that legitimate position.
- He was the officer responsible for the GR Project when Giant Robo was stolen, which is how he came to know the Kusama family. **The protagonist knew him.** In the OVA the boy calls him "Cervantes-san"; in the later manga he is "Uncle Bantes", and he goes with the family to the sea.
- He and Alberto were sworn friends. Taisou killed him. That single event is the origin of the series' best rivalry, and everything Alberto does is downstream of it.
- He appears only in flashback.
- **[S]** One oddity the series never explains: he points a gun at a child, despite being able to melt a steel door with his hands.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He does not. He is remembered, and the remembering is always doing something to somebody who is present.

**[R]** He is the most economical character in the series — a photograph, a grudge, and a name a child says fondly. **A dead NPC costs no screen time and generates a permanent motivation in two living characters.** His absence is the point of him.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **He is inside the protagonist's childhood.** The revelation that the boy's family friend was a senior officer of the organisation that killed his father is not dwelt on, and does not need to be. It reframes everything the audience thought was safe.
- **He gives the rivalry its debt.** Alberto is not angry about a cause; he is angry about a person. Rivalries made of grief last longer and read cleaner than rivalries made of ideology.
- **A legitimate public identity is the most useful thing a villain can have**, because it gives the party somewhere to go, someone to interview, and a reputation to attack — none of which requires a fight.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Dead Friend On The Other Side.* An enemy who died before play began, who was kind to one of the party, and whose death is the reason a living enemy will not let go.

**The one rule:** **He must be genuinely remembered fondly by someone the party trusts.** The archetype does nothing if he is merely a name in a file. It works when a friendly NPC — or a PC's own background — carries an affectionate memory of him that turns out to be true *and* to have been an officer of the enemy.

**Voice:** used only in flashback and in other people's accounts, which means each account can be slightly different and all of them sincere.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *In memory:* he is the adult who explained something patiently, at a table, on an ordinary afternoon.
- *In the record:* the same man, in a group photograph, standing behind people the party know to be enemies.
- *In the grudge:* an enemy who cannot say his name without stopping.

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the enemy officer who was a fondly remembered adult in a PC's childhood — a reveal that costs nothing to set up and pays enormously; the public-facing legitimate identity as a second way into a villain; the rivalry whose cause is a death rather than a doctrine.

**Worth avoiding:** using it on a PC's background without that player's agreement. `design/character-creation-and-progression.md` covers flags and bonds as content requests — a childhood connection to an enemy is exactly the kind of thing a player should have offered rather than been handed.

**Where it might attach:** the campaign's induction process (`design/induction.md`) is where PC childhood material is generated, so it is the natural place for a connection like this to be *offered* as an option. That is a suggestion about method, not a proposal about content.
