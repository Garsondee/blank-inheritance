# Professor Shizuma (シズマ・ド・モンタルバン三世)

*Outside both sides · the celebrated hero who took the credit and knows it. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | シズマ・ド・モンタルバン三世博士 — Shizuma de Montalban III |
| **Role** | Leader of the team that created the Shizuma Drive. **The world's energy supply is named after him** |
| **Borrowed from** | Dr. Dragnet of *Tetsujin 28-go* |
| **Fate** | Shot dead by Ivan, an enemy agent from the town his experiment destroyed |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- The series opens with him running — from Big Fire, carrying a black attaché case he has stolen from them, which turns out to be a key to the enemy's whole operation.
- **He caused the Tragedy of Bashtarle through his own arrogance, and blamed his dead colleague for it.** He then spent ten years accepting credit and decorations for completing the drive, knowing what he had done and who he had let history condemn.
- He is running from his own record as much as from the organisation. His first scene is a rant, and the rant is the heroes' first indication that anything is wrong.
- **[S]** He dies at the hands of a mid-ranking enemy agent who happens to be from Bashtarle and knows the truth. His last words are that this is no more than he deserves — and then he entrusts what he was carrying to a twelve-year-old.
- The first two episodes are, structurally, an escort mission for him and the case.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He arrives being rescued, and he leaves being shot.

**[R]** The series gets an unusual amount out of a man who is only ever cargo, because **what the party is escorting turns out to be the guilty party.** The escort mission is not undermined by this — they still have to keep him alive, and the case still matters — but every protective act acquires a second meaning on re-watch.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **He is the atonement the series does not grant.** He knows exactly what he did, says so, and is killed by someone with a personal right to do it. No redemption arc, no forgiveness, no heroic death that cancels the debt. That restraint is rare and it is what makes his part of the story land.
- **His name is on everything.** Every power source in the world, every piece of infrastructure, every conversation about energy carries his name. **[R] That is the cheapest and most effective way to keep a character's moral weight present in a setting: attach their name to a thing the characters use daily.**
- **The escort target is the culprit.** A strong reusable structure — and it improves on re-play rather than collapsing.
- **His killer is right.** The man who shoots him has the best grievance in the story (see [ivan.md](ivan.md)), and the series does not soften it.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Decorated Culprit.* A celebrated public figure who caused the disaster, let someone else be blamed, and has been accepting honours ever since.

**The one rule:** **He has to be genuinely useful and genuinely frightened when the party meets him.** The players must have a reason to protect him before they have a reason to despise him. Both facts should be true at once for as long as possible.

**Voice:** eminent, brittle, over-explains, and interrupts himself. Talks about his work in the passive voice — things were achieved, decisions were taken — and about the past as though quoting a report. Becomes plain and direct only at the very end.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "The commission's findings are a matter of public record. I have nothing to add to the commission's findings."
- *To a child:* "You have been taught about me at school. Whatever they told you, I would very much like you to keep believing it for a few more days."
- *When cornered:* "It was checked. It was *signed off*. There were eleven of us in that room and I am the one they gave the medal to."
- *At the end:* "Yes. This is correct. This is exactly correct."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the escort target who is the culprit; the name attached to everyday infrastructure; the atonement that is stated and then refused; the killer whose grievance is honest.

**Worth avoiding:** letting him confess early. The entire structure depends on the party not knowing while they carry him.

**Where it might attach:** the campaign has infrastructure named for people and institutions already in the gazetteer, and a founding disaster with an official account. A Decorated Culprit sits at the intersection — **and casting one would be a story-bible decision touching the arc's history, which is the user's, not this shelf's.**
