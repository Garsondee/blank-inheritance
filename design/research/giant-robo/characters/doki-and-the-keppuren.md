# Descendant Lord Doki, and the Blood Wind League (直系の怒鬼・血風連)

*Big Fire, Magnificent Ten · the silent lord whose retinue fights so he never has to. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — these characters do not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 直系の怒鬼 — "Doki of the direct line". His unit is 血風連, the Blood Wind League |
| **Role** | Member of the Magnificent Ten; the only member besides Alberto permitted a private army |
| **Borrowed from** | Doki of *Yami no Dokiya* |
| **Powers** | Master of many martial arts, with a seven-section staff. Never fights in person on screen. Summons his retinue without speaking |
| **Fate** | Survives |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- **He never speaks.** He has no dialogue scenes at all, and he is described as being skilled in many disciplines and physically formidable — none of which the series demonstrates, because he never personally fights.
- **The organisation forbids members of the Magnificent Ten from keeping private forces. Doki is one of two exceptions, and the reason is never given.** The series states the rule, states the exception, and leaves it.
- His retinue, the Blood Wind League, wear deep woven straw hats and carry the same seven-section staffs. He calls them without a word.
- They are individually beatable by an Expert and collectively a serious problem: they fight the heroes' two frontline commanders to a standstill during the assault on the fortress, and are beaten decisively later when more Experts are present.
- **[S]** They also take orders from the strategist Koumei when no member of the Ten is present — which, given that they are Doki's personal troops, is its own quiet piece of politics.
- **[S]** A pointed detail from the Japanese material: they insist on taking the vanguard themselves, which is why Doki never gets a turn.
- He is one of the two members of the Ten without the group's camaraderie. When a colleague is murdered in front of him by another colleague, he does not care — though he does help the murderer escape.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He arrives in the middle of his own people, who do the fighting. He leaves the same way.

**[R]** The design is an efficient way to keep a high-tier villain on the board without spending him. The players fight his men, learn his silhouette, and never get to test themselves against him — so the eventual fight, if it comes, arrives fully charged.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **Undemonstrated skill, protected by a proxy.** The retinue is the reason the audience never finds out what he can do, and the retinue is also the reason nobody minds. Contrast Kawarazaki, whose power is undemonstrated because the series ran out of time.
- **The exception to the rule is better than the rule.** "Members may not keep private armies. Two do." tells you more about the organisation's internal politics than a page of description: there are favours here, and history, and a reason nobody asks.
- **[R] The Blood Wind League are a named unit, and named units outperform mooks.** Straw hats, seven-section staffs, one collective name — that is the entire design, and it converts an anonymous fight into a recognisable recurring opponent the party can learn to handle. A party that says "the straw hats are here" is a party that is tracking the enemy order of battle.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Retinue Lord.* A silent high-tier enemy who is always encountered through his own named soldiers.

**The one rule:** **He is a promise the campaign has to keep.** Fighting his people is the appetiser; if the campaign ends without the party ever facing him, the design has taken a payment and not delivered. Set the session he becomes available, in the notes, at creation.

**Voice:** none. Communicate entirely through what his soldiers do, how they refer to him, and what they will not do without him.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *Appearance one:* the party fights the straw hats and never sees him. Afterwards one of them, dying, looks past the party at a rooftop.
- *Appearance two:* he is visible and stationary throughout the fight. He does not intervene even when it costs his men.
- *Appearance three:* his men stand aside without being told.

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the named elite unit with one strong visual and one weapon; the stated organisational rule with an unexplained exception, which generates player speculation for free; the high-tier enemy held in reserve behind his own troops.

**Worth avoiding:** never cashing him in. See the one rule above.

**Where it might attach:** the battle simulator's enemy roster work (GitHub epic #3 and the `project_combat_ai_roadmap` memory) is where a named recurring unit would actually get tested — a squad with a shared silhouette and a shared tactic is precisely the kind of opponent that benefits from being run through the simulator before it reaches a table. Whether such a unit exists in our setting is not decided here.
