# Big Fire (ビッグ・ファイア)

*The BF Group · the absent king: a boy asleep, whose name is a salute. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | ビッグ・ファイア |
| **Role** | Founder and sovereign of the BF Group. Appears in the last stretch of the series and does nothing |
| **Borrowed from** | **Koichi Yamano — the protagonist of Yokoyama's *Babel II***, with his three servants renamed |
| **Powers** | Described as the strongest all-round esper alive, beyond the reach of the Magnificent Ten. Never demonstrated |
| **Fate** | Wakes from cold sleep at the end of the series and is seen |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- He looks like a boy, with silver hair. He spends the series in cold sleep while ten of the most dangerous people in the world run his organisation in his name.
- **The organisation's salute is his name.** Members give a straight-armed salute and the cry "Hail, Big Fire! Allegiance or death!" Faltering loyalty is punishable by death, as a stated rule.
- His personal guard is three servants — Achilles, Garuda and Neptune, who are Rodem, Ropross and Poseidon of *Babel II* renamed. Even Koumei, the effective number two, addresses them with an honorific.
- **His appearance is the series' single greatest display of authority, and he does nothing to earn it on screen.** When the Magnificent Ten march on the strategist and Big Fire is revealed to be backing him, all of them — people who destroy city blocks casually — kneel.
- He is treated as a Greater-Scope Villain: he never acts, and his intentions are never stated. **[S]** The series was designed so that he and the protagonist would eventually meet: the unmade final arc was to be *The Siege of Babel*, and production notes say the boy, Big Fire and the magician child were to have become friends of some kind.
- **[S]** He and the protagonist are structural mirrors — one boy commanding a giant robot, one boy with three inhuman guardians.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He is seen. That is the whole event.

**[R]** The lesson is about restraint. A villain who is never used is not a wasted villain if the organisation's every action is performed in his name. **The salute does the work.** Every Big Fire agent who shouts it, every argument settled by invoking him, every officer who cannot verify whether an order really came from him — all of that builds a character the audience never meets, so that a single silent appearance is overwhelming.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **His authority is demonstrated by other people's behaviour, never his own.** This is the cheapest possible way to establish the top of a power structure, and it scales indefinitely.
- **Nobody can confirm his orders.** The strategist speaks in his name and may be inventing every word of it. That ambiguity is what generates the enemy's entire internal politics — see [shokatsu-koumei.md](shokatsu-koumei.md).
- **He is a child.** An enormous, sleeping, revered child, whose organisation salutes with a fascist gesture. The series never explains it, and the image alone raises the question of who exactly is being served by all this.
- **[R] The mirror is the intended payoff.** Two boys, both handed enormous inherited power, both surrounded by adults who fight in their name. The unmade ending was going to be an encounter between them, not a battle.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Sleeping King.* An absent, revered authority in whose name everything is done, who may not have given a single order.

**The one rule:** **Never let the party verify him.** The moment the party can confirm what he actually wants, the archetype collapses into an ordinary boss. Everything about him should reach the party at second hand, through people who believe, people who doubt, and people who are exploiting the ambiguity.

**Voice:** none for most of a campaign. When it finally arrives it should be quiet, brief, and not what anyone expected — the register of a child rather than of an emperor.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *Through his people:* two enemy officers disagree, and one ends the argument by saying the name. The other stops.
- *Through the record:* an order with his seal, and a date on which he was demonstrably asleep.
- *In person:* he looks at the party for two or three seconds and everyone who works for him is already kneeling.

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** authority established entirely by subordinates' behaviour; the unverifiable order as the engine of enemy politics; the salute or catchphrase that does characterisation work every time a mook says it; the two-children-mirrored structure.

**Worth avoiding:** **the debt, again.** The series never delivered the meeting, because production ended first. A campaign has an ending, and a Sleeping King who never wakes is a promise the table will remember. If this archetype is used, the waking scene should be scheduled in the arc plan, not left to be reached.

**Where it might attach:** the `feedback_watcher_is_unknown` rule already makes our long-arc antagonist unseen and unwitnessed at campaign start — which is a *related but different* device. Unseen-because-nobody-has-met-it is not the same as absent-but-worshipped. **Do not conflate the two, and do not attach anything from this file to the Watcher.**
