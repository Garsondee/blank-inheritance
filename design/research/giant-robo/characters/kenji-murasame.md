# Kenji Murasame, the Immortal (不死身の村雨健二)

*Experts of Justice · the man who cannot die, written as a cost rather than a cheat. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 不死身の村雨健二 — "Murasame Kenji the Immortal" |
| **Age / role** | 27. IPO Paris branch; one of the organisation's top three agents |
| **Borrowed from** | Kenji Murasame of *Tetsujin 28-go*. **The transfer ran both ways** — when *Tetsujin 28* was remade in 2004 it used this version of him, not the original |
| **Powers** | He cannot die. Also very fast, and a disguise artist |
| **Fate** | Burns himself to charcoal breaking an energy barrier so that Ginrei can reach her brother |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- A bright pink trench coat and hat. Nobody comments on the colour. He is blunt, sarcastic, and warm underneath it.
- **The Japanese material is precise in a way the English releases are not: he is not a man who *does not die*, but a man who ***cannot***.** That is why he treats his own life carelessly — he shoots himself in the head to make a point and sits up afterwards — and why survival is not a comfort to him.
- **Ginrei is his lover, and the one person who understood that being unable to die is a burden.**
- He is supposed to be a master of disguise. He infiltrates an enemy base disguised as one of their agents, and the enemy commander sees through it instantly.
- He had an older brother, Kazuma, who died in the operation that took Giant Robo from Big Fire.
- **He is the series' narrator**, voiced by the same actor.
- His last act is to break an energy field with his own body so that the woman he loves can get through it. He burns away doing it.
- **[S]** A dubbing accident became part of the character: he first appeared investigating murders in Paris, so the original English dub gave him a French accent. The series then kept him for six years, and the later, unrelated dub kept the accent too.
- **[S]** In the manga version he is not immortal at all — the ability belongs to a different character. In the novelisation he is not even an Expert, just a private detective.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He turns up somewhere he has no business being, having already done the dangerous part. He leaves by walking off, or by dying and then not staying dead.

**[R]** The comedy and the tragedy run on the same engine. His deaths are funny — the series knows they are funny, and the running joke of a man wiping himself off and standing up is real — and every one of them is also evidence of a sentence he is serving.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **Immortality framed as a sentence rather than a gift.** This is the single most transferable idea in the file. The same power in a villain would be an unbeatable nuisance; given to a hero who resents it, it becomes the reason he takes the jobs nobody else can survive, and the reason he cannot be comforted.
- **One person understands him.** The relationship with Ginrei is defined by a single specific piece of comprehension rather than by romance-as-decoration, and it is what makes his final act read as a choice rather than as a stunt.
- **He is allowed to be bad at something.** His disguise is famously excellent and it fails immediately. A competence that does not always work is how a character avoids becoming a solution.
- **[R] He is the series' narrator**, which is a quiet joke: the story is being told by the one man who is guaranteed to be around at the end of it.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Man Who Cannot Die.* An agent with no fear of death because death is not available to him, and no comfort in it either.

**The one rule:** **The immortality must cost him socially, not mechanically.** He does not get hurt less; he gets left behind, sent into the things that kill people, and treated as equipment by his own organisation. If the only consequence is that he wins fights, the table will resent him.

**Voice:** flat, sardonic, unimpressed. Says the discouraging thing first and then does the job anyway. Talks about his own injuries the way other people talk about the weather, and stops joking entirely around exactly one person.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "Send me. I will be unpleasant about it the whole way, and I will still be there on Thursday."
- *To a child:* "No, it does not hurt less. It just does not finish. Do not get any ideas about it being lucky."
- *On being thanked:* "Do not. You will feel obliged to do it every time, and there are going to be a great many times."
- *On leaving:* "Give me an hour. I have some getting up to do."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** immortality as a burden; the single relationship built on one specific act of understanding; the narrator who is also a character; the useful-but-fallible signature skill; the loud coat, which is a free characterisation device and costs nothing at a table.

**Worth avoiding:** giving this to a PC. An unkillable player character removes the stakes from every scene they are in and makes the GM's job structurally impossible. As an NPC ally who is periodically, horribly spent, he is excellent. As a PC concept he is a different game.

**Where it might attach:** as an ally NPC, this archetype pairs naturally with any campaign that needs someone to survive being sent somewhere lethal in order to bring information back. `design/mystery-and-investigation-design.md` covers the risk — an agent who always survives can become a clue-delivery service, which removes the investigation. Use him to *open* a route, not to *report* what is down it.
