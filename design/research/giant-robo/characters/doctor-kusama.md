# Dr. Kusama (草間博士)

*Deceased · the dead father whose question is the whole series. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 草間博士. His given name is never stated |
| **Role** | The protagonist's father; builder of Giant Robo; formerly the IPO's director of weapons development |
| **Borrowed from** | Duck, a special-forces soldier under Yomi in *Babel II* |
| **Fate** | Killed by Big Fire for betraying them, immediately after completing GR-1 |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- **He worked for both sides.** He ran the IPO's weapons development, then went over to Big Fire, who put him in charge of the GR Project. The moment Giant Robo was finished, he turned on them. They killed him for it.
- Dying, he handed his son the robot and the wristwatch that commands it, and asked him a question: **can happiness be obtained without sacrifice? Can a new era be achieved without tragedy?**
- That question is the spine of the entire series. The closing credits dedicate the work to fathers and their children.
- **The machine may still contain him.** The series hints at it throughout and shows it in the final episode: the robot's habit of exceeding its orders, its automatic protection of the boy, and its behaviour at the end all point the same way.
- **[S]** In the later manga continuity his conduct is stranger still — he leads a rebellion that nearly destroys Big Fire, and then brings them the GR plan.

## 2. Presence **[S/R]**

**[S]** He appears in flashback and in a machine.

**[R]** He is the most efficient character in the series: three scenes, one question, and a robot. **[R]** The question is what makes him work. He does not leave his son an instruction — he leaves him a *problem*, and the difference between the two is the whole difference between this character and [franken-von-vogler.md](franken-von-vogler.md), whose four-word instruction destroys his own son. **The series contains both, side by side, as a controlled comparison: one father asked a question, the other gave an order, and the boys' lives diverge accordingly.**

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **He is compromised.** He built the enemy's weapon, for the enemy, and then stole it back. He is not a martyr; he is a man who did something enormous and ambiguous and died before he could explain.
- **The inheritance is a question, not a mission.** A mission can be completed or failed. A question has to be lived, which is what makes it last eight episodes and a whole campaign.
- **His will is in the object.** Whether the robot is haunted or merely well-built is never settled, and the ambiguity is doing real work: the boy behaves as though his father is present, and the audience is allowed to decide whether he is right.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Father Who Left A Question.* A dead parent whose legacy is an unresolved problem rather than an instruction, delivered with a machine attached.

**The one rule:** **The question must be answerable only by play.** Not a riddle with a solution, and not a moral the campaign will state at the end. Something the party demonstrates an answer to by what they actually do.

**Voice:** used only in flashback and recording, and therefore always slightly wrong for the moment it is heard in — the recording was made for a different day than the one it plays on.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The handover:* he is dying and he does not give instructions. He asks something, and waits, and does not get an answer.
- *The machine:* the party asks it to do one thing and it does more, in a way he would have done.
- *The record:* a colleague mentions, in passing, which side he was working for at the time. It is not the side the party assumed.

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the inheritance as a question; the compromised parent who worked for the other side; the will that persists in an object without being confirmed as a ghost; the deliberate contrast between two dead fathers, one of whom asked and one of whom ordered.

**Worth avoiding:** answering the question in the text. The series never does, and that restraint is why it has a reputation.

**Where it might attach:** this campaign is called *Blank Inheritance* and the v4 bible already carries its own thesis about inherited situations. **A campaign-level question is precisely the kind of thing that belongs to the user and the story bible, not to a research shelf.** What this file offers is the observation that a question outlasts an instruction, and the paired-fathers structure that makes the point without stating it.
