# Mask the Red (マスク・ザ・レッド)

*Big Fire, Magnificent Ten · the one with no honour, inside an honourable cabal. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- A suit worn over chainmail, a bright red eye-mask and a red scarf.
- **He is a cold-blooded killer who will murder his own colleagues the moment he judges them useless.** When Fitzcarraldo is left half-fused into a rock by an enemy teleport and begs him for help, Mask the Red stabs him with a kunai, telling him that living in that shame would be hard too. The Ten's own code does not apply to him, and the others know it.
- His rival is Kagemaru of the enemy's Big Nine, another ninja. Production material states that an entire ninja community in this world follows their duels.
- He boasts that without the Big Nine present, the heroes' entire fortress cannot survive a combined attack by three members of the Ten — and he is right.
- He is one of only two members of the Ten described as being without the group's camaraderie. The other is Doki, who witnesses the murder and does not care.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He arrives as part of a coordinated assault, with a rock giant, and leaves under his own power when the objective fails.

**[R]** He is written as the exception that proves the faction's rule. Everything the audience has learned about Big Fire — that its people are honourable, that they mourn each other, that they object to atrocity — is established by other members and then violated by him in one gesture. **The murder works because of nine other characters' decency, not in spite of it.**

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **One villain who breaks the code is worth ten villains who have none.** In an organisation that mourns its dead and argues about ethics, a man who kills a wounded colleague to save the embarrassment is genuinely disturbing. In an organisation of monsters, he would be ordinary.
- **The hero recast as a villain.** Japanese audiences knew Akakage as a heroic ninja; the series casts him as its nastiest character without a word of comment. **[R]** It is a private joke that costs nothing to anyone who misses it — and the general principle is portable: an archetype the audience trusts, used straight in the wrong role, is unsettling without any explanation.
- **The offstage rivalry.** He has an opposite number among the heroes' legends who never appears, and a world of ninja who follow the feud. The rivalry implies an entire subculture the story never has to depict.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Defector From The Code.* The member of an honourable faction who is not honourable, tolerated because he is useful.

**The one rule:** **His faction must visibly disapprove and keep him anyway.** That is where the horror comes from — not from what he does, but from the fact that the reasonable people in the room have decided to live with it.

**Voice:** brisk, practical, contemptuous of sentiment. Frames cruelty as efficiency and is faintly puzzled when this does not land. Speaks about people as workloads.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "You are hurt, and you are slow, and I am not carrying you. Nothing personal is intended. Nothing personal ever is."
- *To a child:* "You are the reason they are standing where they are standing. Stay there."
- *To his own side:* "You will all say something about this afterwards. Say it afterwards."
- *On leaving:* "We took what we came for. What you kept, you may keep."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the single dishonourable member of an honourable enemy faction; the offstage rival among the party's own legendary figures; the mercy-killing-as-efficiency that tells the players exactly how this person values them.

**Worth avoiding:** making him the leader, or making him common. His whole function is contrast. Two of him and the faction's code is gone.

**Where it might attach:** if an enemy organisation here ever acquires a code (see [hanzui.md](hanzui.md) §5), this is the second character to write, immediately, and one of the few NPC types worth building at full depth rather than as a stub — contrary to the usual policy in the `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default` memory, because the contrast only works if both sides of it are real.
