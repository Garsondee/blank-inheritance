# The Big Nine (九大天王)

*IPO · offstage legends: seven names, no appearances, enormous weight. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — these characters do not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 九大天王 — "the Nine Great Heavenly Kings" |
| **Role** | The nine strongest Experts of Justice, and the only people the enemy believes can match the Magnificent Ten |
| **On screen** | **Two of them.** Chief Chujo and Taisou. The other seven are names |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

The roster, assembled from a single late-episode line and supporting material:

- **Marshal Kanshin** (軍師・韓信元帥), the Strategist Marshal — Han Xin of the Chu–Han contention. The only one of the IPO's three strategists to hold a seat, regarded as the Nine's leader, and **even Chief Chujo defers to him on planning.** He is mentioned exactly once before the finale.
- **Kagemaru** — the hero of Yokoyama's *Iga no Kagemaru*, and **the rival of Mask the Red of the Magnificent Ten.** Production material says an entire ninja community follows their duels.
- **Genyousai** (無明・幻妖斉), from *Yami no Dokiya*.
- **Tendou**, from *Abare Tendou*.
- **Dick Maki**, from *Earth Number V-7*.
- **Otsuka**, the police chief from *Tetsujin 28-go*.
- **Rinchu** — Lin Chong, the *Water Margin*'s Panther Head. In later material he has been replaced on the Nine by Shishin, the Nine-Dragoned.
- **Shinmei** (霹靂火の秦明), Thunderclap Fire — a former member, dead before the series, and the teacher of Kaei and Koushin.

**[S] The full list is spoken only in the final episode**, when the enemy's commander realises who he has been talking to and names Chujo as one of the Nine.

**[S] Their absence is justified inside the plot.** The enemy's superweapon shuts down transport and communications worldwide, which cripples the IPO's ability to move people to where they are needed. The Magnificent Ten know this and act on it: one of them states plainly that without the Nine present, the heroes' fortress cannot survive three of the Ten at once.

**[S] The enemy's opinion of them is the measure.** When the Ten are told Lord Alberto is dead, they refuse to believe it, because only a member of the Nine could have done it and the one who could is already dead. The Ten's estimate of the heroes is an estimate of nine people, seven of whom the audience never meets.

## 2. Why it works **[R]**

- **Seven names and one sentence of respect from the enemy is enough.** The Nine feel enormous because the people the audience has seen being terrifying are frightened of them. Reputation is transitive, and it is nearly free.
- **The absence is engineered, not excused.** This is the craft point. **A campaign that keeps its legendary NPCs offstage needs the reason to be a property of the plot** — here, a device that breaks global transport — rather than a habit of the GM's. Once the reason is in the fiction, the players can also try to *solve* it, which turns "where is the cavalry" from a complaint into an objective.
- **Each name carries a borrowed legend.** They are cheap for the audience to believe because the names are already famous in their own right — a *Water Margin* general, a legendary ninja, a detective's police chief. See [../the-yokoyama-crossover-method.md](../the-yokoyama-crossover-method.md).
- **One of them has a rival on the other side.** Kagemaru never appears, and neither does his feud, and both are load-bearing: the enemy ninja's stature comes partly from having an opposite number.
- **[R] The list is also a promise.** Seven named legends who never arrive is a debt, and the series never paid it, because it was cancelled. At a table this is the same debt described in [zangetsu.md](zangetsu.md) — **write down which of them will appear, and when, at the moment the list is invented.**

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Offstage Legends.* A named tier of allies stronger than the party, who cannot arrive, for a reason built into the plot.

**The one rule:** **The reason they are absent must be something the party could, in principle, fix.** Then the absence is a quest and not an excuse.

**At the table:** name them all at once, in one document or one overheard conversation, and let the players pick favourites. Let the enemy refer to them with respect. Then bring exactly one of them on, late, and make it enormous.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The list:* a duty roster with nine names, seven of them unavailable, each with a different reason.
- *The respect:* an enemy hears one of the names and changes their plan.
- *The arrival:* one of them, alone, walking, very late, into something the party has already nearly lost.

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the offstage tier whose stature is established by enemy respect; the plot-level reason for their absence; the named rivalry that neither party ever sees; naming a legend after something already famous in the setting's own history.

**Worth avoiding:** the unpaid debt. Nine names is a lot of promise. Three is plenty.

**Where it might attach:** this campaign has an obvious candidate structure in the legacy-Frame graveyard (`data/hangar/legacy-units.json`, currently empty) — machines and pilots who came before, with names and reputations and reasons they are not available. That file's emptiness is a design opportunity, not an omission, and **what goes in it is the user's call.**
