# Shokatsu Koumei (諸葛亮孔明)

*Big Fire, chief strategist · the strategist everyone hates and nobody can touch. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- He gives orders in Big Fire's name. **Whether Big Fire has actually given any orders is never established**, and the Magnificent Ten cannot tell either.
- The Ten dislike him intensely. He knows, and enjoys it: he keeps them in the dark, uses them as pieces, and when they crowd him he asserts his position and dismisses them.
- **Two of the Ten — Kawarazaki and Yuki, between them commanding enormous telekinetic and telepathic power — routinely pair up specifically to work out what he is planning, and cannot.** That is how the series tells you his protection is real.
- Supplementary material gives his origin: he was once an ordinary agent of no particular rank. He ran a scheme that exposed Big Fire's existence to the world and killed a member of the enemy's Big Nine, and was rewarded with the strategist's chair.
- He is the one who recruited Genya. He kidnaps the child Sunny and has her impersonate Big Fire. When the Ten finally move on him, he produces Big Fire's own bodyguards, and they kneel.
- When the operation collapses — with hundreds of his own agents dead, the organisation weakened, and nothing gained — he tells the Ten this was merely a step in the real plan, and it is enough to stop them.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He is already in the room, usually seated, usually amused. He leaves by dismissing everyone else.

**[R]** The device that makes him work on screen is that he is never the one under pressure. Everyone who confronts him could kill him instantly and does not, which converts his every scene into a demonstration of the structure protecting him rather than of the man.

## 3. Why he works — and where he does not **[R]**

**Where he works:** he is a plot engine rather than a threat. He generates the enemy's internal politics: the Ten's resentment, Ko-Enshaku's ambiguous loyalty, Genya's over-promotion, Kawarazaki's surveillance. A faction with a hated strategist writes its own subplots.

**Where he fails, and this matters more:** the series gets real criticism for him. He refuses to explain his plans, at length, while his own people die, and the story never confirms whether he has a plan at all. Viewers call his survival at the end an ass pull. **[R] At a table this character is close to unplayable as written**, for a specific reason: players will simply keep asking. A GM who answers "he won't say" four times in a row has stopped running a scene and started running an obstacle. Whatever is taken from him has to survive contact with a party that interrogates.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Untouchable Adviser.* A non-combatant who outranks every dangerous person in the organisation, on borrowed authority that may or may not be real.

**The one rule:** **The party must be able to make him spend something.** He does not need to be beatable; he needs to be *taxable*. Every encounter should cost him a resource, an ally's trust, or a piece of the secret — otherwise the players are talking to a wall, and they will stop.

**Voice:** courteous, delighted, always answering a question adjacent to the one asked. Refers to the absent leader constantly and in the third person, as a way of ending conversations. Never threatens directly; describes outcomes.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "You are owed an explanation. You are not owed it today."
- *To a subordinate who has failed:* "No, no — stay. I want the others to see that nothing happens to you."
- *When cornered:* "You could kill me before I finished this sentence. I notice you are listening to the end of it."
- *After a disaster:* "Yes. That was expensive. Ask me in a year whether it was cheap."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the hated adviser as a generator of enemy-faction subplots; authority borrowed from an absent power, with genuine ambiguity about whether the absent power has spoken; the origin story where a nobody bought his seat with one spectacular, costly scheme.

**Worth avoiding:** the withholding. Give this character a rule the GM follows: **each time the party engages him, he gives up one true thing.** He can choose which true thing, and it can be the one that hurts them most. That keeps the mystery and removes the wall.

**Where it might attach:** `design/npc-social-intel.md`'s Character Card is built for exactly this NPC — permanent Facts revealed on triggers, a live Attitude, and known facts usable against him. If an Untouchable Adviser is ever cast here, his Facts list is the place where "one true thing per encounter" becomes a real, tracked mechanism rather than a GM promise.
