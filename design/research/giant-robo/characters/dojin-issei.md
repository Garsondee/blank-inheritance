# Dojin Issei (公孫勝・一清道人)

*Experts of Justice · the warrior-monk sent to bring his own senior disciple-brother home. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 公孫勝・一清道人 — Kosonshou Issei Dojin |
| **Role** | Guest Expert attached to the IPO's Peking branch — **not a member; a visitor with his own agenda** |
| **Borrowed from** | Gongsun Sheng 公孫勝, the *Water Margin*'s "Dragon in the Clouds", a Taoist adept |
| **Powers** | Taoist arts: illusions strong enough to hold a member of the Magnificent Ten, shapeshifting, paper talismans, a sword, and spoken incantations |
| **Fate** | Survives, and grieves |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- **The reason he is at the IPO at all is a family matter.** His master, the hermit Rashinjin, ordered him to bring back his own senior disciple-brother — Hanzui, who came down from the mountain in anger at the world and is now the leader of the enemy's ruling council. Issei works with the police because the police are going the same way he is.
- His magic is genuinely top-tier. He boasts that his illusions will trouble even the Magnificent Ten, and he is proved right: Lord Alberto cannot break out of one alone. **He is then beaten by circumstance rather than by power** — Ko-Enshaku simply teleports them both out.
- He works in the field most often with Youshi. Their joint charge to save their commander — two people declaring to each other what they are about to do, over an orchestral cue, with a brush-written line of Buddhist invective on screen — is one of the series' signature moments.
- When he survives something that kills a colleague, he does not accept congratulation. He ignores the boy's relief and grieves for the dead woman instead.
- **[S] A note on the description:** English materials call him a Buddhist monk; the Japanese calls him a Taoist adept (*dōshi*). His trappings mix both, which is normal for the genre and worth knowing before copying the aesthetic.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He arrives ahead of the fight, sets something up, and the setup is the contribution. He leaves for his own reasons.

**[R]** He is the only hero in the series with a **private objective that is not the mission**, and it is what makes him more than a support caster. He is not fighting Big Fire. He is looking for one man inside it.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **The borrowed relationship does all the work.** One fact from a fourteenth-century novel — two sorcerers, one master — gives the series an ally with a personal stake in the enemy's leadership, no explanation required. See [../legendary-sources-china-and-japan.md](../legendary-sources-china-and-japan.md) §4.
- **His best moment is a failure.** The illusion works perfectly and is undone by someone else's ability. **[R] A specialist whose speciality is beaten by circumstance rather than by a stronger specialist stays credible** — the party learns that the counter was situational, not that he is weak.
- **His grief is correctly ordered.** Surviving is not treated as a win. That single reaction beat does more for the tone than a speech.
- **The announced-magic moment** is the clearest example in the series of the calling-your-technique convention doing real emotional work rather than being decoration. See [../ham-and-sincerity.md](../ham-and-sincerity.md).

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Ally With His Own Errand.* A powerful, cooperative outsider attached to the party's organisation for a private reason that overlaps with the mission and is not the same as it.

**The one rule:** **State the errand to the party early, plainly, and once.** The interest comes from knowing what he wants and not knowing what he will do when the two objectives diverge. A hidden agenda is a much weaker version of this.

**Voice:** courteous, formal, slightly archaic. Speaks in declarations before acting — announcing what he is about to do is part of how the magic works, not a flourish. Warm to the young, blunt with authority, and absolutely immovable on the errand.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "We are going the same way for now. I will tell you plainly on the day that we are not."
- *To a child:* "Stand inside the circle and do not step out of it, whatever you see. Especially if what you see is me."
- *On his brother:* "He is not your enemy's leader to me. He is the one who taught me to hold a talisman."
- *After a death:* "Do not thank heaven that I am alive. Ask heaven why the count came out the way it did."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the ally with a declared private errand; the specialist beaten by circumstance rather than by power; refusing congratulation for surviving; and the practice of announcing an effect before resolving it, which is free at a table and makes magic feel like a discipline.

**Worth avoiding:** letting the errand quietly lapse. If a campaign gives an NPC a stated personal objective, the campaign owes him a scene where it either resolves or is refused.

**Where it might attach:** `design/encyclopaedia-checks.md` and `design/dialogue-and-character-voice.md` both benefit from an NPC who explains what he is doing while doing it — an ally who narrates his own procedure is a natural teaching device for a system the players are still learning. That is a technique note, not a casting proposal.
