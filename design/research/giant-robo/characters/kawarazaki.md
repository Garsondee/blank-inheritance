# Old Man Kawarazaki, the Tumultuous (激動たるカワラザキ)

*Big Fire, Magnificent Ten · the retired first leader, still watching the man who replaced him. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- White hair, a moustache, and the most trusted voice in the enemy's council. He is called "grandfather" by people who can level city blocks.
- **He led the Magnificent Ten first, and gave the seat up.** He handed it to Hanzui, whose patron and sponsor he remains, so that he could take a different job: watching Koumei.
- That surveillance is the point of him. He and Yuki the Twilight — a telepath who can read almost anyone — work as a pair specifically to work out what the strategist is doing, on Hanzui's behalf. **They cannot read him.** The series uses their failure to establish that Koumei's protection is real.
- His stated powers are among the largest in the cast and **the series never shows them**. What it shows instead is an old man taking a kite out of a case to fly.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He arrives with company — usually Yuki — and in an advisory posture. He is rarely the person a scene is about.

**[R]** He is the enemy's institutional memory, and his function is to make the organisation feel older than the plot. **A faction acquires history the moment one member has already held the top job and chosen to stop.**

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **Voluntary demotion is a strong character note.** He is not a failed leader or a bitter one; he decided the most useful thing he could do was watch the new power, and he arranged for a successor he could vouch for. That single decision implies decades of politics.
- **He is the reason the young leader is trusted.** Hanzui is backed by the old man, which is why nine extremely dangerous individuals accept him. Authority transferred by endorsement rather than by force is a rarity in villain fiction and reads instantly as a real institution.
- **Undemonstrated power is not wasted.** The audience is told he controls gravity and weather and never sees it. **[R]** That works here because the series has already established the power level of his peers — the claim inherits credibility from Alberto's demonstrated violence. Undemonstrated power only works when someone comparable has demonstrated theirs.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Emeritus.* The faction's previous leader, still present, still respected, now doing the one job he trusts no one else with.

**The one rule:** **He must have chosen his current job, and be able to say why in one sentence.** "I am watching the strategist" is a complete character. An emeritus who is merely old is furniture.

**Voice:** warm, unhurried, faintly amused by everyone's urgency. Uses the party's names before he has been told them. Gives advice to his own side in front of enemies, because he does not consider the enemies present a factor.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "I held that chair when your teacher was a nuisance. Sit in it properly."
- *To a child:* "Stand up straight, and stop apologising to people who are trying to kill you. It confuses them."
- *On his successor:* "He is better at it than I was. That is the whole reason he has it."
- *On leaving:* "Do not follow. I am slow, and I would have to do something about it."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** a faction elder who stepped down on purpose and says why; leadership passed by endorsement; the surveillance pairing, where two senior members of an organisation are quietly investigating their own colleague — which is a subplot the party can discover and exploit without the faction ever intending it.

**Worth avoiding:** promising a power and never spending it. The kite is charming, but a table will read an undemonstrated superweapon as a GM keeping something in reserve. If the Emeritus has a signature capability, **spend it once**, in a scene where it solves something rather than settles a fight.

**Where it might attach:** the Directorate already has three poles rather than a hierarchy (`data/npcs/`, `data/factions/directorate.json`). An Emeritus figure implies a *succession*, which is a structural claim about an institution and therefore the user's call.
