# Zangetsu the Midday (白昼の残月)

*Big Fire, Magnificent Ten · the one the audience never got to see, and the most wanted character in the series. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 白昼の残月 — "the waning moon at midday", the moon still visible in daylight |
| **Role** | Member of the Magnificent Ten. Almost nothing else is established |
| **Borrowed from** | Han Tao 韓滔, "General of a Hundred Victories" of the *Water Margin* — little more than the name |
| **Powers** | Stated but never shown: he turns any material into needles and fires them in numbers enough to shatter an enemy |
| **Fate** | Survives. Appears dead at one point and is fine later |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- A mortarboard-like squared cap, a face covering, and a long *kiseru* pipe permanently in his mouth. That is the entire visual.
- **His background is explicitly a mystery, in-universe.** What is stated is strange: he was admitted to the Magnificent Ten as a reward for "merit" arising from the GR Project — the operation that ended with the enemy's greatest weapon in the hands of a twelve-year-old boy, which is to say, a total failure. Nobody explains why that earned him a seat.
- He is the silent figure standing in the background of an earlier scene, present at the moment Giant Robo first activates. The series shows him there and says nothing about it.
- **The first of the six unproduced arcs was titled "The Birth of Zangetsu the Midday".** It was never made. Imagawa later gave the same title to an unrelated 2007 *Tetsujin 28* film.
- **[S]** Fan reception lists him among the series' most popular characters, on the strength of almost no screen time.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He is present. He does not do anything. He is elsewhere later.

**[R]** This is the most efficient character in the series by a wide margin: a hat, a pipe, a contradiction, and a promise. **The contradiction is what makes him work** — promoted for a catastrophe, standing in the background of the exact scene where the catastrophe happened. The audience assembles a conspiracy out of two facts, and the series never confirms or denies it.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **An unanswered question outperforms an answer**, as long as the question is specific. "Mysterious past" is nothing. "Rewarded for the operation that lost them the war, and was standing right there when it happened" is a hook with a shape.
- **He costs nothing.** No fight choreography, no dialogue, no powers budget. He is a silhouette with a pipe and a rumour.
- **[R] The risk is real and worth naming.** He is beloved partly *because* the story never came back to him — the arc that would have explained him was never produced. A mystery that stays unresolved forever is a promise broken slowly. At a table, where the campaign will actually end, this is a debt that has to be paid.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Standing Question.* A recurring background figure with a striking silhouette, a stated anomaly in their history, and no explanation yet.

**The one rule:** **One concrete anomaly, stated early, and an answer written down in the GM's notes before the second appearance.** The anomaly is what the players chew on; the written answer is what stops the character from becoming an unpayable debt.

**Voice:** almost none. If he speaks at all, it is once, briefly, and about something else. The pipe, the pause and the cap do the work.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *Appearance one:* he is in the background of a photograph the party finds. Nobody mentions him.
- *Appearance two:* he is at the edge of a crowd, and one of the party's contacts goes quiet on seeing him.
- *Appearance three:* he says one sentence, which answers a question nobody asked, and leaves.

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the promoted-for-a-failure anomaly; the silhouette-first design, where a character is memorable from a hat and a pipe before they have a line; using an NPC's presence in an old record as the entire first appearance.

**Worth avoiding:** the debt. **Write the answer down at creation time.** The campaign already has a machine for this: `design/objectives.md`'s accumulating log and `design/npc-social-intel.md`'s Facts-with-reveal-triggers. A Standing Question NPC should exist as a Fact with a trigger, not as a vague intention.

**Where it might attach:** the redacted-document chain idea (`ideas/entries/redacted-document-chain.json`) is the obvious home for a figure who is only ever a name in a record and a shape in a photograph — but that idea's status is the real signal, and this shelf is not changing it.
