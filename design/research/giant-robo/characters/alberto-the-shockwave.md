# Lord Alberto, the Shockwave (衝撃のアルベルト)

*Big Fire, Magnificent Ten · the honourable enemy who upstages the plot and dies for a rivalry rather than a cause. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 衝撃のアルベルト — "Alberto of the Shock"; rendered in English as Shockwave Alberto, Alberto the Impact, Shocking Alberto |
| **Age / origin** | 38; from a European noble family. His blood type is given, seriously, as "Bashtarle A" |
| **Borrowed from** | One of the Earth Watchers of Yokoyama's *Mars*, combined with Sally's father from *Sally the Witch* |
| **Powers** | Shockwaves from the hands — rapid-fire, artillery-scale, and enough to fly on. A multifunction monocle over the right eye socket |
| **Fate** | Dies absorbing the enemy superweapon's entire energy field, to open it for a killing blow |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- **The most frequently appearing member of the Magnificent Ten, and the face of the enemy for the first half of the series.** Impeccable suit and tie, an unusual hairstyle, a cigar permanently in his mouth, and a cybernetic monocle where his right eye used to be.
- His establishing scene is walking into the IPO's Beijing base alone and destroying the garrison, with enormous collateral damage and no apparent effort. Until that moment the audience has no idea a human being in this series can do that.
- **He has a nemesis, and the rivalry is the best-written relationship in the show.** Taisou of the Big Nine took his right eye and killed his sworn friend Cervantes during a past operation. They regard each other as worthy opponents. When Taisou finally takes a fatal blow from him and turns out to have been dying already, Alberto shouts at him not to die — furious at being robbed of the fight he was owed.
- He is disappointed by ordinary opponents. He openly says so. He respects anyone who can actually fight him.
- **He is betrayed by his own side.** Genya, a junior agent nominally under his protection, tries to kill him. Alberto survives, tells nobody, and clings unseen to Giant Robo for several episodes waiting for his moment.
- He has a daughter, Sunny, by his late wife — Hu Sanniang of the *Water Margin*. He has deliberately placed the child in Hanzui's care and avoids direct contact with her, while staying in constant telepathic touch.
- His last act is to jump from Giant Robo, absorb the superweapon's defensive energy with his own shockwaves, light his cigar off the beam, and burn out — because in his judgement a grandstanding boy does not get to decide the fate of the world; that is for Big Fire and the IPO to contest. His final line to the enemy is an order not to underestimate the Magnificent Ten.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He enters alone, into somewhere defended, and demonstrates before he negotiates. He leaves when he has what he came for, or when the fight has stopped being interesting. He is never driven off by force.

**[R]** The series uses him to teach the audience the power scale, then spends the rest of the run trading on that lesson. Every later appearance of a Magnificent Ten member is frightening because of what Alberto did in episode one. **One villain, used early and at full strength, can carry the threat-level of an entire faction for the rest of a campaign.**

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **His loyalty is to the code, not the plan.** He works for Big Fire, dislikes the operation, despises the upstart running it, and dies to stop it — without ever changing sides in his own mind. That is a far more interesting shape than a redemption, and it is available to any villain who is given a code before they are given a plot.
- **He wants a worthy opponent, and says so.** It is the most player-facing motivation a villain can have, because the party can satisfy it by playing well. Every other villain's want has to be discovered; his can be earned.
- **His grief is the proof.** Shouting at a dying enemy not to die is the moment that converts him from a power level into a person.
- **[R] The betrayal is what buys the return.** He does not come back because the audience missed him; he comes back because being murdered by a junior was an insult that required an answer. The reason is in his character, so the survival does not feel like a reversal.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Worthy Opponent.* The strongest enemy the party meets early, who fights by a code, respects competence, and is more offended by his own side's dishonour than by the party's existence.

**The one rule:** **He must be satisfiable.** There has to be something the party can do — get better, fight him straight, keep a promise — that changes his opinion of them, and that change has to show. If nothing the party does can move him, he is scenery.

**Voice:** unhurried, courteous, faintly bored, with the cigar doing the pacing. He speaks to the strongest person in the room and ignores everyone else, which is itself an insult the weaker characters can resent. He never raises his voice until the last scene of his life, and then he does nothing else.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "Good. Now I do not have to be gentle, and neither of us has to pretend."
- *To a child:* "Stand behind the adults. I have no argument with you, and I am not always careful."
- *On leaving:* "Tell your commander I came myself. He will understand what that means, even if you do not."
- *On his own side:* "You gave the order. You did not earn the right to give it."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the early full-strength demonstration that sets the campaign's threat ceiling; a villain whose want is *a better fight* and can therefore be moved by player skill; the enemy who holds his own faction to a standard and will break with it rather than lower it; the grudge-driven return, which is the only version of "he survived" that does not annoy a table.

**Worth avoiding:** letting him win too many times. He appears, wins, and then is *betrayed* — which is what keeps the party from simply being beaten repeatedly by the same man. The plot removes him from the board before the treadmill starts.

**Where it might attach:** an antagonist of this shape needs a faction with a code to be loyal to. Whether any of ours has one — the Claimants have build rules rather than a culture in `data/antagonists/claimants/claimant-guidelines.json` — is an open creative question and not settled here.
