# The Ryozanpaku Rank and File (梁山泊の面々)

*Experts of Justice · named minor staff, and why naming them is worth the ink. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — these characters do not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **The place** | 梁山泊 Ryozanpaku — the IPO's fortress headquarters, named after Liangshan Marsh, the outlaws' stronghold in the *Water Margin* |
| **The founder** | 晁蓋 Chogai — Chao Gai, Liangshan's first chief, who dies in the novel. Here he founded the IPO and is long dead. He is mentioned once |

## 1. Who they are **[S]**

Minor characters, most with a line or two, all with a name and an epithet out of the *Water Margin*:

- **Richu** (打虎将の李忠, "the Tiger-Slaying General") — Ryozanpaku's captain of the guard, in a horned helmet. **Cut in half by Fitzcarraldo during the assault.**
- **Shutsu** (小覇王・周通, "the Little Conqueror") — deputy captain of the guard, rated a B-class Expert, runs communications.
- **Kaichin and Kaiho** (両頭蛇の解珍・双尾蠍の解宝, "the Double-headed Serpent" and "the Twin-tailed Scorpion") — brothers, dressed as hunters in tiger pelts, nearly identical except that the younger has a rounder face. Their unit is called the Twin-tailed Scorpion Rescue Squad, after the younger brother's own epithet.
- **The three Ruan brothers** (阮少二・阮少五・阮少七) — fishermen in the novel. Here, the three of them beat drums in resonance and generate a force strong enough to break a barrier.
- **Ryuto and Koryo** (赤髪鬼・劉唐, 独火星・孔亮) — names on the roster, nothing more.
- **The anonymous staff.** The IPO's ordinary personnel are trained in firearms, capable of high acrobatic leaps, roof-hop onto an airship to evacuate it, and at one point **form a human pyramid to physically block an energy blast.**

## 2. Why any of this matters **[R]**

- **A named minor character is a different object from a mook.** When the guard captain is cut in half, it means something, because he had a name, an epithet, a silly helmet and a job. The series spends almost nothing on him and gets a real death out of it.
- **Epithets scale downward.** The same convention that gives the leads their titles gives the door guard one. That consistency is what makes the organisation feel like a culture rather than a cast plus extras.
- **[R] The most instructive detail is the rescue squad's name.** A unit named after one of its two commanders, using that commander's inherited nickname, is exactly how real units get named — and it implies a whole institutional history in four words.
- **Competent background staff raise the world's floor.** When the ordinary technicians can roof-hop, the audience understands that this world is uniformly extraordinary and that the protagonist's ordinariness is the anomaly. See [../what-makes-it-tick.md](../what-makes-it-tick.md) §1.
- **The dead founder, mentioned once.** One line about a man who is not there gives the organisation a past. In the source novel Chao Gai also dies before the story finishes, which is the kind of joke that costs nothing and rewards the reader who knows.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Named Floor.* The minor staff of an organisation, each given a name, an epithet and one job, so that the organisation reads as a place and not as a set.

**The one rule:** **Three details each, and no more: a name, a descriptive epithet, and a job.** Anything beyond that is a promotion. Anything less is a mook.

**At the table:** this is a preparation habit rather than a performance. Keep a short list of names with epithets and jobs, and use one whenever the party talks to the organisation. The payoff arrives the first time a player asks after one of them by name.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The first meeting:* the quartermaster has a nickname everyone uses and nobody explains.
- *The second:* the party wants something, and the person who can authorise it is the one they were rude to.
- *The cost:* one of the named ones is on the casualty list, and the list is read out.

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** epithets for minor staff; units named after the person who leads them; one mentioned-once dead founder; a competent background staff so the world's floor is high.

**Worth avoiding:** depth. The `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default` memory already sets the policy — create stubs freely, promote only on request. This is that policy applied to an entire building.

**Where it might attach:** the gazetteer already carries 207 locations and their NPCs, which is exactly the material a Named Floor is made of. If our own institutions need staff, the gazetteer is where they should come from rather than from fresh invention — which is the same argument [../the-yokoyama-crossover-method.md](../the-yokoyama-crossover-method.md) makes about casting from an existing catalogue.
