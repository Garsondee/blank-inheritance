# Jujoji of the Bell of Life (命の鐘の十常寺)

*Big Fire, Magnificent Ten · the rhyming priest with a bell that gives and takes life. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 命の鐘の十常寺 — "Jujoji of the Bell of Life" |
| **Role** | Member of the Magnificent Ten; the series says he has the finest mind among them |
| **Borrowed from** | 十常侍, the **Ten Regular Attendants** — the ten corrupt eunuchs whose intrigues open *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*. A collective of ten, compressed into one priest |
| **Powers** | Taoist arts. A hand bell that gives life to inanimate things and takes it from living ones. Recovers from his own death in stages. Also a capable robot pilot |
| **Fate** | Appears dead in one episode, is fine in the next |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Buddhist robes with Taoist trappings, red where the whites of his eyes should be, and a face the series describes as uncanny.
- **He speaks in rhyme** — a peculiar, incantatory delivery, studded with compound words, like a man reciting poetry rather than answering you.
- The bell is the whole character: it animates the inanimate, and it takes the life out of the living. He collects magical objects from around the world and this is the one he uses.
- His control over life extends to his own. He does not resurrect instantly; he comes back **in stages**.
- He can burn several people at once and can direct a monster. He is also, incidentally, good with machines.
- **[S]** Imagawa was considered as his voice actor, and the idea was dropped on the grounds that putting a non-professional among that cast of veterans would have been disrespectful.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He arrives with the assault force and fights with sorcery at range. He leaves by dying and then not being dead.

**[R]** The staged revival is the only version of "he survived" in the series that is honest, because **his power is explicitly control over life, declared before he uses it on himself.** Compare Ko-Enshaku, who survives because he survives, and the two members who simply appear fine later with no explanation and draw criticism for it. The rule is visible here: an escape is legitimate when it is the character's declared power being used as advertised.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **The rhyming voice is the cheapest strong characterisation in the series.** Any GM can run a villain who only speaks in couplets, and no player will ever mistake him for anyone else. It also solves the perennial problem of a villain who must be enigmatic without being a wall: he answers everything, in verse, and the answers are *usable* but oblique.
- **A single object carries the power.** The bell is portable, losable, stealable and describable. It gives the party something to aim at that is not the man.
- **[R] Give and take in one instrument.** The same tool creates and destroys, which means every appearance can be a threat or a gift and the players cannot tell which until he rings it.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Oracular Priest.* A villain-mystic with an object of power, a formal speech pattern, and a reputation as the enemy's cleverest member.

**The one rule:** **He answers honestly and obliquely.** He never refuses a question — he responds in his own form, and the answer is always true and always costs the party work to use. This is the correct fix for the Koumei failure mode described in [shokatsu-koumei.md](shokatsu-koumei.md) §3.

**Voice:** measured, formal, rhythmic. Runs to couplets and repeated structures. Addresses people by function rather than name ("the boy", "the one who carries the axe"). Never surprised.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "You ask me for the hour. The hour is late, and the late are never asked twice."
- *To a child:* "Small hands. Small hands ring nothing, and small hands are rung by nobody. Be glad of it."
- *On leaving:* "I am not finished. I am merely elsewhere. The bell will tell you which."
- *On his own death:* "I have done this before. It takes longer than you would like."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** a declared resurrection mechanism used as advertised; a named object that carries the whole power, so the party has a target other than the person; the formal speech pattern as a recognition device at the table.

**Worth avoiding:** the rhyme in writing, unless the GM is comfortable improvising it live. A speech pattern that only exists in the prep document and not at the table is worse than none, because the players will notice the NPC changing. If rhyme is too much, use a fixed structural tic instead — always three clauses, or always answering with a question first. Cross-reference `design/dialogue-and-character-voice.md` on voice differentiation.

**Where it might attach:** the Encyclopaedia-check pattern (`design/encyclopaedia-checks.md`) pairs well with a character who speaks in riddles — the check is what turns an oblique true answer into a usable one, and failure costs nothing mechanically. That is a suggestion about technique, not a placement.
