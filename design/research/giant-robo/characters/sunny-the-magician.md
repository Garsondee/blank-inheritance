## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- She is a small child with real magic, and she is in the care of the leader of the enemy's council, whom she calls "uncle".
- **Her father put her there on purpose.** Alberto avoids direct contact with her — he is one of the most dangerous men alive and has enemies — and the two of them stay in contact telepathically instead.
- The Magnificent Ten regard her as a future colleague.
- **The strategist kidnaps her and uses her as a stand-in for Big Fire**, making a child impersonate a sleeping king in order to keep nine superhuman officers in line.
- **[S]** She is possibly the only character besides the protagonist who does not know the truth about the atrocity everything else in the series revolves around.
- **[S]** Production notes say she fell victim to story compression: in the unmade final arc she, the protagonist and Big Fire were to have formed some kind of friendship.

## 2. How she enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** She is brought in by an adult, and taken out by an adult.

**[R]** She is the series' proof that the enemy are people. **An organisation that is raising a child is not a monster factory**, and the fact that the child is fond of them is more disarming than any amount of stated honour. Then the strategist uses her as a prop, which tells the audience exactly where his line is — he is the one villain who will spend a child.

## 3. Why she works **[R]**

- **She makes the villains legible in one image.** A council of killers, and one of them is doing the school run. It communicates the faction's internal culture faster than exposition can.
- **She is leverage in both directions.** Her father's enemies could use her; her father's colleagues already are. Any scene she is in has stakes without anyone stating them.
- **Innocence as an audit.** She and the protagonist are the only two who do not know the secret, which means the truth arrives for the audience when it arrives for a child — a reliable way to keep a long conspiracy emotionally legible.
- **[R] The kidnap is the strongest single characterisation of Koumei in the series** and costs one scene.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Ward.* A child raised inside the enemy organisation, fond of the people in it, in no danger from them and in constant danger because of them.

**The one rule:** **She must not be a hostage in her own story.** The archetype fails the moment she becomes an object the party rescues. She should want things, ask for things, and be capable of making a decision that surprises both sides.

**Voice:** cheerful, direct, uninterested in the war. Talks about the enemy officers as family, with specific and unflattering domestic detail. Asks the questions adults have stopped asking.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To a stranger:* "You are one of the police ones. Uncle says you are very serious and that I am not to say so."
- *To her guardian:* "You promised. You said after this one there would not be another one."
- *When used:* "I do not want to wear it. I will wear it. But I want somebody to write down that I did not want to."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** a child inside the enemy faction as the fastest way to humanise it; the parent who keeps their child at arm's length to protect them, and the grief that produces; a character who does not know the campaign's central secret, so that the reveal can be staged through them.

**Worth avoiding:** using her only as leverage. See the one rule.

**Where it might attach:** this campaign is already about children in an institution that is using them, which is close enough to the archetype that it should be handled carefully rather than eagerly. The Ward works here as a *contrast* — a child on the other side, treated better or worse than ours — and any such figure would be a story-bible decision, not a research-shelf one. `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default` applies: a stub is the right first version.
