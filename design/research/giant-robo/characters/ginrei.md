# Ginrei (銀鈴)

*Experts of Justice · the capable agent whose power spends her own life, and whose name is a lie. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 銀鈴 ("silver bell") — **a code name.** Her real name is Farmelle von Vogler |
| **Age / role** | 18. Field agent of the IPO, and the series' female lead |
| **Borrowed from** | Shu Ginrei of *Ōkami no Seiza*, with material from *Sono na wa 101* |
| **Powers** | Exceptional marksmanship, and teleportation that consumes her own life force |
| **Fate** | Teleports an entire fortress across the world, begins to disintegrate, and is shot in the head by her brother |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Silver bell earrings and a pendant that belonged to her mother. A white qipao. She is introduced in disguise, under a mask, and drops the disguise after one sequence.
- **Her teleportation costs her life force**, proportionally to how much she uses, so she almost never uses it. When she finally uses it without restraint — moving the heroes' entire base across the world to where it is needed — parts of her body start disappearing.
- She is a protective older sister to the twelve-year-old protagonist, and **she and Kenji Murasame are lovers.** Tetsugyu is in love with her and appears not to know about Murasame.
- **She is the daughter of Dr. Franken von Vogler**, the scientist the world blames for the atrocity, and the sister of the man running the enemy operation. She survived the atrocity. She has spent her adult life under another name working for the people who buried her father's reputation.
- Her brother kills her.
- **[S]** Because "Ginrei" is a code name rather than a person, production material indicates other Ginreis were intended in later stories.
- **[S]** She was popular enough to be given three spin-off OVAs, which are comedies, and which are where most of the series' fanservice reputation comes from.

## 2. How she enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** She arrives in a working capacity — extraction, escort, cover — and leaves on the job. Her big moments are all acts of transport, which is quietly appropriate for someone whose power is moving things at a cost.

**[R]** The device that makes her more than an archetype is that **her competence is the disguise.** She is the reliable professional in every scene, and the reason she is so reliable is that she is hiding.

## 3. Why she works **[R]**

- **The power that spends her own life is the cleanest "cost" design in the series**, and it does character work rather than just balance work: every time she uses it, the audience learns what she thinks is worth dying slightly for.
- **The code name is a whole backstory in one fact.** She is not called Ginrei. The person the heroes trust is an assumed identity, adopted by a survivor whose family name is a curse.
- **Two incompatible loves.** The brother she cannot save and the boy she is protecting are on opposite sides of the same inheritance, and her death resolves neither.
- **[R] A caution worth recording.** The series is inconsistent about her: a competent, tragic agent in the main story, and the subject of three broad comedies where the joke is frequently her body. `design/dialogue-and-character-voice.md` and the campaign's own tone rules would both be poorly served by copying that split. The tragedy and the comedy are both fine; the specific way the comedy is aimed at her is a product of its era and not worth importing.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Professional Under An Assumed Name.* A trusted, senior field operative whose competence exists to keep everyone from asking who she was.

**The one rule:** **Her power must cost her something visible and cumulative.** The reveal that she is hiding is a one-time payoff; the cost is what makes her present in every scene before it.

**Voice:** calm, dry, practical, and brisk with people who are wasting time. Warm only with the youngest person in the room. Deflects personal questions by giving an order.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "I can do it. I would rather be asked properly, so that it is on somebody's record that I was asked."
- *To a child:* "Behind me. Not because you are small — because you are the only one of us who matters to the mission, and I am extremely good at this."
- *On the cost:* "It works every time. That is the problem with it."
- *On her past:* "You have read my file. My file is accurate."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the self-consuming power as an ever-present cost; the assumed name that turns a trusted ally into a late-campaign reveal; the sibling on the other side; an NPC who is warmest to the party's youngest member.

**Worth avoiding:** the treatment described in §3. Also: killing her off-screen of her own choice is very nearly what the series does — she spends herself moving the base and is then murdered — and that ordering matters. **The sacrifice should be the character's decision, and the death should not be an afterthought to it.**

**Where it might attach:** nowhere mechanical for now. The campaign's own self-costing resource (Bleed) was cut on 2026-09-20. An NPC who visibly pays a price for their power is still a strong way to show the players what something costs before it is their turn.
