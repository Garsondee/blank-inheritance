# Ivan of Orosha (オロシャのイワン)

*Big Fire, B-class agent · the loyal second with the better grievance. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | オロシャのイワン — "Ivan of Orosha", *Orosha* being an old Edo-period word for Russia |
| **Age / role** | 32; Lord Alberto's aide and driver, rated B-class |
| **Borrowed from** | One of the Earth Watchers of Yokoyama's *Mars* |
| **Powers** | No superpower. He is simply extremely good at everything that is not fighting, and he pilots the robot Uranus |
| **Fate** | Killed by Giant Robo |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Shaven head, and a bruise-like scar near the left eye that reddens when he loses his temper. He loses his temper when anyone disrespects his master.
- **He is from Bashtarle** — the small country destroyed in the atrocity that underlies the whole story — and he explains this to the enemy's twelve-year-old protagonist himself. He supports the plan to destroy the world's energy supply because of it.
- He knows, or has worked out, the truth about what happened there, and holds a deep and specific hatred for the celebrated professor who took the credit. He is the one who kills him.
- **He was about to be promoted to A-class and was demoted for this operation instead**, which is why he resents the young agent placed above him and calls him a brat.
- He drives a car that transforms into a giant robot. He is described as the practical complement to his master: Alberto is a weapon, Ivan does everything else.

## 2. How he enters and leaves a scene **[S/R]**

**[S]** He arrives ahead of his master, handles the arrangements, and stays close. He dies in a machine, fighting something far above his weight.

**[R]** He is the only significant Big Fire character with no powers at all, which makes him the organisation's human face — and gives the audience someone in the enemy camp whose viewpoint is ordinary.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **His grievance is better than his master's.** Alberto fights for a code and a grudge. Ivan's home town is gone, and the man responsible was decorated for it. Handing the strongest moral complaint in the story to a mid-ranking henchman is a very deliberate choice, and it is what stops the enemy organisation from being dismissible.
- **He explains himself to a child.** The scene where he tells the protagonist where he is from is the reason his death has any weight. **One quiet conversation with the enemy's most vulnerable member is enough to convert a henchman into a person.**
- **The passed-over promotion.** A single line of career detail gives him a live opinion about his own side, which is the cheapest way to make an organisation feel like a workplace.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Aide With The Real Complaint.* A competent, unpowered subordinate whose personal loss is the most legitimate thing in the villain faction.

**The one rule:** **Let him state his case to the party, calmly, once.** Not as a villain's monologue — as an explanation, in a lull, to whoever will listen. That scene is the entire value of the character.

**Voice:** brisk, professional, deferential to exactly one person and to nobody else. Talks about logistics. Becomes precise and quiet, rather than loud, when he talks about home.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an equal:* "I book the rooms and I fix the cars and I bury the ones that do not come back. You may take that as an answer to your question."
- *To a child:* "You have been told what happened at my town. You have not been told who was thanked for it."
- *On his master:* "Say that again where he can hear it. I would like the afternoon off."
- *On his own side:* "I was two weeks from a promotion. Then they gave me a boy to look after."

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** giving the strongest grievance to a low-ranking enemy rather than to the boss; the unpowered competent aide as the enemy's most human face; the quiet explanation scene delivered to the youngest PC; the career slight as instant organisational texture.

**Worth avoiding:** killing him before the explanation lands. His death only works because the audience has heard him.

**Where it might attach:** the campaign's antagonist material currently has guidelines and a long-arc figure but very little middle (`data/antagonists/`). An aide-level NPC with a legitimate grievance is exactly the sort of stub the `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default` policy says to create freely — a name, a home town, a complaint, and nothing else until he is needed.
