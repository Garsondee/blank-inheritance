# Dr. Franken von Vogler (フランケン・フォン・フォーグラー博士)

*Deceased · the monster of record who was right, and dead, the whole time. Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything.** Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- History records him as the devil who destroyed a country and killed billions in his impatience for credit. **The record is false, and was manufactured by the surviving colleagues who actually caused it.**
- He appears alive in the first episode, in a cathedral bell tower in Paris, beside the bodies of three of his former colleagues, promising revenge. **He is not there.** It is his son wearing a holographic projection of him.
- **The truth, revealed in the last episode:** the Shizuma Drive was generating an undetectable poison that would have killed everything on Earth. Bashtarle was his attempt to fix it. His plan required shutting every drive in the world down briefly so the flaw could be corrected, and the three sample cases everyone spends the series fighting over were the repair.
- **He left his son almost nothing.** A rigged attaché case and an instruction — stop Shizuma — without the reasoning. His son spent his life killing people to carry it out and learned the truth only after killing his own sister.
- **[S] His New Era Speech** is quoted in full by the series and used as the cold open of a later episode: mankind rubbed sticks together for fire, slaughtered the whale and fought over oil, then came the atomic age, and in every chapter we danced with danger — and now, for the first time, we will be delivered from fear, and **the Beautiful Night will embrace us all.** "Beautiful Night" is one of the series' two sets of arc words.

## 2. Presence **[S/R]**

**[S]** He is a recording, a hologram, a rumour and a reputation.

**[R]** He is the purest example of the series' engine. Nothing he did was wrong; everything the world believes about him is wrong; and the person who loved him most did the most damage acting on his behalf. **[R]** Note that even the audience's perception of him zigzags several times: mad scientist, avenger, genius, victim, and finally a man who was simply doing maintenance.

## 3. Why he works **[R]**

- **The reputation is the antagonist.** For six episodes the enemy is a dead man's name. That costs nothing to stage and colours every scene.
- **The speech is the hook.** A single quotable, grandiose, genuinely beautiful piece of oratory — about fear, and fire, and the whale — does more to make a dead character live than any amount of flashback. **[R] If a campaign has a founding figure, write them one speech and quote it repeatedly. It is the cheapest immortality available.**
- **His innocence does not absolve anybody.** He was right, and his son still killed his daughter, and billions still died at Bashtarle. The reveal reframes without forgiving, which is why the ending is bittersweet instead of redemptive.
- **[R] His one real failure is a failure of communication**, and the series knows it — it is stated outright that if he had found an easier way to tell his son what needed doing, none of this would have happened.

## 4. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Monster Of Record.* A historical figure whose documented guilt is an official lie, and whose real work was a repair nobody understood.

**The one rule:** **Let the party meet the lie first, thoroughly, from sources they trust.** The archetype is worthless if the truth is available early. They should be able to quote the official version confidently before anything contradicts it.

**Voice:** grandiose, warm, visionary, a little frightening — the register of a man announcing a new age to a room that is about to be destroyed. Preserved only in recordings, which means the voice never adapts to events.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The record:* the official account, in a textbook, with a photograph and a date.
- *The dissent:* one person who worked with him refuses to discuss it, and the refusal is the first anomaly.
- *The recording:* his own voice, from before, describing a plan that makes no sense until the last piece is in the room.

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth stealing:** the official history that is a cover story; the one quotable speech as a means of keeping a dead character present; the truth that reframes without absolving; the failure of communication as the actual cause of a war.

**Worth avoiding:** the rigged briefcase. See [genya.md](genya.md) §5 — the delivery mechanism is the weakest link in the series' plot. **If a message is incomplete, the reason has to hold up on its own.**

**Where it might attach:** the campaign's founding disaster (`quarantine/ideas/story/dossier-first-impact.md`) and its redaction machinery (`ideas/entries/redacted-document-chain.json`, `design/harvest-inference.md`) are built for exactly this shape of reveal — an official record that is complete, coherent, internally consistent, and wrong. **This shelf proposes no content for either.**
