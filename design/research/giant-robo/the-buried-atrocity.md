# The Buried Atrocity — the Tragedy of Bashtarle as a Story Engine

*Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](README.md). The series' central device: a world of clean prosperity built on a hidden death toll, a scapegoat who was innocent, a will that was misread, and a truth delivered in four passes. This is the most directly reusable structure in the whole sweep. **This is not a decision document** and proposes no history for our setting. Claims are tagged **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

---

## 1. The device, stated

**[S]** Ten years before the series, a team of scientists developing the Shizuma Drive destroyed a small country — Bashtarle — in an accident that killed, by the series' own accounting, billions. The world was then given clean, limitless, recyclable energy and has been prosperous ever since. One scientist, Franken von Vogler, was recorded as the cause and died there. The survivors were decorated.

**[S]** The truth, arriving in the final episode: **Vogler was not the cause.** His colleagues were, through arrogance, and blamed a dead man. And Vogler was not conducting an experiment — he was attempting a repair, because the Shizuma Drive was generating an undetectable poison that would eventually kill everything alive. The enemy's world-ending plan was his repair, misunderstood by his own son and carried out as revenge.

## 2. Why it is the best thing in the series **[R]**

- **The setting is the crime.** Every light, every vehicle, every comfort in the world runs on the thing. The atrocity is not in a vault; it is in the wall sockets.
- **Everybody is a survivor, and they are on both sides.** The heroes' strategist was Vogler's assistant. The heroes' best field agent is his daughter, under a false name. The villain running the operation is his son. A mid-ranking enemy henchman is from the destroyed town. The man who actually caused it is the celebrated professor the heroes spend two episodes protecting. **Six characters, one event, six different partial accounts.**
- **The scapegoat is the antagonist for six episodes without being present.** A reputation does the work of a villain.
- **The reveal reframes without absolving.** Vogler was right; billions still died; his son still murdered his daughter. Nobody is let off.

## 3. The four passes **[S]**

The story is told four times, and each telling is true.

| Pass | What the audience is given | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The rumour** | A new disaster in Paris resembles the old one. The name is mentioned, and two trusted characters react badly to it | Establishes that there is a wound, not what it is |
| **2. The official account** | A full flashback: the mad scientist, the speech, the catastrophe | The audience now believes the version the world believes |
| **3. The correction** | The truth of who caused it. The scapegoating. The survivors' guilt reframed | The heroes' institution is implicated; the villain acquires a legitimate grievance |
| **4. The purpose** | The dead man's own recording: what he was actually doing, and why the enemy's plan is the repair | The war turns out to have been a misunderstanding with a body count |

**[R]** Each pass is a *correction of interpretation*, not a contradiction of fact. Nothing in pass two becomes false; it becomes incomplete. That is what keeps the audience trusting the story instead of feeling jerked around — and it is the specific discipline to copy.

## 4. Building one **[U]**

1. **Name the event, and use the name constantly.** One proper noun, spoken with discomfort, long before it is explained.
2. **Write what actually happened, in full, first.** Then write the official account as a document that is complete, coherent and wrong. `design/mystery-and-investigation-design.md` on fair play applies: the official version must be checkable and must hold up.
3. **Distribute survivors across every faction**, each with a different piece and a different motive for silence — guilt, procedure, shame, loyalty, profit.
4. **Attach the consequence to everyday infrastructure.** The atrocity should be visible in something the party uses every session.
5. **Plan the four passes, and gate each on player action.** This is where the series' method needs correcting for a table: its reveals arrive on a schedule. Ours must arrive because somebody asked, went somewhere, or pushed. `design/ttrpg-campaign-and-session-craft.md` §3 carries the standing audit for exactly this failure — is the discovery reachable three independent ways, or does it hinge on one check on one night?
6. **Decide what the truth does not fix.** If the reveal absolves everyone, it is a twist rather than a tragedy.

## 5. The companion device: the misread will

**[S]** The second half of the engine is a communication failure. Vogler left his son a rigged attaché case and four words of instruction. The son spent his life executing them wrongly, and learned the truth only after killing his sister. The series states the counterfactual outright: had the father found an easier way to explain, none of the war would have happened.

**[R] The device is excellent and the implementation is the series' weakest link.** A dying man who chooses an elaborate puzzle over a sentence is a contrivance, and audiences notice. **[U] If a misread legacy is used, the reason the message was incomplete has to hold up independently** — interrupted, censored, damaged, deliberately sealed by a third party, or written for a reader who was expected to have context they never received.

## 6. The innocent witness **[S/R]**

**[S]** Two characters do not know the truth: the twelve-year-old protagonist, and a small child on the enemy's side. The audience learns when he does.

**[R]** This is a reliable way to keep a decade-old conspiracy emotionally legible. The reveal is not an information event; it is something that happens to a person the audience cares about. **[U] A campaign version: make sure at least one PC-adjacent NPC genuinely does not know, and stage the reveal in front of them.**

## 7. For this campaign **[U]**

The campaign already has a founding catastrophe (`ideas/story/dossier-first-impact.md`), a redaction system in design (`ideas/entries/redacted-document-chain.json`, `design/harvest-inference.md`), and a bible thesis that nobody has ever done anything evil — only flinched, and then built a life on the flinch. **That thesis and this engine are the same shape**, which is the single most useful finding in this shelf: the Bashtarle structure is a worked, seven-hour demonstration of exactly the story our v4 bible says it wants to tell.

**This shelf proposes no content for our history.** What it offers is the four-pass discipline, the distributed-survivors method, and the warning in §5.
