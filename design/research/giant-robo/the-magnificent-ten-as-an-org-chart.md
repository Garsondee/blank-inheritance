# The Magnificent Ten as an Org Chart

*Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](README.md). The enemy organisation read as a structure rather than as a cast: who reports to whom, which rules exist, and which of those rules generate plots without the heroes doing anything. **This is not a decision document** and proposes no faction for our campaign. Claims are tagged **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

---

## 1. The chart

**[S]**

```
              BIG FIRE  (asleep; a boy; never gives a verifiable order)
                  |
        +---------+---------+
        |                   |
  THE THREE SERVANTS   SHOKATSU KOUMEI  (chief strategist, No. 2,
  (personal guard;      |                speaks in Big Fire's name)
  outrank Koumei        |
  socially)             +-- KO-ENSHAKU  (his direct agent; minder,
                        |                watches the Ten, obeys no one else)
                        |
              THE MAGNIFICENT TEN  (a council of equals, each running
                        |           their own operations)
                        |
              A / B / C-CLASS AGENTS  (Genya is A; Ivan is B; the
                        |              masked rank and file are C)
              MONSTER ROBOTS, built by one man, Dr. Black
```

The heroes mirror it almost exactly: **Yellow Emperor Raise** at the top, who never appears in the OVA at all; **the Big Nine**, nine strongest; branch chiefs such as Chujo, who is also one of the Nine; Experts rated by class; and the fortress Ryozanpaku, whose founder has been dead for years.

## 2. The rules, and what each one generates

**[S]** The organisation's stated rules, from the series and its supplementary material — with **[R]** the plot each one produces.

| Rule **[S]** | What it generates **[R]** |
|---|---|
| "Hail, Big Fire! Allegiance or death!" — faltering loyalty is punishable by death | Every conversation between members has a floor under it. Nobody can openly disagree with an order attributed to the leader, which is exactly what makes the strategist's monopoly on that attribution so powerful |
| Members of the Ten are **equal in status** to one another | No internal chain of command, so disputes have to be settled by persuasion, precedent or violence — and all three are interesting |
| Each member runs **their own operations**; when a project is big enough to affect the whole organisation, they share the burden on a rota | A built-in reason for a different villain each arc, and for all of them to resent whoever is currently in charge of the shared project |
| **Private armies are forbidden**, with exactly two exceptions | The exception is better than the rule. Nobody knows why Doki is allowed one, and the question sits there |
| The strategist speaks for a leader **nobody can consult** | The organisation's entire internal politics. See [characters/shokatsu-koumei.md](characters/shokatsu-koumei.md) |
| Seats are inherited — the leadership passed from Kawarazaki to Cervantes to Hanzui | A succession history, an emeritus with an opinion, and a vacancy the moment anyone dies |
| The enemy believes **only the Big Nine can kill one of the Ten** | A clean threat ladder the audience can hold in their head, and an enormous event when it is violated |

## 3. What makes it work as a *villain* structure **[R]**

- **A council of peers is a renewable antagonist.** Kill one and the organisation is not weakened structurally; it acquires a vacancy, a grudge and a promotion fight. The party can win permanently against an individual without the campaign losing its enemy. This is the cheapest recurring-villain solution in [the-recurring-antagonist-engine.md](the-recurring-antagonist-engine.md) §2.
- **The frictions are pre-loaded.** Before the plot starts there is already: a strategist the council hates, an emeritus watching him, a telepath who cannot read him, a minder they suspect of spying on them, a junior agent promoted over a senior one, one member with a private army and no explanation, and one member with no honour at all. **Seven live tensions, none of which require the heroes to exist.**
- **Two exceptions to the code make the code real.** See [characters/mask-the-red.md](characters/mask-the-red.md).
- **Honour between members is what makes them recur.** They mourn each other, investigate each other's deaths, and decline to obey plans they consider dishonourable or stupid. That is a society, and a society is a thing a campaign can be *about* rather than merely fight.

## 4. The honest criticism **[R]**

**Ten is too many.** The series meaningfully develops about six, uses two as functionaries, and leaves two as names. In a seven-episode OVA this is survivable because the roster's *size* is itself the threat. **At a table it is not survivable**: ten named enemies with ten powers is more than a group can track, and undifferentiated villains are worse than fewer ones.

**[U] The recommended shape for a campaign:** four to six developed seats, plus two or three names that are never used. Every developed seat gets a distinct power, a distinct temperament, one relationship inside the council and one opinion about the leadership. The unused names exist to make the council feel larger than the party's experience of it — the same trick as the Big Nine.

## 5. Building one **[U]**

A checklist, in the order the series' own structure suggests.

1. **The absent authority.** Who is invoked and never consulted? What is the salute or phrase that invokes them?
2. **The speaker.** Who claims to relay their orders? What did they do to earn the seat, and who resents it?
3. **The council.** Four to six seats. For each: power, temperament, one ally inside the council, one opinion about the speaker.
4. **The code.** One rule everyone follows, stated in a sentence. One rule with a known exception, unexplained.
5. **The exception to the code.** One member who does not follow it, and is kept anyway.
6. **The succession.** Who held the top seat before, and are they still alive?
7. **The outsider.** One agent who is not in the council and answers to nobody in it.
8. **The ladder.** Who is the party allowed to beat, and what is the stated reason the top tier is out of reach for now?

**[U]** Steps 4, 5 and 8 are the ones most often skipped, and they carry most of the weight.
