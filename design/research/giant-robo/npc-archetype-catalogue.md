# The NPC Archetype Catalogue

*Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](README.md). The whole sweep compressed: thirty-two archetypes, each with its source character, the single rule that makes it playable, and what it is for. Use this to pick a shape; use the character file to fill it in. **This is not a decision document and it casts nobody.** It does not name, place or assign any NPC in our setting, and it resolves nothing in `data/campaign/open-questions.json`. Claims are tagged **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

---

## 1. How to use this

**[U]** Three steps.

1. **Find the shape.** Scan the tables for a row that matches the job the campaign needs filling — a recurring enemy, a mentor, an institution's face.
2. **Read the character file.** Each row links to one. The file carries what the series actually did, why it worked, the voice, and audition lines.
3. **Check it against the project's own rules before writing anything.** `design/quests-objectives-and-threads.md` (every NPC needs one legible want — depth is free, wants are not), `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default` (a stub is the correct first version), `quarantine/ideas/story/bible/` and `LEDGER.md` (the only sources of truth for who exists), and `design/craft-library.md` §1 for the writing task itself.

**[R]** The archetype is a skeleton, not a character. Two NPCs built from the same row and given different wants will not resemble each other.

## 7. Casting balance

**[U]** A suggested distribution for a campaign of this shape, derived from what the series actually used rather than from what it listed. The numbers are a starting point, not a rule.

- **One** Sleeping King. **One** Untouchable Adviser. **One** Minder.
- **Four to six** enemy council seats — and among them, necessarily, one Reasonable Chairman, one Defector From The Code, and one One-Gimmick Killer. Plus two or three seats that are only names.
- **One or two** recurring escaping villains at most. More than that and the escapes become the campaign's texture rather than its exception ([the-recurring-antagonist-engine.md](the-recurring-antagonist-engine.md) §8).
- **One** Elder Brother, **one** Buffoon With The Buried Crime, **one** Professional Under An Assumed Name, **one** Assistant Who Was There, **one** Inseparable Pair.
- **Three** dead: the Father, the Monster Of Record, the Decorated Culprit. They are the cheapest characters in any campaign and they carry the history.
- **Eight or so** Named Floor entries per major institution.
- **Two or three** Offstage Legends. Not nine.

## 8. What this shelf does not supply

**[R]** An honest audit, because a catalogue that pretends to be complete is worse than a short one. Giant Robo has no equivalent of the following, and our campaign needs several of them:

- **A peer rival of the PCs' own age.** There is no schoolmate-antagonist, no rival pilot, nobody the protagonist competes with as an equal. For a campaign with six kids in a school, this is the largest gap in the sweep. The **Evangelion sweep**, since done, is the better source for it.
- **Named civilians.** The world is made of institutions. Almost nobody in it is a shopkeeper, a neighbour or a parent who is not also a scientist.
- **Teachers, and school life of any kind.** Nothing.
- **A female member of the enemy council.** The cabal's one notable woman is dead before the series and appears as a line in a husband's biography; the other is a child. If a council is cast here, do not inherit that shape by accident.
- **Sustained romance.** One relationship is stated and barely dramatised.
- **A living defector.** The one enemy who turns dies doing it. A campaign usually wants at least one enemy who changes sides and has to live among the party afterwards.
- **Ordinary ambition.** Nobody in this series wants money, promotion for its own sake, or a quiet life — except one henchman who was passed over. `design/quests-objectives-and-threads.md` already flags Wealth as a genuinely unclaimed motivational lane in our own material; this sweep does not fill it.
- **An ally the party can disappoint.** Loyalty in the series is assumed and permanent. A campaign benefits from relationships that can actually degrade — which is what `design/npc-social-intel.md`'s Attitude scale exists to track.

## 9. What a curated roster would need next

**[U]** Turning this catalogue into an actual cast for our adventure requires decisions this shelf cannot make:

- **Which factions exist**, beyond the Directorate (`data/factions/directorate.json`, `data/npcs/`) — a council-shaped enemy is a structural claim about the setting.
- **Names.** The naming placeholders in `data/campaign/open-questions.json` are the register, and the naming conventions are in `design/prose-craft-and-naming.md` §11–13 and [epithets-and-name-calling.md](epithets-and-name-calling.md).
- **The six kids and their Frames**, which CLAUDE.md reserves explicitly as collaborative creative work with the user, and which nothing on this shelf touches.
- **The open questions** that any antagonist economy would brush against, in `data/campaign/open-questions.json`.

**[U]** What can be done without any of those: **stubs**. Per `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default`, a placeholder NPC with a role, an epithet and one want is a legitimate thing to create freely as soon as the lore needs a role filled, and this catalogue is a list of roles worth filling.
