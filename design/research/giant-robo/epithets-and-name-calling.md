# Epithets, Name-Calling and Named Techniques

*Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](README.md). Every significant character in the series carries a descriptive title used as their name, and the convention is inherited from wuxia via Yokoyama's *Water Margin* adaptation. This document catalogues the patterns, gives generators, and covers the named-technique convention that goes with them. **This is not a decision document**; it names nobody in our setting and does not touch `data/campaign/naming-placeholders.json`. Claims are tagged **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

---

## 1. The convention

**[S]** In wuxia, characters carry nicknames alluding to their weapon, their appearance or their temperament. The *Water Margin*'s 108 heroes each have one. Imagawa followed the convention explicitly, following Yokoyama's adaptation, and the production notes name three examples: **Shockwave Alberto, Silent Chujo, Kenji the Immortal.**

**[S]** The epithet is not a title placed beside the name. **It replaces the name in ordinary speech.** Characters are introduced, addressed, announced and threatened by epithet.

## 2. The patterns, with the source examples

**[S]** Five shapes account for nearly every name in the series.

| Pattern | Source examples | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **[Phenomenon] + Name** | Shockwave Alberto (衝撃のアルベルト), Silent Chujo (静かなる中条), the Immortal Murasame Kenji (不死身の村雨健二) | The workhorse. Works in English without strain |
| **[Creature/Force] compound as the whole name** | the Black Whirlwind (黒旋風), the Blue-faced Beast (青面獣), the Demon King of Chaos (混世魔王) | Inherited wholesale from the *Water Margin*. Reads as a name, not a description |
| **Time-of-day image** | **Zangetsu the Midday** (白昼の残月, "the waning moon at midday"), **Yuki of the Gathering Dusk** (暮れなずむ幽鬼, "the lingering ghost of the slow-falling evening") | **[R] The series' own signature, and the most distinctive naming in it.** Both describe a *condition of light* rather than a power |
| **[Quality] + the splendid/dazzling** | Fitzcarraldo the Splendid (素晴らしき), Cervantes the Dazzling (眩惑の), Kawarazaki the Tumultuous (激動たる) | An adjective doing the work of a reputation. The grandiosity is the point |
| **Function as identity** | Mask the Red, Descendant Lord Doki (直系の怒鬼), Jujoji of the Bell of Life (命の鐘の) | Names an object or a lineage rather than a trait |

**[R]** The third row is where the series stops borrowing and starts inventing. "The waning moon at midday" is not a power, a weapon or a temperament — it is an image of something present and barely visible in daylight, which is a far stranger and better piece of naming than anything in the source novels. It is worth stealing as a *pattern* rather than as a phrase.

## 3. Generators

**[U]** Roll, pick or ignore. Three tables, matching the three most useful patterns above.

**a. [Phenomenon] + Name.** The phenomenon should be something that happens *to* the world, not something the character owns.

> Silent · Shockwave · Immortal · Tumultuous · Dazzling · Gathering · Unburnt · Unhurried · Iron · Quiet · Tidal · Hollow · Splendid · Patient · Sudden · Late

**b. Compound-as-name.** [Colour or element] + [creature, weather, or office].

> Colour/element: black, blue, white, ash, rust, salt, copper, ember, tin
> Noun: whirlwind, ox, lantern, beast, dog, king, monk, engine, bell, crane, tide, furnace, needle, kite

**c. The time-of-day image.** [Time or light condition] + [a thing that persists in it]. This is the hardest and the best.

> Light: midday, first light, last light, gathering dusk, blackout, the hour before the siren, noon in winter
> Persisting thing: the waning moon, the lamp still lit, the unswept shadow, the standing smoke, the open shutter, the second bell

**[U] Three rules for using any of them.**

1. **Say it aloud before adopting it.** `design/prose-craft-and-naming.md` §11 covers sound; an epithet is spoken far more often than it is read.
2. **Not everyone gets one.** In the source, epithets mark people who are *known* — which means being given one is itself a plot event, and a character who has one and should not is a clue.
3. **Do not translate twice.** Pick the English form the table will actually use and keep it. The series' own English releases wobble between Shockwave Alberto, Alberto the Impact and Shocking Alberto, and the wobble costs the character something every time.

## 4. Named techniques

**[S]** Three shapes in the source:

- **The plain powerful compound.** The *Big Bang Punch* (ビッグバン・パンチ). Blunt, and it works because it is blunt.
- **The classical four-character name.** Kaei's 追魂奪命剣 — "soul-chasing, life-taking sword" — a single arrow becoming a rain of arrows. This is the register of a real martial tradition, and it implies a school, a teacher and a lineage.
- **The descriptive noun.** Fitzcarraldo's 真空破, "vacuum-break". Names the physics, not the drama.

**[S]** Incantation is a fourth and separate thing: Issei's spoken formula is part of how the magic operates rather than a flourish — "Sixteen in the Heavens! Eight on Earth!" — and the series pairs it with brush-written kanji on screen.

**[U] Rules at a table.**

1. **One named technique per character.** A signature is singular.
2. **Say the name before the roll, not after.** Before the result, it is a declaration and a threat; after, it is narration.
3. **Match the register to the character's origin.** A classically trained fighter gets the four-character name; a European aristocrat with a cybernetic eye does not.
4. **Give the players the same privilege.** The convention is worth nothing if only the GM's characters get to announce things. `design/ux-doctrine.md` treats SF2E as a permissions game — a named technique is a piece of player-facing vocabulary, not GM colour.

## 5. The name card

**[S]** The series announces characters and declarations visually — brush-written kanji over the action at a moment of resolve.

**[U]** At our table the equivalent is the interface. `design/scene-runner` material and `design/objectives.md` both already own the idea of showing the table a persistent named thing. **If an enemy announces themselves, their epithet should appear where the party can see it and keep seeing it.** That is a UI suggestion for whoever builds the scene panel, not a specification — and the surrounding work is tracked under the console epics rather than here.

## 6. Cautions

**[R]**

- **An epithet has to survive the table's mouth.** If the group shortens it, that shortened form is the name now. Test it.
- **Do not epithet the whole cast at once.** The convention derives its force from marking the notable. The *Water Margin* gives one to 108 people because the 108 are the subject; a campaign that gives one to everybody has made them meaningless.
- **Translation register matters more than accuracy.** "Hanzui the Demon King of Chaos" and "Hanzui the Evil Messiah" are both used for the same character; they are not the same character.
