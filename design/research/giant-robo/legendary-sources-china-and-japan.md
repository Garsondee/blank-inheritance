# Legendary Sources — Chinese and Japanese Figures Pressed Into a Retrofuturistic World

*Part of the [Giant Robo research shelf](README.md). The OVA's heroes are, almost to a man, characters out of the *Water Margin* and *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* — kept by name, kept by epithet, given a superpower, and put in a 1950s suit. This document lists who is who, gives the real epithets, and extracts the method so it can be run on other material. **This is not a decision document.** It proposes no cast for our campaign and claims no legendary corpus for it; that call belongs to the user. Claims are tagged **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

---

## 1. The claim, stated plainly

**[S]** When Imagawa took the project he was forbidden to use the supporting cast of the original *Giant Robo* manga or its live-action series. With Mitsuteru Yokoyama's permission he populated the series from Yokoyama's entire back catalogue instead — which includes Yokoyama's long manga adaptations of the *Water Margin* (水滸伝, *Suikoden*) and *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* (三国志, *Sangokushi*). The consequence is that the International Police Organization's strike force is, structurally, the outlaws of Liangshan Marsh with energy weapons. Their base is even called **Ryozanpaku** (梁山泊) — the Japanese name of Liangshan Marsh itself, the outlaws' stronghold. Their founder, mentioned once and long dead, is **Chogai** (晁蓋), Liangshan's first chief, who in the novel also dies before the story completes.

**[R]** The reason to document this in detail is not trivia. It is that the method is reusable, cheap, and produces a cast that feels like it has a shared history before a single word of backstory is written.

## 3. The Three Kingdoms and the rest

**[S]**

- **Shokatsu Koumei** 諸葛亮孔明 is Zhuge Liang, the most famous strategist in Chinese literature, name untranslated. He is the enemy's chief strategist and is written as infuriatingly clever — the joke being that everyone in the audience already knows how the original ends up regarded.
- **Jujoji** 十常寺 takes his name from the 十常侍, the *Ten Regular Attendants* — the ten eunuchs whose corruption opens *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*. A collective of ten becomes one priest. **[R]** Inside an organisation whose leadership is also ten, this is a deliberate echo.
- **Kanshin** 韓信 is Han Xin, the great marshal of the Chu–Han contention, taken from Yokoyama's *Xiang Yu and Liu Bang*. In Giant Robo he is the IPO's strategist-marshal and the only one of the Big Nine the Chief defers to on planning — and he never appears.
- **Big Fire** himself is Koichi Yamano, the protagonist of Yokoyama's *Babel II*, with the same three servants renamed: Rodem, Ropross and Poseidon become Achilles, Garuda and Neptune.
- **Mask the Red** is Akakage (赤影) from *Kamen no Ninja Akakage* — **the hero of his own series, cast here as the cruellest villain in the cabal.** His rival among the Big Nine, Kagemaru, is the hero of *Iga no Kagemaru*.
- **Genya**, **Yuki** and the manga-only **Tenki** all come from *Iga no Kagemaru* as well; **Doki** and **Genyousai** come from *Yami no Dokiya*; **Sunny the Magician** is Sally, of *Sally the Witch*, under her original pre-publication name.
- **Ginrei** comes from *Ōkami no Seiza* and *Sono na wa 101*; **Kenji Murasame**, **Dr. von Vogler** (as 不乱拳 Furanken), **Dr. Shizuma de Montalban** (as Dragnet) and **Dr. Shikishima** all come from *Tetsujin 28-go*; **Alberto**, **Kawarazaki** and **Ivan** come from *Mars*; **Cervantes** is a whip-user from *Babel II*; **Chief Chujo** is *Babel II*'s security chief, renamed after a real actor, Shizuo Chujo.

## 4. The method, in four steps

**[R]** Reconstructed from what the transplants actually kept and dropped.

1. **Take the epithet, not the biography.** Almost nothing of Li Kui's actual plot survives into Tetsugyu. What survives is "Black Whirlwind, two axes, terrible temper". The epithet is a compression of a character into three words, which is exactly the size a supporting cast member needs to be.
2. **Literalise one element of the epithet as a power.** "Blue Faced Beast" becomes actual blue skin. "Magic Traveller" becomes super-speed. "Double Clubs" becomes twin clubs in a world of energy weapons. "Black Whirlwind" becomes axes that generate a whirlwind. The move is always the same: read the metaphor as a specification.
3. **Keep exactly one relationship from the source text and let it carry plot.** The best case in the series: in the novel, Gongsun Sheng and Fan Rui are connected through the same Taoist master. Giant Robo keeps it — and so the hero monk is at the IPO *because his master ordered him to bring his own senior disciple-brother home*, and that senior brother is the leader of the enemy's council. One inherited relationship generates a whole standing conflict for free.
4. **Put them in a suit.** The productive friction is between the ancient name and the modern dress: a Song-dynasty outlaw with a bow standing on a zeppelin, a Taoist adept in a Buddhist robe fighting a man in a tuxedo who kills by snapping his fingers. The series never comments on the friction, which is what makes it work.

## 5. What the method buys

**[R]**

- **Relationships that predate the story.** Borrowed characters arrive with each other already attached. A cast assembled this way has texture the first time it appears.
- **An implied offstage world.** Seven members of the Big Nine never appear and are only named, and the names are enough to make the organisation feel deep — because each name is a real figure with a real legend behind it (see [the-big-nine.md](characters/the-big-nine.md)).
- **Names that cohere as a set.** Nobody has to invent thirty names in the same register; the register comes from the source corpus, which was internally consistent centuries ago.
- **A fast generator.** A list of 108 heroes with 108 epithets is a lifetime supply of supporting characters, each pre-compressed to three words.

## 6. Cautions

**[R]** **The borrowed name has to work cold.** No viewer outside Japan is expected to know who Huyan Zhuo was; Ko-Enshaku works because a silent masked swordsman in red who walks out of shadows is legible without any of it. The legend is a scaffold for the writer, not a prerequisite for the audience. If a character only makes sense to someone who has read the source, the transplant has failed.

**[R]** **Use the forms, do not wear the culture as costume.** `design/japanese-narrative-conventions.md` covers this distinction properly. Giant Robo is Japanese pop culture drawing on Chinese classics through a specific Japanese author's long adaptations of them — that is a chain of transmission, not appropriation-by-aesthetic. Anything we do should be equally traceable: name the corpus, name the route, and get the epithets right rather than approximate.

**[R]** **Do not borrow a character whose legend contradicts the job.** The series is careless about this in one place — the Ten Attendants are a byword for corrupt court eunuchs, and Jujoji is a mystic priest with a bell. It survives because the name is only a sound to most of the audience, but the technique is weaker than the Fan Rui case, where the legend does real work.

## 7. For this campaign

**[U]** Two observations, both suggestions.

**We already have the catalogue.** The equivalent of "Yokoyama's back catalogue" for us is the material already written: 207 gazetteer locations and their NPCs, `quarantine/ideas/story/bible/`, the species and location files. The crossover method (see [the-yokoyama-crossover-method.md](the-yokoyama-crossover-method.md)) says to cast from the existing pile rather than invent fresh — which for us means an NPC roster built out of people the gazetteer already implies.

**On borrowing a legendary corpus.** The setting is a Japanese city with a locked 1950s tone pivot, which is precisely the position Giant Robo wrote from, so the move is available. Which corpus — the *Suikoden* and *Sangokushi* route Yokoyama took, Japanese folklore, the Heike, Edo-period *onmitsu* and *jitte*-carrying figures, or something else — is a creative call for the user, and the naming placeholders in `data/campaign/open-questions.json` are where the results would land. **This shelf does not choose it.** What the shelf does claim is narrower and testable: **if a corpus is chosen, take epithets rather than biographies, literalise one element as a power, and inherit exactly one relationship per pair of characters.**
