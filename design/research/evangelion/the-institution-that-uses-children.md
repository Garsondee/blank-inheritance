# The Institution That Uses Children

*Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](README.md). **Mined for structure, not for register** — see [tone-guardrail.md](tone-guardrail.md). The organisation as employer, guardian and hazard at once. **This is not a decision document and none of it is lore.** Claims are tagged **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

---

## 1. What the organisation actually does to them

**[S]** Stated plainly, because the accumulation is the point:

- **It numbers them.** The pilots are called "Children" both individually and collectively — First, Second, Third, Fourth. That is their designation in operational use.
- **It recruited one by paying for medical care.** Toji agrees to pilot on one condition: that his injured sister be transferred to a better hospital. The organisation accepts the deal.
- **It stocked a classroom.** Every student in the pilots' class is a candidate. None of them knows.
- **It teaches them a false history** through that classroom ([school-life-and-the-ordinary-day.md](school-life-and-the-ordinary-day.md) §1).
- **It built their replacement.** An autonomous dummy system exists, was opposed internally as inhuman, and is used once — to make one child's unit destroy another child's unit with that child inside.
- **It made the guardian the commanding officer.** The captain who orders a fourteen-year-old into a battle is also the adult he lives with.
- **It is itself being deceived** by the committee that funds it, and its commander is separately deceiving the committee. Nobody in the building has the whole picture, including the people giving orders.
- **It employs a man to spy on it**, who is simultaneously working for the government, the committee, and himself.

**[R]** Nothing in that list is an atrocity. Each item is a defensible decision made by a competent professional under pressure. **That is the design.** The horror is cumulative and administrative, and it is far more usable than a villainous organisation, because everybody in it can be sympathetic and the sum is still wrong.

## 2. The best single idea: the guardian who is also the commander

**[S]** The operations captain is the boy's legal guardian, housemate, and commanding officer. Character material states the conflict directly: she cannot be a surrogate mother while also being the officer who sends him out to die, and he cannot fully trust her as one while she is the other.

**[R]** This is the most portable institutional idea on either shelf. It requires no setting, no technology and no conspiracy — only one NPC holding two roles that are in genuine conflict. Every scene she is in has a live question underneath it: **which one is she being right now?** And the answer is often "both, badly".

**[U]** If a campaign has an adult who is both a PC's caregiver and their commanding officer, that NPC needs only one additional thing to work: **a scene where the two roles give opposite instructions, and she has to pick.** Write the scene before the campaign needs it.

## 3. What the adults are good at

**[R]** Worth stating, because the series' reputation obscures it. The adults are **extremely competent at their jobs**: the machines are maintained, the intelligence is good, the research works, the plans are sound, and the support staff save the day at least once entirely on their own. What they fail at is being what the children actually need, which is not tactical support.

**[U] That split is the useful one.** An institution can be excellent and still fail its people, and showing both is more interesting than showing either. It also means the party's employer can be genuinely worth working for, which a warm campaign needs.

## 4. The honest reckoning for this campaign

**[R]** This campaign is about children in an institution that uses them. That is not an analogy; it is the premise. So this file is closer to our material than anything else on either shelf, and it needs a clear statement of where the two diverge.

**[S]** The v4 bible's thesis is that **nobody in our story has ever done anything evil** — every catastrophe is an ordinary person flinching and then building a life on that moment.

**[R]** Those two engines are compatible, and the combination is the interesting one:

- Evangelion supplies **the mechanisms** — numbering, purchased consent, the candidate pool, the role conflict, the replacement threat, the false history taught sincerely.
- Our bible supplies **the reason** — not malice, but a series of ordinary flinches that nobody has ever gone back and corrected.
- The Giant Robo shelf supplies **the people** — adults who are warm, funny, competent and on the children's side, which is what keeps the result playable rather than bleak.

**[U]** The suggestion is only this: when writing our institution, take the mechanism from here, the motive from the bible, and the temperature from the other shelf. **What our institution actually does, and to whom, is the user's call** — `data/factions/directorate.json`, `data/npcs/`, `design/induction.md` and `data/campaign/open-questions.json` are where those decisions live.

## 5. Three mechanisms worth stealing, restated as instructions **[U]**

1. **Give the party a designation, and use it in official contexts only.** A number or a call-sign that appears on paperwork and in orders, while everybody who likes them uses their name. The gap between the two registers does all the work.
2. **Buy one PC's or NPC's consent with something they could not otherwise get**, and let the deal be honoured. The institution keeping its promise is more unsettling than breaking it.
3. **Let one competent professional inside the organisation object, on the record, and be overruled.** That character is the audience's proof that the wrongness is visible from the inside.
