# The NPC Archetype Catalogue (Evangelion)

*Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](README.md). **Mined for structure, not for register** — [tone-guardrail.md](tone-guardrail.md) governs every row below. Twenty-one archetypes, each with its source character and the one rule that makes it playable, plus an audit of what this source cannot supply and a cross-shelf table for using it alongside [Giant Robo](../giant-robo/npc-archetype-catalogue.md). **This is not a decision document and it casts nobody.** Claims are tagged **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

---

## 1. How to use this

**[U]** Same three steps as the other shelf — find the shape, read the character file, check it against the project's own rules (`design/quests-objectives-and-threads.md` for the want, `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default` for the first version, `quarantine/ideas/story/bible/` and `LEDGER.md` for what actually exists).

**[U] One additional step here, and it is not optional: read [tone-guardrail.md](tone-guardrail.md) first.** Every archetype below came out of a work whose register this campaign has explicitly rejected. The shapes are sound; the temperature is not. Where a row has a warmth problem, the "one rule" column is usually the correction.

## 2. The peers and the school

**[R]** This is the section the Giant Robo sweep could not produce at all. Its own audit named the missing pieces — a peer rival of the PCs' age, school life, teachers, named civilians — and these seven rows are them.

| Archetype | Source | The one rule | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Competence As Armour** | [Asuka](characters/asuka-langley-soryu.md) | Her competence is real, and fails at something specific and public — with a route back that does not require her to stop being proud | **The peer rival.** The single biggest gap this sweep fills |
| **The One Who Notices** | [Hikari](characters/hikari-horaki.md) | She keeps approaching after being rebuffed, and the rebuff costs her something visible | Ordinary authority, and the only functioning care in the story |
| **The Friend Who Started As An Enemy, Correctly** | [Toji](characters/toji-suzuhara.md) | His grievance is true and is never retracted | An ally introduced as an aggrieved party with a real case |
| **The One Who Wants The Job** | [Kensuke](characters/kensuke-aida.md) | He asks on screen and is refused, and the refusal is neither cruel nor explained away | Recalibrating how strange the party's life is |
| **The Competent Person Who Hates The Job** | [Shinji](characters/shinji-ikari.md) | **He always goes.** The complaint is real; the refusal is not | The cost of the work, said out loud, without stalling the table |
| **The One Who Was Made For This** | [Rei](characters/rei-ayanami.md) | Somebody notices her early, over something small, and it is granted | Institutional horror that is administrative rather than gothic |
| **The Late Arrival Who Accepts You** | [Kaworu](characters/kaworu-nagisa.md) | His kindness is genuine and stays genuine after the reveal | An enemy the party likes, met one-to-one |
| **The One Who Enjoys It** | [Mari](characters/mari-makinami.md) | Her enjoyment is sincere and hides nothing | A contrast engine — and a caution about adding characters late |

**[U]** The two rows where a machine acts on its own (the Mother in the Machine, §4, and Unit-01 above) must stop short of a PC's own machine acting without its player: this campaign rules that out (`quarantine/ideas/story/bible/LEDGER.md` #31, D11).

## 6. Casting balance **[U]**

Derived from what the series actually used, corrected for our register:

- **One** officer-guardian. **One** committee above the employer. **One** untouchable parent-in-the-chain, if any.
- **Three** support staff with one opinion each — the cheapest strong content on this shelf.
- **Two to four** peers in the school, including **one rival, one who notices, and one who wants the job.**
- **One** investigator who pays, spent when the campaign needs its conspiracy to feel real.
- **Two or three** dead: the predecessor in the system, the casualty of the earlier programme, and the parent whose choice built the technology.
- **One** unexplained harmless thing. Exactly one. It stops working in the plural.
- **At least two warm, competent adults who are succeeding** — **which this shelf cannot supply.** Take them from [the Giant Robo catalogue](../giant-robo/npc-archetype-catalogue.md).

## 7. What this shelf does not supply

**[R]** The honest audit, as on the other shelf.

- **A warm adult who is good at the caring part.** Every guardian figure here is failing at it, by design. This is the largest gap and the most important to fill from elsewhere.
- **An honourable enemy, or an enemy society.** The Angels do not talk and SEELE is a committee. There is no code, no rivalry, no mourned opponent — all of which the other shelf has in quantity.
- **A recurring antagonist who escapes.** The enemies here are destroyed, one per encounter. **For the original brief about villains who announce themselves, taunt the party and get away, this shelf has nothing** — that is entirely [the other shelf's antagonist engine](../giant-robo/the-recurring-antagonist-engine.md).
- **Comedy as structure.** The series is funny, and its broadest comedy was quarantined into spin-offs. Our tone rule puts comedy in the main line, so the model has to come from elsewhere.
- **Ensemble teamwork.** The pilots cooperate properly in about one episode. A party-based campaign needs far more, and the one example here — six days of rehearsal to land a synchronised attack — is worth more than the rest of the series put together on this point.
- **Epithets, named techniques, or theatre.** Nobody announces anything.
- **A mentor who succeeds**, and **a friendship between adults that lasts.**
- **Resolution.** See [withheld-information-and-the-ending-problem.md](withheld-information-and-the-ending-problem.md).

## 8. Using the two shelves together

**[U]** Now that both exist, this is the table that matters. Where they disagree, the right answer is usually "structure from one, temperature from the other".

| The job | Take from | Because |
|---|---|---|
| **Tone and register** | **Giant Robo, always** | The standing rule. `feedback_tone_giant_robo_not_eva` |
| **Recurring villains who get away** | Giant Robo | This shelf has none |
| **Enemies as encounter design** | **Evangelion** | Sixteen puzzles, sixteen solutions, no repeats |
| **Allies, mentors, warm authority** | Giant Robo | This shelf's adults are all failing on purpose |
| **The institution the party works for** | **Evangelion** | Numbering, purchased consent, role conflict, the replacement threat |
| **School, classmates, the ordinary day** | **Evangelion** | Giant Robo has none of it |
| **A peer rival of the PCs' own age** | **Evangelion** | Named as Giant Robo's largest gap |
| **Relationships that change over time** | **Evangelion** for the mechanism, Giant Robo for the direction | One tracks decay; the other assumes loyalty. The truth is both |
| **Names** | Both — epithets from Giant Robo, a corpus from Evangelion | Reputation versus register. They compose |
| **A long buried truth** | **Giant Robo's four passes** | Both withhold; only one pays |
| **The machine as a character** | Either — they agree | Both conclude a mecha becomes a character when it does slightly more than it was told |
| **Institutional comedy** | Both | The paperwork gag here, the whole register there |
