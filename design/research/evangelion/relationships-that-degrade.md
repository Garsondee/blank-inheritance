# Relationships That Degrade

*Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](README.md). **Mined for structure, not for register** — see [tone-guardrail.md](tone-guardrail.md). The Giant Robo shelf's audit named this as its own largest gap: in that series, loyalty is assumed and permanent. Here, every bond is a live value that moves. **This is not a decision document and none of it is lore.** Claims are tagged **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

---

## 1. The thesis, and why to keep only half of it

**[S]** Episode 4 is titled *Hedgehog's Dilemma*, and the idea is used throughout: Schopenhauer's parable of porcupines who must huddle for warmth and wound each other with their quills. Anno built the protagonist around it explicitly, alongside other Freudian material. The series' position is that closeness injures, so people withdraw, and the withdrawal is itself the injury.

**[R] Take the mechanism; refuse the verdict.** The mechanism — *intimacy has a cost, and people manage that cost badly* — is true, useful, and produces drama at every table. The verdict — *therefore connection is a trap* — is the thing the guardrail exists to keep out. A campaign that proves reaching out makes things worse will train its players to stop reaching out, and a table that has stopped reaching out is not playing.

## 2. The eight relationships, and which way each moves

**[S]**

| The pair | Starts as | Ends as | What moved it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Misato and Ritsuko** | University friends, close, easy | A fractured friendship neither can repair | Professional secrets each is required to keep from the other, then resentment about the keeping |
| **Shinji and Asuka** | Rivalry with obvious mutual attraction | Mutual contempt and one unforgivable scene | Neither can admit need; each reads the other's approach as an attack |
| **Shinji and Toji** | Toji punches him, twice, with cause | The closest friendship either has | Toji learns what the job actually costs, and says so out loud |
| **Asuka and Hikari** | Nothing; Hikari is the only classmate who tries | Hikari takes her in when she collapses | One person kept approaching after being rebuffed |
| **Gendo and Ritsuko** | A secret relationship | She tries to destroy the base; he shoots her | She works out she was being used, and that she was a replacement for her own mother |
| **Ritsuko and Maya** | Junior's professional veneration | Unspoken and unreciprocated, never resolved | Nothing. It is simply never addressed, which is its own kind of accurate |
| **Misato and Kaji** | Ex-lovers, unfinished | Reunited briefly, then he is assassinated | He tells her the truth she needed and dies for having found it |
| **Shinji and Kaworu** | Complete, immediate, unconditional acceptance | Shinji kills him at his request, in about a day | The whole relationship lasts one episode, which is why it is the one everybody remembers |

**[R]** Three of those improve. That matters more than the five that fail, because it proves the model is a **direction of travel** rather than a downhill slope. The improving three all share one property: **somebody kept showing up after being rebuffed.**

## 3. Why the failures work dramatically

**[R]**

- **They fail for institutional reasons, not personal ones.** Misato and Ritsuko do not fall out over a betrayal; they fall out because their jobs require them to lie to each other and they are both good at their jobs. That is a far more usable engine than a falling-out, because it can happen to characters who like each other and keep liking each other.
- **The cause is always legible to the audience and invisible to the characters.** The viewer can see exactly what broke and when. Nobody inside can.
- **The worst one has a specific trigger.** Ritsuko's collapse is dated: the moment she understands she was chosen because she resembled her mother, who had the same relationship with the same man.
- **[R] The series is less good at repair.** Almost nothing is mended. That is its main structural weakness for our purposes, and the reason §4 exists.

## 4. Running this at a warm table **[U]**

Four rules. All four are corrections to the source rather than copies of it.

1. **Degradation must be recoverable.** Every relationship that can get worse must have a stated, achievable way to get better. The players do not need to be told what it is; the GM needs to have written it down.
2. **The cause must become legible.** Not immediately — but the players must be able to find out *why* an NPC cooled toward them, by asking, watching, or asking someone else. An unexplained attitude change is a bug.
3. **Institutional causes beat personal ones.** Prefer "she was ordered not to tell you" over "she decided she dislikes you". It is more interesting, it is recoverable, and it aims the players at the organisation rather than at each other.
4. **Keep at least one bond that only improves.** The friend who keeps showing up. The series has three, and they are the only reason it is bearable.

**[U] The machinery already exists.** `design/npc-social-intel.md` gives every NPC an **Attitude** on SF2E's real five-step scale (Hostile → Unfriendly → Indifferent → Friendly → Helpful) and a set of permanent **Facts** with reveal triggers. That is exactly the structure this document is describing. **What is missing is not a system; it is the habit of moving the value, and a written reason each time it moves.** A one-line note per NPC — what raises this, what lowers it — would make the existing field a live one.

## 5. The one to be careful with **[U]**

**[S]** The relationship between the two teenage pilots ends in a scene of attempted strangulation, and the series treats the mutual cruelty between them as its most honest material.

**[U]** Do not import PC-to-PC hostility as a designed feature. Player characters in conflict is a table-management question, not a story device, and it is one the group agrees to in advance or not at all (`design/ttrpg-campaign-and-session-craft.md` on safety tools; `design/agency-and-player-choice.md` on what may and may not be imposed). **The degrade-and-repair model belongs on NPC relationships.**
