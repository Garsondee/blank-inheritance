# Naoko Akagi (赤木ナオコ)

*Deceased, 2010 · the predecessor whose work everyone still depends on. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | A world-renowned scientist at the predecessor research centre; the chief scientist's mother |
| **Naming** | After an acquaintance of Anno's from primary school — one of the few names not from the naval register |
| **Fate** | Killed a child in a moment of rage in 2010 and then killed herself, by throwing herself into the computer she had built |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- She knew the truth about the disaster and about what the machines were for.
- **In 2008 she completed the fundamental theory behind the MAGI**, the three supercomputers the organisation runs on — and she did it by **transcribing three aspects of her own personality into them: the woman, the scientist, and the mother.** The system that makes every decision in the building is three copies of one dead person.
- She was in a secret relationship with the commander while he was married. She worked out that she was being used. She continued.
- In 2010 the First Child — a small girl wearing the face of the commander's dead wife — called her an old hag. She strangled her, and then killed herself against the computer carrying her own maternal personality.
- **[S]** Her daughter later dyes her hair to look less like her, joins the same organisation, takes the same job, and enters the same relationship with the same man. At the end, the computer containing her mother's *woman* aspect refuses her daughter's order to self-destruct.

## 2. Why she works **[R]**

- **The infrastructure has her opinions in it.** This is the idea worth the whole file. The organisation's decision-making system is literally three facets of a dead woman, which means every automated ruling the party receives is, in some sense, her — and at the critical moment the machine acts like a person rather than a program, because it is one.
- **She is the pattern her daughter repeats.** The series does not state the parallel; it lays the two lives side by side and lets the dates do it. See [ritsuko-akagi.md](ritsuko-akagi.md).
- **Her worst act is small and human.** Not a grand betrayal — an insult from a child, a resemblance she could not bear, and a few seconds. **[R] The series is careful that the thing which destroys her is ordinary**, which is more frightening than a plot would have been.
- **[R] She is also the reason the organisation cannot simply be rebuilt elsewhere.** Institutions accumulate irreplaceable people, and this one accumulated hers into the hardware.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Predecessor In The System.* A dead expert whose personal judgement is embedded in the equipment, the procedures or the rules everybody still follows.

**The one rule:** **Her decisions must constrain the party in the present, in a way they can trace back to her.** A rule nobody can override, a system that refuses a command, a procedure that makes no sense until you learn who wrote it and why.

**Presence:** she is a name on old documentation, a voice in a recording, and the reason a machine says no.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The constraint:* the system refuses an authorised instruction, and the error message cites a rule nobody currently employed has ever read.
- *The trace:* the rule has an author and a date, and the date is before anyone the party knows worked there.
- *The reason:* somebody who knew her explains what she was protecting against. It is still a good reason. It is also ruining the party's plan.

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the dead expert whose judgement is baked into the infrastructure; a system that refuses an order for a reason that turns out to be personal; the repeated pattern across two generations, shown rather than stated; a catastrophic act that is small and ordinary rather than villainous.

**Worth avoiding:** the manner of her death as spectacle. It is one line in a history, and that is the right weight for it. `design/ttrpg-campaign-and-session-craft.md` on safety tools applies to anything heavier.

**Where it might attach:** `data/hangar/legacy-units.json` is empty and `design/strategy-layer.md` already models an institution with standing rules and Clearance rungs. **A rule with an author is better content than a rule without one**, and this archetype is the cheapest way to give an institution a history: one dead name, attached to one restriction the party keeps running into. Whether our institution has such a person is the user's call.
