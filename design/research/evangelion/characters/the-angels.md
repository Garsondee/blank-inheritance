# The Angels (使徒)

*The enemy · sixteen enemies that never speak and never repeat themselves. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — these do not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use. The full encounter-by-encounter catalogue is in [the-enemy-as-puzzle.md](../the-enemy-as-puzzle.md); this file covers them as a class.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 使徒 (*shito*) — literally "apostle", not "angel" |
| **Number** | Seventeen, prophesied and known in advance. **Reduced from a planned twenty-eight during production** |
| **Names** | Taken from angels of Judeo-Christian apocrypha, matched loosely to domains — the angel of fish for the aquatic one, of rain for the acid one, of birds for the one in orbit, of music for the one that splits in two |
| **Shared properties** | An AT Field; a core; roughly 99.89% genetic affinity with human beings |

## 1. What they are **[S]**

- **They never speak.** Not one of them communicates in language, negotiates, threatens or explains — until the seventeenth, who is a boy and does all four.
- **They have no stated motive** beyond reaching the being held in the basement of the party's own headquarters.
- **They are individually distinct to an unusual degree.** A geometric solid, a fungal parasite, a nanomachine colony, a shadow, an orbital light, an insectile walker, a whale-sized swimmer. Production notes record the non-anthropomorphic approach as partly a resource decision: bodies that need not be animated as bodies.
- **They are counted.** Everyone in-universe knows how many are coming.
- **They are nearly us.** The genetic affinity figure is stated, and the implication — that the enemy and humanity are two versions of the same thing — underlies the ending.

## 2. Why the class works **[R]**

- **Silence is the design.** Because they never explain, everything the party learns about them is learned by observation, autopsy, instrumentation and combat. **[R] An enemy that cannot be interrogated makes the science staff into investigators and turns every fight into a source of evidence.**
- **The shared rule makes them a species; the gimmicks make them problems.** See [the-enemy-as-puzzle.md](../the-enemy-as-puzzle.md) §1 for the full statement, which is the most portable thing on this shelf.
- **The count is a clock.** A known, finite enemy list means every encounter is an instalment in a countable story, and the audience can feel the end approaching without being told.
- **The near-identity is the payload.** The reveal that the enemy is 99.89% the same as us converts a monster series into something else entirely, and it was planted as a technical detail in an early briefing.
- **[R] The naming is doing quiet work.** Apocryphal angels with assigned natural domains give a coherent set of names that sound systematic without requiring a system, and the mismatch between the domains and the creatures is part of the strangeness. Compare the naming method on the other side of the show ([warship-names-and-the-naming-method.md](../warship-names-and-the-naming-method.md)) — **two different corpora, two different registers, one for the people and one for the enemy.**

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Enemy That Never Explains.* A counted, finite class of adversaries sharing one universal property, each with a unique capability, none of which communicate.

**The one rule:** **No dialogue, ever, except for exactly one of them at the very end.** The silence is what makes the last one matter, and one exception is the entire dividend of sixteen instances of discipline.

**How to run them:** describe behaviour, not intent. Never narrate what it wants. Let the party's specialists infer, be wrong, and correct themselves. Put the evidence in the debris.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *Arrival:* it is detected before it is seen, by an instrument, and the reading is wrong in a way nobody can explain.
- *During:* it does one thing the party has never seen anything do, and it does it without any indication of effort.
- *After:* something is left behind, and the science staff spend the next session arguing about it.

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the silent enemy read through evidence; the counted, finite enemy list as a campaign clock; one universal property plus unique gimmicks; a separate naming corpus for the enemy; the single late exception that speaks.

**Worth avoiding:** the ambiguity about what they were for. The series never fully commits, and it is one of the things the ending is criticised for.

**Where it might attach:** `data/antagonists/claimants/claimant-guidelines.json` holds build rules and lore for Claimants, and no individual Claimants exist yet. **Everything in §3 is a method for populating that file, not a proposal about what a Claimant is** — which is the user's to settle (the Watcher's children, `quarantine/ideas/story/dossier-observer.md`).
