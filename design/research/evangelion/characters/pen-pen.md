# Pen Pen (ペンペン)

*Misato's flatmate · the warm-blooded joke that keeps a household real. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What he is** | A hot-spring penguin — a biotechnology experiment, with a serial number on his collar |
| **Where he lives** | In the captain's flat, in his own refrigerated room, alongside two teenagers and a lot of empty beer cans |
| **Design** | Originally conceived as a monkey, for the hot-springs association, and rejected as unattractive; drawn instead from a 1986 anime about a genetically modified penguin |
| **Fate** | **He is evacuated to a country house with the class representative's family, and he is fine** |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- High enough intelligence to bathe himself, understand human speech, read a newspaper, and live in the flat as a third occupant rather than as a pet.
- He is a laboratory product. The serial number is on his collar and nobody comments on it.
- He is the household's third character in every domestic scene: the fridge, the bath, the argument about the washing-up, the new lodger arriving.
- **[S]** When the city is evacuated he is sent to the country with a classmate's family. The series makes a point of it.

## 2. Why he matters **[R]**

This entry is not a joke, and the case for it is specific.

- **He is the single most tone-compatible element on this shelf.** A campaign locked to a warm register — `feedback_tone_giant_robo_not_eva` — can take Pen Pen wholesale and change nothing. Almost nothing else here survives that test unmodified.
- **He makes the flat a home rather than a set.** The domestic scenes work because there is a third party in them with his own routine and his own opinions about the bathroom. **[R] A household needs one member who is not part of the plot.**
- **He is evidence about the captain.** She is a chaotic, hard-drinking officer who is also keeping a laboratory animal alive and happy in a properly refrigerated room, and who arranges his evacuation before her own. Nobody has to say she is a carer; the penguin says it.
- **He is never explained and never plot-relevant.** He is not a secret, a weapon, or a metaphor. **[R] The discipline of leaving one element unexplained and harmless is worth more than it looks: it tells the audience that not everything strange is a threat**, which is exactly the lesson a warm campaign in a strange world needs to teach early.
- **He survives.** In a series that kills most of its cast and breaks the rest, the penguin gets a country house. That is a deliberate act of mercy by the writers, and it is the cheapest possible reassurance to an audience.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Unexplained Harmless Thing.* A strange, funny, non-hostile presence in the party's domestic life, with no plot function and no explanation.

**The one rule:** **Never make it important.** No secret, no reveal, no sacrifice, no hidden power. The moment it matters to the plot, it stops doing the job it was there for.

**How to run it:** give it exactly three habits and repeat them. It is present in domestic scenes, absent from operational ones, and it has opinions about one specific thing — the bath, the chair, the fridge, the noise.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *Establishing:* it is already in the best seat when the party arrives, and it does not move.
- *Running:* something catastrophic has happened, and it is eating.
- *The payoff:* somebody the party is worried about has, without being asked, arranged for it to be somewhere safe.

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** all of it. The household member who is not part of the plot; the laboratory origin left uncommented; the officer characterised by how well she looks after something small; the deliberate survival of one harmless thing.

**Worth avoiding:** explaining it later. The temptation will come.

**Where it might attach:** our campaign has kids with homes, a school, and a city rendered from real data with 207 gazetteer locations. **A shared domestic fixture — an animal, a machine, a neighbour's something — is a free warmth generator and costs one stub** (`feedback_npc_stubs_by_default`). This is one of very few places where this shelf and the Giant Robo shelf want exactly the same thing, and it is worth noticing that the thing they agree on is a joke that everybody is fond of.
