## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

This is a short entry because the facts are few and heavy, and they are stated plainly rather than dwelt on.

- In 2005 she was the subject of a contact experiment with Unit-02, the machine she had campaigned to build. She suffered mental contamination and severe psychological damage.
- Hospitalised, **she no longer recognised her daughter.** She spoke instead to a cloth doll, which she believed was her child.
- Her husband began a relationship with another woman.
- Shortly after her daughter was selected as a pilot, the daughter found her hanged.
- **[S]** A portion of her soul — the maternal part — is understood to remain inside Unit-02, the machine her daughter now pilots.

## 2. Why it matters structurally **[R]**

- **The party's equipment has a casualty list.** The daughter climbs into the thing that did this, every time, and nobody in the organisation treats that as remarkable. **[R] That single arrangement says more about the institution than any scene of villainy.**
- **The programme has a prehistory.** Somebody tried this before the party arrived, and it went badly, and the results were kept. This is how a campaign's technology acquires weight without a single page of technical exposition: not by explaining how it works, but by naming who it was tested on.
- **The doll is the detail that does the work.** One concrete, ordinary object carries the whole tragedy. It is far more effective than a description of her condition, and it is the model for how to handle material this heavy — **one specific image, stated once, not elaborated.**

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Casualty Of The Earlier Programme.* Someone harmed by the party's own technology during its development, whose case is on file and whose family is still in the story.

**The one rule:** **The party uses the equipment that did it, and the institution has never apologised.** Both halves. Without the first it is backstory; without the second it is a resolved matter.

**Presence:** a file, an old photograph, a ward, an object left behind, and a living relative who does not talk about it.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The record:* an incident report from before the party's time, with a name, a date, and a euphemism where the outcome should be.
- *The object:* something small and domestic among the technical effects, which nobody has thrown away.
- *The relative:* somebody the party works with every week, who changes the subject.

## 4. Handling **[U]**

**[U]** This material includes a parent's suicide discovered by their child. It is legitimate subject matter and this campaign is not forbidden it — but it is a content decision made in advance with the table, with safety tools in place (`design/ttrpg-campaign-and-session-craft.md`), and never used as atmosphere. [tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md) §3.7 sets the rule for the whole shelf.

**[U]** The portable half, which carries none of that weight: **the earlier programme, the people it hurt, and the fact that the file is still in the cabinet.** A campaign can have all of that without staging anything.

## 5. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the prehistory of the technology, told through one casualty; one concrete domestic object standing in for a long history; a living relative in the present who will not discuss it; equipment with a name attached to it in an old report.

**Where it might attach:** `data/hangar/legacy-units.json` is empty, and `ideas/entries/redacted-document-chain.json` describes a chain of redacted documents. **An incident report about the programme that preceded our Frames is the intersection of those two** — and what happened in it, and to whom, is a story-bible decision for the user, not this shelf's.
