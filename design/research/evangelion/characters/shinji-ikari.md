# Shinji Ikari (碇シンジ)

*Pilot, Evangelion Unit-01 · the reluctant protagonist, and why reluctance is a trap at a table. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. ## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Summoned to a city he does not know by a father who has not spoken to him in years, and told to get into a machine. He does.
- **Emotionally hypersensitive.** He is often obedient out of fear of rejection rather than out of duty, and he repeatedly refuses to pilot because of what it does to him.
- His motto is that he must not run away — and **Anno has said explicitly that this is not the series' message.** The final episodes tell him it is acceptable to leave situations that hurt him. The actual theme is making a choice instead of remaining in limbo.
- He runs away several times and comes back each time.
- Assistant director Kazuya Tsurumaki's assessment is worth more than the popular reading: Shinji is **not as weak as he is usually taken to be** — he is obstinate, and he does not really pay attention to other people. Tsurumaki compares him to Anno himself.
- His best stretch is early: he acquires friends, a guardian, a place to live, and does the job competently for several episodes before the accumulation gets to him.

## 2. Why he works, and where he stops working **[R]**

**He works** because the audience is placed exactly at his eye level. He is the only person in the series who is told nothing, and so everything the audience learns arrives when he learns it. That is an efficient way to run a conspiracy story.

**He stops working the moment the format changes.** In a fixed narrative the audience cannot leave, so a protagonist who will not act creates tension. **At a table there is no such tension — there is a stalled session.** A PC who refuses the premise stops the other five players, and a GM who builds a campaign around persuading a reluctant protagonist has built a campaign about arguing.

**[R]** The distinction worth keeping is narrow and real: **hating the job is playable; refusing to do it is not.** A character who shows up, does it, and says plainly what it costs gives the table everything the archetype has to offer and none of the paralysis.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Competent Person Who Hates The Job.* Someone who performs a dangerous duty well, without ever pretending to want it.

**The one rule:** **He always goes.** The complaint is real, the reluctance is real, the refusal is not. Everything interesting about the archetype survives that constraint; everything unplayable about it is removed by it.

**Voice:** flat, apologetic, over-agreeable in company and privately obstinate. Says sorry as punctuation. Answers questions accurately and volunteers nothing. Becomes suddenly and startlingly direct about once a session, usually at the worst moment.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To an adult:* "I'll do it. I'm not saying I'll be all right afterwards. You didn't ask about that, so I thought I'd say it now."
- *To a peer:* "You think I'm brave. I'm not — I just can't think of the sentence that gets me out of it."
- *Refusing, correctly:* "No. Not that one. I'll do the next one, and the one after. Not that one."
- *After:* "Is somebody going to tell his family? Can it be someone who knew him?"

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the viewpoint character who is told nothing, so the audience learns with him; the person who does the job and says out loud what it costs; the early stretch of genuine competence that makes the later strain mean something.

**Worth avoiding:** reluctance as an engine (see [tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md) §3.2); and handing this shape to a PC. As an NPC it is fine. As a PC concept it needs the player to understand they are signing up to show up.

**Where it might attach:** our campaign has six kid PCs and a Frame each, and `design/induction.md` already treats character creation as an in-world intake process. **The useful import is not a character but a permission**: the intake should let a player build someone who is frightened of this and does it anyway, with mechanical support rather than mechanical penalty. Whether it does is a question for `design/induction.md` and the user.
