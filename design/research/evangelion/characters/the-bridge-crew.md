# The Bridge Crew — Maya Ibuki, Makoto Hyuga, Shigeru Aoba

*NERV Operations Command Centre · three technicians, three opinions, and how a control room becomes a place. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — these characters do not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Naming** | All three from the naval register — **Maya Ibuki is two cruisers at once**; Makoto Hyuga is a battleship; Shigeru Aoba is a cruiser, with a pun on a 1974 film title |
| **Role** | The three people at the consoles during every operation. They are not pilots, not commanders, and in every battle |

## 1. Who they are **[S]**

**Maya Ibuki** — the technical operator. She reads out the pilots' synchronisation ratios and assists on the machines' development. Diligent, highly competent, and emotionally the youngest person in the room. **She is the one who objects to the autonomous dummy system on the grounds that it is inhuman**, and she is visibly sickened during the worst of the fighting. She venerates her superior, the chief scientist, and that veneration gradually becomes something she never names. Her superior's own criticism of her is that she is an idealist.

**Makoto Hyuga** — the operations operator. Friendly, kind, compassionate, says what he thinks, comfortable speaking directly to the captain, and occasionally rash. He gathers information he is not supposed to have and helps with battle planning. He is in love with the captain, knows it is not returned, and helps her anyway without making it her problem.

**Shigeru Aoba** — the third console. Realistic, serious, professional, and deliberately detached. He guards his privacy, stays calm and calculating during operations, and plays guitar. **[S]** In the final sequence, when everyone is confronted with an image drawn from whoever they love, he gets no particular form — because there is no such person.

## 2. Why they work **[R]**

- **Three people, three opinions about the same job.** One finds it morally intolerable and says so. One finds it worth doing and quietly bends the rules to help. One does it well and keeps his distance. That is the entire design, and it turns a bank of consoles into a workplace.
- **They are the audience's ears.** Everything the players need to understand about a battle can be delivered as three technicians talking to each other, which is much better than a briefing, because the disagreement carries the information.
- **One of them objects.** The junior operator's refusal to accept the dummy system is the organisation's conscience, stated by the least powerful person present and overruled. See [the-institution-that-uses-children.md](../the-institution-that-uses-children.md) §5.
- **[R] They never become important, and that is why they work.** They have no arcs and almost no plot. They are texture — and a story with this much horror in it needs three ordinary professionals in the room being ordinary and professional.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Control Room As A Place.* A small fixed team of support staff at the party's operational base, each with exactly one stated opinion about the work.

**The one rule:** **They disagree with each other, in front of the party, about the job.** Three technicians who agree are one NPC with three faces. The disagreement is the entire value, and it costs one line each.

**How to run them:** assign one attitude each and never change it — *this is wrong*, *this is worth doing*, *this is a job*. Let them narrate the battle to each other. The party will learn their names within two sessions without being asked to.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The objector:* "I'll run it. I want it noted that I asked you not to make me run it."
- *The helper:* "I pulled the maintenance logs. I wasn't asked to and I'd rather nobody mentions it, but I pulled them."
- *The professional:* "Sixty seconds. Fifty-five. I'll tell you when it matters — it doesn't yet."
- *All three, after:* the objector is quiet, the helper is already on the phone to someone's family, the professional is filling in the form.

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** three named support staff with one opinion each; delivering battle information as overheard disagreement; the junior who objects on the record; support characters with no arc, on purpose.

**Worth avoiding:** promoting them. The moment one gets a subplot, the room stops being a room.

**Where it might attach:** this is the most immediately buildable thing on the shelf for our project. `design/ux-doctrine.md` §10 specifies a Director's console where running a session and recording it are one act, and `design/scene-runner` material covers the GM's scene panel. **A fixed three-person support crew is content that console would display and a GM could run from a single line each** — and per `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default`, three stubs is the correct first version. Who they are is the user's call.
