# Kensuke Aida (相田ケンスケ)

*Class 2-A · the one who desperately wants the job the protagonist hates. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Classmate. Military enthusiast. Never a pilot |
| **Naming** | From Ryu Murakami's *Ai to gensō no fascism* |
| **Family** | Motherless, like most of the cast; his father may work in the organisation's administration |
| **Fate** | Relocates with the school when the city is evacuated |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- An obsessive military enthusiast who films everything, plays survival games alone in the countryside, and argues well.
- He is desperate to pilot. He asks. He is never selected.
- He is one of only two people the protagonist's own age who treat the protagonist as a person rather than as an asset.
- He is evacuated with everyone else and the story moves on without him.

## 2. Why he works **[R]**

- **He is the audience's envy, standing next to the audience's reality.** The protagonist has the thing every viewer would want and does not want it; Kensuke wants it desperately and is refused. Putting those two boys in the same conversation is the cheapest way to say something true about the job without a single line of exposition.
- **His enthusiasm measures the strangeness.** He is thrilled by things the pilots find routine. An NPC who is excited by the party's ordinary Tuesday tells the players how unusual their lives have become, which they stop noticing about two sessions in.
- **He never gets it, and that is the point.** The series does not reward the wanting. **[R] This is a small piece of honesty that most stories skip: some people want the extraordinary life, are perfectly suited to it, and are simply not chosen.**
- **[R] He is also slightly uncomfortable to watch, deliberately.** His enthusiasm for military hardware sits next to real casualties, and the series lets that sit without comment.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The One Who Wants The Job.* A friend with no special gift, who would take the party's burden in a heartbeat and is never going to be offered it.

**The one rule:** **He must ask, on screen, and be refused — and the refusal must not be cruel or explained away.** The scene where he is told no is the whole character. Anything that softens it (a secret talent, a late selection) throws away what he is for.

**Voice:** fast, enthusiastic, over-informed, full of specifications nobody asked for. Delighted by details. Completely unembarrassed. Occasionally, and briefly, says something that shows he knows exactly where he stands.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *Enthusiasm:* "Do you have any idea what that is? That's the third one they've ever built. You get to *sit* in it."
- *The ask:* "I'd be good at it. I've done the reading — I've done more of the reading than you have. Just ask them. You don't even have to recommend me, just ask whether they'd look."
- *The refusal, afterwards:* "No, it's fine. It's fine. I'd have said no to me as well."
- *Later, quietly:* "You know you're allowed to hate it, right? I'd still swap. But you're allowed."

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the friend whose excitement recalibrates the party's sense of their own strangeness; the honest refusal; a peer NPC who is neither a rival nor a victim, just someone standing next to the story.

**Worth avoiding:** making him a joke. He is funny, and he is not the butt of the series; the one time he is truly earnest it lands because the comedy was affectionate.

**Where it might attach:** our campaign has six kid PCs who pilot or support Frames, in a school full of children who do not. **The classmate who wants in is free content and costs one stub**, and it pairs with the finding in [school-life-and-the-ordinary-day.md](../school-life-and-the-ordinary-day.md) that the ordinary layer is where the party's life gets measured. Who exists in that classroom is a story-bible decision.
