# Asuka Langley Soryu (惣流・アスカ・ラングレー)

*Pilot, Evangelion Unit-02 · competence as armour, and what happens when it stops working. ## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Brave, loud, positive, enormously skilled, and **actually the best pilot of the three** when she arrives. The confidence is not a bluff; the achievement is real.
- Underneath it, a pronounced inferiority complex. Her psychological profile is described in terms of masculine protest, reaction formation, repression and narcissism.
- **Her mother lost her mind in an experiment with the machine Asuka now pilots**, could no longer recognise her daughter, and spoke instead to a cloth doll she believed was Asuka. Shortly after Asuka was selected as a pilot, she found her mother hanging.
- She vowed as a child never to cry.
- Her worth is measured publicly, by a number, in front of everyone. **When her mind goes, the number goes down where the whole control room can see it**, and it keeps going down.
- An Angel attacks her mind from orbit, forcing her to relive all of it. She does not recover.
- The one person who reaches her at the end is a classmate with no powers and no role in any of it, who simply takes her in.

## 2. Why she works **[R]**

- **The armour is made of something real.** She is not pretending to be good; she is good, and she has decided that being good is the only reason anyone would keep her. That is a much sturdier construction than insecurity alone, and it is why her collapse lands.
- **Her decline is measurable and public.** The synchronisation ratio is the single best mechanical idea attached to any character in the series: a private psychological state rendered as a number that colleagues read aloud. **[R] It means her worst moments are visible to everyone and deniable by her, which is exactly how that kind of pride actually fails.**
- **She is rescued, partly, by the least important person in the story.** Not by a pilot, a commander or a scientist — by a class representative who kept approaching after being rebuffed. See [relationships-that-degrade.md](../relationships-that-degrade.md) §2.
- **[R] The caution.** The series takes her apart with unusual thoroughness and does very little to put her back together, and several of the worst moments are also its most criticised. The archetype is worth having; the trajectory is not a template.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *Competence As Armour.* The best in the room, loudly, because being the best is the only reason she can imagine being kept.

**The one rule:** **Her competence must be real, and it must fail at something specific and public.** Not a general decline — one visible failure at the thing she is known for, in front of people whose opinion she needs. And there must be a route back that does not require her to stop being proud.

**Voice:** fast, blunt, funny, mocking, switches language when angry. Announces her results before anyone asks. Rejects help in the same sentence as she requires it. The insults are precise, which is the tell: she has been paying attention.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To a peer:* "I already did the thing you're about to suggest. Twice. Better. You can still watch, if you want to learn something."
- *To an adult:* "Don't congratulate me for the easy one. Congratulate me when it's hard, or don't bother doing it at all."
- *When it slips:* "That's not my number. Run it again. — Run it *again*."
- *To the one who keeps trying:* "Why are you still here. I've been horrible to you all week. — ...Fine. Sit down, then."

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the peer rival of the PCs' own age — **which the Giant Robo shelf could not supply at all**, and which is the single most important gap this sweep fills; the public performance number as a psychological instrument; the rescue delivered by an ordinary person with no special standing.

**Worth avoiding:** the full trajectory. A rival who is dismantled and left dismantled is not compatible with our tone rules. Build the pride, build the specific public failure, and **build the repair**.

**Where it might attach:** a peer rival is a shape our campaign has an obvious slot for — six kid PCs in a school, with `ideas/entries/school-schedule-system.json` and the day ring already modelling school time. **Who occupies it is a story-bible and casting decision for the user**, and `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default` says the first version is a stub.
