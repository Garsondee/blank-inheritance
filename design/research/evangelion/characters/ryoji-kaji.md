# Ryoji Kaji (加持リョウジ)

*NERV Special Investigations · the triple agent who tells one person the truth and dies for it. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Special Investigations officer. Simultaneously working for the Japanese government, the secret committee, his own employer, and himself |
| **History** | Met Misato and Ritsuko at university; lived with Misato until the relationship ended two years later; transferred to Germany as the Second Child's guardian |
| **Design** | Based on Captain Foster from the British series *UFO* |
| **Fate** | Betrayed by an informant and assassinated by a third party. He leaves his findings to Misato |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- He arrives escorting a pilot from abroad, and carrying a smuggled embryo for the commander in the same luggage.
- **He is a triple agent and everyone senior knows it.** The commander is aware and tolerates him because the arrangement is useful. He spends the series prying into his own organisation.
- He sabotages the rival unmanned mech programme.
- He is charming, evasive, and treats nearly everything as a joke until, twice, he does not.
- **He finds out what the organisation is actually for.** He is then betrayed by an informant and killed by someone unidentified — the production line is that SEELE or a security service did it, not, as fan theory once held, his ex-partner.
- **[S]** Before he dies he passes everything he has learned to one person, and that inheritance is what lets her act later.

## 2. Why he works **[R]**

- **He is the series' proof that the truth costs something.** The information exists, a competent professional goes and gets it, and the getting kills him. **[R] That single arrangement does more to make a conspiracy feel real than any amount of withholding**, and it is the technique [withheld-information-and-the-ending-problem.md](../withheld-information-and-the-ending-problem.md) §1 recommends over the series' own default.
- **Multiple loyalties, all sincere.** He is not a traitor to any of his masters; he is genuinely serving four agendas that overlap, and his own is the one nobody can name. That is much more interesting than a double-cross, and it survives contact with players, who will try to work out whose side he is on and should never quite manage it.
- **He hands the torch to a person, not a document.** The plot survives his death because a relationship does.
- **[R] He is also the series' most functional adult.** He listens to the children, answers their questions more honestly than anyone else does, and treats them as people. That is not incidental — the character most willing to tell the truth is the one who gets killed for it.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Investigator Who Pays.* A charming, unreliable, genuinely competent intelligence officer inside the party's own organisation, who is working for several people and will eventually die for finding something out.

**The one rule:** **He gives the party one true thing, and the cost lands on him, visibly.** Not a warning, not a hint — a real piece of the answer, and then a consequence the party can see and could not prevent.

**Voice:** light, teasing, deflective. Answers serious questions with jokes and joke questions seriously. Uses first names too early. Drops the act once per appearance, for one sentence, and then picks it straight back up.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *Deflecting:* "I'm an errand boy with an expensive coat. Ask me something I'm allowed to be wrong about."
- *To a child:* "You want the real answer or the one that lets you sleep? — All right. Sit down. The real one takes longer."
- *On his loyalties:* "Four people pay me, and not one of them has asked whether I'd want the world they're building. Interesting, isn't it, what nobody asks."
- *The handover:* "If I don't come back, this is yours. Don't open it here, and don't open it alone."

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the investigator whose death is the price of a fact; multiple sincere loyalties instead of a double-cross; the adult who answers the children honestly; a legacy handed to a person rather than left in a file.

**Worth avoiding:** the ambiguity outlasting him. His actual employer is never fully resolved, which is fine in a series and less fine in a campaign, where the party will keep pulling that thread. **Decide who he really worked for before he appears.**

**Where it might attach:** our campaign's institution has clearance rungs and buried records already in design, and the Giant Robo shelf supplies an [aide with a real complaint](../../giant-robo/characters/ivan.md) as a related but different shape. An internal investigator is the natural bridge between the party and a conspiracy they cannot access directly — **a stub with a name, a cover role and one thing he is looking for is the correct first version** (`feedback_npc_stubs_by_default`).
