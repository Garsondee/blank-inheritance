# Misato Katsuragi (葛城ミサト)

*NERV, Captain of Operations · commanding officer and guardian at once — the role conflict that defines the series. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Captain of the Operations Department. Plans and commands every battle. **Also the protagonist's legal guardian and housemate** |
| **Naming** | From the Imperial Japanese Navy's naval register, per the series' convention |
| **Design** | Conceived as an older "girl next door"; Sadamoto's blunt working description was a promiscuous loser who failed to take life seriously. An expy of Usagi Tsukino, down to sharing her voice actor |
| **Fate** | Dies covering the protagonist's escape during the assault on headquarters |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- **Two completely different people depending on the room.** At work she is decisive, technically excellent, and the author of the series' best operations. At home she is chaotic, slovenly, drinks heavily, and is very funny.
- She survived the disaster as a child; her father died in it. The compensating personality was built on that.
- **She is the protagonist's commanding officer and the adult he lives with**, and the series states the conflict plainly: she cannot be a surrogate mother while also being the officer who sends him out to die, and he cannot fully trust her as the first while she remains the second.
- Anno's assessment pairs her with Shinji: both are **afraid of being hurt**, and both lack the positive attitude people expect of an adventure's heroes.
- Her voice actor described her as a lonely, insecure woman hiding darkness and loneliness.
- She plans **Operation Yashima** — the single-shot national-power-grid sniper operation — and she is the one who climbs onto a runaway nuclear mech to key in a shutdown code by hand after the authorisation chain tells her the clearance will arrive with the paperwork.
- An ex-lover, an intelligence officer, is assassinated after passing her what he found out. She carries the findings afterwards.
- By the end she has become something close to a mother to the second pilot.

## 2. Why she works **[R]**

- **The role conflict is the best institutional idea on this shelf**, and it needs no setting to reproduce: one NPC holding two roles that genuinely conflict, so that every scene has a live question — which one is she being right now? See [the-institution-that-uses-children.md](../the-institution-that-uses-children.md) §2.
- **The home/work split is the source of the series' warmth**, and it is the part of her most compatible with our register. The beer, the appalling flat, the penguin, the terrible cooking, the teasing. **[R] The domestic comedy is not a break from her characterisation — it is the evidence that she is trying to give a fourteen-year-old something resembling a home.**
- **She is good at her job, visibly.** The plans work. That matters: an authority figure the players can respect professionally can be forgiven a great deal personally, and the reverse is not true.
- **Her defiance is administrative.** Her most heroic moment is deciding to be in trouble later. That is a kind of courage a campaign can use every session.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Officer Who Is Also The Guardian.* The adult who gives the orders and also makes the dinner.

**The one rule:** **Write the scene where the two roles give opposite instructions, before the campaign needs it.** She has to choose in front of the party, and whichever she picks has to cost her the other.

**Voice:** two registers, switched deliberately. On duty: clipped, precise, technical, no wasted words. Off duty: loud, teasing, over-familiar, drinks at breakfast. The moment to play is the one where the wrong register slips out in the wrong room.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *On duty:* "Forty seconds of power, and I want thirty of it left when you reach the line. Do not be brave in the corridor."
- *Off duty:* "There's food in the fridge. I say food. There's a container in the fridge."
- *The conflict:* "As your commanding officer I'm sending you. As the person who has to sit in that kitchen afterwards, I want you to know I hate it. Both of those are true and neither one gets you out of the sortie."
- *To the chain of command:* "Then I'll do it without the clearance and you can put that in the report too."

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the dual role and its unresolvable conflict; the domestic scenes as evidence of effort rather than as comic filler; the officer whose defiance is bureaucratic; a commander who plans a genuinely clever operation on screen so the players can respect her.

**Worth avoiding:** the elements the guardrail quarantines — her private despair is written as inevitable, and our register wants an adult who is struggling *and* succeeding at parts of it.

**Where it might attach:** the Directorate's three poles are unnamed placeholders with open questions attached (`data/npcs/`), and our PCs are children in an institution. An officer-guardian is an obvious shape for that gap — **and it is the user's call.** What this file contributes is the instruction in §3: if such an NPC exists, the opposite-orders scene must be written in advance.
