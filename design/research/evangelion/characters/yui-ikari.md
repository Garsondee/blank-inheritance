# Yui Ikari (碇ユイ)

*Deceased, 2004 · the mother inside the machine. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- A Kyoto University student in 1999, discussing artificial-evolution research. She married the man her professor was suspicious of, and went to work on the project.
- **In 2004 she volunteered as the experimental subject for Unit-01's activation.** She was absorbed. Her body disappeared.
- **The official record calls it an accident. It was not.** The final episodes establish that she chose it, and that her soul remained inside the unit.
- From there, she occasionally moves the machine herself. The unit's habit of exceeding its orders, and of protecting her son without being asked, is her.
- At the end, she is what gives him the resolve to choose a future for humanity rather than the alternative.
- Her remains were used, separately and without her knowledge, to create the First Child.

## 2. Why she works **[R]**

- **The machine acts on her behalf long before anyone knows she is in it.** The audience watches a robot behave strangely for twenty episodes and is then given the reason. **[R] That is the correct order: the anomaly first, the explanation later** — and it means every earlier scene gains a second meaning on a rewatch without a single frame changing.
- **Her choice is the series' one act of pure agency by an adult.** Every other adult is reacting to a loss. She decided something, executed it, and left everyone else to live inside the consequence — which is exactly what the commander has spent fifteen years trying to reverse.
- **She is the reason the whole system works.** The stated rule about who can pilot is a cover story; the real reason is that each unit contains a pilot's mother. Putting the mechanism's explanation inside a dead woman's decision makes a technical fact into a family one.
- **[R] The casting link does the heaviest lifting and costs nothing.** The mother and the clone share a voice. Nobody has to say anything.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Mother In The Machine.* A dead parent whose deliberate choice is the reason the party's equipment works, and who is still, quietly, present in it.

**The one rule:** **The machine must act on its own first, several times, before anybody explains why.** The explanation is worthless without the accumulated anomalies, and the anomalies are free.

**Presence:** she has almost no dialogue and does not need any. She is a recording, a photograph the commander destroyed, and a machine that moves when it should not.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The anomaly:* the machine is told to stop and does not, and what it does instead is more careful than what it was told.
- *The record:* an old personnel file with a date, a photograph missing, and the word "accident" in a form somebody else filled in.
- *The confirmation:* somebody who was there says one sentence about what she was like, and it matches the way the machine behaves.

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the anomaly-before-explanation ordering; a dead parent's deliberate choice as the foundation of the campaign's central technology; an official record that says "accident" about something chosen; a casting or descriptive link that makes a connection without stating it.

**Worth avoiding:** revealing it as a twist. It is not a twist; it is an explanation the audience has been owed for twenty episodes, and it works because it was owed.

**Where it might attach:** our Frames are PCs with their own sheets rather than haunted equipment, so this does not transfer directly — but `data/hangar/legacy-units.json` is empty, and a legacy unit with a history and a former pilot is precisely where a shape like this would live. **What goes in that file is the user's call**, and the Giant Robo shelf's [father who left a question](../../giant-robo/characters/doctor-kusama.md) is the warmer sibling of this same idea. Both shelves independently arrive at a machine carrying a dead parent's intention, which is worth noticing before either is used.
