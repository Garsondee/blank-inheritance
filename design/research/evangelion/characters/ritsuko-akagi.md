# Ritsuko Akagi (赤木リツコ)

*NERV, head of Technology Section One · the scientist who built it, knows what it is, and cannot say. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Chief scientist; lead developer of the Evangelions |
| **History** | Met Misato at university in 2005; joined the predecessor organisation in 2008 as head of Project E. **Dyed her hair blonde to look less like her mother** |
| **Naming** | From the naval register, per the series' convention |
| **Fate** | Tries to destroy headquarters; her mother's personality, inside the computer, refuses the order; Gendo shoots her |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Rational, disciplined, outwardly detached, and internally anything but — the series is explicit that she has decided to hide what she feels rather than that she feels little.
- **She built the machines and knows what they actually are.** She knows about the clones. She knows what is in the basement. She explains to the pilots only what they need, and the explanations she gives are true and incomplete.
- **She is in a secret relationship with the commander, and she is repeating her mother's.** Her mother had the same relationship with the same man, and Ritsuko eventually works out that she was chosen for the resemblance.
- She is jealous of the First Child, who receives the attention she does not.
- **Her friendship with Misato fractures** — not over a betrayal, but because their jobs require each of them to keep things from the other, and then over the resentment that produces.
- **[S] Her end is the best-constructed beat involving any adult in the series.** She orders the base's computers to self-destruct. The three computers contain the transcribed personality of her mother — as a scientist, as a woman, and as a mother. **The one carrying the woman refuses.** She is left standing in front of a machine that will not let her die, and the commander shoots her.

## 2. Why she works **[R]**

- **She is the party's most useful ally and the reason they cannot find anything out.** Everything she says is true. Every briefing is accurate. The omissions are the whole problem, and they are professional rather than malicious — which makes her much harder to be angry with than a liar.
- **Her tragedy is inherited and precise.** Not "she made bad choices", but: she became her mother, in the same building, with the same man, and the proof of it is a computer that overrules her at the last moment. **[R] Putting the dead parent inside the equipment, and then having the equipment disobey the daughter, is the tightest piece of writing in the series.**
- **The friendship with Misato fails for institutional reasons.** See [relationships-that-degrade.md](../relationships-that-degrade.md) §3. Two competent people who like each other, prevented from being honest by their own clearance levels, is a far more usable engine than a falling-out.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Engineer Who Knows What It Is.* The organisation's chief technical authority, indispensable to the party, permanently unable to tell them the thing that matters.

**The one rule:** **Everything she tells them is true.** She never lies; she answers the question asked and not the one implied. When the party later learns what she left out, the betrayal is real and she has not broken a single word — which is much worse, and much better.

**Voice:** precise, dry, faintly tired. Explains technical things clearly and well, because she likes explaining. Deflects personal questions with a change of subject so smooth it takes a moment to notice. Smokes, or an equivalent.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *Briefing:* "That is an accurate summary of what you need. Yes. I noticed the word 'need' as well."
- *Deflecting:* "You're asking me a question I can answer or a question I can't, and I'd rather you decided which before I start talking."
- *The crack:* "I have read my mother's notes. She was right about a great deal. I would rather she had been right about less of it."
- *At the end:* "Of course it won't. Of course it won't. She never did like being told what to do by me either."

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the indispensable expert whose omissions are professional; the inherited pattern, proved by an object; a friendship that fails because of clearance rather than character; truthful-but-incomplete answers as a standing method for a campaign with buried history.

**Worth avoiding:** the relationship with her commander as the explanation for her competence. The series entangles the two; her expertise stands on its own and should.

**Where it might attach:** this campaign has a founding disaster, a redaction system in design, and an institution with clearance levels (`design/harvest-inference.md`, `ideas/entries/redacted-document-chain.json`, `design/strategy-layer.md`'s Clearance rungs). **An expert NPC whose answers are gated by a rung the party can see is a way to make a clearance system dramatic rather than administrative.** Whether one exists is the user's call; `design/npc-social-intel.md`'s Facts-with-reveal-triggers is the existing machinery for it.
