# Toji Suzuhara (鈴原トウジ)

*Class 2-A; later the Fourth Child · the friend who starts as an enemy for a legitimate reason. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Classmate; recruited mid-series as the Fourth Child, pilot of Unit-03 |
| **Naming** | From Ryu Murakami's novel *Ai to gensō no fascism*, like several of the classmates |
| **Fate** | His unit is infected by an Angel on activation and destroyed by an autonomous system with him inside. He loses his left leg and survives. **In the manga he is killed instead** |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Projects toughness, and is honest, kind, humble and fairly introverted underneath it.
- **His younger sister was badly hurt in the protagonist's first battle.** He knows it, and he punches the protagonist for it in the school corridor — twice, with cause.
- He works through the guilt himself, and becomes genuinely concerned for the boy he hit.
- **He agrees to pilot on one condition: that his sister be transferred to a better hospital.** The organisation accepts the deal.
- His first activation is his last. The unit is infected before he has done anything, and the command staff order an autonomous system to destroy it. He is inside.
- **[S]** The series is explicit that his humane, direct manner has a positive effect on the protagonist and the other pilots. He is one of the very few relationships in the series that improves and stays improved.
- **[S]** In the *Rebuild* films he does not pilot at all; he marries the class representative and has a child.

## 2. Why he works **[R]**

- **His grievance is correct.** That is the whole engine. He is not mistaken, not manipulated, and not being unreasonable — the protagonist's actions did injure his sister. An antagonist who is right is worth ten who are wrong, and it means the reconciliation has to be earned rather than explained.
- **He reconciles by understanding the job**, not by being told he was wrong. Nobody argues him out of it. He finds out what piloting actually costs, and adjusts.
- **He is the one whose consent was bought.** See [the-institution-that-uses-children.md](../the-institution-that-uses-children.md) §1. The organisation did not coerce him; it offered him something he could not otherwise get, for his sister, and honoured it. That is more unsettling than a threat.
- **[R] The cruelty of his ending is deliberate and instructive.** He does nothing wrong, gets one scene in the machine, and is destroyed by his own side's decision. The series wanted a cost that fell on the most decent person available. **At our table that exact beat is available but expensive** — it is the sort of thing to agree in advance rather than spring.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Friend Who Started As An Enemy, Correctly.* Somebody the party hurt, through no malice, who confronts them about it and later becomes their closest ally.

**The one rule:** **His grievance must be true and must never be retracted.** He does not come round because he was wrong; he comes round because he learns what the party's job costs them. The original injury stays on the books.

**Voice:** blunt, regional, unpretentious, funny. Insults his friends as a form of affection. Speaks in plain short sentences and is much more articulate than he pretends. Gets quiet and precise when it matters.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The confrontation:* "I'm not asking whether you meant it. I know you didn't mean it. I'm telling you what it did, and then you can have the second one."
- *Later:* "I've seen the inside of that thing now. You can consider us square, and don't make it a whole speech."
- *The deal:* "You're offering me the hospital. Fine. Put it in writing, and I'll get in your machine."
- *To a friend:* "Sit down and eat something. You look like a man who's been thinking."

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the ally introduced as an aggrieved party with a real case; reconciliation through understanding rather than apology; consent purchased with something the institution alone can provide; a friendship that improves and holds, which our tone rules need more of than the source provides.

**Worth avoiding:** the ending, unless the group has agreed to that kind of content in advance (`design/ttrpg-campaign-and-session-craft.md` on safety tools).

**Where it might attach:** any Frame-scale fight in a populated city makes collateral harm to civilians and classmates structurally likely. **An NPC whose family was hurt by the party's own operation is the natural consequence** — a stub with a name, a relative and a grievance is the right first version.
