# Rei Ayanami (綾波レイ)

*Pilot, Evangelion Unit-00 · the person who does not know she is allowed to want things. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | The First Child; pilot of Unit-00 |
| **What she is** | A clone, created from the salvaged remains of Yui Ikari, the protagonist's mother |
| **Naming** | *Rei* (零, "zero, nothingness"), deliberately set against *Yui* (唯, "only one") |
| **Design** | Conceived as a representation of Anno's unconscious; an early plan had her as a schizophrenic character; partly drawn from Paul Gallico's *The Snow Goose* |
| **Fate** | Destroys herself and her unit to stop an Angel, and is replaced by the next copy |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- Taciturn, mechanically obedient, minimises contact with everyone except the commander, to whom she is close.
- **She knows she is replaceable.** There are more of her in a tank, and if she dies another is activated. Her poor sense of self-worth is stated to come directly from that knowledge.
- She initially dislikes the protagonist for not trusting his father. After a battle they survive together, she takes a friendly liking to him — and the moment the series is remembered for is small: she smiles.
- She was strangled as a child by a scientist who mistook her for the woman she was cloned from, and who then killed herself.
- She ends by self-destructing her own unit to stop an enemy that is trying to merge with her.
- **[S]** She became so popular that the press called her "Premium Girl", and her design and manner were copied across the industry for a decade.

## 2. Why she works **[R]**

- **The horror is administrative, not gothic.** Nobody torments her. She is filed, maintained, and replaced, and the institution considers this reasonable. That is far more disturbing than cruelty and much easier to run at a table.
- **The change is tiny and enormous.** Her entire arc is measured in one expression and a handful of sentences. Because the baseline is so flat, a single degree of warmth reads as a transformation. **[R] A character with almost no affect is the cheapest possible emotional payoff, provided the writer has the discipline not to spend it early.**
- **She is the reveal.** Understanding what she is answers most of the series' questions about the commander, the machines and the project. Putting the central secret inside a person rather than a document means the plot's answer is also someone's life.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The One Who Was Made For This.* A quiet, obedient, apparently affectless person who belongs to the institution more completely than anyone realises, including her.

**The one rule:** **Somebody must notice her, early, over something small — and it must be granted.** A shared task, a remembered preference, a question about what she likes. The archetype is not about the reveal; it is about the first time an ordinary kindness lands on someone who has no procedure for receiving one.

**Voice:** short declarative sentences. Answers exactly what is asked. No filler, no softeners, no jokes. Does not initiate. When she does initiate, that is the scene.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *To a peer:* "You are asking whether I mind. I do not know how to check that. I will tell you if I find out."
- *To the institution:* "Yes. I understand. It is not important."
- *The turn:* "You said my name before you said the order. Nobody does that. — No. It is not a complaint."
- *At the end:* "I am not afraid. I was not made for very much, and this is one of the things."

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the institutional horror of a person treated as a maintained asset; the flat baseline that makes one degree of warmth enormous; putting the campaign's central secret inside a person the party likes.

**Worth avoiding:** the replaceability played for shock. If the party discovers there are more of her, that is a *fact about the institution* and it should cost the institution something with them. It is not a twist to be sprung and left.

**Where it might attach:** this campaign already runs on an intake process (`design/induction.md`) and an institution with hidden records (`ideas/entries/redacted-document-chain.json`). An NPC who is on the roster in a way nobody has looked at closely fits both. **Whether such a person exists is a story-bible decision** — and `feedback_npc_stubs_by_default` says the first version is a stub with a role and one want.
