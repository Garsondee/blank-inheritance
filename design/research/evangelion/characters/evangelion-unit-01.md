# Evangelion Unit-01 (エヴァンゲリオン初号機)

*The machine that is not a machine, and occasionally acts alone. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | Not a robot. A living thing, restrained by its own armour, with a human soul inside it |
| **Whose** | Yui Ikari's — the pilot's mother, absorbed during its activation in 2004 |
| **Cover story** | The organisation calls it a robot. That is a deliberate public lie, and a rival unmanned mech exists partly so nobody can compare them |
| **Power** | An external cable. One minute at full output, five reduced — **and the limit turns out to be about the pilot, not the machine** |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- The pilot sits inside it, in fluid, connected mentally rather than mechanically; the quality of that connection is read aloud as a percentage.
- **The armour is restraint.** The plating exists to hold the thing in, not to protect it.
- **It disobeys.** It moves when it should not, protects its pilot without being asked, and interprets rather than executes.
- **The berserk state.** When control is exceeded it acts on its own, ignores the power cable entirely, and in its most famous sequence tears an enemy apart and eats it, absorbing something permanent in the process. The pilot is inside and decides none of it.
- Late on, it absorbs the pilot himself.
- **[S]** The stated operational rule that only children born after the disaster can pilot is a cover story. The real rule is that each unit contains the pilot's mother.

## 2. Why it works **[R]**

- **The reveal is a rule, not a fact.** The audience uses the age rule for twenty episodes before learning it was a fiction covering something else. **[R] Revealing that a rule the audience has been reasoning with is false is far stronger than revealing new information** — see [what-makes-it-tick.md](../what-makes-it-tick.md) §4.
- **The constraint turns out to be a leash.** Everyone accepts the five-minute limit as a technical fact. It is a control mechanism, and the berserk state demonstrates that by ignoring it. That single inversion reframes every earlier fight.
- **It is frightening on its own side.** The best mecha sequence in the series is horrifying rather than triumphant, because the thing winning is not taking instructions.
- **[R] The convergence worth noting.** The Giant Robo shelf's [inherited weapon](../../giant-robo/characters/giant-robo.md) is a slow, obedient machine carrying a dead father's will and following the gist of an order. This one is a restrained animal carrying a dead mother's soul and ignoring orders entirely. **Two unrelated series, opposite temperaments, identical conclusion: a mecha becomes a character the moment it does slightly more than it was told, for a reason the audience can attribute to a person.**

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Machine That Is Not A Machine.* Equipment the institution describes in technical language that is not true, which occasionally acts in its user's interest without being asked.

**The one rule:** **It acts on its own before anyone explains why, at least twice.** The explanation is worth nothing without the accumulated anomalies, and the anomalies cost nothing to stage.

**Presence at the table:** describe it as an animal when it is winning and as equipment when it is being maintained. The gap between those two registers is the characterisation, and it requires no dialogue at all.

**Audition beats** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *The anomaly:* it is ordered to hold position, and it does — except that it has moved one arm, and nobody can say when.
- *The cover story:* a technician uses an engineering word for something the party has just watched behave like a living thing, and does not blink.
- *The berserk:* every instrument reads wrong at once, the cable is lying on the ground, and it is still going.

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the stated technical rule that is a cover story; the constraint that turns out to be a control measure; the machine that is described in two registers; a victory that frightens the people who won it.

**Worth avoiding:** **the loss-of-control mechanics, imported wholesale.** Our campaign already has a comparable system, and [the-machine-and-the-pilot.md](../the-machine-and-the-pilot.md) §5 is the standing warning: `design/resonance-and-bleed.md` and the open questions in `data/campaign/open-questions.json` govern that ground, and this shelf does not touch them.

**Where it might attach:** our Frames are PCs with their own character sheets, not NPC equipment, so the archetype does not transfer to them directly. **Where it could live is the legacy hangar** (`data/hangar/legacy-units.json`, currently empty) — a machine from the previous programme that the institution describes in language the party will eventually discover is false. Whether anything goes in that file is the user's call.
