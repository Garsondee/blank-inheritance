# Mari Illustrious Makinami (真希波・マリ・イラストリアス)

*Rebuild continuity only · the late addition who enjoys the job — and the risk of adding one. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — this character does not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Continuity** | **Not in the television series.** Introduced in *Evangelion: 2.0*, the second of the four *Rebuild* films |
| **Role** | A pilot from the European branch, originally from England |
| **Origin** | **Requested by the producer to attract new audiences.** Her concept went through numerous revisions and the writers were undecided about her until the deadline |
| **Development** | Entrusted to Kazuya Tsurumaki specifically so that she would not replicate the existing characters |
| **Design** | Deliberately distinct from the other pilots; inspirations cited include *Space Battleship Yamato*, the series *UFO*, and a Yui Aragaki role |

## 1. What she is for **[S/R]**

**[S]** She was added to a finished ensemble, from outside, on a commercial brief, and the production openly says so.

**[R]** What she contributes is one thing the original cast structurally cannot: **she likes piloting.** In a story whose protagonist is defined by dreading the job, a competent pilot who finds it exhilarating changes the temperature of every scene she is in without any argument being made.

**[S]** Her reception is genuinely mixed and has stayed mixed. She has been criticised as excessively sexualised with an insignificant role, and praised as a good alternative to the existing cast. The final film deepened her and the evaluations remained split — some finding her underdeveloped, others appreciating the psychology and the battle sequences.

## 2. The lesson, which is about production rather than character **[R]**

This entry exists mainly as a caution, and it generalises well beyond anime.

- **A character added to satisfy an external brief arrives without a hole to fill.** Everyone else in the ensemble is load-bearing; she has to earn a slot that was not left for her, and the audience can feel the difference.
- **Concept churn shows.** Revised repeatedly, undecided until the deadline, and deliberately designed not to resemble anybody — that is a description of a character defined by negatives.
- **She still works, partly, and for one reason: she brings an attitude nobody else has.** **[R] That is the whole test for a late addition — not "is there room", but "does this person make the existing cast behave differently?"** Enjoying the job does exactly that, because it forces everyone else's dread to become visible by contrast.

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The One Who Enjoys It.* A competent peer who finds the party's grim, dangerous work genuinely fun, and says so.

**The one rule:** **Her enjoyment must be sincere and must not be hiding anything.** The moment it turns out to be a mask for trauma, she becomes another instance of the cast she was added to vary, and the contribution is gone.

**Voice:** cheerful, irreverent, over-familiar, nicknames everybody immediately. Treats danger as interesting. Talks during operations. Is not troubled by things that trouble the others, and does not moralise about it either.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *On the job:* "Oh, that was *good*. Can we do that again — genuinely, can we, or is there a form?"
- *To a reluctant peer:* "You hate this. I love this. We can both be right and still get it done, you know."
- *Under fire:* "Right, that's new. That's a new one. Hello."
- *When asked why:* "Because I'm good at it and it's enormous and it goes fast. I've thought about it more than that and the answer didn't improve."

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the peer who enjoys the work, as a contrast engine; and the production lesson — **any character added late must change how the existing cast behaves, or they will read as an addition.**

**Worth avoiding:** the circumstances. A character introduced to broaden appeal, without a gap to fill, is the failure case this file documents. Also the treatment her reception criticises, which the guardrail already rules out for this project ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md) §3.6).

**Where it might attach:** our campaign has six kid PCs whose roster already exists in draft (`data/characters/`), and the `project_kids_frames_draft_roster` memory notes CLAUDE.md is stale on that point. **A character who enjoys the work is a shape a player might choose**, and player characters are not this shelf's business. What is transferable to a GM is the test in §2 for any NPC added mid-campaign.
