# Keel Lorenz and SEELE

*The Human Instrumentality Committee · the committee above your employer, seen only as monoliths. Part of the [Evangelion research shelf](../README.md). **Research, not lore — these characters do not exist in our setting, and nothing here decides anything. Mined for structure, not for register** ([tone-guardrail.md](../tone-guardrail.md)). Tags: **[S]** source, **[R]** reading, **[U]** suggested use.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Keel Lorenz** | President of the committee and head of SEELE. 67 years old, German, calm and rational, with **multiple cybernetic components grafted into him to sustain his life** |
| **Naming** | After the biologist Konrad Lorenz; he was "Konrad" in the original presentation document |
| **SEELE** | The secret organisation that funds and directs the party's employer, and is being deceived by it |
| **Their emblem** | **Seven eyes in an asymmetrical triangle** — the same symbol that appears on the mask of the being in the basement |

## 1. What the series gives us **[S]**

- SEELE funds, directs and ultimately owns the organisation the protagonists work for. Its committee manipulates world events toward a planned outcome.
- **They are almost never present.** They appear in meetings as identical black monoliths, numbered, labelled to indicate that only sound is being transmitted. A room of slabs with voices.
- They operate from prophecy — the Dead Sea Scrolls — and refer to events as proceeding "according to the scenario". **[S]** Their commander does the same, and the two scenarios are not the same scenario, which nobody says out loud until very late.
- When they realise the commander is betraying them, **they send an army division to exterminate his organisation.** Not a strike team: a military assault on their own subsidiary.
- **[R] Their logo was copied from something found in a basement**, with the implication running that way rather than the other. The institution's branding is a piece of evidence.

## 2. Why they work **[R]**

- **The monolith is the best design decision attached to any antagonist on this shelf.** A committee that appears only as numbered slabs solves a real production problem — how to stage a conspiracy without animating a room of old men — and produces something far better than the realistic version: faceless, identical, plural, and unreachable. **[U] At a table it costs one sentence to describe and can be reused every session.**
- **They are the party's employer's employer.** The most useful thing about them is not their plan; it is that **the party's boss is frightened of them.** An authority figure the players respect, visibly deferring to someone they have never met, establishes an entire tier above the campaign for free.
- **Two scenarios, one vocabulary.** Both sides say everything is going according to plan, and both mean different plans. The audience cannot tell which is which until the divergence arrives, and it is retroactively obvious.
- **[R] The escalation is instructive.** When the deception surfaces, the response is not a confrontation but an administrative decision to liquidate a subsidiary — carried out with an army. Institutional power expressed as institutional violence is more frightening than a villain, and it is the natural end point of [the-institution-that-uses-children.md](../the-institution-that-uses-children.md).

## 3. The archetype **[U]**

**Name:** *The Committee Above Your Employer.* A body that funds and directs the party's organisation, appears only through a medium, and can terminate everyone in the building with a decision.

**The one rule:** **They are never in the room, and the party's own authority figures are afraid of them.** The fear is the characterisation. If the party ever meets them in person, most of what they are for is gone.

**Voice:** plural, overlapping, unhurried, procedural. They ask for status and give approvals. They never threaten, because they have never needed to. They speak of the plan as a thing that is already finished and merely awaiting arrival.

**Audition lines** (written for this shelf, not quotations):

- *In session:* "The schedule is unchanged. Your report is noted. Continue."
- *On a deviation:* "That was not in the scenario. We will assume you have an explanation, and we will hear it at the next session."
- *On people:* "The pilots are an expense. You have been asked about the expense twice."
- *The decision:* "The subsidiary is no longer performing its function. Withdraw the funding, and then the rest of it."

## 4. For Blank Inheritance **[U]**

**Worth taking:** the presentation device — a committee that appears only as an identical repeated object; the tier established purely through the party's boss's deference; two parties using the same word for different plans; an emblem that is also evidence; termination as an administrative act.

**Worth avoiding:** explaining them. Their plan is the least interesting thing about them, and the series' attempt to explain it is where it comes apart ([withheld-information-and-the-ending-problem.md](../withheld-information-and-the-ending-problem.md)).

**Where it might attach:** the Directorate exists as a faction file with three unnamed poles, and `design/strategy-layer.md` already models an Observer's turn above the session. **Careful here:** the `feedback_watcher_is_unknown` rule states that our long-arc antagonist is unseen and unwitnessed at campaign start and must not appear in any location's lore. **An unseen funding committee and an unwitnessed antagonist are different devices and must not be merged.** Nothing on this shelf is proposed for the Watcher.
