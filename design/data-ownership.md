# Field Ownership: one authoritative writer, declared and enforced

*Written 2026-09-18, in response to Ingram's direct question after the Approvals Desk design: "You've
identified lots of systems pulling in different directions. How can we fix that? How can we burn it
into memory? How can we change this project so that this can't happen again? ... how can we make
sure that offline tools and the foundry module always stay in step with each other and don't fight
each other?"*

This is a standing policy, not a one-time cleanup. It does not resolve the ~43-storage-bin data
fragmentation crisis tracked separately (that consolidation is Ingram's own architectural call, same
as it has always been) — it is the mechanism that stops the crisis from growing a 44th bin, and stops
two tools from quietly fighting over one field the way the gazetteer and the Approvals Desk just did.

---

## 1. The pattern, seen twice in one afternoon

**First, as six review queues.** `design/approvals-desk.md` §6 found that "human reads drafted
content and grades it" had been independently built six separate times — `location.authoring.status`,
`data/audio/review.json`, the `asset-review-pipeline` idea, `lore-instructions.json`,
`gazetteer-requests.json`, `open-questions.json` — six vocabularies, none aware of the others, none
referencing a shared design.

**Second, as two authorities for the same seal.** While that document was being written, the same
failure showed up a level down: `tools/gazetteer/`'s Approve endpoint and the not-yet-built Foundry
Approvals Desk were both on track to claim the right to write `authoring.status: "approved"` on a
location. Ingram caught it before it shipped and resolved it directly: *"We make the Approvals Desk
in Foundry VTT the final word in what is considered approved. The gazetteer is a tool for drawing
shapes and drafting things."* `design/approvals-desk.md` §7 records the fix, already shipped in
`tools/gazetteer/server.py` and `test/gazetteer/test_server.py`.

**Both are the identical shape of bug, at different scales.** A capability (a field, a concept, a
status) exists in more than one system, no rule says which one is the real one, and the two drift —
or, same outcome, both start writing and now there are two answers to "what does this field say."
The general fix has to work at the scale of a whole schema, not just get applied by hand every time
someone happens to notice a collision, because noticing is exactly the step that failed once already
(the gazetteer's own 2026-09-13 comment records this having happened before: an unguarded write path
"got INTO the review-mode editor unguarded... exactly how it got in the first place").

---

## 2. The rule

> **Every field that more than one system could plausibly write has exactly one declared
> authoritative writer. Every other system either reads it or is structurally refused from writing
> it — never silently overwritten, never silently ignored.**

This is not "one authoritative writer per *file*" or "per *directory*" — CLAUDE.md's directory
structure section already does a version of that in prose (`tools/city-map/` "does not import"
`tools/map-generator/`, `data/audio/library.json` is "GENERATED by the pipeline," `review.json` is
"never written by a tool"). That prose convention has held up reasonably well precisely because it is
specific about direction. The gap is **fields inside a schema that multiple tools legitimately touch
for different reasons** — a location's geometry (drawn by the gazetteer), its lore (written by
Claude), its approval status (about to be owned by the Desk) all live in the same JSON file, and nothing
today declares that split machine-checkably. It is currently true only because every writer happens
to have been coded correctly by hand.

---

## 3. Extending the field taxonomy that already exists

`design/foundry-module-design.md` §4 already classifies every field in the Foundry sync design into
three categories, and this is the right taxonomy to extend rather than replace:

| Category | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **authored** | Repo always wins. A Foundry-side edit never survives a push. | A Claimant's stat block |
| **promotable** | Either side may write; a genuine conflict (both changed since the last common point) is detected and surfaced to a human, never auto-resolved | A scene's journal-page prose |
| **runtime** | Neither side ever reads the other's copy | A token's HP during combat |

**A fourth category is needed, and the Approvals Desk's `authoring.status` is its first real
instance:**

| Category | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **lifecycle** | One named system is the *only* legal writer of anything above the field's baseline value. Every other system may only ever write the baseline. Harvest is **mandatory and one-directional** — not the optional GM-judgment merge `promotable` gets, because there is nothing to adjudicate: only one side was ever allowed to write the advanced values, so a "conflict" cannot legitimately exist. | `authoring.status`: repo/generators may only ever emit `drafted`; the Foundry Approvals Desk is the sole writer of `sound`/`final`/`changes`/`cut`, harvested straight back |

The distinction from `promotable` matters and is worth stating plainly: `promotable` fields assume
**symmetric** risk — either side might have made the better edit, so a human adjudicates. `lifecycle`
fields are **asymmetric by design** — the whole point of the Approvals Desk is that the repo-side
process is structurally *not allowed* an opinion on its own promotion. Treating a lifecycle field as
promotable would reintroduce exactly the two-authorities bug, just moved into the conflict-resolution
step instead of out of it.

The mechanical carrier for `lifecycle` fields is the harvest pipeline that already exists and is
already tested (`tools/module-sync/sync-harvest.mjs`, `storage/harvest/`, pulled by WinSCP) — nothing
new needs building at the transport layer. What's new is the resolution rule at the far end: a
`lifecycle` field's harvested value always overwrites the repo's, because the repo was never a
legitimate second writer of it. That is a small, precise change to the merge step, not a new sync
system.

## 4. How ownership gets declared — in the schema, not beside it

A separate manifest file mapping "field X → owner Y" was considered and rejected. It would be bin
#44: a second place, disconnected from the schema itself, that goes stale the moment the schema's
shape changes — which is the exact failure this document exists to stop, not repeat.

**Ownership is declared inline, in the schema file that already defines the field**, using JSON
Schema's own `$comment` keyword — valid, standard, ignored by every validator, and already the right
tool for an author-facing note that isn't user-facing documentation. It sits next to the field's
existing narrative `description` (which this project's schemas already write generously) rather than
replacing it:

```jsonc
"authoring": {
  "type": "object",
  "$comment": "OWNER: foundry-approvals-desk. status may only be raised above 'drafted' by the Desk's own promote() path (design/approvals-desk.md §3); every generator, script and sync path must emit 'drafted' or leave the field absent.",
  "properties": {
    "status": {
      "enum": ["drafted", "sound", "final"],
      "description": "drafted: written, unread, grants nothing. sound: substance is right, may be built on and cited. final: the words are right, safe to read aloud verbatim."
    }
  }
}
```

One field gets an `$comment` ownership tag only when it is genuinely contested ground — reachable by
more than one system. Most fields in this project have exactly one obvious writer already and need
no tag at all; tagging everything would be noise, and noise is exactly what makes a real rule stop
getting read. The six review queues and the location's `authoring` block are the first real
candidates.

### Ownership may be split by entity type — but only explicitly, and only per value

**Added 2026-09-22, when `authoring.status` became the first field with two legitimate owners.**
`design/scene-signoff.md` gave scenes their own top rung, `live`, written by
`tools/scene-signoff/seal.mjs` rather than by the Approvals Desk. That is a second writer on a
`lifecycle` field, which is precisely what this document exists to prevent, so it needed either a
refusal or a rule. The rule:

> A `lifecycle` field may have more than one owner **only if each owner's authority is stated as a
> specific set of values on a specific set of entity types**, with no value owned twice. The
> `$comment` names every owner and what each may write. Anything vaguer than that — "the Desk
> generally, but the Sign-Off for scenes" — is the ambiguity, not a resolution of it.

Here: the Desk owns `sound`/`final` on every entity type; the Sign-Off owns `live` and the `review`
block, on scene briefs only. No value has two writers, so there is still nothing to adjudicate —
which is the property that made `lifecycle` a usable category in the first place, and the thing a
split must not give up.

Two tests hold it: `assertNotPromotedStatus()` refuses all three advanced values from any other
caller, and `test/tier1-writer-audit.test.mjs` asserts that exactly one file in
`tools/scene-signoff/` writes the field and that it delegates the decision to the guarded path rather
than assigning the status itself. The canary earned its keep immediately — it caught a *comment* in
`board.mjs` that spelled out the pattern it was warning about.

## 5. Enforcement: a test, because a comment already failed once

Both real collisions this project has actually had — the gazetteer's approve endpoint getting into
its own unguarded review-mode editor (2026-09-13), and the gazetteer/Desk near-collision this
document exists because of (2026-09-18) — happened *despite* a correctly-written comment stating the
rule. A rule that only lives in prose survives exactly until the next tired edit doesn't re-read it.
So the declaration in §4 is necessary but not sufficient; it has to be checked by code.

**The test** (tracked as its own GitHub issue, cross-referenced from `design/approvals-desk.md` §10,
not folded into that epic since it is bigger than one feature):

1. Walk every `data/schema/**/*.schema.json`, collect every `$comment` beginning `OWNER:`.
2. For each declared-owned field, statically or behaviourally confirm that only the named owner's
   code path can write a non-baseline value to it — the same technique `design/approvals-desk.md`
   issue #167 already specifies for the promotion path specifically (*"enumerate every write path in
   the repo and assert none can emit a promoted status"*), generalised to walk the declaration table
   instead of one hardcoded field.
3. Fail loudly, naming the field and the second writer, if two are found — the same "refuse loudly,
   never silently downgrade" principle already used throughout the gazetteer's own write paths.
4. Runs in the same `node --test` suite as everything else (`design/testing-strategy.md`), so it is
   not an opt-in audit someone has to remember to run — it is part of the same green bar.

This turns "did anyone remember the rule" into "does the test pass," which is the same shift
`design/approvals-desk.md` §3 already makes for the promotion path specifically. This document just
says: do that everywhere a schema declares an owner, not only for `authoring.status`.

## 6. What this does *not* claim to fix

Worth being explicit, since overclaiming here would be its own small instance of the disease.

- **It does not consolidate the ~43 storage bins.** That inventory and its resolution stay Ingram's
  architectural call, exactly as `project_ongoing_crisis_data_fragmentation` already states. This
  document's mechanism makes it *safe to leave bins alone* that are genuinely single-owner already,
  and *loud* when a bin turns out not to be — it does not merge anything.
- **It does not retroactively audit every existing field.** Only fields worth tagging (§4) get
  tagged, starting with the ones already known to be contested. A full sweep of every schema is a
  separate, larger piece of work if Ingram wants it, not implied by adopting this rule.
- **It does not replace `design/foundry-module-design.md` §4's Safety section** on why world-document
  writes must go through Foundry's own Document API. That is about *how* a write happens; this is
  about *who is allowed* to make it. Both apply at once to anything Foundry-side.
- **It is not a new store.** No new JSON file, no new schema of its own. The declaration lives inside
  the schemas that already exist; the test lives inside the suite that already exists.

---

## 7. Status

**A proposal, following directly from a decision Ingram already made** (§1's gazetteer/Desk
resolution) rather than a speculative addition. The taxonomy extension (§3) and the declaration
convention (§4) are design calls open for his preference the same as anything else here; the
enforcement principle (§5, "a test, not a comment") is already this project's own stated practice
elsewhere (`design/testing-strategy.md`, the gazetteer's own approve-endpoint comments) and is
proposed here mainly to be applied consistently rather than invented fresh.
