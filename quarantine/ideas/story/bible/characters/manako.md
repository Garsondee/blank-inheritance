# Manako

*The Chemist. Founder of Cicada. The campaign's primary human antagonist — the Watcher itself is the adversary (LEDGER #31) — and the first friendly stranger the party ever meets.*

---

## Read this first

**What he says freely, and what he never says (Ingram, LEDGER #45):** he gives his real name. He'll talk about being a
chemist, about being hazed by the Directorate the same way the party is (he came back empty-handed and was laughed
at, and has spent forty years thinking what he should have done), and about his bitterness toward it. He is fatherly
with recruits, which is part of why he makes an effective cult leader, and he loves tweaking a horrible officer's
nose in a way that hurts nobody. He ruminates on his failures often. **He never mentions the Watcher.** A player
who impresses him gets his phone number; the tea-house message book is gone.

**Handle every scene with him carefully (Ingram, LEDGER #44):** he is too important to put in front of the group
carelessly. Treat each one as a risk of leaking a secret or explaining something early, and check it for both.

**He is the linchpin.** The Watcher — the campaign's adversary, played by Claude — has an objective of its own (to multiply, to expand, to keep observing) and no personality to pursue it with. It took the shape of the strongest coherent signal it could find, and for forty years the only coherent signal on this planet has been this man. **His signal steers it, and it feeds through him. Its behaviour is a portrait of his grievance; its appetite is its own.** Everything else in the campaign runs through him: Nobu's guilt, Shucks's past, Nozomi's refusal, Shinobu's condition, the Claimants the Frames fight, and the party's own investigation, which feeds the thing every time they use a lens. *(Reframed 2026-09-24 to LEDGER #31; this said "the Observer has no personality of its own".)*

**Read `ideas/story/dossier-cicada.md` before writing him at all** — §3 (the fall), §4 (the operator reveal, where "operator" now means steering and supply), §5 (the Pivot, and the slide), and **§13 (the arc)**. This file is the at-the-table voice reference; the dossier is the truth.

**The one thing that must never leak: he does not know what he is to it** — not its servant, and not its master: its steering, and its supply. He believes he is a faithful worshipper interpreting divine intent, and there is nothing to interpret. He can be written talking freely and honestly about anything, because the only fact that matters is one he would sincerely deny.

**A second secret sits above him, and he doesn't know it either** (GM-only, LEDGER #32). **William Charles Baldwin**, the old campaign's mitflit-hunting lord, is alive two hundred years on and is a criminal lord, arming criminal gangs with incredible weapons made by essentially enslaved mitflits (canon). *(Proposal, LEDGER #32:)* his is the organisation Manako works for, at arm's length; Manako has never met him and does not know who is at the top. The reveal is held until the right moment (`ideas/story/bible/characters/william-charles-baldwin.md`).

## What's established

**Manako is an old word for the eye. A man called Eye, who nobody would look at.** And the thing he found grew eyes.

- **A chemist, disgraced.** Handed a shelved, century-old sample nobody senior thought was worth their time. Cracked it open, a fragment of the cloud fed on it and became briefly corporeal, he panicked, a bunsen burner went over, there was a small fire. Job gone, reputation gone.
- **Elderly now, and physically frail.** He runs the corporeal side of an entire war from a position of complete bodily weakness, and has spent decades biding, building and choosing the moment.
- **The first failed demonstration.** Before the conference he tried to show colleagues, and the chemical worked perfectly — the Watcher had simply moved. Nothing happened and he was finished as a serious person. **He still has that speech.**
- **The conference.** Having checked first, he detonated a canister in a crowded hall without warning anyone. A room of scientists concluded a colleague had gone off the deep end.
- **Peaceful, before.** His original aims and methods were peaceful: a small circle, private showings, patience (Ingram, 2026-09-24, LEDGER #28).
- **The Pivot — the campaign's inciting incident.** The Watcher's Envoy, its first tangible child, came to make contact. A police officer, **Hori**, alone with no backup at a local disturbance (LEDGER #33), fired at it in fear, trying to kill what he took for a threat, and shot it dead; one of his rounds missed it and hit the cultist standing behind it — **Aoi**, Manako's wife. **Manako was there, and broke down at the scene.** Hori rushed Aoi to hospital and tried everything to save her, and failed. In the confusion the circle scattered, Manako with them. Nobody planned it. Before: he wanted the city to see. After: he wanted the city to pay. *(Updated 2026-09-24 to LEDGER #31 item 18, then to LEDGER #32 item 15, which puts Manako's breakdown in the street rather than at the hospital.)*
- **Hori, afterwards.** Cleared of any unlawful killing by a very strict police process, including a complex court proceeding on whether the killing was lawful; retired anyway, in self-imposed disgrace; regarded by his colleagues as a man of great honour. Dutiful, honourable, regretful. **He learned Aoi's name at that court proceeding** (LEDGER #33), and outside Manako's own circle he is the one who kept it. He may never have learned who the broken man in the street was (open). **Manako may have been arrested, though not imprisoned, as part of the proceeding, or not** (Ingram's "maybe", LEDGER #33; `open-questions.json#manako-arrested-after-the-envoy`). If he was, it was as a man at a public disturbance, before Cicada existed, so it does not identify him as its founder (LEDGER #17 stands); but a police record with his name in it would exist, for the players to find themselves. *(LEDGER #35: and it would never have reached the Directorate; the police and the Directorate distrust each other.)* `ideas/story/bible/characters/hori.md`.
- **What the Directorate knows of him** (LEDGER #34). He is the scientist it employed, and disgraced, decades ago. It may suspect that the tea-house owner is the same man, and it isn't alarmed; it may even salve its conscience to see him running a successful small business. **It has no link between him and the cult**, which is largely peaceful and largely a non-issue to it until the adventure properly starts: a thin file, not enough to understand the cult's aims, and nothing about the Watcher. *(Proposal: Shucks, who supervised the disgrace, is the likeliest to have noticed.)*
- **The crime lords, and the following.** Grief and angry ambition took him into bad company. He supplies the city's crime lords — at first only with work picked so he never sees what it is part of — and they give him venues, crowds and ways to be noticed. And he starts gathering a following, because he can show people something that is really happening and can only be seen with the chemical he spent his career trying to understand. What he makes for them, specifically, is open. **GM-only:** who is at the top is the second secret above. Baldwin is alive and a criminal lord (canon); that his is the organisation Manako works for, and that Nobu's laundering, the Fixer's crime partners and the heist crews all run through it, are proposals (LEDGER #32; `dossier-cicada.md` §5, §8).
- **Sap — what he synthesises for the movement — is not fuel.** It is raw building material for the Watcher's nanomachines — the generation ship's own stock for its repair nanites (canon since LEDGER #32 item 9) — and what its children are built from when they manifest. The Directorate catalogued it as Inert 12. The fuel in this story is the exotic matter, which is a different thing (LEDGER #31).
- **He was never believed. He was echoed** — and fed on. He already half-suspects the first half and has arranged forty years of his life so the question cannot be asked. He does not suspect the second: the thing had an objective of its own, and he was useful to it.
- **Killing him is the worst available move — and it is the table's to make.** Without him the Watcher loses the only signal steering it and becomes a dangerous, unguidable problem over a populated region. Keeping him alive does not solve it (that needs the mitflit home world); it keeps it guidable until then. *(Updated 2026-09-24 to LEDGER #31 items 5–6.)*

### What he becomes — the slide

*(LEDGER #31, D12, canon. Ingram, verbatim:)*

> "Manako is an extremely gifted scientist and chemist. Think about Heisenberg / Walter White. Not a bad person at the start, but once he develops angry ambition after the death of Aoi he needs access to money, at first the work he does for the criminals is all carefully picked so that he's never exposed to what he's actually taking part in and this allows him to delude himself into thinking he's not too bad. Eventually things slide down hill to the point where he accepts that he's becoming a monster. He doesn't like that or enjoy it, but eventually he accepts it as a role."

**What that means at the table.** The first meeting does not change. The man in the tea house is the man he used to be, and — in the proposed reading in `dossier-cicada.md` §13.1 — he still believes he is that man, because fifteen years of arm's-length work have let him. The kindness is real for exactly that reason. The acceptance comes later, in play, and the party causes it: he stops asking how things are done, the Young One kills someone in a heist, he finds out and does nothing. **After that, play him as a man who does monstrous things without relish.** He doesn't like it. He has accepted it as a role.

### His arc, in five movements

**Patient → Delighted → Cornered → Frightened → Useful.** Each turn caused by the party. Full table, with what turns each one, in `dossier-cicada.md` §13.1. **The Claimants are his mood ring** (§13.2) — what manifests is downstream of which movement he is in, which is why his arc and the campaign's escalation curve are the same curve. The Watcher's objective is its own; the mood ring governs *how* it pursues it, not *why*.

**The slide runs underneath the movements** (proposal, `dossier-cicada.md` §13.1): arm's length through I and II; the acceptance in III; and in IV the one thing he cannot accept — that he was an echo. He can live with being a monster, because a monster chose something.

## Voice

**Register.** Unhurried, precise, warm. An academic's diction worn soft by decades out of academia. Delighted by a good question and visibly out of practice at being asked one. **Openly bitter about the Directorate, and fatherly to recruits** (D045). He talks freely about everything except the Watcher, which he never mentions.

**Tics.**

- Handles the object before he speaks — turns it over, holds it to the light, reads the back.
- Answers the question asked, then the better question they should have asked.
- Apologises for talking too much, and carries on talking.
- Refers to the Directorate with perfect courtesy and no warmth whatsoever.

**Samples** (from the first meeting — `data/scene-briefs/episode-c1e1--the-errand.json`):

> "Ah — no, they wouldn't know that one, and it isn't their fault. Nobody's used that name since before the shop. Before the street, probably."

> "I was a chemist. Not for a long while now."

> "Can I tell you something? You don't have to do anything with it, I just — I think you should hear it, that's all. The people you're going to work for dig things up. They have done since before either of us was born. Whatever they find goes into a vault, and it's never spoken of again. That isn't wicked, mind. Some things ought to be locked away. But it gets to be a habit, keeping quiet. I've just always thought that was worth knowing, about the people you're going to go and work for."

*(Rewritten 2026-09-27, LEDGER #41: the line used to point at the founding charter's painted-over paragraph, which is cut. It is quoted from the scene brief, so change both together.)*

> "If they send you out with another of those, bring it here. It's a kindness to me, mind, not the other way about. Nobody's asked me anything in a very long time."

**Later movements shift the register and nothing else.** Delighted: faster, more generous, more corrections. Cornered: shorter sentences, and he stops apologising for talking. Frightened: he talks *over* things.

## Tone and register — what NOT to do

1. **Do not make him mysterious at the first meeting.** No significant pause, no watching them leave, no ominous parting line. He is helpful and then he finishes his tea. The whole force of the eventual reveal depends on that scene being exactly what it looks like.
2. **Do not play him as insane, or as evil.** Ingram, 2026-09-13: the first meeting must demonstrate "that they aren't an insane evil bad guy." He is a courteous, intelligent, grieving old man who is catastrophically wrong about one thing. **Later, once he has accepted what he is becoming (LEDGER #31, D12), he does monstrous things — and still never with relish.** No gloating, no cackling, no enjoyment. He doesn't like it; he has accepted it as a role.
3. **Do not have him lie.** The cult never lies and neither does he. He is a reliable narrator of everything except the fact he cannot see.
4. **Do not let him recruit them on day one.** He is not pitching Cicada at the tea house. He says one true thing and offers a way to be contacted, and that is all.
5. **Do not let the party kill or capture him early.** Confirmed 2026-09-13. The henchmen are the recurring fights; he is the rare event, perhaps four appearances in the whole campaign. **Late in the campaign, killing him becomes a real choice the table may make — and the wrong one** (LEDGER #31 item 6): without him the Watcher is unguidable. Do not block it. Make sure the evidence that it is wrong has been there to find.
6. **Do not have him explain the Watcher.** He would explain it *wrongly*, sincerely — and a GM improvising his cosmology risks accidentally writing the real answer into his mouth.
7. **Do not hand the table the *conclusion*.** His name is safe and should be given freely when asked — nobody can look him up, because the Directorate has never identified Cicada's founder. What must never be handed over is the deduction: **no NPC ever tells the party that Cicada has an author, and no NPC ever points at him.** Both halves are theirs to assemble. `dossier-cicada.md` §13.5.
8. **Do not let his lines sound written.** Flagged directly 2026-09-19, Ingram reading his own "what he thinks of your employer" line back: *"It sounds a bit like nonsense... this sounds a lot like Fable LLM nonsense dialogue, not what a real human would say."* Two lines fixed the same day (see Voice above) — the failure mode both shared was a clever, symmetrical, self-aware turn of phrase no one actually reaches for out loud ("past tense's doing a good deal of work in that sentence," "the people who feed you"). Before finalizing any line of his, read it as something spoken, not written: real speech hedges, trails off, repeats itself, and reaches for a plain word over a resonant one. **This is flagged as a likely systemic issue across this project's dialogue generally, not unique to Manako** — a full pass hasn't been scoped or run yet; don't assume any other NPC's existing voice samples are clean just because they haven't been flagged.
