# Hori

**Category:** character
**Status:** partial. What happened and who he is are established (LEDGER #28, #31, #32, #33). His voice and the scene where the
party meets him are not written yet.
**Sources:** `ideas/story/bible/LEDGER.md` #28, #31, #32, #33; `ideas/story/dossier-cicada.md` §5; `ideas/story/dossier-observer.md`
§8; `ideas/story/blank-inheritance-v4.md` §2 (its §11, removed 2026-09-25, is carried by this file).

## Read this first

**Hori is a dutiful, honourable and regretful man** (Ingram, LEDGER #31). Fifteen years ago, as a police officer on his own
with no backup, he shot the Watcher's first child dead in fear, and one of his rounds killed the woman standing behind it. He did
everything he could to save her. He has carried it alone ever since. **He is not a villain and must never be played
as one.** He is the campaign's thesis in human form: an ordinary person doing an ordinary human thing, and building
the rest of his life around that moment.

**The party meets him when they are ready to learn a great deal about the inciting incident**, not before. In the
current draft that is around Chapter 11.

## What happened

Ingram, verbatim (LEDGER #31):

> "They accidentally killed Aoi. They shot The Watcher's first child dead in fear at the grotesque alien creature
> appearing before their eyes. They tried to save Aoi's life, they did everything they could but were unsuccessful.
> The Police HQ took them through a very strict process and found them not responsible for an unlawful killing but Hori
> voluntarily retired in self imposed disgrace and their work colleagues feel that Hori went above and beyond the call,
> that he did the right thing and that Hori is a person of great honour."

And on the scene itself (LEDGER #32, N13), verbatim:

> "That is correct. An accident, the police officer was trying to kill what he thought was a threat, the bullets did
> kill The Watcher's child but also killed Aoi. Manako broke down upon the scene, Hori rushed to hospital with Aoi and
> in the confusion the cults members scattered, including Manako."

And on the day as he lived it, and how he learned her name (LEDGER #33, N22), verbatim:

> "Hori didn't know Aoi's name until they were part of a complex court preceding to determine if the killing was
> lawful. At the time the cop was just dealing with a local disturbance, without back up and things escalated.
> Manako may have even been arrested but not imprisoned as part of this, but maybe not. I see no issues here."

- **He was on his own.** He was dealing with a local disturbance, with no backup, and it escalated (LEDGER #33).
  *(Proposal, LEDGER #33: the disturbance was the circle's meeting itself, people gathering in a public street to meet
  something nobody else could see, and drawing a crowd.)*
- **The Envoy** came to a meeting Manako's circle had arranged, a disfigured doll of a person lurching into a public
  street. Hori fired in fear, trying to kill what he took for a threat. **It died.**
- **One of his rounds struck Aoi**, Manako's wife, who had come to meet it.
- **Manako was there, and broke down at the scene.**
- **Hori rushed her to hospital** and did everything he could. She died anyway.
- **In the confusion the circle scattered, Manako with them.** None of them went with her to the hospital.
  *(Corrected 2026-09-24, LEDGER #32 item 15: this said Hori was still standing at the hospital when an unremarkable
  middle-aged chemist arrived and came apart in front of him. The breakdown happened in the street, and the circle,
  Manako included, scattered from it.)*
- **So Hori may never have learned who the broken man in the street was.** He was trying to keep her alive, and in the
  confusion the man was gone. Likely, but **open, not decided** (open question 4). If Manako was arrested as part of the
  court proceeding (Ingram's "maybe", LEDGER #33), Hori may have seen him there.
- **Police HQ put him through a very strict process**, including **a complex court proceeding to decide whether the
  killing was lawful** (LEDGER #33). It found him not responsible for an unlawful killing. **That court is where he
  first learned Aoi's name.**
- **He retired anyway, in self-imposed disgrace.** His colleagues think he went above and beyond, did the right
  thing, and is a man of great honour. He does not agree with them.

## The file, and the name

**He learned her name in court** (LEDGER #33). Not on the day, and not in the car to the hospital: he heard it at the
proceeding that decided whether the killing was lawful.

**The Directorate's file never learned it.** *(Proposal, LEDGER #31, extended in #33.)* The police examined Hori's use
of force, and the court cleared him. The **Directorate** took jurisdiction over the alien and the scene, and its report,
written at the scene on the day, filed the dead woman as **one deceased, unidentified**, a cultist casualty in a
public-order incident. A young Directorate officer on that cordon, later **the Soldier**, helped write it
(`dossier-cicada.md` §9). Nobody went back to correct it: to the Directorate the cult was a non-issue, and its dead
were not worth the paperwork (LEDGER #34). **Its official line** (LEDGER #35): *a cultist was killed during a confrontation in which someone magically summoned a monster.*

*(LEDGER #35, canon.)* **The police and the Directorate distrust each other**, and what happens with the police doesn't
reach the Directorate. The proceeding was the police's, and its record stayed with the police. *(Corrected 2026-09-24,
LEDGER #35: this said the hearing was held behind closed doors because an alien was part of the evidence. That was
Claude's proposal, and it is withdrawn.)* **So, outside Manako's own circle, Hori is the one person who carried her name out of that
room and kept it.** He has been waiting fifteen years for somebody to ask.

The police's record is a second, harder way for the party to find her name, harder because the police don't help the
Directorate's people (LEDGER #35), and any survivor of the old circle is a third. Hori is the easiest of the three, and the only one who will simply tell them.

*(Corrected 2026-09-24, LEDGER #33: this section used to leave open how he learned her name, and called him "the only
person alive who knows her name". The court heard it too; he is the one who kept it.)*

## What he gives the party

- **Aoi's name.** It is the key to understanding Manako, and nobody else will give it to them.
- **What the Envoy was.** It wasn't an attack; it was a greeting, and she had come to meet it. That is the start of
  the party understanding what the Watcher learned that day.
- **How to be believed.** A decorated officer telling the truth about his worst day. The save condition on the Peril
  Roster was always simply: *being asked, and being believed.*

## Ties

- **Hori and the Commander** *(named Oda in older notes; the name is unsettled)*. Both men fired when frightened, one in a street and one in a cockpit. They have never met, and
  never will unless the party arranges it (v4 §2).
- **Hori and the Soldier.** The officer who fired and the officer who filed the report. Neither knows the other
  carries the same day.
- **Hori and Manako.** Manako was at the scene and saw who fired (LEDGER #32). Hori may never have known who the man
  breaking down in the street was. Whether Manako has ever gone looking for Hori is open. Manako may also have been
  arrested, though not imprisoned, as part of the court proceeding, or not (LEDGER #33, open:
  `open-questions.json#manako-arrested-after-the-envoy`). If he was, the two men were in the same proceeding, and Hori
  may know his face or his name.
