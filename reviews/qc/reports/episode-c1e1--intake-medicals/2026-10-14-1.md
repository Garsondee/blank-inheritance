*qc-episode-c1e1--intake-medicals-2026-10-14-1 · claude-sonnet-5-5 (a worked example written by hand in a Claude Code session, to show the format before the bot's first run) · 2026-10-14 · scene hash `b0d3824c9fc31880`*

## How this scene works

- **Why the players come:** The gate guard waves every new recruit on to the infirmary, so this is the first room all six players share. They come because there is nowhere else to go yet, and they leave with a Section Nine card each.
- **What happens:** A slow queue under two contradictory signs (FORMS FIRST taped over MEDICALS FIRST). The clerk hands out Form 12-B with every name already typed, and boxes 4 and 19 both ask for the mother's maiden name. Then Doctor Mordant, a skeleton in a white coat, sees each player alone: a blood sample, two or three questions about them, and the same sentence for all six: your screen came back irregular, it does that, nothing to worry about. Six optional looks sit around that (the form, the staff talking, his badge, his hand, asking him straight, working out the screen). In the corridor the clerk hands out six Section Nine cards with the track left blank, and the scene is really about whether the six notice they were all flagged.
- **Where it leads:** A bell, and a staff officer marches the whole intake to the drill hall for Assembly, where Section Nine sits together at the front because of those cards.
- **What it already does well:** The read-aloud puts the room in front of the table in three short paragraphs, smell first. Every check is optional, costs nothing, and goes wrong harmlessly (a wrong belief, never a lost option). The all-six moment can't be missed: if nobody compares cards, a candidate from another queue asks out loud.
- **What the GM has to carry:** Six private conversations in front of the whole table is the heavy part. The GM gives each player different questions and says "irregular" the same way six times while five people watch. The optional checks give the five watchers something to do.

## What I'd change (5)

### 1. Let someone who knows machines read the typed form

*route · I'm high on this*

- **Right now:** Only Perception can spot that the typed name on the form was struck by a faulty typewriter. A character built around machines, repairs or making things has nothing to roll here, even though the clue is a worn typewriter.
- **The change:** Add Crafting at the same DC 15, with a line of its own: the e is struck low and faint by a worn type bar, and the acceptance letter has the same fault, so one machine typed both. The four results stay exactly as they are.
- **Why:** The clue is literally a typewriter fault, so a maker's eye is the most natural way to find it. It also gives builds without good Perception a way into the first clue of the episode, and stops Perception carrying three of the scene's six checks.
- **What else it touches:** Nothing else in the scene. It tells the table the same fact the check already tells (the forms were typed ahead, on the acceptance-letter machine), so what is held back stays held back. The plan row doesn't mention this check.
- **Risk:** Low. Crafting also appears on the hand check (proposal 3), so a mechanic gets two rolls in this scene. That seems right to me, because both are about made things, and they come at different points in the scene.
- **What I'd do:** Add it.
- **Your call:** Should a Crafting character be able to read the form?

### 2. Let a reader of paperwork work out the screen

*route · I'm high on this*

- **Right now:** Only Medicine or Nature can work out what the screen tests for, but the best clue in the room is on the form, not in a blood sample.
- **The change:** Add Society at the same DC 17, with a line of its own: the form's result box offers four words, and "irregular" is not one any standard screen uses, so this is a custom test. The four results stay exactly as they are.
- **Why:** The form is the scene's own evidence. Your note on the blank form says the IRREGULAR box is printed, not added by hand, and that somebody expected the result often enough to have it typeset. Reading that is a Society skill (how institutions run and what their paperwork is for), not a medical one. Without it, a player who studied the form closely has nothing to roll for the deduction the form was built to prompt.
- **What else it touches:** No facts. The critical success already says the test is for something inherited; the Society line stops short of that on purpose, so the heritable marker stays held back until later. Nothing else in the scene changes.
- **Risk:** Low. The check is still rolled blind and a critical failure still gives a harmless wrong belief.
- **What I'd do:** Add it.
- **Your call:** Should reading the form's printed result box count as working out the screen?
